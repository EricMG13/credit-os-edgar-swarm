## Findings

### Bottom line
JetBlue's fundamental credit profile is weak: the airline has real franchise assets but its earnings do not cover interest, so creditors depend on USD 1,626 million of cash and investments, a USD 600 million undrawn line and aircraft collateral until margins recover.

Derived EBITDA of USD 115 million for the twelve months to September 2026 covers cash interest of USD 493 million only 0.23x, and free cash flow was USD 1,193 million negative. Net debt of USD 7,135 million is as reported at September 30, 2026; pro forma figures for the October 2026 slot purchase and the expected USD 250 million aircraft financing are not established. The primary downside is fuel and labour cost outrunning fares before USD 1,835 million of debt falls due in 2029.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5–7, 11, 14; calculation F01; calculation F05; calculation F10; calculation F21

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest evidence against a weak reading is that revenue momentum and liquidity are better than the margin suggests. Third-quarter unit revenue rose 18.4% while capacity rose 2.3%, management says about 87% of the higher fuel cost was recaptured, and the adjusted operating margin improved to a 3.9% loss from a 4.1% loss a year earlier. JetForward is said to have added USD 470 million of cumulative earnings to June 2026 toward USD 850 million to USD 950 million a year by the end of 2027. Liquidity of USD 2.2bn includes an undrawn line to October 2029, a further USD 250 million of aircraft financing is expected, and management says about USD 7 billion of assets are unencumbered, so secured refinancing of the 2029 maturities is plausible.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 29, 33–34, 51; EX-99.1_2026-10-26_ex991-earningsreleaseq32026.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2; EX-99.1_2026-07-28_ex991-earningsreleaseq22026.htm.jsonl, page 2

These points are management statements or unaudited, and the unencumbered figure is not reconciled with the 66% of owned property and intangibles that the quarterly report says is pledged or committed. The view would improve if the fourth-quarter margin gain of 1 to 2 percentage points arrives with fuel near the USD 4.64 guide, the USD 250 million financing funds, cash and investments stop falling and the 2027 operating profit stays on track. It would worsen if cash and investments approach the USD 1,211 million of maturities due through 2028, if remaining unencumbered assets are given a first lien, or if pilot and inflight settlements add cost without matching fare recapture.

### Company description
JetBlue is a point-to-point passenger airline with 298 operating aircraft, six focus cities and a loyalty programme, selling mainly leisure and visiting-friends-and-relatives travel in the Northeast, Florida and the Caribbean. Passenger travel is 84.9% of nine-month revenue and loyalty-related revenue 12.8%, which gives some visibility through prepaid tickets and card spending, but demand is discretionary, seasonal and exposed to weather and air traffic control in the home market. The model supports debt service only when unit revenue covers fuel and labour, and at present it does not, so cash-flow visibility rests on advance bookings and the loyalty programme rather than on operating profit.

### Ownership and group structure
JetBlue is a widely held public company with no sponsor, controlling holder or dividend in the supplied filings, so there is no leakage to owners and no owner to support creditors. JetBlue Airways Corporation is the issuer or borrower of the instruments the notes describe, with JetBlue Loyalty LP as co-issuer of the loyalty financing and unnamed subsidiary guarantors; who owns that partnership and which subsidiaries guarantee are not stated. Business & governance assessed sponsor and governance risk as Medium because ownership and board facts are missing, not because of observed extraction.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power
Revenue is discretionary, cyclical and concentrated in a congested home region, but it is not structurally declining. FY2025 revenue fell 2.3% on weaker demand and tariff uncertainty, then nine-month 2026 revenue rose 13.7%, mostly on price: third-quarter yield rose 21.2% and average fare 19.2% while revenue passengers rose 2.2% and load factor fell 1.5 percentage points. Domestic and Canadian flying is 60.4% of nine-month revenue and Caribbean and Latin America 34.0%, so regional shocks in the Northeast, Florida or the Caribbean reach most of the revenue. Price recovery protects margin only if fares keep pace with fuel, which they did for about 87% of the increase in the third quarter by management's measure. Tickets and credits expire within about a year and the air traffic liability of USD 2,696 million is 1.66x cash and investments, so bookings fund operations until they weaken.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_jblu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 81, 86–87, 125; 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 27, 34; EX-99.1_2026-10-26_ex991-earningsreleaseq32026.htm.jsonl, page 1; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F13

### Cost structure and margin resilience
Margins are not resilient to input shocks because the largest costs are either unhedged or contractually rising. Fuel was 28.5% of nine-month operating expense and salaries, wages and benefits 32.9%, and fuel was unhedged at September 30, 2026. Unit cost excluding fuel rose 7.0% in the third quarter against capacity growth of 2.3%, with about four points of disruption cost from weather and air traffic control, and the pilot contract has been amendable since February 2025. Operating expense rose USD 1.26 for each USD 1.00 of extra revenue in nine months, so operating leverage is currently negative. Costs can be defended only through fares and capacity discipline, and management cut planned second-half capacity by at least two to three points for that purpose.

### Competitive forces in credit terms
- Rivalry is intense and rivals are larger: the annual report says most competitors have greater financial resources, and industry consolidation or capacity added in JetBlue's focus cities can undo yield gains, which matters because pricing is carrying the margin recovery. Spirit's exit from Fort Lauderdale is described by management as one of the most significant opportunities in years, which helps unit revenue until others redeploy capacity.
- New entry is limited by airport access rather than capital: slots at JFK and LaGuardia, 30 Boston gates and a Terminal 5 lease to 2042 protect the network, and the 22 LaGuardia slots bought for USD 58.5 million add to it, but the bankruptcy of Spirit, the seller, shows that access alone does not make fares profitable.
- Substitutes are a limited threat: the annual report cites surface transport and video conferencing, which matter most when the economy is weak, and they are less relevant on the Caribbean, Latin American, transcontinental and transatlantic routes that make up 67.6% of 2025 capacity, so substitution is not a primary credit driver.
- Buyer power is high: leisure customers compare fares, and the loyalty programme and co-brand cards are the main tools that blunt it, which is why loyalty revenue growth of 13.1% in nine months matters.
- Supplier power is high: four aircraft and engine types from Airbus, Pratt & Whitney and IAE support every flight, two aircraft were grounded for engine availability at September 30, 2026 and the Fly-Fi service depends on one antenna supplier, so a fleet defect cannot be diversified away.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_jblu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 26, 30, 43–44, 78; 10-Q_2026-06-30_jblu-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 33, 44; calculation F17

### Macro and policy factors in credit terms
Policy and macro factors are a primary credit driver here, because they set fuel, capacity and cost. Political and regulatory: air traffic control shortages and slot rules constrain Northeast capacity, federal rules require US citizen control, tariffs on aircraft and parts could raise fleet costs, and the company cites Middle East conflict as a source of fuel volatility. Economic: fuel is the dominant sensitivity and the interest-rate exposure is smaller, with USD 2.1bn of floating-rate debt so that a 100 basis point rise would add about USD 21 million of annual interest. Social: 49% of crewmembers are unionised and new groups have organised in 2026, which raises the cost of settlements. Technological: a new revenue management system and a premium cabin programme are supporting yield, but their contribution is management's claim.

### Credit-translated SWOT
Strengths: Boston, San Juan and Fort Lauderdale seat shares and airport access; loyalty revenue up 13.1% and a premium product; an all-Airbus fleet with 288 of 298 aircraft owned; liquidity of USD 2.2bn with an undrawn line to October 2029; and no sponsor leakage. Weaknesses: cover of 0.23x, negative free cash flow, unhedged fuel, labour exposure, a structure that is 88.2% secured and equity at 8.4% of assets. Opportunities that could improve credit quality: Fort Lauderdale growth after Spirit's exit, the United partnership and BlueFirst premium cabin, and fuel falling toward the USD 3.00 assumption. Threats that could weaken it: fuel at the USD 4.64 fourth-quarter guide or higher, union settlements, Northeast disruption, engine groundings and delivery delays, and the Northeast Alliance antitrust case, whose loss cannot be estimated.

The financial profile is weak on coverage, free cash flow, leverage and equity, and adequate only on near-term liquidity. The main supports are USD 2.2bn of liquidity, no maturity larger than USD 582 million before 2029 and USD 8.0bn of pledged assets plus management's claim of about USD 7 billion unencumbered against USD 8,450 million of debt; the constraints are cover of 0.23x, negative cash flow, a secured-heavy structure and a shrinking cushion. ### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds
Over the next 12 to 24 months the outlook turns on fuel against fares. Management guides the fourth quarter to 18.0% to 22.0% unit revenue growth and a 1 to 2 percentage point margin gain, full-year adjusted operating margin of a 4.5% to 6.5% loss and sustained operating profit in 2027; tailwinds are Spirit's exit, loyalty and card growth and BlueFirst from December 8, 2026, and headwinds are fuel guided at USD 4.64 per gallon for the fourth quarter, labour talks, Northeast disruption and a USD 1,211 million maturity run through 2028. Over three to five years the 77 firm aircraft deliveries, the 2029 maturity cluster and the 2028 earnings target are the defining items: delivery of the order book adds capital needs just as USD 1,835 million of debt falls due, while the earnings target assumes fuel at USD 3.00 per gallon. Each tailwind supports revenue and margin; each headwind pressures cash flow and refinancing confidence.

### Downside scenario
The realistic downside starts with fuel staying near the USD 4.64 fourth-quarter guide through the winter while Northeast weather and engine groundings cut completed capacity again and the pilot and inflight settlements add cost. Fare recapture then falls short of the 87% reached in the third quarter, so the operating margin stalls near the 5.1% loss of the third quarter instead of improving by 1 to 2 percentage points, and free cash flow keeps running near the USD 1,193 million negative twelve-month rate. Cash and investments of USD 1,626 million fall toward the USD 1,211 million of maturities due through 2028; covenants on minimum liquidity and minimum balances, whose levels are not printed, could bind before cash is exhausted. Management levers are capital spending cuts, further secured financing including the USD 250 million expected in the fourth quarter, delivery deferrals and capacity cuts, and resilience looks sufficient to avoid a liquidity crisis through 2027 only if those levers are used. The primary downside path is cash burn from unhedged fuel and labour cost into the 2029 maturity cluster, financed by ever more secured debt that pushes the USD 995 million of unsecured debt further back in the queue.

### Overall credit view
Overall, JetBlue presents a weak but not hopeless business profile supported by an established Northeast and Caribbean network, a loyalty programme that earns 12.8% of revenue and an unsponsored, undistributing capital policy, but credit quality is constrained by earnings that cover cash interest only 0.23x, negative free cash flow and a structure that is 88.2% secured. Cash-flow durability appears weak because fuel and labour absorb the revenue gains. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on secured aircraft and loyalty-backed funding, on the undrawn line to October 2029 and on delivering the margin recovery management guides. The primary downside path is cash burn into the 2029 maturities of USD 1,835 million. Further analysis would require covenant levels, a lender-defined EBITDA, the proxy statement, ratings and market prices. Fundamental credit is complete. Top risk: unhedged fuel and labour cost outrunning fares while cash and investments fall.
