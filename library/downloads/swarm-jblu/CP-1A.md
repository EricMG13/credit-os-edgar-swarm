## Request
Business & governance for JETBLUE AIRWAYS CORP (JetBlue Airways Corporation, NASDAQ: JBLU) as of 2026-11-05 under the credit snapshot pathway. The scope is the consolidated group's business, ownership, operating model, transactions and governance to Q3 2026, with FY2025 as the prior year. JetBlue has no sponsor, so the sponsor-behaviour work is applied to public-company governance and financial policy.

## Findings

### Bottom line
JetBlue has no sponsor or controlling holder in the supplied filings, so creditors face no distribution risk but have no owner to call on: a USD 823 million nine-month loss cut equity to USD 1,337 million, leaving collateral as the protection.

Secured debt is 88.2% of gross debt and 66% of owned property and intangibles is pledged or committed, so the USD 995 million of unsecured debt sits behind collateral that has grown. We rate sponsor and governance risk Medium: conduct toward lenders is orderly and disclosure is audited, but ownership and board facts are missing.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 8, 11, 47; calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C05; calculation C06

### Key findings
No sponsor, controlling holder or dividend stream is visible, so the creditor risks that come with a leveraged buyout, such as dividend recapitalisations, fees and sponsor-led exchanges, are absent from the record. The shares are spread over a few hundred holders of record, no dividend has been paid, there is no repurchase programme, and federal law caps foreign voting at 25%. The picture is incomplete because the beneficial ownership table sits in a proxy statement that was not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_jblu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 29, 80, 165–167; 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 1

Management has financed the airline by pledging assets rather than by selling equity, which pushes unsecured creditors to the back of the queue. Secured debt is 88.2% of USD 8,450 million gross debt, loyalty-programme financing alone is 32.3% and aircraft financings are 50.4%, so the only unsecured debt is USD 460 million of 2029 convertible notes and USD 535 million of government payroll-support loans, set against USD 8.0bn of pledged assets.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 47; calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C03; calculation C04; calculation C05

Conduct toward lenders has been refinancing rather than extraction, but the encumbrance of assets has risen. The record shows USD 425 million of 0.50% convertible notes retired in 2024 with proceeds of the new 2.50% notes, the remaining USD 325 million repaid at par on April 1, 2026, and a USD 500 million new aircraft-secured loan; no exchange offer, uptier, drop-down or distribution appears in the supplied filings. Against that, pledged assets grew to USD 8.0bn from USD 7.3bn in nine months, the pledged or committed share of owned property and intangibles rose to 66% from 61%, and a further USD 250 million aircraft financing is expected in the fourth quarter.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_jblu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 97, 132–135; 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 13–14, 47; calculation C05; calculation C21; calculation C22

The equity cushion is shrinking fast: the nine-month net loss reduced stockholders' equity by USD 783 million, or 36.9%, so equity is now 8.4% of total assets against 12.8% at year-end 2025. Employee plans lifted the weighted share count by 4.2% and no outside equity was raised. Conversion of the 2029 notes could add 19.9% to the share count, but the only market value in the filings, from June 30, 2025, points to a share price of roughly USD 4.13, below the USD 6.12 conversion price, so conversion should not be counted on as deleveraging.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 5–8, 15; 10-K_2025-12-31_jblu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 135; calculation C06; calculation C07; calculation C08; calculation C19; calculation C26

The failed Spirit merger shows a willingness to pursue a large acquisition, but it was reversed and the stated policy is now capital-light growth. Terminating the merger on March 1, 2024 cost USD 532 million of special items, including a USD 69 million break-up fee; since then the only acquisition in the filings is USD 58.5 million of LaGuardia slots, and the financing terms of the merger are not in the supplied filings.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_jblu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 88, 91, 161–162; 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 31, 33, 44; calculation C23

Earnings, not structure, is the weak link: fuel absorbed 85.7% of the nine-month revenue gain. Revenue rose 13.7% to USD 7,749 million but fuel cost rose USD 798 million against a USD 931 million revenue gain, the fuel price rose 50.2%, nothing is hedged, and a 10% price increase would add about USD 376 million over twelve months. The operating loss margin was 6.6% for nine months, and management expects fourth-quarter margin to improve by 1 to 2 percentage points and sustained operating profit in 2027.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 33, 38–41, 53; EX-99.1_2026-10-26_ex991-earningsreleaseq32026.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation C13; calculation C15

The franchise is concentrated in a congested home market and one growth bet, which makes revenue more volatile than the six-focus-city structure suggests. Management says 95% of routes touch one of six focus cities, Northeast weather and air traffic control constraints cut completed capacity and added crew cost in 2026, and Fort Lauderdale, where capacity rose more than 65% and unit revenue nearly 15% in the third quarter, is the main growth driver.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_jblu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 29–31; EX-99.1_2026-04-28_ex991-earningsreleaseq12026.htm.jsonl, page 3

Loyalty is the most encumbered earnings stream: it supplies 12.8% of nine-month revenue, up 13.1% while the airline lost money, yet its financing is 32.3% of debt. Lenders hold a first lien on certain loyalty-programme collateral whose exact scope is not published, and the most significant points-sale contract is with one card partner, so the programme concentrates both earnings and claims on a few contracts.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_jblu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 101, 129–131; 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 11; calculation C03; calculation C10; calculation C11

Labour is the largest expense line and its pending contract cost is not disclosed: 49% of crewmembers are unionised, the pilot contract has been amendable since February 2025 with talks still open, the inflight contract becomes amendable on December 13, 2026, and salaries and benefits already take 35.1% of revenue.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_jblu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 21–22, 88; 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20; calculation C14

Reporting is adequate for monitoring but incomplete for judging governance. The annual statements carry an unqualified audit opinion and controls are reported effective, yet board composition, beneficial ownership and related-party dealings sit in the proxy statement, and the liquidity measure changed basis in the third quarter. Sponsor and governance risk is therefore Medium: no extraction conduct is documented, but unsecured creditors are being subordinated step by step and the evidence on who controls the company is missing.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_jblu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 111–112, 165–167; 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 33, 47, 53

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest evidence against a cautious reading is management's claim of real headroom. It reports about USD 7 billion of unencumbered assets, including USD 2.8 billion of aircraft and engines, and USD 2.2bn of liquidity including the undrawn USD 600 million Citibank line. The adjusted operating margin improved to a 3.9% loss in the third quarter from a 4.1% loss a year earlier, JetForward is said to have delivered USD 470 million of cumulative incremental earnings before interest and tax to June 2026, and management targets earnings of at least USD 1.00 per share in 2028. That target implies net income of at least USD 377 million at today's share count against the USD 823 million nine-month loss, and it assumes jet fuel at USD 3.00 per gallon.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-26_ex991-earningsreleaseq32026.htm.jsonl, page 2; EX-99.1_2026-07-28_ex991-earningsreleaseq22026.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 29, 33; calculation C25

The unencumbered figure is management's own, unaudited and on a different basis from the 66% of owned property and intangibles that the quarterly report says is pledged or committed, so it cannot yet be treated as collateral headroom. The view would improve if the proxy statement shows no large holder with board control, the USD 250 million aircraft financing closes without further pledging of loyalty collateral, fuel falls toward the USD 3.00 per gallon assumption and a positive operating margin arrives in 2027. It would worsen to High if remaining unencumbered assets are given a first lien, a covenant waiver or exchange offer appears, or an activist or concentrated holder is shown to seek distributions.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-26_ex991-earningsreleaseq32026.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 47

### Limitations
- Board composition, committees, beneficial ownership above the reporting threshold, executive pay and related-party dealings are in a proxy statement that was not supplied, so the conclusion that no controlling holder exists rests on the filings' silence and would reverse if a large holder or board nominee is found.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_jblu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 165–167
- Who owns JetBlue Loyalty LP, which co-issues the loyalty financing, and which subsidiaries guarantee it are not stated, so the structure map cannot show which legal entity holds the loyalty collateral; the secured-share conclusion holds, but who bears each claim is not established.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_jblu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 129–131
- Management's unencumbered-asset figures are unaudited and no appraisal or asset list is given, so the contrary case for collateral headroom cannot be verified and the Medium risk level leans on the pledged-asset disclosure alone.
  Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-26_ex991-earningsreleaseq32026.htm.jsonl, page 2
- No current share price or agency rating is supplied and the one market value is sixteen months old, so how near the 2029 convertible notes are to conversion and how the market prices the unsecured layer are not established; the 19.9% dilution is a ceiling, not an expectation.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_jblu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 59, 135
- The JetForward earnings and 2028 per-share targets are management outlook, unaudited and conditional on fuel, so the recovery path in the contrary case is not evidence of improvement.
  Basis: EX-99.1_2026-07-28_ex991-earningsreleaseq22026.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3

### Transaction summary and new debt issues
JetBlue has no sponsor transaction, leveraged buyout, dividend recapitalisation or share repurchase in the supplied filings. The large transaction on record, the merger with Spirit signed on July 28, 2022, was terminated on March 1, 2024 at a cost of USD 532 million of special items, and the financing terms of the merger are not in the supplied filings.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_jblu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 88, 161, 168; calculation C23

The 2026 financing was secured and incremental. On April 14, 2026 JetBlue agreed up to USD 500 million of debt secured by owned A321, A320 and A220 aircraft, borrowed all USD 500 million by June 30, 2026 at maturities from 2033 to 2036, and in the third quarter satisfied the conditions to draw a further USD 250 million that it expects to fund in the fourth quarter. On April 1, 2026 it repaid USD 325 million of 0.50% convertible notes and USD 1 million of interest, using in part the USD 100 million held in escrow under the Citibank facility.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 13–14; 10-Q_2026-06-30_jblu-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-03-31_jblu-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13; calculation C21; calculation C22

The structure these deals sit on was built in August 2024. JetBlue and JetBlue Loyalty LP issued USD 2,000 million of senior secured TrueBlue notes due 2031 and borrowed a USD 765 million TrueBlue term loan due 2029, both secured by a first lien on loyalty-programme collateral, and JetBlue separately issued USD 460 million of 2.50% convertible senior notes due 2029 with a conversion price of about USD 6.12 per share. The Citibank revolving facility of USD 600 million runs to October 21, 2029 and was undrawn at September 30, 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_jblu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 129–130, 135, 137; 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 14; calculation C03; calculation C08

Two smaller transactions add assets or vendor support. JetBlue bought 22 LaGuardia slots from Spirit's bankruptcy estate for USD 58.5 million, completed in October 2026 after a USD 6 million deposit, and it received vendor credits of up to USD 105 million from the engine maker, usable until the end of 2027, which lower future cost rather than provide cash.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_jblu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 31, 33, 44–45; 10-Q_2026-06-30_jblu-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 53
