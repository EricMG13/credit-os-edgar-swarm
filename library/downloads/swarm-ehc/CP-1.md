## Findings

### Bottom line

Encompass Health's leverage is low and still falling, but only because earnings are growing: debt has not fallen and 2026 shareholder returns exceeded free cash flow after joint-venture distributions, so creditor protection rests on management's capital allocation.

At 2026-09-30 total debt was USD 2,599.1 million: 1.67x trailing reported EBITDA and 1.88x the issuer's own Adjusted EBITDA, which deducts joint-venture partners' income. Liquidity was USD 947.6 million, and no significant debt falls due before 2028. Interest cover is 10.55x on cash interest paid for FY2025, but cash interest for the last twelve months and the covenant ratios achieved are not printed, so cover at the latest date and covenant headroom are not established.

### Key findings

Leverage fell on earnings growth, not repayment. Total debt was USD 2,497.8 million at 2024-12-31, USD 2,490.8 million at 2025-12-31 and USD 2,599.1 million at 2026-09-30, while reported EBITDA rose from USD 1,186.6 million in FY2024 to USD 1,404.1 million in FY2025 and USD 1,552.6 million over the last twelve months. Total debt to reported EBITDA therefore moved from 2.11x to 1.77x to 1.67x, and net debt to reported EBITDA from 2.03x to 1.72x to 1.62x. Debt rose by USD 108.3 million in the nine months to 2026-09-30, the period in which the company replaced its revolving facility, used new 2034 notes to redeem half of the 2028 notes and sold a further USD 100.0 million of the 2034 notes in August 2026 to repay part of the revolver.

The issuer's own earnings measure is the more conservative guide to cash that reaches the parent. The issuer states only an Adjusted EBITDA, which deducts the income of noncontrolling interests, USD 160.9 million in the nine months to 2026-09-30, and adds back share-based compensation. Reported EBITDA, calculated here from pre-tax income, interest and depreciation, is USD 134.9 million higher than the issuer's measure for that period. On the issuer's measure total debt is 2.26x, 1.96x and 1.88x Adjusted EBITDA at the three dates, in line with the issuer's own rounded 2.0x at 2025-12-31 and 1.9x at 2026-09-30. Joint-venture partners received USD 141.8 million of distributions in the nine months, so the deduction is a cash cost and not an accounting convention.

Cash generation is strong but is largely absorbed by growth spending and distributions. In FY2025 net cash from operating activities of USD 1,175.6 million funded USD 736.4 million of capital spending and USD 152.1 million of joint-venture distributions, leaving USD 287.1 million; dividends and repurchases took USD 229.1 million of that and left USD 58.0 million. In the nine months to 2026-09-30 the same sequence left USD 198.7 million before dividends of USD 57.9 million and repurchases of USD 203.7 million, a shortfall of USD 62.9 million in a period when total debt rose by USD 108.3 million. Growth capital spending was USD 451.2 million in those nine months and capital spending ran at 12.88% of net operating revenues, so the pace at which leverage keeps falling is mainly a management choice.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against a benign reading is that growth and returns are being financed at the margin by debt. Capital spending of USD 617.2 million in nine months compares with a 2026 plan of USD 920 million to USD 995 million and about USD 535 million still to spend on projects under construction, and the board raised the repurchase authorization in July 2026. Receivables absorbed USD 89.5 million of cash in the nine months against USD 18.8 million a year earlier, and revenue reserves for bad debt rose to 2.7% of revenue in the third quarter from 1.9%, which the issuer attributes to a billing and collecting disruption after a system conversion. If operating cash flow does not cover the remaining planned spending, or repurchases continue above free cash flow, leverage on the issuer's measure would stop falling. The view would improve if debt were repaid from free cash flow while EBITDA keeps growing, and weaken if trailing EBITDA fell, if the revolver were drawn to fund distributions, or if the covenant ratios, once supplied, showed less headroom than the compliance statements imply.

### Limitations

EBITDA is not stated by the issuer, so reported EBITDA is calculated and every leverage and cover ratio rests on that calculation; the issuer-adjusted ratios are shown beside it. Last-twelve-month figures are constructed from the annual and nine-month statements, and the nine-month statements are unaudited. The covenant ratios achieved, their headroom, the leverage-test definition's pro forma adjustments and the 2026 revolver margin are not printed, and the executed credit agreement and indentures are not supplied, so covenant capacity is not established. Cash interest paid is printed only for the full years, cash taxes only for the full years and nine months 2026, and the third-quarter cash flow statement only as operating cash flow in the slides. Year-end 2024 revolver availability uses the commitment the annual report states in the present tense. The figures agree with the evidence trace in the Source readiness work and none of it is corrected.
