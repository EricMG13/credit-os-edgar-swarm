## Findings

### Bottom line

Encompass Health is a sound credit with low leverage whose main threat is Medicare payment policy and claim review, so creditors are protected today but depend on management keeping growth spending and repurchases within cash flow.

Total debt of USD 2,599.1 million at 2026-09-30 was 1.88x the issuer's Adjusted EBITDA, liquidity was USD 947.6 million and no significant debt matures before 2028. Medicare and Medicare Advantage paid 81.8% of nine-month revenue, and claim review will reach about 63% of Medicare claims from 2027-01-01, up from about 11.9%. In the nine months, shareholder returns exceeded the cash left after capital spending and partner distributions by USD 62.9 million while total debt rose USD 108.3 million, and about USD 955 million of repurchase authorization remains. No rating is assigned, and covenant headroom is not established.

### Key findings

Payment policy and claim review, not demand, are the dominant credit risk. Medicare and Medicare Advantage supplied 81.8% of nine-month revenue, and the final rule for fiscal 2027 lifts Medicare rates by only 2.3% from 2026-10-01, below the 3.5% to 4.0% rise the company guides for salaries and benefits per employee, a gap of 1.2 percentage points to 1.7 percentage points on its largest payer. Claim review compounds this: coverage rises from about 11.9% of Medicare claims to about 63% on 2027-01-01, 5.29x as many, and in Alabama, the first state reviewed, no company hospital reached the validation rate needed to opt out of full pre-claim review in the second or third cycle. Bad-debt reserves reached 2.7% of Q3 revenue against 1.9% a year earlier, and receivables absorbed USD 89.5 million of cash in nine months against USD 18.8 million. Delay and denial therefore reach cash flow before they reach reported demand.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a sound reading is the pairing of rising shareholder returns with wider claim review. Returns have begun to exceed cash after growth spending, total debt rose USD 108.3 million in nine months, lender limits on restricted payments were relaxed in March 2026 and about USD 955 million of repurchase authorization remains; at the same time review reaches about 63% of Medicare claims from 2027-01-01, no Alabama hospital reached the opt-out rate in the second or third review cycle, and a Q3 bad-debt reserve of 2.7% of revenue shows how quickly collection can slip. The strongest evidence in favour is the company's own room to respond: growth capital spending guided at USD 695 million to USD 755 million is 6.26x to 6.80x the USD 111.1 million fall in Adjusted EBITDA that would take it back to its FY2025 level, where total debt would be 2.05x Adjusted EBITDA, and management guides Q4 bad-debt reserves back to 2.0% to 2.5% of revenue.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ehc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 31, 35, 44; 10-K_2025-12-31_ehc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 174; EX-99.2_2026-10-28_ehcq32026earningsslides_.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 8; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C3; calculation C12; calculation C13; calculation C25

The view would weaken if the bad-debt reserve stayed above 2.7% of revenue after the effect of the Q2 system conversion fades, if the revolver were drawn to fund repurchases, or if total debt over Adjusted EBITDA rose above its FY2024 level of 2.26x. It would improve if debt were repaid from free cash flow while earnings keep growing, or if the executed credit agreement and indentures showed wide room under the leverage and restricted-payment tests.

### Limitations

- No rating, market price or peer was supplied, so the comparison of this business with typical leveraged-credit issuers and any relative-value view are not established, and the sound-credit conclusion rests on the issuer's own history and filings.
- Review outcomes are given only in words for the Alabama hospitals, with no validation rates, denial amounts or payment timing, so the sensitivity to a payment delay of 10 days is an illustration and not a forecast, and the claim-review risk to cash flow could be larger or smaller than shown.
- No independent forecast was run, so the conclusion that earnings keep growing and leverage keeps falling reads management's 2026 guidance and not a tested projection.
- The earnings-fall scenario is anchored to the FY2025 level of Adjusted EBITDA and not to any covenant test, so it says nothing about how far earnings could fall before a covenant test bound, and no covenant headroom is stated.
- The issuer's guided cash interest is USD 19.5 million below the cash interest paid reported for 2025 for reasons the filings do not give, so the cash-basis interest-cover conclusion could be somewhat lower than the ratios shown.

### Company description and operating model

Encompass Health owns and operates inpatient rehabilitation hospitals in 39 states and Puerto Rico, 67 of them joint ventures with health systems, and reports one segment, so the credit rests on a single service line paid mostly by one federal payer. Revenue of USD 6,338.1 million over the last twelve months is inpatient discharges multiplied by a net price per discharge: in the nine months discharges rose 5.20% and price 3.89%, and new hospitals supplied 2.9 percentage points of the 5.2% discharge growth, so about half of volume growth needs capital before it earns. The model supports cash-flow visibility because payment is set by rule in advance and the conditions treated are not deferred, but it weakens liquidity preservation whenever growth capital is committed ahead of earnings.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ehc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 34, 36; EX-99.2_2026-10-28_ehcq32026earningsslides_.htm.jsonl, page 9; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C21

Cyclicality is low and concentration is high. Medicare and Medicare Advantage together paid 81.8% of nine-month revenue, 92% of patients arrive from acute-care hospitals and Florida and Texas hold 31.21% of hospitals, so a payment rule, a referral change or a regional disruption each reaches a large share of cash flow at once. Cash generation is real, with operating cash flow of USD 1,303.7 million over the last twelve months, but 73.10% of nine-month capital spending is growth, so the cash left for debt reduction depends on management's choices more than on the business.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ehc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-K_2025-12-31_ehc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 110; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C4; calculation C10; calculation C21

### Ownership and group structure

Encompass Health is a listed company with no sponsor or controlling shareholder identified, so sponsor-extraction risks are absent, but the beneficial-ownership table sits in a proxy statement that was not supplied and concentration of holdings is not established. Subsidiary guarantors guarantee the credit agreement, which is secured by personal property, and give full and unconditional senior unsecured guarantees of the notes, while non-guarantor subsidiaries are not summarised separately, so the claims that rank ahead of the notes at those subsidiaries cannot be measured. Joint-venture partners hold noncontrolling interests of USD 821.4 million plus redeemable interests of USD 58.6 million, and their share of income equals 13.66% of nine-month reported EBITDA, so that part of group earnings does not reach the parent.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Volume is the stronger driver and price is set mostly by rule. Discharges rose 5.7% in Q3, 3.0% same-store and 2.7% from new hospitals, and Q3 occupancy of 77.0% leaves room to fill existing beds, which is cheaper growth than building. ### Competitive forces

Rivalry is local and moderate. The industry outside the company is fragmented and the main competitors are rehabilitation units inside acute-care hospitals, which can be formidable where a large system dominates the local market, and one public company that mainly provides other post-acute care also runs 38 rehabilitation hospitals. Because 92% of patients are referred by acute-care hospitals, a system that opens its own unit can divert referrals, a volume risk market by market and not a pricing risk, since Medicare sets the price.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ehc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 14

New entry is limited by capital and regulation but the limit is eroding at the edges. Certificate-of-need laws cover about 36% of licensed beds, yet North Carolina repealed its requirement for certain providers in July 2026 and the company accelerated the amortization of its asset there by USD 2.9 million. Entry still needs hospital-scale capital and Medicare qualification, including the rule that a set share of admissions must come from listed medical conditions, so it is slow, but where approval is no longer needed a rival's new beds would compete for the company's referral sources directly.

### Downside scenario

The scenario that fits this issuer is payment friction meeting pay growth. From 2027-01-01 about 63% of Medicare claims, up from about 11.9%, go through pre-claim or post-payment review, while the Medicare rate rises 2.3% and pay per employee 3.5% to 4.0%. In Alabama, the first state reviewed, no company hospital reached the opt-out validation rate in the second or third cycle, so full pre-claim review stayed in force there. The first break is in collections: review lengthens the time to payment and lifts denials, bad-debt reserves stay at the 2.7% of revenue seen in Q3 instead of easing to the guided 2.0% to 2.5%, and the receivables build of USD 89.5 million in nine months repeats.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ehc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 31, 35; 10-K_2025-12-31_ehc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 174; EX-99.2_2026-10-28_ehcq32026earningsslides_.htm.jsonl, page 7; calculation C11; calculation C27

Revenue is delayed before it is lost, so cash falls before earnings. Holding the reserve 80 bp above its year-earlier level costs USD 12.88 million a quarter, 3.71% of Q3 Adjusted EBITDA, and each 10 days of added delay on the Medicare revenue under review ties up about USD 67.0 million. A delay of 30 days, with Adjusted EBITDA also falling back by USD 111.1 million to its FY2025 level, would absorb USD 312.1 million, which is 32.94% of liquidity of USD 947.6 million, and total debt would be 2.05x Adjusted EBITDA against 1.88x. The length of delay is an assumption for illustration, because the filings print no payment timing for reviewed claims.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ehc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 31, 35; calculation C1; calculation C12; calculation C14; calculation C15

Management has levers well above that size. Growth capital spending is guided at USD 695 million to USD 755 million, repurchases were USD 203.7 million in nine months and dividends are about USD 79 million for 2026, so liquidity distress is avoided if growth and repurchases are slowed when collections weaken. The scenario fails only if repurchases and the committed projects of about USD 535 million continue while collections are delayed and the revolver funds the gap, which would raise secured debt ahead of the notes and shorten the runway to the USD 436.0 million of 2028 maturities. We do not assume default: the evidence shows enough resilience to avoid distress with an ordinary management response, but covenant headroom is not established, so the point at which the tests bind is unknown.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Encompass Health presents an adequate business profile supported by owned scale in a rule-priced, non-discretionary service and an earnings base that is growing, but credit quality is constrained by payer concentration under widening claim review, capital allocation that has outrun cash flow and pay growth above the Medicare update. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because issuer-defined free cash flow converts 60.78% of Adjusted EBITDA before growth capital, although taxes and growth spending are lowering the share. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on market access for the 2028, 2030 and 2031 maturities, which are 68.51% of face value, backed by liquidity of USD 947.6 million and a revolver to 2031. A comparison with typical leveraged-credit issuers in the sector is not established because no peer or rating data were supplied. The primary downside path is slower Medicare collection under wider review combined with pay growth above the rate update while repurchases continue. Further analysis would require the covenant ratios achieved, the executed credit agreement and indentures, claim-review outcome data, ratings, peer data and the proxy statement.
