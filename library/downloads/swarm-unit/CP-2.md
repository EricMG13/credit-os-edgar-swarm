### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better view is rising earnings guidance together with cheaper secured funding. Management raised the 2026 adjusted EBITDA outlook midpoint by USD 25.0 million between March and July, consumer fiber revenue is growing at 19% pro forma, and the July 2026 securitization carried a weighted average coupon of about 6.18% against 8.25% on the 2031 secured notes, so the cost of capital is falling as the securitization market absorbs the build. Floating-rate exposure is small, with USD 345.0 million unhedged and USD 3.5 million of added annual interest for a 100 bp rise, and management states that liquidity covers the next twelve months and beyond. The view would improve if service revenue declines narrow toward the low single digits, adjusted EBITDA exceeds the top of the outlook range, free cash flow after grant funding turns positive, the secured repayment offers retire the full USD 500.0 million and both revolvers are extended beyond 2027. It would worsen if second-half adjusted EBITDA falls below the USD 676.3 million implied by the outlook midpoint, further guarantor assets move into the ring-fenced pool without matching repayment, or a securitization or revolver extension cannot be completed on similar terms.

### Limitations

- Trailing adjusted EBITDA omits Windstream's earnings for July 2025 and no full-year pro forma figure is printed, so leverage of 7.13x is overstated and cover of 1.94x understated against a like-for-like base; the weak assessment holds, but the gap to an average assessment is smaller than the ratios suggest.
- The sources give no capital expenditure outlook and no split between maintenance and growth spending, so the conclusion that free cash flow stays negative until the build ends rests on first-half run rates and would change if spending is slowed.
- Net proceeds and issuance costs of the July 2026 securitization are not stated, so liquidity after June 30, 2026 and net debt after July are not established and the liquidity runway may be understated.
- Kinetic and Uniti Solutions did not exist before the merger, so the 8.93% service revenue decline rests on the issuer's unaudited pro forma comparison and carries the uncertainty of that presentation.
- Ratings, market prices and a peer set are not supplied, so the conclusion that market access is open but costly is drawn from the issuer's own transactions and cannot be tested against secondary pricing or peers.
- The second-quarter report prints no covenant levels or reported ratios, so covenant headroom is not calculable and the conclusion that covenants are not the near-term constraint rests on the stated compliance alone.

### Company description

Uniti is a three-segment fiber and broadband operator formed by the August 2025 merger of Old Uniti and Windstream. Kinetic sells broadband and voice to consumers, businesses and wholesale buyers in former Windstream markets, Fiber Infrastructure leases dark and lit fiber, mainly to carriers and hyperscalers, and Uniti Solutions sells managed network services to enterprises. First-half 2026 revenue of USD 1,897.2 million was 56.14% Kinetic, 24.27% Fiber Infrastructure and 19.59% Uniti Solutions, and no customer exceeds 10% of revenue after the merger, which removes the single-tenant dependence that gave Windstream 68.3% of Old Uniti's revenue.
Basis: calculation C19; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_unit-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-06-30_unit-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 47–48

The mix supports cash-flow visibility only in part. Fiber Infrastructure and managed services rest on contracts, with remaining performance obligations of about USD 2.3 billion at year-end, whereas Kinetic's consumer base faces cable, fiber overbuilders, fixed wireless and satellite competition and is losing copper customers. The credit consequence is that debt service depends on Kinetic's conversion from copper to fiber and on contract renewals at Fiber Infrastructure, not on lease income, which ended with the merger.

### Ownership and group structure

Ownership is concentrated but not controlling. Elliott holds about 25.2% of the common stock and may designate two of nine directors, the two largest holders and insiders hold about 36.1%, and Old Uniti's five-member board forms the board majority. The credit facilities and notes are issued by Uniti Services LLC and its finance subsidiaries, with the credit facilities guaranteed by Uniti Group Inc., Old Uniti and subsidiary guarantors, while the asset-backed notes sit at bankruptcy-remote unrestricted subsidiaries that no other group entity guarantees. ### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is structurally declining at Kinetic and Uniti Solutions, growing at Fiber Infrastructure and lumpy through sales-type leases. Pro forma service revenue fell USD 83.6 million in Q2 2026, and the USD 13.0 million gain at Fiber Infrastructure came mainly from price increases for transport and TDM services and favorable reserve adjustments, which are not repeatable volume growth. Consumer fiber revenue grew 19%, but the DSL base that it replaces is still larger than fiber revenue in FY2025, and customer bankruptcies in Q2 pushed credit loss provisions up by USD 12.1 million. The credit consequence is that pricing power is limited to the fiber product and the contract base, so earnings durability rests on the conversion pace.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry and substitutes press the legacy base hardest. The annual report names cable operators as the largest competitor for residential and small business broadband, with fiber overbuilders including utilities and municipalities, fixed wireless and satellite all increasing, and says few locations in the footprint lack meaningful competition. Cable and wireless pricing keep churn pressure on copper customers, which is why service revenue falls, and entrants add to the cost of the fiber build because customers must be won rather than inherited.

The credit consequence is that Fiber Infrastructure earnings are strong but lumpy and renewal-dependent, while Kinetic competes for every subscriber.

Revenue of USD 3,537.1 million over the trailing twelve months and a national fiber footprint give shock absorption, and no customer exceeds 10% of revenue, but Kinetic is an incumbent facing cable, fiber overbuilders and fixed wireless, so scale does not protect its margin. Diversification across three segments is real but uneven, with Kinetic at 56.14% of first-half revenue and losing copper customers.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months the outlook is for flat to slightly weaker earnings and continued negative free cash flow. Management expects adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,450 million to USD 1,500 million, which implies USD 676.3 million in the second half, and net interest expense of USD 805 million, which implies USD 421.1 million, a second-half cover of 1.61x. Tailwinds are record consumer fiber additions, record fiber bookings and the July 2026 securitization at a weighted average coupon of about 6.18%; headwinds are the roll-off of first-quarter sales-type lease revenue, the January 2027 revolver step-down, the December 2027 revolver maturity and the USD 306.5 million of convertible notes due December 1, 2027.

### Downside scenario

A realistic downside starts with legacy erosion outrunning fiber conversion while the hyperscaler contracts stop producing sales-type lease revenue. Service revenue keeps falling at the pro forma pace of 8.93% in Q2 while Kinetic revenue slips from USD 539.0 million and contribution margin from 42.37%, so second-half adjusted EBITDA lands at or below the USD 676.3 million implied by the outlook midpoint and cover on net interest of USD 421.1 million stays near 1.61x. Capital expenditure staying at the Q2 level of USD 450.0 million then keeps the free cash outflow near the first-half USD 459.8 million, which the USD 1,447.0 million of liquidity would cover 3.15 times if nothing were raised.
Basis: calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C6; calculation C7; calculation C14; calculation C15; 10-Q_2026-06-30_unit-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 73–74; EX-99.1_2026-07-30_unit-ex9912q26.htm.jsonl, page 2

The financial transmission runs through the revolvers and the securitization market: liquidity would be drawn down before the revolvers expire in December 2027, further securitizations would move assets from the guarantor pool to the ring-fenced pool, and the restricted group's cover of 1.82x would weaken. Management levers are slowing success-based capital expenditure, monetizing non-core assets, grant funding and further securitization; the sources do not state how much spending is discretionary. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress through 2027 on the stated liquidity, with no default assumed, but refinancing the 2028 and 2029 maturities and extending the revolvers become the decisive tests, observable at the third quarter and fourth quarter reports.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Uniti presents an adequate business profile supported by national fiber scale, contracted fiber demand and demonstrated market access, but credit quality is constrained by leverage of 7.13x, negative free cash flow of USD 459.8 million in the first half and shrinking recurring service revenue. Cash-flow durability appears weak because capital expenditure absorbs all adjusted EBITDA and the 2026 outlook implies lower second-half earnings. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the securitization market, the extension of both revolvers beyond December 2027 and execution of the fiber conversion. The primary downside path is legacy erosion outrunning fiber conversion while debt keeps funding the build. Business profile against typical leveraged-credit issuers is not established because no peer set is supplied. Further analysis would require executed debt documents, third quarter 2026 results and the net proceeds and use of the July 2026 securitization.
