## Findings

### Bottom line

Governance and business facts support a Medium sponsor and governance risk for creditors: the largest shareholder holds 42.2% of the voting shares and is affiliated with four of eight directors, and distributions are large while acquisitions have enlarged the group. Dividends declared were USD 1,048 million in 2025 and USD 755 million in the first nine months of 2026, which compares with equity free cash flow of USD 867 million for the same nine months.

### Key findings

Control rests with one family group, and creditors should read every capital decision with that in mind. Atlas Investissement, ultimately owned by the Niel family, held 42.2% of the voting shares excluding treasury shares at 31 December 2025, and the Niel family group reported 44.1% including shares it expected to buy by 30 June 2026. Four of eight directors are affiliated with it, the company is not a controlled company under Nasdaq rules because the holding is below 50%, and the annual report itself says the holder can influence director elections and corporate actions. The consequence for creditors is that dividend, acquisition and related-party decisions can reflect the shareholder's priorities, while the board's audit committee, which is wholly independent, is the main check.
Basis: 20-F_2025-12-31_tigo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 68; 20-F_2025-12-31_tigo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 157; 20-F_2025-12-31_tigo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 162–163; 20-F_2025-12-31_tigo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 295; calculation B07

Shareholder distributions are large against current earnings and absorb most free cash flow. Declared dividends were USD 1,048 million in 2025, against net profit attributable to owners of USD 1,316 million, and USD 755 million in nine months 2026, against net profit attributable to owners of USD 316 million and equity free cash flow of USD 867 million, so declared dividends were 0.87x equity free cash flow. A dividend payable of USD 579 million sat on the balance sheet at 30 September 2026 and the board states a policy of sustaining or growing dividends while maintaining a prudent capital structure. The mechanism is that payouts competing with acquisition funding and debt repayment for the same cash leave little retained cash to reduce leverage, and dividends are limited to distributable reserves, with USD 757 million of retained profits at 30 September 2026 not distributable.

The consequence is integration and restructuring cost, with Colombia carrying USD 116 million of one-off costs in nine months, and growth figures that cannot be read like for like.

Related-party exposure to the controlling shareholder's group is small in flows but has two features worth monitoring. The balance sheet showed USD 195 million payable to the Iliad Holding group at 31 December 2025 against USD 2 million a year earlier, and the filings do not state what it is. Separately, the Chile acquisition in February 2026 was made through a vehicle owned 49% by Millicom and 51% by NJJ Cactus, which the filings do not describe as a related party although its name matches the NJJ Holding that the related-party note says Xavier Niel owns, with a Millicom call option in the fifth and sixth years and a later option for the partner to buy Millicom's interest. The consequence is that any later funding of those options or of the vehicle's losses, which already exceed Millicom's recognised interest by USD 41 million, would draw group cash, and whether that is a related-party use of cash is not established.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for lower risk is that all eight directors are non-executive, the audit committee consists only of independent directors and reviews related-party transactions, the auditor reported effective internal control, no amendment, priming or exchange affecting creditors is documented, and management has set a year-end 2026 leverage target of below 2.5x. The view would move to Low if the target is met while dividends stay within equity free cash flow, if the debt documents show restricted-payment limits that bind the shareholder, and if the Chile options are priced and funded without recourse to the group. It would move to High if declared dividends continue to exceed internally generated cash, if distributions or related-party funding are paid from new borrowing, or if the controlling shareholder's holding passes 50% and the group elects controlled-company exemptions.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Between October 2025 and April 2026 the group completed six acquisitions or buy-outs that together changed its scale, and it sold most of its tower portfolio. Uruguay (enterprise value USD 440 million, closed 7 October 2025) and Ecuador (enterprise value USD 380 million, closed 30 October 2025) added mobile operators and their debt and leases. In Colombia the group bought out its partner's UNE shares for about USD 571 million on 29 January 2026, acquired Telefonica's 67.5% of Coltel for about USD 214.4 million on 6 February 2026, of which USD 169 million had been paid at that date, and bought the remaining Coltel shares on 27 April 2026. Coltel brought USD 1,424 million of other debt and financing and USD 515 million of lease liabilities.
