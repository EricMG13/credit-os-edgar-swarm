## Findings

### Bottom line

Thor's low leverage, large liquidity and distant maturities protect creditors through another weak year, but earnings have fallen three years running and payouts now exceed free cash flow, so the trend, not the level, is the risk to watch.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

Reported EBITDA fell 12.00% to USD 541,912 thousand in fiscal 2026, lifting total debt to EBITDA from 1.50x to 1.60x, yet cash plus unused facility availability of USD 1,296,988 thousand is 5.79x one year of payouts and 98.09% of debt principal falls due in fiscal 2030 and fiscal 2031. Cash use is the swing factor: dividends and repurchases of USD 223,891 thousand exceeded free cash flow of USD 168,836 thousand.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense and entry is easy: about 80 manufacturers compete in the United States and Canada, the principal rivals are Forest River and Winnebago Industries, and the industry has low barriers to entry, so share and price are contested and pricing power is limited. Buyer power is high because one consolidating dealer is 13.0% of sales and two floor-plan lenders decide how much inventory dealers can carry, which can cut orders quickly. Supplier power is concentrated in chassis, where a limited number of suppliers allocate supply in shortages and chassis cannot easily be substituted, and substitutes include used vehicles and other leisure spending, which gain in downturns. Each force acts on volume and margin, not on solvency, and Thor's scale and low leverage let it absorb more of that pressure than a smaller maker.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Thor presents an adequate business profile supported by North American market leadership, a three-way segment split and a low-leverage balance sheet, but credit quality is constrained by three years of falling earnings, payouts above free cash flow and contingent dealer obligations.
