## Findings

### Bottom line
Excelerate is a contracted LNG-terminal and gas-supply business whose credit strength rests on long-term take-or-pay income from a mix of state-owned, utility and industrial customers, so creditors should weigh counterparty, concentration and deployment risk, and the control held by one shareholder, above scale.

The contracted base is real but moving: the Iraq terminal start has slipped from the third quarter of 2026 to the second quarter of 2027, four customers took 50% of nine-month revenue, and one holder controls 72.5% of the voting power. Contract expiries, executed debt documents and board composition are not supplied, so legal protection and renewal risk are not established.

### Key findings

- Contracted terminal income is the credit anchor but not the majority of revenue: terminal services were 45.70% of nine-month 2026 revenue and the rest was LNG, gas and power sales, which pass most commodity cost through and carry a thinner margin, since the cost of LNG, gas and power equalled 81.59% of those sales. Minimum contracted cash flows at the 2025 year-end were USD 3,315.2 million for terminal services with a weighted average remaining term of 5.8 years, and USD 17,031.2 million for LNG, gas and power with 9.3 years.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ee-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ee-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ee-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25–26; calculation C01; calculation C09
- Counterparty and concentration risk is the main revenue risk, because four customers each took at least 10% and together 50% of nine-month 2026 revenue, and the largest alone took 16%. Customers include state-owned energy companies in emerging markets, so credit support depends on sovereign-linked payment performance; the filings report no write-down of trade receivables on lease or time charter services in the nine months of 2026 or 2025.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ee-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ee-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-K_2025-12-31_ee-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; calculation C03
- Control sits with one shareholder and the company is a controlled company relying on exemptions from independence requirements: a holding company controlled by Kaiser owns all Class B shares and 72.5% of the combined voting power, and also receives payments under a tax receivable agreement. Distributions to public and Class B holders are pro rata, but the revolver permits general distributions only while liquidity is at least USD 200.0 million and leverage is at most 3.25x, with separate baskets for tax, tax receivable agreement and overhead payments, and no sponsor extraction beyond pro rata payouts is documented.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against a cautious reading is the size and tenor of contracted revenue: remaining performance obligations on long-term customer arrangements were USD 18,329.6 million at 2026-09-30, terminal customers carry most operating costs, management says contracts are regularly amended and extended, and the filings report no write-downs of lease or time charter receivables in the nine months of 2026 or 2025. The controlling holder has also supported obligations, giving an uncapped construction and operating guarantee for a deepwater port and a letter of credit for its decommissioning, and no dividend recapitalisation, priming or creditor exchange appears in the filings.
The view would improve if executed debt documents showed tight limits on distributions and asset transfers, a contract schedule showed most terminal charters running well beyond the 2029 revolver maturity, and the Iraq and Colombia terminals started on the revised dates. It would worsen if a state customer delayed payment, force majeure turned into termination, or leverage moved toward the 3.25x level at which general distributions stop being permitted.

### Limitations

- Contract expiry dates and day rates by terminal are not in the extracted text, so the conclusion that contracted income anchors credit cannot be dated and stays qualitative until a contract schedule is supplied.
- No executed credit agreement or indenture was supplied and a seventh amendment is not described, so the restricted-payment gate of 3.25x and USD 200.0 million, taken from the 2025 annual report, may have changed and the control conclusion rests on a summary.
- Board composition, the stockholder's agreement terms and the income-statement related-party amounts are not in the extracted text, so the governance conclusion is limited to the controlled-company structure and cannot assess board independence or fees.
- Customers are anonymised as lettered customers, so the counterparty conclusion cannot rest on any named credit standing or country limit.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues
The defining transaction is the purchase of New Fortress Energy's Jamaica business, which closed on 2025-05-14. The initial cash price of USD 1,055.2 million fell by about USD 28.7 million after adjustments for cash, debt, expenses, working capital and inventory to USD 1,026.5 million, buying the Montego Bay and Old Harbour LNG terminals and the Clarendon power plant. The company recorded goodwill of about USD 235.0 million and customer-relationship intangibles of USD 369.0 million, and the business contributed USD 262.5 million of revenue and USD 53.9 million of net income from the closing date to year-end 2025. The company printed pro forma 2025 revenue of USD 1,376.5 million and net income of USD 201.7 million, as if the purchase and its funding had occurred on 2024-01-01.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ee-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 148–149; 10-K_2025-12-31_ee-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 87; calculation C05

The new debt was one issue: the operating partnership's USD 800 million of senior unsecured notes due May 2030, issued at par under an indenture dated 2025-05-05 and guaranteed by certain direct and indirect restricted subsidiaries. Interest is paid each May and November. The proceeds, with USD 201.8 million of net equity proceeds from 8,000,000 new Class A shares and cash on hand, paid for the acquisition, repaid the term loan in full and covered fees. Alongside it the revolver was amended three times in 2025: the Fourth Amendment on 2025-03-26 added acquisition and debt baskets and a broader collateral maintenance covenant, the Fifth on 2025-04-21 extended maturity to March 2029 and raised commitments to USD 500.0 million, and the Sixth on 2025-09-26 gave more flexibility for investments and restricted payments. A Seventh Amendment dated 2025-12-31 is listed as an exhibit to the annual report but not described.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ee-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 103–105; 10-K_2025-12-31_ee-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 120; 10-K_2025-12-31_ee-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 155–157; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ee-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–19

No new debt was raised in the nine months to 2026-09-30. Instead the company signed growth commitments that will need funding: the acquisition of an LNG carrier in July 2026 for a first floating storage and regasification conversion, with a ten percent downpayment in the third quarter and the remainder due in the first quarter of 2027; a conversion contract awarded to Seatrium in October 2026; and a share repurchase program of up to USD 75.0 million approved in December 2025, under which USD 36.246 million of authorisation remained at 2026-09-30. Neither the carrier price nor the conversion contract value is stated, so the borrowing need for 2027 is not established.
