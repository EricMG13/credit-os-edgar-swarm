## Findings

### Bottom line

Phinia's fundamental credit profile is adequate: a higher-margin aftermarket arm, moderate leverage and no sizeable maturity before July 2028 support debt service, but creditors depend on management holding shareholder returns to free cash flow after the stoba purchase.
Basis: calculation Q7; calculation Q12; calculation Q14; calculation Q11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_phin-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 15

Net leverage was 1.35x at 30 September 2026 on reported EBITDA, or 1.64x after the USD 136 million stoba purchase paid on 1 October 2026, and USD 813 million of liquidity was measured before that payment, while the figure after it is not established. The primary downside path is a softer original-equipment market while tariff-timing gains reverse and buybacks continue, which would lift leverage ahead of the 2028 revolver and 2029 note maturities.
Basis: calculation Q12; calculation Q16; calculation Q5; calculation Q15; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Key findings

The business profile is adequate: LTM sales of USD 3,619 million, 62.05% of nine-month 2026 sales in vehicle-facing Power Systems and 37.95% in aftermarket-facing Aftermarket Solutions, with General Motors 18% of FY2025 sales and 63% of sales outside the United States. Combustion and hybrid products and a customer at 18% of sales make the profile cyclical, so scale supports debt service only while vehicle volumes and the largest customer hold.
Basis: calculation Q1; calculation Q7; calculation Q18; 10-K_2025-12-31_phin-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-12-31_phin-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10

Aftermarket Solutions is the main credit support because it earns the higher margin and a disproportionate share of profit: 17.1% segment margin against 10.8% at Power Systems, and 49.17% of segment adjusted operating income from 37.95% of sales in the nine months of 2026. Management says growth in the vehicle parc supports aftermarket demand, so this profit pool should be less tied to new-vehicle build rates, although the filings give no aftermarket volumes through a downturn.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a better view is that Phinia has stayed cash generative, kept net leverage near 1.3x on the issuer's own definition before the stoba purchase and holds BB+ and Ba1 ratings with stable outlooks as stated in the FY2025 annual report. Free cash flow covered dividends in every year shown and reported EBITDA rose in FY2024 through a sales decline, so the profile could be called strong. That case loses force because profit growth in 2026 rests on tariff timing and currency, one customer is 18% of sales, the history holds only one mild decline, and the covenant definitions that would test leverage are not supplied.
Basis: calculation Q3; calculation Q5; calculation Q6; calculation Q9; calculation Q12; 10-K_2025-12-31_phin-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 57

The view would improve if the first full stoba quarter adds EBITDA without further revolver drawing, returns stay at or below adjusted free cash flow, and the July 2028 revolver is extended well before maturity. It would weaken if the tariff gains reverse, Power Systems margin falls below its 10.8% level, or buybacks continue while the revolver stays drawn.

### Limitations

- No peer set, agency report, market quote or sector dataset is supplied, so the adequate profile is judged against Phinia's own history and risk factors; comparable issuers or a current agency view could move it up or down.
- The supplied history holds one mild volume decline and no severe downturn, so the margin and cash-conversion judgement has not been tested by a deep cycle; a steeper fall in sales would weaken it.
- No source states stoba's own earnings, debt or cash, or the company's position after the payment, so the leverage and liquidity conclusions carry a one-quarter lag and leverage after the purchase could differ from the pro forma 1.64x in either direction.
- Covenant definitions and headroom are not available, so moderate leverage is measured against the company's own ratios and not against a maintenance test; a tight definition could make the covenant the binding constraint.

### Company and group structure

Phinia is a standalone, listed maker of fuel systems, electrical systems and aftermarket products, spun off from BorgWarner on 3 July 2023, with two reportable segments, Power Systems and Aftermarket Solutions after a 1 September 2026 rename. No sponsor or controlling shareholder is identified, the former parent is a counterparty to a settlement and tax agreement rather than an owner, and the debt is issued by the parent with guarantees from certain domestic restricted subsidiaries, so creditor protection rests on the debt agreements and on management policy more than on owner support. The 52.5% joint venture in India is equity-accounted and not controlled, so its earnings reach creditors as equity income rather than consolidated cash.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_phin-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_phin-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 102

### Revenue, margin and cash conversion

Revenue is cyclical and partly pass-through rather than recurring. OEM sales run on negotiated annual contracts and long-term supply agreements subject to production schedules, and the FY2025 sales increase of USD 80 million came from tariff recovery of USD 38 million and currency and other of USD 45 million while volume and mix were USD 2 million negative. Pricing power is limited: customer pricing added USD 2 million in FY2025 and took USD 7 million off the nine months of 2026, and tariff recoveries are contractual pass-throughs that earn no margin.
Basis: calculation Q22; calculation Q27; 10-K_2025-12-31_phin-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-12-31_phin-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 51; 10-Q_2026-09-30_phin-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33

Cost structure gives a margin that is defended rather than expanded. Reported EBITDA margin was 12.00% in FY2023, 12.64% in FY2024 and 12.12% in FY2025, and 13.40% over the last twelve months, with supplier-cost savings of USD 25 million offsetting employee-cost increases of USD 25 million in the nine months of 2026. Net research cost of USD 105 million is partly customer-funded, with 44.74% of gross R&D reimbursed, and unallocated corporate cost took 22.86% of segment adjusted operating income in FY2025, so fixed overhead limits how far margins fall with volume.

### Competitive forces and external factors

Competitive rivalry is high: major competitors including Robert Bosch, Cummins and Denso are larger and have more diverse portfolios, so price pressure and the need for continued research spending limit margin expansion. Entry barriers are moderate, supported by the company's patents and long-term supply agreements, which reduces the threat of new entrants. The substitution threat is the main long-run risk, because faster electric-vehicle adoption would shrink combustion-linked demand, although management cites hybrids, alternative fuels and aerospace and defence as offsets. Buyer power sits with a few OEMs, led by General Motors at 18% of sales, and customers often expect annual price reductions; supplier power is limited by dual sourcing and long-term contracts, supplier concentration is not stated, and supplier costs were a USD 25 million benefit in the nine months of 2026.

### Downside scenario

The realistic downside starts with the softer original-equipment market that management already expects, continuing into 2027 while tariff-timing gains reverse as refunds are returned to customers. The first operating break would be Power Systems, where third-quarter 2026 segment adjusted operating income already fell USD 8 million and the margin slipped to 12.1% from 13.3%. Lower volume at that margin, with overhead fixed, would cut EBITDA and free cash flow; in FY2023, the weakest year shown, free cash flow was USD 100 million, which still covered dividends. If buybacks continued at the 2026 pace of USD 165 million over nine months, the shortfall would be met from cash and the revolver, lifting net leverage above the pro forma 1.64x, with the revolver maturing in about 19 months and the 2029 secured notes after that.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Phinia presents an adequate business profile supported by an aftermarket arm that earns 49.17% of segment profit from 37.95% of sales, but credit quality is constrained by shareholder returns that match or exceed free cash flow, earnings that lean on tariff timing and currency, and one customer at 18% of sales. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow was positive in every period shown and spending can be cut. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on extending the July 2028 revolver before the 2029 secured notes approach and on leverage staying near 1.64x pro forma. The primary downside path is a softer original-equipment market with reversing tariff gains and continued buybacks. Further analysis would require stoba's financials, the covenant definitions and a maturity schedule at the latest balance sheet. Top risk: shareholder returns and debt-funded acquisitions lifting leverage before the 2028 and 2029 maturities.
