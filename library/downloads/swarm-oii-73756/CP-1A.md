## Findings

### Bottom line
Oceaneering is a listed public company with no sponsor or controlling shareholder identified in the documents supplied, so creditor risk sits in the company's own financial policy, which has been creditor-aligned: no dividend since 2017, modest buybacks and a voluntary refinancing.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_oii-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-K_2025-12-31_oii-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 48; calculation A10; calculation A13

The main operating risk is concentration: Subsea Robotics produced 52.03% of segment operating income on 30.72% of 2025 revenue, and demand follows offshore energy spending. Legal capacity could not be tested because the credit agreement and indenture are not in the file, so the low reading rests on conduct.

The shares are listed on the New York Stock Exchange, no holder is named in the reports supplied, and the ownership table sits in a proxy statement that is not in the file; one consolidated subsidiary has a small minority interest. Decisions on distributions therefore rest with a board that, by its own account, reviews the dividend each quarter.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest case against a low-risk reading is that documented conduct is not documented capacity. The board reviews the dividend every quarter, says repurchases will continue and restates a strategy of further acquisitions, while the credit agreement and indenture, which set the real limits on distributions, investments and secured debt, are not supplied; the secured-debt limit in the new 2034 indenture moved from an asset-based test to the greater of a fixed amount and EBITDA as the indenture defines it, and neither can be computed from the summaries. The refinancing also added USD 130 million of secured revolving capacity, which would rank ahead of any unsecured claims if drawn. The operating case is also weaker than the five-segment spread implies, because half of segment operating income comes from one segment, Subsea Robotics, whose fourth-quarter operating income is guided lower on weaker ROV utilization. A debt-funded acquisition, distributions larger than free cash flow, revolver borrowing to fund buybacks or a holder building a controlling position would move the view to medium, as would restricted-payment baskets that prove wide; baskets that prove tight would firm the low reading.

### Overall governance view
Overall, Oceaneering presents low sponsor and governance risk on documented conduct. The key supported behavior is restrained distributions funded from cash flow and a voluntary refinancing that extended maturities, which matters because debt-funded distributions and priming are the usual ways governance erodes creditor recovery, and neither appears in the sources. Countervailing evidence is that legal capacity cannot be assessed and that repurchases are guided to continue; further analysis requires the credit agreement, the indenture and the proxy statement.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues
The one transaction in the period is the refinancing completed on 2026-07-06. The company placed USD 500 million of senior notes due 2034 at a coupon above that of the 2028 notes, with proceeds of about USD 491 million after discounts and expenses, and used them with cash on hand to buy about USD 397 million of the USD 500 million 2028 notes in a tender offer for about USD 414 million including accrued interest; it redeemed the remainder on 2026-07-25 at principal, accrued interest and a make-whole premium of USD 1.9 million. Cash paid to retire the old notes exceeded the new notes' proceeds by USD 18.14 million, and the loss on extinguishment was USD 19.03 million.
Basis: calculation A13; calculation A14; calculation A15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_oii-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18

The revolving credit facility was amended the same day: commitments rose from USD 215 million to USD 345 million, maturity moved from April 2027 to July 2031 and margins fell by 25 bp at both ends of each range. It remains guaranteed by certain wholly owned subsidiaries and secured by first-priority liens on certain assets, with no borrowings and no letters of credit outstanding under the facility at 2026-09-30. The security and guarantors of the 2034 notes are not stated; their indenture limits secured debt to the greater of USD 350 million and the company's EBITDA as defined there, which replaces the asset-based test in the 2028 indenture, and the definitions needed to compute either limit are not supplied.
