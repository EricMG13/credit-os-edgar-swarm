## Findings

### Bottom line

Leverage has fallen from its January 2, 2026 peak but remains high: net debt was 4.62x reported EBITDA and cash interest cover only 2.63x for the twelve months to July 3, 2026, so creditors still depend on the earnings recovery holding.
Basis: calculation N5; calculation LE3; calculation LI3

The improvement comes from reported EBITDA rising 16.58% above its fiscal 2025 level while principal fell to USD 1,097.5 million, not from any change in the debt stack, which is entirely floating-rate senior secured term debt. Net debt here leaves out finance leases and receivables sold, and the issuer's own leverage measure of 4.10x uses a different EBITDA and a wider definition.
Basis: calculation G3; calculation B3; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 19–21; EX-99.1_2026-08-11_vstsq3fy26earningsrelease-.htm.jsonl, page 16

### Key findings

Reported leverage rose and then fell with earnings, not with borrowing. Net debt was 3.73x reported EBITDA at the end of fiscal 2024, 5.89x at the end of fiscal 2025 and 6.24x at January 2, 2026, then 5.04x and 4.62x at the next two quarter ends. Reported EBITDA fell 36.21% in fiscal 2025 while debt at carrying value rose 0.65%, so the ratio worsened with almost no net new borrowing; net debt has since fallen 8.61% from the fiscal 2025 year-end through repayments funded by operating cash flow, and the recovery in twelve-month EBITDA has done the rest.
Basis: calculation G1; calculation G2; calculation G4; calculation N1; calculation N2; calculation N3; calculation N4; calculation N5

Cash interest cover shows the same shape: 3.09x in fiscal 2024, 2.04x in fiscal 2025, 1.96x at January 2, 2026 and 2.63x at July 3, 2026. Cash interest paid on principal debt fell 9.62% to USD 84.6 million over the last twelve months, helped by repayments and a weighted-average term loan rate of 6.23% for nine months against 6.80% a year earlier, but cover remains well under three times, so a renewed earnings fall would erode it quickly. Interest paid on finance leases and the securitization fees sit outside this figure, which flatters cover.
Basis: calculation G6; calculation LI3; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 21; EX-99.1_2026-08-11_vstsq3fy26earningsrelease-.htm.jsonl, page 14

Cash generation improved by far more than earnings. Free cash flow, meaning operating cash flow less purchases of property and equipment, equalled 61.27% of reported EBITDA over the last twelve months against 3.02% in fiscal 2025. Part of the swing is working capital: receivables, inventories and rental merchandise released cash and the receivables sale facility was used more heavily, so the improvement is not all repeatable.
Basis: calculation LF3; calculation F2; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 50–51

Liquidity, cash plus the undrawn revolver, rose at each of the three 2026 quarter ends and was 18.09% higher at July 3, 2026 than at fiscal 2025 year-end. The revolver was undrawn at both April 3 and July 3, 2026, but the USD 250 million receivables facility, which is off balance sheet, is scheduled to end on August 2, 2027, inside twelve months of the as-of date, and no renewal or replacement is stated in the sources read.
Basis: calculation G8; calculation Q2; calculation Q3; calculation Q4; calculation Q5; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 37

The debt is concentrated and floating-rate. USD 436 million of Term Loan A-2 falls due in fiscal 2028, with the undrawn revolver, and USD 661.5 million of Term Loan B-1 in fiscal 2031, so the first debt maturity is fiscal 2028, where Term Loan A-2 alone is 1.96x reported EBITDA against 4.93x for all principal. The annual report's maturity table shows no scheduled principal before fiscal 2028, while the latest quarterly report still describes quarterly amortization on Term Loan B-1; the two statements are not reconciled.
Basis: calculation S2; calculation S3; 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 113, 116; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20

Definitions matter as much as the trend. Net debt here is carrying-value debt less cash, so it omits finance leases, letters of credit and receivables sold under the securitization facility. The issuer's Net Debt adds finance leases and letters of credit to principal debt and divides by a covenant-adjusted EBITDA higher than reported EBITDA, giving 4.10x against 4.62x here, yet neither figure includes the USD 212.3 million of receivables sold at July 3, 2026, up from USD 202.5 million at October 3, 2025, although the credit agreement's debt definition counts advances under a receivables facility. Whether those advances equal the sold balance is not established without the credit agreement.
Basis: calculation B1; calculation B2; calculation B3; calculation G10; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 37, 52; EX-99.1_2026-08-11_vstsq3fy26earningsrelease-.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 16

No event after July 3, 2026 that changes net debt is stated in the sources. The transformation plan that the annual report describes as a subsequent event was approved in the first quarter of fiscal 2026, before this balance sheet, so it is already in the 2026 figures and was kept out of fiscal 2025. Fiscal 2025 adjusted EBITDA is stated two ways, in the release reconciliation and as USD 272.6 million in the outlook table, and the releases do not explain the difference; this analysis uses the reconciliation figure because it carries the add-backs.
Basis: 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 147; EX-99.1_2026-08-11_vstsq3fy26earningsrelease-.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 14; calculation G12

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case that credit quality is better than 4.62x suggests rests on the issuer's own measures: adjusted EBITDA for the last twelve months was 30.46% above reported EBITDA, net debt over adjusted EBITDA was 3.54x, and the issuer reports 4.10x on its covenant basis. It would win if the severance, transformation and separation add-backs prove one-off and the savings plan delivers, because adjusted EBITDA would then track cash earnings.
Basis: calculation G11; calculation A1; calculation B3

The opposite case is that the ratio flatters. The twelve-month window contains the 14-week fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, working capital released cash, and finance leases and receivable sales sit outside net debt while the securitization fees are a recurring funding cost that the issuer adds back. It would win if fiscal fourth-quarter 2026 reported EBITDA fails to hold the recent level, which the covenant ceiling of 4.75x for the quarter ending October 2, 2026 and 4.50x after would then test; the issuer reports 4.10x at July 3, 2026 against a 5.00x maximum, and headroom cannot be judged without the credit agreement.
Basis: calculation A1; calculation Y3; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 37; EX-99.1_2026-08-11_vstsq3fy26earningsrelease-.htm.jsonl, page 16

### Limitations

Reported EBITDA is calculated because the issuer states none, and adjusted EBITDA, net debt and the leverage ratio the issuer prints appear only in the unaudited earnings releases; fiscal 2024 adjusted EBITDA is not supplied. Interest paid is stated only to USD 0.1 million and only for principal debt.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-08-11_vstsq3fy26earningsrelease-.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 16; 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 113

The balance sheet series has five dates and no prior-year interim balance sheet is supplied, so year-on-year leverage can be compared only at fiscal year-ends; fiscal 2025 has 53 weeks and every twelve-month figure to July 3, 2026 spans 53 weeks. The credit agreement is not supplied, so covenant definitions and headroom are the issuer's statements only.
Basis: 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 65; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 20; calculation Y4; calculation Y5; calculation Y6
