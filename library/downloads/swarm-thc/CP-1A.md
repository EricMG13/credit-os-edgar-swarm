## Findings

### Bottom line

Tenet is publicly held with no sponsor in the documents read, so creditor risk rests on its own financial policy, which has turned toward large share repurchases that the secured note indentures do not restrict.

Hospital Operations produced 75.73% of FY2025 net operating revenues, but Ambulatory Care produced 44.37% of segment Adjusted EBITDA, and 39.35% of Ambulatory Adjusted EBITDA for nine months 2026 belongs to minority partners. Repurchases of USD 1,615 million and distributions to minority partners of USD 614 million took 63.56% of nine-month operating cash flow.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 148; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 161; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 173–174; 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_thc-20260930ex991earningsr.htm.jsonl, page 3; calculation CALC-A1; calculation CALC-A5; calculation CALC-A8

### Key findings

No sponsor or controlling shareholder is identified, so creditor alignment depends on board policy rather than on a sponsor's exit horizon. The common stock is listed on the New York Stock Exchange and held by a broad base of record holders, and the beneficial-ownership detail sits in a proxy statement that was not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 55; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 177

The two segments carry different credit characteristics. Ambulatory Care earned an Adjusted EBITDA margin of 39.17% in FY2025 against 15.74% in Hospital Operations, but its profit is shared with physician and health-system partners, and a hospital network concentrated in a few states carries the reimbursement exposure.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 173–174; calculation CALC-A6; calculation CALC-A7

Reimbursement policy is the main external credit driver. Managed care payers supplied 45.50% of FY2025 net operating revenues and Medicare and Medicaid together 17.10%, and the issuer warns that the 2025 federal budget law could cut Medicaid supplemental payments from 2027 and that expired exchange subsidies may raise the uninsured share.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 161; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; calculation CALC-A2; calculation CALC-A3

Shareholder returns have grown faster than debt reduction. Repurchases rose from USD 672 million in FY2024 to USD 1,386 million in FY2025 and USD 1,615 million in nine months 2026, and the board added USD 2,000 million of authorization in June 2026, leaving USD 1,875 million available at 2026-09-30.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 56; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 120–121; 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 50

Capacity for distributions is wide but behavior has so far been funded from operating cash flow and refinancing. The secured note indentures state no restriction on additional debt, restricted payments, dividends or repurchases, yet in nine months 2026 borrowings of USD 2,041 million were slightly below repayments of USD 2,087 million while operating cash flow was USD 3,507 million, so repurchases did not rely on new net debt.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 148; 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4

Refinancing has cut secured debt and pushed maturities out. The September 2026 transaction cut secured notes from USD 9,800 million to USD 8,300 million by moving the 2027 secured maturity into unsecured notes due 2034, which lengthens maturities but leaves more debt at the unsecured rank, and the revolver stayed undrawn.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 144; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 145; calculation CALC-A9

The CommonSpirit agreement converted a long contract into a receivable and a full ownership interest. Tenet regained 100% of Conifer from 23.8% minority ownership and ended a revenue cycle contract that had run to 2032, in return for a note of USD 1,360 million repayable in three annual installments of about USD 453 million from January 2027, which adds near-term cash but removes contracted revenue and creates exposure to CommonSpirit's payment performance.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 176

Governance disclosure supports monitoring but not control analysis. The audited statements carry unqualified opinions and adjusted measures are reconciled to GAAP, but the proxy statement, board composition, large-holder ownership and executive pay were not supplied, and one executive holds both the chairman and chief executive roles.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 113; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 184; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 177

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious reading is that large repurchases coincided with lower leverage: the issuer reports net debt to Adjusted EBITDA of 2.11x at 2026-09-30 against 2.25x at 2025-12-31, the revolver has no borrowings and secured notes fell by USD 1,500 million in September 2026. The view would turn more cautious if repurchases were funded by revolver draws or new debt, if leverage rose while the CommonSpirit note was still outstanding, or if state Medicaid supplemental payments were cut from 2027 without offsetting costs; it would turn more favorable if the board held repurchases within free cash flow and applied part of the CommonSpirit receipts to debt.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-29_thc-20260930ex991earningsr.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 17

### Limitations

- The conclusion that no controlling shareholder exists rests on the annual report's record-holder count and could change if the proxy statement shows a holder of 10% or more.
- The conclusion that distribution capacity is wide rests on the issuer's summary of the secured note indentures, and the executed indentures and credit agreement were not supplied to confirm it.
- The conclusion on financial policy rests on observed actions only, because no management presentation or call transcript states a leverage target or a repurchase pace.
- The conclusion on earnings concentration cannot be restated after minority interests for the Hospital segment, because its minority share is printed only for the year.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Three transactions shaped the capital structure since the last fiscal year end. On 2026-01-27 Tenet agreed with CommonSpirit Health to end the Conifer revenue cycle contract, redeem CommonSpirit's minority interest in Conifer for USD 540 million with effect from 2026-01-01, and receive USD 1,900 million of consideration, of which USD 540 million was set off and USD 1,360 million is a non-interest-bearing note payable in three annual installments of about USD 453 million, the first due in January 2027.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10

In November 2025 Tenet issued USD 1,500 million of first lien notes due 2032 and USD 750 million of unsecured notes due 2033, redeemed USD 1,500 million of second lien notes due 2027 and USD 750 million of its unsecured notes due 2028, and replaced its revolving credit facility with a USD 1,900 million asset-based facility maturing on 2030-11-04. In September 2026 it issued USD 2,000 million of unsecured notes due 2034 and used the proceeds with cash on hand to redeem all USD 1,500 million of the first lien notes due 2027 and USD 500 million of the unsecured notes due 2028.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 145; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 146–147; 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_thc-20260930ex991earningsr.htm.jsonl, page 2

The sources read describe no acquisition financing beyond internal cash: nine-month 2026 acquisition spending was USD 201 million against USD 266 million a year earlier, and no new sponsor, merger or leveraged transaction is disclosed.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23
