## Findings

### Bottom line

Primo Brands is a scaled single-segment water business whose main discretionary risk to creditors is shareholder payouts, and the sponsor's veto over them probably fell away in August 2026, leaving the board and the debt covenants as the only limits.
Basis: calculation C05; calculation C06; 10-K_2025-12-31_prmb-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 46–47

Operations are not the weak point: net sales were USD 6,664.0 million in 2025 and management net leverage fell to 3.32x by 2026-09-30. Payouts are the exposure: 2025 dividends and buybacks of USD 572.8 million were 232.94% of free cash flow of USD 245.9 million, and a new USD 300.0 million buyback program followed on 2026-11-03.
Basis: calculation C15; calculation C16; calculation C17; 10-K_2025-12-31_prmb-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 86; 10-K_2025-12-31_prmb-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 127–128; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_ex991.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_prmb-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 62

### Key findings

Sponsor control is ending in steps, and the consent rights that let the sponsor restrain payouts most likely lapsed with the August 2026 sale. The sponsor group held 32.0% of the Class A shares on 2026-02-24, and its approval is required for dividends above USD 175.0 million a year, most repurchases and debt that takes total net leverage above 3.5x only while it holds at least 30%. The August 2026 sale and repurchase would leave it near 26.51%, an estimate that assumes those shares came from the group because no filing states the holding. If that holds, the board's own financial policy is the operative restraint on payouts.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C03; calculation C04; calculation C05; calculation C06; 10-K_2025-12-31_prmb-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 46–47; 10-K_2025-12-31_prmb-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 78; 10-Q_2026-09-30_prmb-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19

Company cash has been used to support the sponsor's exits, which is the clearest documented link between company liquidity and the sponsor since the merger. The company bought shares alongside all three sponsor offerings, USD 224.1 million in total, and 52.60% of the buybacks identified for 2025 were tied to sponsor sales; the company received none of the offering proceeds. Creditors bear this only through cash that is not used to repay debt.
Basis: calculation C07; calculation C08; calculation C09; calculation C11; calculation C12; calculation C13; calculation C14; 10-K_2025-12-31_prmb-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 101–102; 10-Q_2026-09-30_prmb-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_prmb-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 58

Payout policy is the swing factor for leverage. In 2025 dividends and buybacks exceeded free cash flow by USD 326.9 million; in the first nine months of 2026 they took USD 201.3 million, or 62.98% of free cash flow of USD 319.6 million. Dividends declared for 2026 come to about USD 175.2 million, level with the USD 175.0 million consent threshold, and the new USD 300.0 million program was approved while management net leverage was 3.32x, under the 3.5x level in the sponsor's consent test, a level that management's own measure exceeded at 3.52x on 2026-03-31, although the agreement's definition may differ.

Earlier-period comparisons therefore mix a 2023 base that holds one of the two businesses with later periods that hold both.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a payout-risk view is that the sponsor may still hold at least 30%, because the 32.0% figure is rounded and the August 2026 shares may not all have come from the group; if so, the consent rights survive. Payouts also look affordable in 2026 at 62.98% of free cash flow, net leverage has fallen to 3.32x, the only distribution made to the sponsor alone, USD 448.6 million, pre-dates the merger, and none of the 2025 or 2026 refinancings is described as a priming, uptier or drop-down. The term loans described at 2025-12-31 also required prepayment from half of excess cash flow, subject to leverage step-downs, although the terms after the March 2026 amendment are not restated.
Basis: calculation C05; calculation C20; calculation C28; 10-K_2025-12-31_prmb-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 104; 10-K_2025-12-31_prmb-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 94; 10-K_2025-12-31_prmb-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 46–47; 10-Q_2026-09-30_prmb-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19

The view would soften if a filing or the proxy statement shows the sponsor at or above 30%, if payouts stay below free cash flow after the new program starts, and if net leverage keeps falling toward management's aim. It would harden if payouts exceed free cash flow again, if net leverage rises above 3.5x after buybacks begin, if the sponsor's margin loan or forward is enforced, or if any distribution is funded with new debt.
Basis: calculation C20; calculation C21; calculation C34; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_ex991.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2025-12-31_prmb-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 46–47

### Limitations

- The executed Stockholders Agreement, credit agreement and indentures are not supplied, so the consent thresholds, restricted-payment baskets and change-of-control definitions rest on the issuer's summaries; this limits the finding that payouts are restrained only by the board and the covenants, which could be stronger or weaker once the documents are read.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The 2024 merger created the issuer and its sponsor-controlled starting share register. On 2024-11-08 Primo Water Corporation and Triton Water Parent, Inc. (BlueTriton, the parent of the former Nestlé Waters North America business) became wholly owned subsidiaries of the new parent, Primo Brands Corporation. BlueTriton was the accounting acquirer, former Primo Water holders and the sponsor group owned about 43% and 57% immediately afterwards, and the USD 3,950.3 million purchase price was paid in shares and replacement awards, with goodwill of USD 2,766.5 million, 70.03% of the price. No cash sources and uses or entry multiple is stated.

Exchange offers for the three senior note series drew participation of 99.97%, 98.20% and 99.53%, and the new notes are co-issued by Primo Water Holdings Inc. and Triton Water Holdings, Inc. and guaranteed by the parent and substantially all material wholly owned domestic subsidiaries.
