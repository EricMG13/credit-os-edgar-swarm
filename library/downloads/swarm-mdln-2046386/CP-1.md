### Analysis

Gross debt at carrying value fell from USD 16,494 million at the end of FY2024 to USD 12,561 million at the end of FY2025 and stands at USD 12,580 million at September 26, 2026. The fall came from the IPO, whose net proceeds of USD 7,048 million funded a net repayment of borrowings of USD 4,092 million in FY2025 while free cash flow was USD 1,297 million, so deleveraging was a one-time change in the capital structure and not the result of cash generation. Reported EBITDA, which carries the distribution center fire losses, refinancing costs and other charges the issuer adds back, was USD 2,687 million for the twelve months to September 26, 2026 against USD 3,071 million in FY2025, so leverage on reported EBITDA rose from 4.09x to 4.68x while leverage on the issuer's adjusted EBITDA eased from 3.62x to 3.49x.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_mdln-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 142; 10-K_2025-12-31_mdln-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 163; 10-Q_2026-09-26_mdln-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-09-26_mdln-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-26_mdln-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 62; calculation CALC-01; calculation CALC-02; calculation CALC-03; calculation CALC-10; calculation CALC-12

The gap between the two measures is USD 914 million of add-backs over the twelve months, 25.38% of adjusted EBITDA, of which other non-core charges are USD 476 million and the non-cash inventory adjustment for last-in, first-out reserves is USD 199 million. The mechanism for creditors is that market and covenant style leverage uses the adjusted figure, while cash must still pay for the fire, recall, remediation and refinancing costs that the add-backs remove: the issuer excludes estimated costs of the bed discontinuation of USD 50 million to USD 200 million (USD 60 million to USD 210 million in the discussion) from adjusted EBITDA yet expects to incur them in the fourth quarter of 2026 and 2027. Reported figures for nine months 2026 also include net tariff refund benefits of USD 324 million that the full-year guidance excludes.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-11-04_ex9913q26pressrelease.htm.jsonl, pages 2–4; EX-99.1_2026-11-04_ex9913q26pressrelease.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12; 10-Q_2026-09-26_mdln-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 45; 10-Q_2026-09-26_mdln-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 51; 10-Q_2026-09-26_mdln-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 62; calculation CALC-04

Cash interest paid fell to USD 700 million over twelve months from USD 944 million in FY2025, lifting cover on reported EBITDA to 3.84x from 3.25x, because the IPO repayment and the second-quarter refinancing lowered the cost of debt: variable-rate debt averages 6.07% against 7.10% at the end of FY2025. Operating cash flow of USD 2,152 million for nine months 2026 against USD 1,515 million a year earlier is flattered by a USD 606 million swing in working capital, an inflow of USD 105 million, which includes USD 446 million from accrued expenses, against an outflow of USD 501 million, and by tariff refunds received of about USD 399 million, part of which is owed to customers. Free cash flow of USD 1,804 million for nine months should therefore be read as above a sustainable level.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-26_mdln-20260926.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12; 10-Q_2026-09-26_mdln-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-26_mdln-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 49; 10-K_2025-12-31_mdln-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 161; 10-K_2025-12-31_mdln-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 162; calculation CALC-09; calculation CALC-10

Liquidity is USD 4,570 million, made up of cash and short-term investments of USD 3,608 million and an undrawn revolver of USD 962 million, against scheduled principal of USD 69 million across the rest of 2026, 2027 and 2028, so there is no refinancing pressure before 2029. In 2029 USD 8,027 million, 62.99% of principal, falls due across the secured and unsecured notes, and the revolver, which matures in 2029, carries a springing maturity 91 days before the maturity of the notes and term loans. The springing first-lien net leverage covenant of 8.3x applies only if more than 35% of the revolver is drawn and was not tested because nothing is drawn.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-26_mdln-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-09-26_mdln-20260926.htm.jsonl, pages 22–23; 10-Q_2026-09-26_mdln-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 69; calculation CALC-07; calculation CALC-08

After quarter end the issuer prepaid USD 750 million of the term loan from cash on hand on 2026-10-22, which lowers gross debt and cash equally, so net debt stays at USD 8,972 million and net leverage on reported EBITDA stays at 3.34x before accrued interest, which the filing does not state; interest expense should fall in later periods and a loss on extinguishment of about USD 21 million is expected in the fourth quarter. The repurchase authorisation of up to USD 1 billion, with nothing yet repurchased, is the main use of cash that could reverse part of the net debt reduction.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-26_mdln-20260926.htm.jsonl, pages 44–45; 10-Q_2026-09-26_mdln-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 65; calculation CALC-11

Outside Total Debt the balance sheet carries a tax receivable agreement liability of USD 4,403 million (USD 4,415 million in the note, which includes USD 12 million due within a year), noncontrolling interests of USD 7,585 million, and, at the end of FY2025, operating lease liabilities of USD 452 million inside accrued and other liabilities. The tax receivable agreement is an obligation of Medline Inc. rather than the borrower, but it is funded by distributions from Medline Holdings, so it can draw on the same group cash that services debt. Customer rebate accruals of USD 406 million at the end of FY2025 and trade payables of USD 1,209 million at September 26, 2026 are non-debt float that helped fund working capital. Other claims are small against debt: unconditional purchase obligations of USD 710 million over five years and beyond, a pension projected benefit obligation of USD 61 million at the end of FY2025, and an ethylene oxide settlement liability of USD 174 million at the end of FY2024 that was paid in cash of USD 166 million during FY2025, leaving none outstanding.

The strongest contrary evidence is that adjusted leverage of 3.49x and net leverage of 2.49x on adjusted EBITDA are well below the 8.3x springing covenant level, although that covenant uses its own EBITDA definition, with liquidity of USD 4,570 million and no maturity before 2029; reported EBITDA is also depressed by fire losses that were net of USD 100 million of insurance recovered and by USD 46 million of refinancing costs, so recovery of the remaining insured loss would move reported leverage towards the adjusted figure. What would worsen the view is growth in the costs the issuer excludes, such as the bed discontinuation of up to USD 210 million and restructuring of USD 20 million to USD 30 million, or a revolver draw above 35% that would bring the covenant into test.
