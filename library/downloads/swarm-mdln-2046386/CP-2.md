Medline's credit profile is adequate: liquidity of USD 4,570 million and no material maturity before 2029 give creditors time, but weak earnings quality, product-quality enforcement risk and USD 8,027 million due in 2029 make the next two years of results decisive.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is that the issuer reaffirmed Adjusted EBITDA guidance, raised organic sales guidance to 10.0% to 10.5% from 9.0% to 10.0%, grew third-quarter net sales 10.41% and Adjusted EBITDA 11.76%, although the latter includes USD 81 million of refunds, and holds equity worth about USD 45.6 billion at the September 25, 2026 price against total debt of USD 12,580 million, so a very large cushion sits under creditors and a weak quarter would not threaten debt service before 2029. The issuer attributes the pressure on margin to inventory cost inflation, higher operating expenses and customer mix, some of which could fade, but the filings do not show that it will. The view would improve if fourth-quarter Adjusted EBITDA reaches the top of the implied range, the warning letters close without restrictions, and the 2029 notes are refinanced early; it would worsen if the fourth quarter falls below USD 828 million, if more than 35% of the revolver is drawn, if repurchases begin while remediation costs run, or if a large Prime Vendor or purchasing group relationship is lost.

### Limitations

- No credit rating, agency view or market price for the debt is supplied, so the conclusion on refinancing cost rests on the issuer's own issuance record and cannot be tested against what the market would charge for the 2029 amount.
- The adjusted figures use the issuer's own add-back definitions and the refund-free figures in this answer are calculated on the assumption that the net refund benefit sits fully inside Adjusted EBITDA, as the issuer states for nine months; if later refunds or customer repayments change, the earnings-quality conclusion moves with them.
- Covenant headroom is not calculable because the credit agreement's definition of consolidated EBITDA is not supplied, so the statement that the covenant is not tested rests on the issuer's own disclosure and on undrawn revolver borrowings.
- Guidance covers FY2026 only and no forecast is built here, so the view that the 2029 amount can be refinanced assumes earnings stay near the twelve-month level and is not a projection.
- Pro forma cash after the October 22, 2026 prepayment is not stated by the earlier financial analysis, so liquidity after that date is described only as lower by the amount prepaid.

### Business model and operating drivers

Medline manufactures about one-third of its own-brand medical-surgical products and distributes the rest of that brand and third-party products through a network of 70 sites and an owned truck fleet, serving hospitals, post-acute and physician offices under multi-year Prime Vendor agreements. The credit mechanism is that distribution volume and contract tenure give revenue visibility, while the own-brand mix earns the margin: Medline Brand's margin was 26.1% in nine months 2026 against 4.7% for Supply Chain Solutions, so the shift of growth towards Prime Vendor distribution raises revenue faster than earnings. Cost flexibility is moderate: capital expenditure is low at 1.57% of net sales, but inflation in inventory cost, customer mix and operating expense took the gross margin to 26.13% in nine months 2026 from 27.14% in nine months 2025 even with tariff refunds in cost of goods sold.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_mdln-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 11–15; 10-Q_2026-09-26_mdln-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 43; EX-99.1_2026-11-04_ex9913q26pressrelease.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation CALC-11; calculation CALC-13

### Market frameworks

Competition is a creditor concern because it sets pricing: the issuer competes with large distributors for Prime Vendor agreements and faces buying groups that can push prices down, so a lost agreement removes volume and margin together, as the USD 227 million of FY2025 sales lost to departing Prime Vendor customers shows. Supplier power is limited, with no sourcing partner above 5% of spend, but 5% of cost of goods sold comes from China, which ties the cost base to tariffs. The political and regulatory factors that matter for credit are tariffs and their refunds, FDA enforcement and health policy affecting hospital spending; the first adds volatility to margin, the second can limit products and the third can limit pricing, and none of them is quantified beyond the amounts the issuer reports.

### Financial profile and credit quality

The financial profile is stronger than at the end of FY2024 but the trend is flattered by one-offs. Scale is large and cost and capex flexibility adequate; margin stability is weak because the 2026 gain rests on refunds; free cash flow stability is moderate for the same reason; liquidity is strong at USD 4,570 million; the ability to refinance is adequate but concentrated in 2029; financial policy and governance are adequate with owner claims on cash as the weak point. Total debt fell to USD 12,580 million from USD 16,494 million at the end of FY2024, and leverage on reported EBITDA was 4.09x at the end of FY2025 and 4.68x at September 26, 2026, down from 5.34x at the end of FY2024, the rise since FY2025 reflecting fire and other charges that lowered reported EBITDA; adjusted leverage fell to 3.49x from 4.91x.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next one to two years the tailwinds are organic sales growth guided at 10.0% to 10.5% for FY2026, a lower interest bill, the expected recovery of the remaining insured fire loss, and USD 80 million to USD 90 million of annual savings targeted from the October 2026 restructuring. The headwinds are the excluded costs of bed exit and restructuring in the fourth quarter of 2026 and in 2027, tariff costs of about USD 277 million in nine months 2026 before refunds, FDA remediation costs, and customer mix. Over three to five years the 2029 maturities, the unit exchanges that raise the tax receivable agreement liability, and the repurchase authorisation decide whether deleveraging continues; the issuer states no medium-term target, so none is assumed.

### Downside scenario

The most credible downside starts with product quality, not demand. An FDA action following the three unresolved warning letters, at the Northfield, Glens Falls and Waukegan sites, could restrict shipments of affected Medline Brand products; because that segment earned 82.84% of segment Adjusted EBITDA, group earnings would fall faster than sales, the tariff refunds, of which USD 399 million of an expected USD 507 million has been received, would not support later periods, and the excluded costs of the bed exit and restructuring would still be paid in cash. Liquidity of USD 4,570 million and USD 69 million of scheduled principal before 2029 mean the group would not face a liquidity shortfall in this case; the pressure would come as leverage stays near the 3.84x refund-free adjusted level or higher into 2028, when the USD 8,027 million due in 2029 would be refinanced and the revolver's maturity springs. Management levers are the unused repurchase authorisation, which need not be used, and the capital expenditure plan of up to USD 600 million for FY2026, which is mainly growth spending on automation, new distribution centers and kitting capacity and could be slowed, although the sources do not say how much is committed.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-26_mdln-20260926.htm.jsonl, pages 50–51; 10-Q_2026-09-26_mdln-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 64; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation CALC-02; calculation CALC-05

### Overall credit view

Overall, Medline presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, a contract base and a profitable own-brand segment, but credit quality is constrained by earnings that include one-off refunds, product-quality enforcement risk and a concentrated 2029 maturity wall. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow is positive and capital needs are low, though 2026 is flattered, and refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the fourth-quarter result, the closing of the warning letters and market conditions in 2028. The primary downside path is an FDA action on own-brand products while refunds end. Further analysis would require the executed debt documents, a rating or market price, and a forecast. Top risk: earnings quality.
