## Findings

### Bottom line

CACI's fundamental credit profile is adequate: durable federal demand and USD 1,674.39 million of liquidity support creditors, but leverage of 3.92x after a debt-funded acquisition and dependence on federal funding timing mean deleveraging must come from free cash flow.

The main downside path is a federal funding disruption after the continuing resolution ends on 11 December 2026, delaying awards while debt service and the floating-rate cost of the acquisition debt continue. Free cash flow of USD 725.96 million over the last twelve months is 58.82% of EBITDA, and the company's guidance of at least USD 900 million for fiscal 2027 would speed repayment if met.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17; EX-99.1_2026-10-21_fy27-q1caci20261021ex991.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation D1

### Key findings

The business profile is the strongest support. Revenue of USD 9,567.78 million in fiscal 2026 came almost entirely from federal agencies, backlog stood at USD 34.0 billion at 30 September 2026, and cost-plus contracts that pass cost increases to the customer supplied 56.0% of revenue, so demand and margin are well supported. Federal dependence also means no commercial customer can replace a lost agency, which is why funding timing, not demand, is the risk.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 52; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20

Leverage is the main financial constraint. Total debt was 3.92x EBITDA and net debt 3.69x at 30 September 2026 on a last-twelve-months basis, against 3.02x and 2.91x at fiscal 2025 year-end, because the acquisition added debt before its earnings were fully counted. Cash interest cover of 5.60x flatters the current burden, since the September 2026 quarter's interest expense annualised gives 4.21x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D1

Liquidity and the maturity profile leave no near-term refinancing pressure. Cash of USD 279.39 million plus USD 1,395.0 million of undrawn revolver gives USD 1,674.39 million against USD 46.75 million of principal scheduled for fiscal 2027, the revolver and term loan mature in November 2030, and the largest step, USD 1,706.75 million or 34.61% of principal, falls in fiscal 2031. The weakness is that the receivables-sale facility of up to USD 350.0 million has a stated term ending 18 December 2026 and its renewal is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 85, 89; calculation D1; calculation D2

Interest cost is the transmission channel from rates to cash flow. The floating-rate debt of USD 3,364.44 million is 41.61% covered by swaps of USD 1,400.0 million, and swaps of USD 350.0 million mature within twelve months, so each 100 bp move on the unhedged USD 1,964.44 million changes annual interest by about USD 19.64 million. That is small against EBITDA but compounds the leverage constraint if EBITDA stalls.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9; calculation D3; calculation D4

Cash conversion supports deleveraging if guidance is met. Operating cash flow excluding the receivables sale was 71.73% of fiscal 2026 EBITDA, and free cash flow over the last twelve months was 58.82% of EBITDA, so conversion is solid but was helped by lower cash taxes. Applying the guided USD 900 million of fiscal 2027 free cash flow to debt with EBITDA unchanged would bring net leverage from 3.69x to 2.96x, which is an illustration, not a forecast.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-10-21_fy27-q1caci20261021ex991.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 11; calculation D5

Financial policy and governance are not the pressure point, but they are not established beyond the filings. No dividend or sponsor extraction is documented and repurchases are small, while acquisition appetite is the one behaviour that adds debt, and the executed debt documents that would show how far that can go are not supplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 46, 88

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a stronger credit view is that leverage fell from 4.18x at fiscal 2026 year-end to 3.92x within a quarter, that earnings of the acquired business are only partly counted so true run-rate leverage is lower, and that the backlog and margin trend are rising. The strongest evidence for a weaker view is that organic growth slowed to 2.1% in the September 2026 quarter and interest expense on an annualised basis implies cover of 4.21x. The view would improve if full-year earnings of the acquired business or covenant levels showed sufficient headroom, and worsen if the continuing resolution lapsed into a shutdown, the receivables-sale facility were not renewed, or a further debt-funded purchase were announced before leverage returned toward the fiscal 2025 level of 3.02x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.1_2026-10-21_fy27-q1caci20261021ex991.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation D1; calculation D6

### Strengths and weaknesses

The first strength is a demand base that is large and spread across agencies with which the company has long-term relationships. Federal agencies supplied 95.6% of fiscal 2026 revenue and total backlog of USD 34.0 billion, with funded backlog at 18.24% of it, gives revenue visibility that a funding delay can postpone but not remove, which supports debt service through a cycle.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; calculation K10; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The second strength is liquidity and a long maturity profile. Liquidity of USD 1,674.39 million against fiscal 2027 principal of USD 46.75 million, and a revolver and term loan that run to November 2030, mean the first refinancing event is some years away and a funding disruption would be met from available cash and the revolver.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D1

The third strength is cost pass-through and rising margin. Cost-plus-fee work was 56.0% of fiscal 2026 revenue and the EBITDA margin rose from 11.21% in fiscal 2025 to 12.65% over the last twelve months, which supports interest cover as the debt stack grows.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 52; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D1

The fourth strength is financial policy that has not extracted cash. No dividend has ever been paid and repurchases fell sharply in fiscal 2026, so earnings have stayed available for debt service.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 46, 67; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The first weakness is leverage after the acquisition. Total debt of 3.92x and net debt of 3.69x LTM EBITDA at 30 September 2026 are above the 3.02x and 2.91x of fiscal 2025 year-end, which reduces the cushion for creditors if earnings fall.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D1

The second weakness is the dependence on federal funding timing. A continuing resolution funds the government to 11 December 2026 and a shutdown in the autumn of 2025 shows the risk is real, so a delay would hit awards and cash while debt service continues.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17

The third weakness is interest cost and hedge roll-off. Floating-rate debt is only 41.61% hedged and USD 350.0 million of swaps mature within twelve months, so a rate rise passes quickly into interest cost at a time when cover is already lower on an annualised basis.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9; calculation D3; calculation D4

The fourth weakness is acquisition appetite combined with slowing organic growth. Organic growth was 2.1% in the September 2026 quarter against 7.2% for fiscal 2026, so growth has relied on purchases that are funded with debt.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-21_fy27-q1caci20261021ex991.htm.jsonl, page 2; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Company description

CACI provides technology and expertise to national security customers in intelligence, defense and federal civilian agencies through a holding company with subsidiaries in the United States and Europe. Domestic Operations produced 96.8% of fiscal 2026 revenue, and the company grew through eight acquisitions in three fiscal years, the largest being ARKA in March 2026, which adds space-domain capability. Cash-flow visibility comes from long, multi-year contracts and a large backlog, so debt service is supported, while refinancing confidence depends on continued federal funding because no commercial customer base offsets it.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 7, 9; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Ownership and group structure

CACI International Inc is a listed holding company with no sponsor or controlling holder disclosed in the supplied documents, so there is no sponsor whose willingness to support or extract cash needs to be judged. Debt is issued or borrowed at the holding company with guarantors that are not named, which means structural priority cannot be read from the filings and is passed to the module that handles it. The financial policy evidence is a record of debt-funded acquisition, no dividend and falling repurchases.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 46, 104; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is predictable but concentrated rather than recurring in the commercial sense. Federal agencies supplied 95.6% of revenue, the ten largest contracts 22.5%, and the company wins work by competitive bid, so growth comes from new awards and exercised options and not from pricing power. Total backlog includes unexercised options and only USD 6.2 billion is funded, so revenue beyond the funded work depends on appropriations, and organic growth of 2.1% in the latest quarter shows demand is steady rather than accelerating.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; EX-99.1_2026-10-21_fy27-q1caci20261021ex991.htm.jsonl, page 2

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins are defended by contract structure but exposed to the shift to fixed-price work. Direct costs of 66.80% of fiscal 2026 revenue are largely labour and subcontractors that cost-plus contracts pass through, which protects margin when volumes or wages move, while fixed-price work rose from 26.3% to 30.4% of revenue and to 33.2% in the latest quarter, so the company now carries more overrun risk for a higher margin. EBITDA grew 21.42% in fiscal 2026 against revenue growth of 10.89%, which shows operating leverage working upward and would work downward if revenue stalled.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 52, 66; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 6; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation D7

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital needs are light and cash conversion is the main support for deleveraging. Capital expenditure was 1.11% of fiscal 2026 revenue, so most operating cash flow is available for debt service, and free cash flow over the last twelve months was 58.82% of EBITDA. Cash interest and acquisition-related integration costs absorb the rest, and cash taxes fell in fiscal 2026, which means part of the conversion may not repeat.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 67; calculation D1

### Competitive forces

Buyer power is high because essentially all contracts allow the government to terminate for convenience, and the customer sets contract terms, so a lost recompete removes revenue without recourse. Rivalry is intense with larger competitors and non-traditional entrants in cloud, cyber and satellite operations, which pressures pricing at recompete, while indirect competition from agencies doing work in-house limits the addressable market. Supplier power cannot be sized because subcontractor cost sits inside direct costs, but cleared labour is scarce, which raises wage cost, and in 9.91% of revenue the company is itself a subcontractor, so a prime contractor's cash problems can reach it; the threat of substitution appears low for classified mission work.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Policy and macro factors

Federal budget policy is the one macro factor that changes credit quality. Continuing resolutions delay new programs and awards, the reconciliation law made about USD 156 billion of defense funding available regardless of annual appropriations, and the government is funded only to 11 December 2026, so a lapse would test liquidity while longer-term funding looks supported. Interest rates matter through the floating-rate debt, and technology change is a revenue driver rather than a credit threat in the period reviewed.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17; calculation D3

### Credit-translated SWOT

Strengths are the funded federal demand, backlog, cost pass-through and liquidity described above. Weaknesses are post-acquisition leverage, dependence on one customer, the fixed-price shift and interest cost. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are guided free cash flow of at least USD 900 million, margin expansion and conversion of the acquired space business into earnings that lower leverage. Threats that could weaken it are a funding lapse, a further debt-funded acquisition, non-renewal of the receivables-sale facility and loss of a large recompete.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-21_fy27-q1caci20261021ex991.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation D5

### Financial profile and credit quality

The financial profile is adequate and trending toward better leverage but not yet there. Scale is large with revenue of USD 9,567.78 million, margin is rising, liquidity is USD 1,674.39 million, and conversion supports repayment, while leverage of 3.92x and the cost of floating-rate debt constrain it. Refinancing capacity looks sound for the next several years because the nearest large maturity is in fiscal 2031, though capital-market access is judged only from that profile because ratings and market prices are not supplied. The missing datapoints that would sharpen the view are covenant levels, pro forma earnings and ratings.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D1; calculation D8

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

For the next twelve to twenty-four months the company guides fiscal 2027 revenue of USD 10,700 million to USD 10,900 million, which implies growth of 11.83% to 13.92% over fiscal 2026 once the full year of the acquired business is included, and free cash flow of at least USD 900 million. Tailwinds are the backlog, a 2.0x book-to-bill in the latest quarter, Department of War revenue growth of 14.8% and mandatory defense funding; headwinds are the 3.7% fall in federal civilian revenue, slowing organic growth and the funding lapse risk. Medium-term outlook over three to five years is not established, because the company states targets without figures in the supplied documents.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-21_fy27-q1caci20261021ex991.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 4–5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18; calculation D9

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a federal funding lapse when the continuing resolution ends on 11 December 2026. The first operational break is delayed new awards and program starts, which would slow growth beyond the 2.1% organic rate and could slow collections, since days sales outstanding already rose to 59 days. The financial transmission is lower operating cash flow while cash interest and scheduled principal continue, and if the receivables-sale facility were not renewed after 18 December 2026 up to USD 350.0 million of financing would need replacing. The liquidity consequence is a draw on the revolver from USD 605.00 million, but liquidity of USD 1,674.39 million is 4.78 times that facility, so the scenario does not point to liquidity distress, and leverage would rise only through lower EBITDA. Management levers are cutting acquisitions, repurchases and capital expenditure, and the observable triggers are the funding status after 11 December, revolver drawings and the receivables-sale renewal.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 16–17, 21; EX-99.1_2026-10-21_fy27-q1caci20261021ex991.htm.jsonl, page 2; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D1; calculation D2

### Overall credit view

Overall, CACI presents a business profile that is large and supported by funded federal demand, but credit quality is constrained by leverage after a debt-funded acquisition. Cash-flow durability appears adequate due to cost pass-through, light capital needs and a rising margin. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the next two to three years of free cash flow and on the November 2030 maturities. The primary downside path is a federal funding disruption that delays awards while interest on partly unhedged floating-rate debt continues. Further analysis would require covenant levels, pro forma earnings for the acquired business, and ratings and market prices. Top risk: federal funding timing.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation D1
