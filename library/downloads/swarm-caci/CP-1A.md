## Findings

### Bottom line

CACI is an NYSE-listed US national-security contractor with no sponsor identified, no dividend history and no creditor-adverse conduct in the filings, but serial debt-funded acquisitions and missing ownership and debt documents leave sponsor and governance risk at Medium for creditors.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 46, 79, 103; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 9; calculation C3

Revenue is 95.6% federal, so cash flow follows appropriations, and the mix is moving toward fixed-price work, which carries more cost-overrun risk; only USD 6.2 billion of USD 34.0 billion of backlog is funded. Principal debt rose from USD 2,942.63 million at 30 June 2025 to USD 4,931.13 million at 30 June 2026, mainly through the ARKA acquisition, while the USD 42 million Al Shimari judgment was vacated on 21 October 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 43, 86–89; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 20; calculation C3

### Key findings

- Customer concentration is high but spread across agencies. Federal contracts were 95.6% of fiscal 2026 revenue, of which the Department of War was 53.6%, the Intelligence Community 24.6% and federal civilian agencies 17.4%, and the ten largest contracts were 22.5% of revenue. A budget lapse or a termination for convenience therefore reaches cash flow across the group, although the company calls terminations rare and the agencies fund different missions, which limits the damage from the loss of one program.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 12; EX-99.1_2026-08-05_fy26-q4caci20260805ex991.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10
- Backlog gives visibility but little of it is funded. Total backlog was USD 34.0 billion at 30 September 2026, up 0.3% on a year earlier, of which USD 6.2 billion, 18.2%, was funded; the rest depends on future appropriations, so a continuing resolution can delay conversion into revenue even in a quarter with a book-to-bill of 2.0x.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21; EX-99.1_2026-10-21_fy27-q1caci20261021ex991.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 4; calculation C6
- The contract mix is shifting toward fixed-price work, which raises margin volatility. Cost-plus-fee work fell to 52.8% of revenue in the September 2026 quarter from 60.5% a year earlier while fixed-price work rose to 33.2% from 26.7%; the contractor bears overruns on fixed-price work, so a cost overrun on one large program would reduce EBITDA and cash flow in a way cost-plus work would not.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6–7; EX-99.1_2026-10-21_fy27-q1caci20261021ex991.htm.jsonl, page 8
- Acquisitions are the behaviour that matters most for creditors, and they have been funded largely with debt. Acquisition spending was 2.97x operating cash flow in fiscal 2026 and 3.10x in fiscal 2025, net borrowings funded 75.9% of fiscal 2026 acquisition spending and 82.1% of fiscal 2025 spending, and principal debt rose by USD 1,988.50 million in the year, so the ARKA acquisition for USD 2,642.7 million left goodwill and intangibles at 72.40% of total assets and tangible equity negative at USD 4,094.75 million; recovery therefore rests on contract cash flows rather than hard assets.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 65–67, 79–81, 86–89; calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C13
- Shareholder distributions are small and no sponsor exists to extract value, but buybacks remain authorised. CACI has never paid a cash dividend and states no intention to; repurchases in the cash flow statement were USD 161.49 million in fiscal 2024, USD 168.56 million in fiscal 2025 and USD 17.08 million in fiscal 2026, and USD 187.3 million of authorisation remained at 30 September 2026, so repurchases could resume from cash that would otherwise repay acquisition debt.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 46, 67, 92; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 12
- Funding choices show terming-out and extension rather than creditor-adverse moves. Revolver borrowings were repaid with USD 1,000.0 million of 2033 Notes in June 2025 and USD 500.0 million more in March 2026, the credit facility maturity was extended on 25 November 2025, a USD 1,300.0 million bridge was left undrawn and ended at closing, and the company reports compliance with every covenant since inception. No waiver, exchange or priming transaction appears in the filings, but the executed debt documents that would show the capacity for one are not in the set.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 28, 81, 88–89; 10-Q_2025-12-31_caci-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 7
- Governance structure cannot be assessed from the filings, and disclosure quality is adequate rather than strong. Board, compensation, ownership and related-party information is incorporated by reference to a proxy statement that is not in the set, so control rights and conflicts between shareholders and creditors are not established. The filings do show clean audit opinions from PricewaterhouseCoopers on the statements and on internal control, effective disclosure controls, ARKA elements left out of the first-year control assessment at 1.5% of assets and 1.0% of revenue, a board chair separate from the chief executive officer, and a policy that significant fixed-price contracts need the approval of at least two executives.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 60, 101–103, 107
- Legal exposure fell on 21 October 2026. The USD 42 million Al Shimari judgment, for which no amount was recognised, was vacated and the case dismissed after the Supreme Court's June 2026 Cisco ruling, equal to 3.01% of undrawn revolver capacity of USD 1,395.0 million; the plaintiffs may seek Supreme Court review and the related Abbass action remains stayed, so the effect on creditors is timing and headline risk rather than liquidity.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 43–44, 99; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 21, 29–31; calculation C9

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a lower level is conduct: no dividend has ever been paid, repurchases fell to USD 17.08 million in fiscal 2026, the bridge was never drawn, covenants have been met since inception and net repayment of borrowings in the September 2026 quarter was USD 66.69 million. The level would move to Low if the proxy statement shows an independent board with no controlling holder and the credit agreement shows tight restricted payment and investment baskets. The strongest evidence for a higher level is eight acquisitions in three fiscal years, the largest funded mainly with new debt, with USD 187.3 million of repurchase authorisation still open. The level would move to High if a later report showed debt-funded repurchases or an amendment that loosened covenants to allow them.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 28, 46, 67, 81; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 12; calculation C10

### Limitations

- Board composition, independence, ownership and compensation are in a proxy statement that is not supplied, so the structural side of the Medium level rests on conduct alone; a controlling holder or weak independence would raise it and a clean structure would lower it.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 103
- The executed credit agreement and indentures are not supplied, so legal capacity for restricted payments, investments and additional debt is not established and willingness is judged from actions only, which keeps the level from moving to Low.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 104–105
- The filings do not split repurchases between the buyback programme and purchases that offset employee share issuance, so the repurchase amounts may overstate discretionary distributions by an amount that is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 46, 92; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 31
- The sources end on 22 October 2026, before the December 2026 coupon dates, the end of the stated receivables purchase term and the six-month transition period, so conduct after those dates cannot change the level here.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 34

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

CACI bought ARKA Group L.P. on 9 March 2026 for USD 2,642.7 million net of cash acquired, which the September 2026 quarterly report restates at USD 2,637.7 million after a USD 5.0 million working-capital refund, and funded it from increased borrowings and cash on hand. The new debt was a USD 800.0 million Term Loan B-2 amended onto the Term Loan B on the closing date, with the same guarantees and collateral, and USD 500.0 million of additional 2033 Notes issued on 12 March 2026 at a premium for net proceeds of USD 516.5 million that repaid revolver borrowings; a USD 1,300.0 million bridge commitment dated 19 December 2025 was never drawn and was terminated at closing. The notes are senior unsecured and subordinated to the senior secured facilities, with a repurchase right at 101% after a change of control accompanied by a ratings decline. The consequence for creditors is a larger secured term loan layer ahead of the notes, with sources and uses and pro forma leverage not printed.
