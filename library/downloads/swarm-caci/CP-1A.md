## Findings

### Bottom line

CACI is a diversified but federally dependent contractor with no sponsor or controlling owner disclosed in the supplied filings, so creditor risk rests on acquisition appetite and government funding timing, not on sponsor behaviour.

Federal agencies supply 95.6% of revenue and the largest customer group, the Department of War, supplies 53.6%. Two debt-funded acquisitions in two years, USD 1,681.9 million in fiscal 2025 and USD 2,642.7 million for ARKA in fiscal 2026, show a financial policy that favours growth by purchase over shareholder distributions, and the company has never paid a cash dividend.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 46, 79, 82

### Key findings

Revenue is concentrated in one customer but spread across agencies, which makes cash timing the main business risk rather than demand. Federal contracts supplied 95.6% of fiscal 2026 revenue, the Department of War 53.6% and the intelligence community 24.6%, and the ten largest contracts supplied 22.5% of revenue, or USD 2.2 billion, so no single program dominates. Continuing resolutions and shutdowns delay new programs and awards, and the government can terminate contracts for convenience, so a funding gap hits revenue timing and cash before it hits underlying demand.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 12, 50; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17

The contract mix is moving toward fixed-price work, which raises margin potential and cost-overrun risk together. Fixed-price contracts supplied 30.4% of fiscal 2026 revenue against 26.3% a year earlier, and 33.2% in the September 2026 quarter against 26.7% a year earlier, while cost-plus-fee contracts fell from 60.5% to 56.0% for the year and to 52.8% in the quarter. Direct costs fell to 64.6% of revenue in the quarter from 67.6%, which the company attributes to a favourable revenue mix, so the margin gain is the visible reward and an overrun on a fixed-price program is the exposure.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 19; EX-99.1_2026-10-21_fy27-q1caci20261021ex991.htm.jsonl, page 8

Backlog is large but only a small part is funded, and organic growth has slowed. Total backlog was USD 34.0 billion at 30 September 2026 and funded backlog USD 6.2 billion, 18.24% of the total, up from 15.93% a year earlier. Organic growth was 2.1% in the September 2026 quarter against 7.2% for fiscal 2026, so most of the quarter's 8.2% revenue growth came from acquisitions, while Department of War revenue rose 14.8% and federal civilian revenue fell 3.7%.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 20; EX-99.1_2026-10-21_fy27-q1caci20261021ex991.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 4; 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 50; calculation K10; calculation K11

Acquisition appetite is the main financial-policy signal, and it has been funded with debt. The company completed eight acquisitions in three fiscal years, three in fiscal 2025 for USD 1,681.9 million and ARKA in fiscal 2026 for USD 2,642.7 million net of cash acquired, funded from increased borrowings and cash, while principal debt rose by USD 1,988.50 million in fiscal 2026. Share repurchases fell from USD 168.56 million in fiscal 2025 to USD 17.08 million in fiscal 2026, which is consistent with cash being directed to acquisitions and debt service, and USD 187.3 million of the repurchase authorization remained at 30 September 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 67, 79, 82, 86; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 12; calculation K6; calculation K8

Ownership and governance cannot be read as a sponsor case. The company is listed in New York with a small number of record holders, has never paid a cash dividend, and states a policy of retaining earnings, so no distribution or sponsor extraction is documented. The percentage held by any holder, the board's independence, related-party dealings and compensation sit in a proxy statement that is not supplied, so those questions are not established.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 46, 103

Disclosure quality is good on the financial statements but thin on creditor protections. The auditor gave an unmodified opinion and found internal control effective, quarterly results are published within about three weeks of quarter end and annual results within about five weeks, and the debt note prints every instrument, but covenant levels, guarantor names and the legal ranking among secured facilities are not printed. A creditor can therefore monitor performance well and cannot test the debt documents' limits on further borrowing or distributions.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 60, 88–89, 104; EX-99.1_2026-10-21_fy27-q1caci20261021ex991.htm.jsonl, page 1

A long-running civil action turned in the company's favour in October 2026 but is not finally closed. A jury verdict of USD 42 million in November 2024 was affirmed on appeal in March 2026, no amount was ever recognized, the Fourth Circuit directed dismissal in August 2026, and on 21 October 2026 the District Court vacated the judgment and dismissed the case, while the plaintiffs have said they may seek Supreme Court review. The amount is small against the company's liquidity, so the creditor consequence is limited to a tail risk and a second stayed suit that the District Court will dismiss or hold according to what the plaintiffs do.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 99; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 30–31

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against concern about acquisition-funded leverage is that the acquired businesses are being absorbed while the company still grew organically, margins widened and funded backlog rose, and that no distribution, waiver or exchange has been documented. If the company disclosed a leverage target, stated that further large purchases are paused until a ratio is reached, or supplied the debt documents showing tight limits on new debt and distributions, the view would move toward lower governance risk. The view would worsen if organic growth stayed near the September 2026 quarter's 2.1% while another debt-funded purchase was announced, or if the continuing resolution that funds the government to 11 December 2026 lapsed into a shutdown that delayed awards.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18, 20; EX-99.1_2026-10-21_fy27-q1caci20261021ex991.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 46

### Limitations

- No ownership percentages, board independence or compensation data are supplied, so the finding that no sponsor or controlling holder is disclosed rests on the filings' silence and could change if the proxy statement named a large holder.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 103
- Legal capacity under the debt documents is not available, so the separation of what the company may do from what it has done cannot be made, and a low-risk reading of acquisition appetite cannot be tested against debt-incurrence limits.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 88–89
- The acquired business's standalone earnings, synergies and integration costs are not disclosed beyond a combined post-acquisition contribution with a smaller purchase, so the leverage effect of acquisition appetite stays directional.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 80–81
- Customer concentration is available by agency group and for the ten largest contracts but not by individual contract or agency, so the concentration finding could be sharper or weaker than stated.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 50

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

ARKA Group L.P. was acquired on 9 March 2026 for USD 2,642.7 million net of cash acquired at 30 June 2026, later USD 2,637.7 million after a USD 5.0 million working-capital refund, and was funded from increased borrowings and cash on hand. The company had signed a commitment letter on 19 December 2025 for a senior secured bridge facility of up to USD 1,300.0 million, which stayed undrawn and ended on closing. The acquisition adds space-domain capability and, together with a smaller purchase, contributed USD 166.8 million of revenue in fiscal 2026. The pro forma results were judged not material by the company, so no combined earnings are available.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 79–81; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation K9

New debt was raised around the purchase. A Term Loan B-2 of USD 800.0 million, senior secured with guarantees and collateral identical to the existing Term Loan B, was added on 9 March 2026, and USD 500.0 million of additional 2033 senior unsecured notes were issued on 12 March 2026 at a premium for net proceeds of USD 516.5 million, used to repay revolver borrowings. The credit facility had been amended on 25 November 2025 to extend its maturity, with a revolver of USD 2,000.0 million and a term loan of USD 1,250.0 million, so the secured debt carries a majority of the company's assets as collateral while the notes sit behind it.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 88–89; 10-Q_2026-03-31_caci-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15
