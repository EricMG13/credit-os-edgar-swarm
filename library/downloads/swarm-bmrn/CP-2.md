## Findings

### Bottom line

BioMarin's rare-disease franchises still generate operating cash, but the Amicus financing moved the company from net cash to net debt, and creditors now depend on integration lifting earnings before the 2027 convertible maturity.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bmrn-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9; calculation C1

Net debt is USD 3,437.68 million at 2026-09-30, or USD 3,507.68 million pro forma for the October 2026 licence payment, which is 9.07x trailing reported EBITDA of USD 378.82 million, or 9.26x pro forma; that EBITDA holds Amicus for only about five months and a USD 287.69 million acquired research charge. Liquidity of USD 1,336.01 million covers the USD 660.00 million of principal due in 2027, so the near-term risk is earnings delivery under a covenant package that is not supplied, not a funding gap.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C2

### Key findings

Leverage is the dominant credit issue and its true size cannot be measured from the supplied documents. Net debt moved from a net cash position at the 2025 year end to USD 3,437.68 million, which is 9.07x trailing reported EBITDA, or 9.26x with the USD 3,507.68 million pro forma figure for the October 2026 licence payment. The trailing EBITDA holds Amicus from 2026-04-27 only, so the ratio is too high on a full-year basis, but no pro forma earnings are printed to say by how much, and the company reports covenant compliance against a maximum total net leverage ratio of 3.50x whose definition is not supplied.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bmrn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 50; calculation C1

Interest is the transmission channel. Coupon interest in the third quarter, the first full quarter with the new debt, was USD 52.68 million, or USD 210.73 million annualised, against USD 69.72 million of cash interest paid in the trailing twelve months. Trailing reported EBITDA covers the annualised coupon 1.80x and, before the acquired research charge, 3.16x; the third quarter alone covered cash interest 0.78x and, before the charge, 5.39x. These are analyst figures that mix periods, shown to size the burden, not covenant ratios.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_bmrn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C3

Cash generation is the main support. Operating cash flow was USD 576.97 million and free cash flow after plant and equipment purchases USD 498.94 million in the nine months to 2026-09-30, down 20.78% and 25.09% from a year earlier; the company attributes the lower operating cash flow mainly to lower net income and the timing of payments to vendors, partly offset by lower inventory purchases. Free cash flow excludes the USD 277.88 million paid for acquired research assets and the USD 5,067.63 million paid for Amicus, so it measures operating capacity, not total cash after deals.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bmrn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bmrn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47; calculation C5

Growth is mostly acquired and the largest product is slowing. Revenue rose 18.49% in the nine months, but without the USD 336.42 million Amicus products contributed it rose 4.15%; VOXZOGO, 28.78% of 2025 revenues, grew 9.78% in the nine months after 26.10% in 2025. The three largest products were 68.43% of 2025 revenues, so diversification is average, not strong.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bmrn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 28; 10-K_2025-12-31_bmrn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 209; calculation C6; calculation C7

Liquidity and the 2027 maturity look manageable. Cash of USD 736.01 million alone covers the USD 660.00 million due in 2027 1.12x and cash plus the undrawn revolver covers it 2.02x; the USD 600.00 million convertible notes due in May 2027 had a fair value of 97.65% of principal, which suggests cash repayment rather than conversion. The revolver depends on covenant compliance that cannot be tested, so cash is the firmer cushion, and pro forma cash after the October 2026 licence payment is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C2

Financial policy is restrained on distributions and active in acquisitions. The company has paid no dividend and made no buyback, pre-funded the acquisition notes into escrow and cut the bridge to zero, but it bought three businesses and licensed a programme in barely more than a year and ranks secured lenders ahead of the convertible holders. Business & governance rates owner behaviour medium risk, and this analysis agrees.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bmrn-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 22–25

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a better view is that the covenants were met at 2026-09-30 against a 3.50x maximum, which allows 4.00x for certain acquisitions, so the contract definition of EBITDA must differ materially from reported EBITDA, although its content is not supplied; management aims for gross leverage below 2.50x by mid-2027 and expects about USD 280.00 million of GAAP cost synergies by 2028. Free cash flow of USD 498.94 million in nine months and the high gross margin support debt service, and no dividend leaves the group. That case would win if the credit agreement defines EBITDA to include the missing months of Amicus and synergies and if the fourth quarter shows interest covered comfortably.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_bmrn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 50; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_earningsrelease-6aug26ex991.htm.jsonl, page 3; EX-99.1_2026-11-02_earningsrelease-2nov26ex991.htm.jsonl, page 3; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C5

The case against is that leverage is high on any supported measure: adding back stock-based compensation and the acquired research charge still leaves net debt at 3.88x, in the range of the stated covenant maxima of 3.50x and 4.00x, so there is little evidence of wide headroom unless the covenant definition allows further add-backs, and the 4.00x acquisition allowance is temporary. Organic revenue growth is low, VOXZOGO growth has slowed, and revenue guidance was cut. The view would worsen if fourth-quarter revenue came in at the bottom of the guided range, if a further debt-funded deal was announced, or if the credit agreement defined covenant EBITDA narrowly; it would improve on a pro forma EBITDA that shows run-rate leverage near the covenant, or on a refinancing of the 2027 notes.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bmrn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 50; EX-99.1_2026-11-02_earningsrelease-2nov26ex991.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation C4; calculation C7; calculation C8

Operating cash flow of USD 576.97 million and free cash flow of USD 498.94 million in nine months, with purchases of plant and equipment at only 3.20% of 2025 revenues, give the company internal funds for interest and scheduled amortisation, which supports deleveraging and the 2027 repayment.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_bmrn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 183–184; calculation C5

Strength: liquidity and a pre-funded structure. Cash of USD 736.01 million, an undrawn revolver of USD 600.00 million and escrowed acquisition notes mean no funding gap before the May 2027 maturity, which lowers refinancing pressure; pro forma cash after the October 2026 licence payment is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C2

Strength: a diversified rare-disease franchise with exclusivity into the 2030s. Nine commercial therapies, VOXZOGO patents to 2035 and 2036 and orphan exclusivity to 2030, and acquired Fabry and Pompe products, reduce the risk that one product loss stops debt service.

Net debt of USD 3,437.68 million at 9.07x trailing reported EBITDA, or USD 3,507.68 million and 9.26x pro forma for the October 2026 licence payment, with annualised coupon interest of USD 210.73 million, leaves a thin cushion against an earnings shortfall until Amicus earnings are fully counted.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C3

### Company description

BioMarin develops, makes and sells medicines for rare genetically defined diseases, with one operating segment and nine commercial therapies after the Amicus acquisition. Revenue comes from product sales to a small number of specialty pharmacies, distributors and governments, and from a collaboration in which Sanofi sells ALDURAZYME. That model gives visibility because patients are chronic and treatments are not discretionary, but it also concentrates cash flow in a few products and in lumpy government and international orders; both facts shape debt service capacity and make quarterly cover ratios unreliable.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_bmrn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 7–18

### Ownership and group structure

The company is publicly held with no controlling holder identified in the sources, wholly owns Amicus, Alesta and Inozyme, and has paid no dividend. Holder percentages, board independence and related-party dealings are not supplied, and guarantor subsidiaries are unnamed, so structurally senior claims at subsidiary level cannot be sized. Financial policy is shown by behaviour: a conservative record on distributions and an acquisitive record on deals, with leverage tolerance revealed by funding about two thirds of the Amicus price with new debt.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Volume growth from patient additions drives VOXZOGO, while the enzyme therapies are mature; revenue is recurring, regulated and concentrated, not cyclical. The risks are price controls and special access programmes in international markets, where about 65.12% of 2025 net product revenues arose, substitution by rival products in achondroplasia, and generic competition for KUVAN. Switching costs are high for chronic enzyme therapy, which supports retention, but one international order can move revenue between quarters.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bmrn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 5–7; 10-K_2025-12-31_bmrn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19; 10-K_2025-12-31_bmrn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 209; calculation C6

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Research and development and selling, general and administrative costs were 64.41% of 2025 revenues, and most are discretionary, so management can cut them if earnings fall, as it is doing after Amicus through restructuring. Cost of sales is a smaller share, giving a high gross margin, but 2026 margins carry inventory step-up amortisation, restructuring charges and integration cost. Margins can be defended under mild volume pressure through cost cuts, but acquired research purchases and fixed interest are not flexible and would dominate in a sharp revenue shortfall.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_bmrn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 181–182; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bmrn-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–30; calculation C8

### Capital intensity and free cash flow conversion

Capital spending is low at 3.20% of 2025 revenues, and free cash flow exceeded reported EBITDA in each period shown because stock-based compensation, impairments and non-cash acquired research reduce EBITDA but not operating cash. Working capital absorbs cash as receivables and inventory grow with revenue, and 2026 adds restructuring cash and higher cash interest. EBITDA translates into free cash flow that can fund debt service, but conversion will fall as annual interest reaches the annualised level.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bmrn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10; calculation C3; calculation C5

### Porter's five forces

Rivalry in achondroplasia is rising: named clinical-stage competitors are developing rival products, and a competitor's product is on the market under a royalty settlement, which caps pricing power and growth. New entrants are limited by orphan exclusivity, patents to 2035 and 2036 for VOXZOGO and the small patient pools, but exclusivity for VIMIZIM in the United States ends in 2026 and NAGLAZYME and ALDURAZYME patents have expired. Buyer power is moderate: three customers take 37% of net product revenues and governments and payers press on price. Supplier power is moderate because some raw materials come from a sole or single source and manufacture sits in two sites, which creates supply-interruption risk to cash flow.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bmrn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 16–19; 10-K_2025-12-31_bmrn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 23–25; EX-99.1_2026-11-02_earningsrelease-2nov26ex991.htm.jsonl, page 3

### PEST analysis

Regulation and currency are material to credit. Government price controls and healthcare reform can cut revenue, and approvals set the pace of growth: the FDA acts on VOXZOGO applications on 2027-02-28 and 2027-05-30. About 65.12% of net product revenues arise outside the United States, mostly in euros, and cash flow hedges with a notional USD 1,748.17 million protect forecast flows for up to two years. Social and technological factors are not primary credit drivers on the provided materials.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bmrn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 5–7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bmrn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21; EX-99.1_2026-11-02_earningsrelease-2nov26ex991.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C6

Competitive advantage is average: patents and orphan exclusivity protect VOXZOGO but rivals are advancing and older enzyme patents have expired. Financial policy and governance is average: no distributions and escrowed financing against repeated deals and unseen covenants. ### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over 12 to 24 months the tailwinds are Amicus cost synergies, a full year of Amicus sales, the FDA action dates for VOXZOGO on 2027-02-28 and 2027-05-30, and the Ascendis royalty; the headwinds are interest on the new debt, the May 2027 convertible maturity, order timing and a lower revenue guidance range. Guidance implies fourth-quarter revenue of USD 1,019.44 million to USD 1,119.44 million against USD 1,024.65 million in the third quarter. Over three to five years the tailwinds are patent protection to 2035 and 2036 and pipeline data in 2027 and 2028; the headwinds are generic and biosimilar entry on older products, price regulation and contingent milestone obligations of up to USD 510.00 million for the Dimerix licence plus milestones on other licences.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-11-02_earningsrelease-2nov26ex991.htm.jsonl, pages 2–5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bmrn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-K_2025-12-31_bmrn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 23–25; calculation C9

### Qualitative downside scenario

The initiating condition is a shortfall in the largest products: the delayed international VOXZOGO order slips again, or a competing achondroplasia product takes new patients, while Amicus integration costs run over. The first operational break is lower VOXZOGO and enzyme sales in a quarter, which cuts revenue and, with high fixed research and selling costs, cuts EBITDA by more than revenue. The financial transmission is lower operating cash flow against fixed interest on the new debt and scheduled amortisation, which pushes reported leverage up and puts pressure on a covenant defined on EBITDA that is not disclosed. Management levers are cuts to discretionary research and selling spend, pausing acquired research purchases, and drawing the revolver, but the revolver is only available while covenants are met. The resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress through 2027, because cash alone covers the principal due, but the refinancing of the May 2027 notes and any covenant breach are the observable triggers.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.1_2026-11-02_earningsrelease-2nov26ex991.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bmrn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 50; calculation C2

### Overall credit view

Overall, BioMarin presents an adequate business profile supported by cash generation, low capital needs, a diversified rare-disease portfolio with long patent protection and a pre-funded financing, but credit quality is constrained by high leverage after the Amicus acquisition, concentration in VOXZOGO and a few customers, and covenants that are not visible. Cash-flow durability appears adequate due to high conversion of EBITDA to free cash flow, with order timing and rising interest as the swing factors. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on delivery of Amicus earnings, covenant headroom and market access ahead of the May 2027 convertible maturity. The primary downside path is a revenue shortfall in VOXZOGO or the enzyme therapies against the new fixed interest burden, which would push reported leverage up and test an undisclosed covenant. Further analysis would require pro forma EBITDA, the credit agreement and its definitions, and the proxy statement. Fundamental credit completed.
