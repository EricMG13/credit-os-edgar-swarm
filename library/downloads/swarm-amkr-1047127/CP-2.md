## Findings

### Bottom line
Amkor is a low-leverage, liquid credit entering a heavily debt-funded capacity build, so creditors are exposed to execution and customer-funding risk rather than to coverage or near-term maturities. Net leverage rose from 0.06x at the end of 2025 to 0.61x at September 30, 2026, or 0.64x pro forma for the Arizona land purchase expected to close in the fourth quarter of 2026, largely because nine-month capital spending of USD 1.24 billion exceeded operating cash flow of USD 868.35 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5–8; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation K01; calculation K03

Management guides to about USD 2.5 billion of capital spending in 2026, and customers are expected to prepay USD 1.9 billion within twelve months, but those advances are obligations to deliver capacity and have not been received. We read the credit as adequate today and weakening in direction.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 36–37; calculation K04

### Key findings
Debt service is not the constraint; the investment program is. Last-twelve-month EBITDA of USD 1.41 billion covers accrued interest expense 19.30x, and in the first nine months of 2026 interest income of USD 59.12 million exceeded interest expense of USD 52.60 million, helped by the 0.00% coupon on the largest new issue, the convertible notes due 2031, and by interest capitalized on the Arizona build. Cash interest cover was 15.07x in FY2025 and cash interest for 2026 is not printed. The implication is that the debt stack could absorb a materially weaker earnings year before coverage became the issue, so the credit question is whether cash generation keeps pace with spending.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest case against a weakening view is that Amkor starts the build from low net leverage of 0.61x, or 0.64x pro forma for the expected land purchase, with net interest income, USD 3,376.2 million of cash, short-term investments and undrawn revolver, little principal due before 2030 and customers who are prepaying for capacity, which suggests the demand is contracted rather than hoped for. Capital spending that is not yet contracted can be deferred, and EBITDA margin moved up to 20.66% in the third quarter from 16.23% a year earlier. The case that the credit is weaker than that is that reported cash flow already leans on advances, commitments nearly doubled in one quarter, third-quarter sales barely grew, the largest customer is 29.8% of sales and covenants cannot be tested. The view would improve if advances arrive on schedule while 2027 spending is held back, if the USD 407 million government award starts to be received, or if quarterly free cash flow turns positive. It would worsen if advances slip, the revolver is drawn to fund spending, a major customer's programs are lost, the mix-driven margin gain reverses, or payouts continue after cumulative free cash flow falls below the level that supports them.

### Company description
Amkor is the largest US-headquartered outsourced semiconductor assembly and test provider. It packages and tests chips designed by integrated device makers, fabless companies and foundries, usually on silicon wafers that the customer consigns, and reports one operating segment. The business model supports cash-flow visibility only in part: the filings describe short customer commitments and no backlog, so there is little contractual protection, but consigned wafers and customer responsibility for unused materials limit the inventory risk Amkor carries, and the move of sales toward computing and automotive reduces dependence on smartphones. ### Ownership and group structure
Amkor is a listed company whose votes are substantially influenced by one family. Susan Y. Kim, the Chairman, and family members and affiliates own about 49.5% of the shares and can determine or substantially influence shareholder votes and board elections; about 29.6 million of those shares, received in a 2013 note exchange, are subject to a voting agreement that requires them to be voted neutrally to the extent the family's aggregate voting holding exceeds 41.6%. For creditors the mechanism is that a leveraged recapitalization or sale needs family support and family interests are tied to the franchise, which lowers takeover risk, while payout policy is set by a family-influenced board. The parent borrows and issues the notes; the Korean and Japanese subsidiaries borrow locally, Guardian Assets, Inc. guarantees the notes and the revolver, and Amkor Technology Singapore Holding Pte. Ltd. also guarantees the revolver, so debt of the parent is structurally behind subsidiary liabilities.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power
Revenue is cyclical, concentrated and exposed to program timing, not recurring. FY2025 net sales were USD 6,707.981 million, with 29.8% from the largest customer, 11.1% from a second and 72% from the ten largest; communications fell as a share from 46% in FY2025 to 37% in the third quarter of 2026. Management describes the semiconductor industry as cyclical and lists the absence of backlog and the short-term nature of customer commitments among the factors that move results, so volume visibility beyond the advance payment agreements is limited. Pricing power is not evidenced: the gross margin gain came from a lower-material-content mix and higher utilization, not from stated price increases. The credit consequence is that a program transfer, lost design or inventory correction at one or two customers moves revenue and utilization faster than costs can adjust, while growth in computing and in automotive and industrial of 37% each in the third quarter shows where new volume is coming from.

### Cost structure and margin resilience
Margins can be defended in a growth quarter but not shown to hold under volume pressure. Materials were 50.7% of third-quarter net sales against 57.5% a year earlier, and because customers consign wafers and bear unused materials, materials behave largely as a pass-through, so the margin is set by what remains after materials. Net sales less materials rose 16.15% to USD 981.264 million while conversion costs rose 10.25%, which is operating leverage working in Amkor's favor; with a substantial fixed cost base, the same leverage reverses when volume falls. Labor was 10.1% of net sales and depreciation 8.6%, and depreciation will rise as the Arizona facility is placed in service. The implication is that EBITDA margin of 20.66% in the third quarter should be read as a favorable-mix level until a falling-volume quarter shows the floor.

### Covenants, liquidity detail and claims outside debt
The agreements governing the debt contain a minimum interest coverage ratio and a maximum consolidated leverage ratio, and management states that Amkor was in compliance at September 30, 2026 and expects to remain so for at least twelve months; no level, step-down or definition is printed, so headroom is not calculable and the leverage computed here, carrying debt over calculated EBITDA, may differ from the covenant definition. ### Porter's five forces
Competitive rivalry is moderated by technical barriers at the advanced end, where flip chip, memory and wafer-level processing were 81.82% of nine-month sales, but the annual report names established providers mainly in Asia, contract foundries and electronics manufacturers offering some advanced packaging, Chinese firms building a China-for-China supply chain and customers' own in-house capabilities; mainstream wirebond products, 18.18% of sales, are where that rivalry is least cushioned by technology. Buyer power is the dominant force: the ten largest customers were 72% of net sales and the largest alone 29.8%, and commitments are short, so a large customer can move programs between suppliers and sites, as the transfer from Korea to Vietnam shows; credit consequence is volatile utilization and a margin that depends on retaining a few programs.
