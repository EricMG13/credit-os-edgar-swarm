### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a better view is that reported earnings understate the run rate: GESA and Omega are in results only from July 1 and September 1, 2026, the filing shows pro forma nine-month revenue of USD 1,249.4 million against USD 756.7 million reported, much of the USD 1,382.4 million of capital spend has not yet started earning rent, and the company states compliance with a net leverage limit of 5.25x. Contracted lease payments of USD 801.0 million fall due in 2027 alone, and the largest customers are affiliates of investment-grade technology companies. The view would improve if the Stateline leases start and the Hatchbo deployments begin in the first quarter of 2027 as planned, if pro forma EBITDA and the covenant ratios show leverage well inside the limits, and if the October 2026 notes leave liquidity above commitments. It would worsen if lease starts slip, if a large customer uses a termination right, or if the acquired businesses show control weaknesses when first tested.

### Company, ownership and group structure

Solaris leases power generation equipment and sells related engineering, construction and operations services to data center, energy and industrial customers, and still builds oilfield proppant-handling equipment. Power Solutions made 75% of revenue in the first nine months of 2026, while Logistics Solutions revenue fell 13% to USD 185.6 million on lower last-mile activity. Lease payments are fixed for the lease term, which gives visibility once equipment is on rent, whereas services and construction revenue is project-based and the oilfield segment follows drilling activity, so cash-flow visibility, and with it debt service and refinancing confidence, depends mainly on how fast contracted leases start.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_sei.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sei.htm.jsonl, page 37; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sei.htm.jsonl, page 69; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sei.htm.jsonl, page 71

Solaris Energy Infrastructure, Inc. is a listed holding company that consolidates the operating company, Solaris Energy Infrastructure, LLC, and Stateline Power, LLC, which it owns 50.1% with the customer holding the rest and whose term loan of USD 408.2 million is non-recourse but counted in group debt. The senior notes are issued by the operating company and guaranteed by the listed company and its restricted subsidiaries, the revolver is secured on substantially all their assets, and the convertible notes are obligations of the listed company that rank behind the senior notes to the extent of the subsidiary guarantees, so creditors of the convertible notes are structurally junior. The filings describe no controlling holder: Class B holders, including Yorktown, the Chairman and legacy sellers of Mobile Energy Rentals, held about 22% of votes in early 2026, and a charter waiver lets designated parties compete and take corporate opportunities, which is a governance weakness without evidence of use.

### Revenue, margin and cash conversion

Revenue is predictable once leases start but concentrated, partly cyclical and changing in mix. Leasing revenue rests on contracts of two to fifteen years with extension options, services revenue on project work, and oilfield logistics on drilling and completion activity; one customer was 59% of third-quarter revenue and 67% of receivables, and Power Solutions was 75% of revenue. The credit consequence is that a delay or exit by one counterparty would move cash flow immediately, while the contract book of USD 6,751.5 million supports the long-run level.

### Industry forces, macro factors and SWOT

Buyer power is high and supplier power is meaningful. A few large investment-grade customers can negotiate or exit, since the first-signed Hatchbo contract could be ended on 30 days notice for a payment of 50% of remaining rent, and one supplier was 38% of purchases while turbine delivery runs to 2029; the credit effect is pressure on renewal terms and on build timing. Rivalry and substitutes are rising: the filings expect significant competition as distributed power develops and note that distributed power competes with grid access in some uses, so lease renewals after the first term are not assured.

### Downside scenario

The most credible downside is a delay or loss of a large lease while debt-funded commitments continue. The initiating condition is a late turbine delivery or other delay in making equipment available, since each lease starts only when equipment is ready for the customer, or a customer using its right to end the Hatchbo contract on 30 days notice; the first operational break is equipment that sits idle without rent, as at Stateline where no lease had started by September 30, 2026. Revenue and free cash flow then fall short of plan, while coupons on the senior notes of USD 170.4 million a year, purchase commitments of USD 1,323.9 million and about USD 1 billion of capital spend continue, so liquidity of USD 924.5 million, down USD 474.6 million in one quarter, would be drawn down further before the October 2026 notes are counted. Leverage rises further from 8.83x, the Stateline loan faces its own covenants from the quarter ending March 31, 2027, and the credit agreement can force repayment or cut borrowing if a material contract ends early, which turns a revenue problem into a liquidity problem. Management can cancel purchase commitments at a penalty of 5% to 90%, defer capital spend, stop the dividend of USD 0.12 a share a quarter and issue shares, and resilience appears sufficient to avoid distress for the next twelve months only if markets stay open; no stress figure is produced because no run-rate earnings or covenant ratios were supplied. The observable triggers are lease commencement dates, revolver availability and any change in the compliance statement.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Solaris presents a business profile that is not fully assessable against peers but is supported by contracted lease income with investment-grade counterparties, proven market access and low cash leakage, and credit quality is constrained by leverage of 8.83x reported EBITDA, negative free cash flow and concentration on a few customers. Cash-flow durability appears weak today and adequate only once leases start, because operating cash flow of USD 573.9 million covers about a third of capital expenditure. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on lease starts on schedule, open convertible and bond markets, and the 2031 wall of 85.34% of principal. The primary downside path is a lease delay or customer exit while debt-funded commitments continue. Further analysis would require pro forma EBITDA, covenant ratios and definitions, the customer contracts and the effect of the October 2026 notes on cash. The monitoring triggers are lease commencement dates, liquidity against commitments, total debt to EBITDA and any change in the covenant compliance statement. Top risk: lease starts slipping or a large contract ending while debt-funded commitments are outstanding.
