## Findings

### Bottom line

Net debt reached 4.69x last-twelve-months EBITDA at 30 September 2026, up from 1.61x at the end of 2024, and EBITDA covered cash interest paid only 1.23x, which leaves creditors little cushion before the Beazer acquisition adds further debt.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dfh.htm.jsonl, page 70; 10-K_2025-12-31_dfh.htm.jsonl, page 90; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dfh.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dfh.htm.jsonl, page 51; calculation C3; calculation C8; calculation C10

The issuer's EBITDA adds back interest charged to homebuilding cost of sales, which makes up 56.15% of last-twelve-months EBITDA against 29.75% for 2024, so these ratios depend on that definition and a lender who excludes the add-back sees much weaker coverage.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dfh.htm.jsonl, page 70; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dfh.htm.jsonl, page 51; calculation C12; calculation C13

Pro forma net debt for the Beazer acquisition that closed on 20 October 2026 is not established: the sources give USD 435.3 million of Series B preferred proceeds and USD 572.1 million of land bank proceeds, but not the Credit Agreement drawing at closing, Beazer's cash or how the land bank funding is recorded.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_dfh.htm.jsonl, pages 29–30; EX99.1_2026-09-30_dfh_ex991closingpr-htm.htm.jsonl, page 2

The maturity ladder is also not established, because the sources give no maturity dates for the warehouse lines or other borrowings and do not split the revolver drawings between its two tranches.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_dfh.htm.jsonl, pages 9–13
