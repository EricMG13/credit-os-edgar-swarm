## Findings

### Bottom line

Columbus McKinnon has an adequate business and a stretched financial profile, so creditors depend on Kito Crosby earnings and synergies arriving while liquidity and long maturities buy time.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation N01

Liquidity of USD 531.98 million and no scheduled principal above USD 69.90 million before 2031 support the credit, but quarterly interest cover is 2.31x, free cash flow was only USD 4.60 million in H1 FY2027 and the issuer's 4.9x leverage figure rests on annualised earnings. The main downside is a margin reversal in a distributor-led, steel-exposed business that carries fixed interest charges of USD 185 million to USD 190 million a year.

Net debt of USD 2,301.41 million at 2026-09-30, or USD 2,303.44 million pro forma for the common dividend payable on 2026-11-16, is 6.95x issuer-adjusted twelve-month EBITDA of USD 331.19 million, and 6.95x pro forma. ### Company description

Columbus McKinnon designs, makes and markets lifting, rigging, conveying and motion-control products and, since 2026-02-03, includes Kito Crosby, a lifting and securement business that supplied 56.50% of H1 FY2027 net sales. The issuer describes its consumables as safety-critical and frequently replaced, which is management language but points to repeat demand tied to inspection cycles, while hoists, cranes and conveyors depend on industrial capital spending. Sales go mainly through distributors and crane builders, 44% of FY2026 sales were outside the United States, and there is one reporting segment. For creditors the replacement element supports cash-flow visibility and debt service, the cyclical element limits it, and the larger scale after the acquisition helps access to capital markets without removing the integration risk.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is partly recurring and partly cyclical. In FY2026 price added USD 21.54 million and volume USD 10.15 million to net sales, and in Q2 FY2027 price added USD 10.06 million against USD 2.34 million from volume, so price supplied 81.14% of the organic gain in the quarter. That points to pricing power, but it also means volume growth is thin, and the issuer ties demand to industrial capacity utilisation, production indices, tariffs and interest rates. Backlog rose 61.12% to USD 519.64 million by March 2026 mainly through Kito Crosby, long-term backlog was 50.8% of the total, and book-to-bill was 1.0x in Q2 FY2027. ### Qualitative downside scenario

The initiating condition is an industrial slowdown with steel and tariff cost pressure that leads distributors, who hold the stocking decision and have no written agreements, to cut orders. The first operational break is a fall in short-cycle orders, with book-to-bill below 1.0x, followed by price giving way to volume and gross margin falling back from the 34.1% Adjusted gross margin of FY2026. Financially, on illustrative arithmetic that returns the Adjusted EBITDA margin to the 15.20% of FY2026 on the guided sales midpoint of USD 2,150 million, Adjusted EBITDA would be USD 326.80 million, interest cover 1.74x, and EBITDA less guided interest and capex USD 89.30 million before taxes, integration and working capital. Cash taxes at the FY2026 level of USD 45.85 million would leave USD 43.45 million, so the planned USD 150.00 million paydown would not be met from cash flow and the revolver would fund the gap. Revolver borrowings could rise by USD 90.36 million, or USD 71.08 million if letters of credit count, before the 30% level at which the first lien leverage covenant is tested, and the ratio against its 7.75x ceiling is not reported. Management levers are capex, which can be cut, the in-kind preferred dividend, a common dividend of USD 8.04 million a year, working capital release from USD 567.55 million of inventory and synergy savings. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid a liquidity crisis within 12 to 24 months because no principal above USD 69.90 million falls due before 2031, but it is not sufficient to protect the deleveraging plan, and the observable triggers are book-to-bill, the Adjusted EBITDA margin, revolver use, and any new AR facility waiver.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Columbus McKinnon presents an adequate business profile supported by replacement-driven products, a wide channel and added scale, but credit quality is constrained by leverage of 6.95x on issuer-adjusted EBITDA (6.95x pro forma for the dividend payable on 2026-11-16), thin free cash flow and integration risk. Cash-flow durability appears weak today and not yet demonstrated, because free cash flow was 2.07% of H1 Adjusted EBITDA. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on cutting leverage before the 2033 term loan and notes come due, on extending the AR facility due 2028-08-11 and on keeping the revolver unused near the 30% covenant level. The primary downside path is a margin reversal on fixed interest charges that forces revolver use and defers deleveraging. Further analysis would require the credit agreement and first lien ratio, the investor agreements, Kito Crosby's standalone earnings, a rating and market prices. Top risk: leverage that depends on annualised earnings and unproven free cash flow.
