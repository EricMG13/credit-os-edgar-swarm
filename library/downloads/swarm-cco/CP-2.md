## Findings

### Bottom line

The fundamental credit profile is weak on leverage but supported by a regulated, growing outdoor advertising business, so creditors depend on refinancing the 2028 and 2029 maturities and on how the pending take-private is financed, not on cash generation.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C8

Total debt is 9.38x Adjusted EBITDA and cash interest absorbs 71.67% of that EBITDA, so a fall of 15.48% in Adjusted EBITDA would remove free cash flow. Liquidity of about USD 398 million covers the USD 197 million of cash interest due in the second half of 2026 twice over, and no debt matures before 2028. Net debt of about USD 4.9 billion is not pro forma for the Spain sale completed on 4 August 2026, whose effect on net debt is not established.
Basis: calculation C3; calculation C1; calculation C7; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Key findings

- Leverage, not liquidity, is the binding constraint. Total debt is 9.38x Adjusted EBITDA and 10.46x reported EBITDA, cash interest of about USD 390 million costs 7.64% of debt and absorbs 71.67% of Adjusted EBITDA, and free cash flow after interest and capital expenditure is only about USD 84 million. A fall of 15.48% in Adjusted EBITDA removes it, and a fall of 28.33% leaves earnings equal to cash interest. Debt barely moved (up 0.09% from year-end 2025), so the improvement from 10.11x came from earnings and stays hostage to the advertising cycle, not to repayment.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C14; Financial foundation (CP-1)

- The 2028 and 2029 maturities decide the credit, and the Merger can move them either way. Maturities of about USD 2.2 billion fall due in those two years, 43.33% of principal, starting with the 2028 senior notes about 17 months after the as-of date. About USD 1.8 billion of unsecured 2028 and 2029 notes carry conditional redemption notices that bite only if the Merger completes. If the acquirer retires them with equity, refinancing risk falls; if it funds the redemption with secured debt, the secured share of principal, now 64.87%, rises and the secured claim grows ahead of what remains. The financing is not disclosed, so the direction is not established.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C9; calculation C16; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-06-30_cco-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-06-30_cco-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 34

- Business quality is better than the capital structure it carries. The company is a regulated United States outdoor operator whose revenue grew 6.57% in 2025 and 10.17% in the first half of 2026, with digital displays at 44.1% of revenue and an Adjusted EBITDA margin of 32.41%. Barriers to new inventory and exclusive airport and street furniture contracts protect the franchise, but site leases cost 38.91% of revenue and are partly fixed minimums, so profit moves by more than sales when demand turns.
Basis: calculation C14; calculation C18; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 57; Business & governance (CP-1A)

- Earnings quality and leases qualify the headline leverage. Adjusted EBITDA is 11.50% above reported EBITDA and its last twelve months include a calculated quarter, so the 10.46x reported ratio is the more demanding read. Operating lease liabilities equal 26.66% of total debt and sit outside the debt ratios, so the true fixed-charge burden is heavier than any ratio shown.
Basis: calculation C13; calculation C15; Financial foundation (CP-1)

- Liquidity is adequate for about a year, not for a prolonged downturn. Cash and undrawn facilities of about USD 398 million cover second-half 2026 cash interest 2.02x but 2027 cash interest of about USD 394 million only 1.01x, and receivables availability is limited by a borrowing base. A first lien leverage covenant of 7.10x applies only if the revolver is drawn or undrawn letters of credit exceed USD 10 million, and its headroom cannot be calculated here. A fall of 20% in Adjusted EBITDA would turn free cash flow negative at about USD 25 million with cover of 1.12x.
Basis: calculation C7; calculation C5; 10-Q_2026-06-30_cco-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-06-30_cco-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 109

- Financial policy has favoured creditors, but control is about to change. No dividend has been paid, disposal proceeds prepaid the term loan of a former subsidiary and notes were bought back at a discount, yet the cost of debt rose in the 2025 refinancing and the creditor consents to the Merger remove change-of-control protection. The acquirer's behaviour cannot be inferred from its identity, so the governance assessment stays at average with high uncertainty.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 73; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 102; 10-Q_2026-06-30_cco-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a better view is that earnings are compounding while debt is flat: revenue grew 10.17% in the first half of 2026 and Adjusted EBITDA for the last twelve months is 7.82% above 2025, no debt matures for about 17 months, and the Merger arrives with creditor consents, a receivables facility to be extended to five years from completion and conditional redemption notices already issued for USD 1.8 billion of unsecured notes. If earnings kept growing at the recent pace with debt unchanged, leverage would keep falling without any refinancing.
Basis: calculation C14; calculation C9; calculation C16; 10-Q_2026-06-30_cco-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The view would improve if the acquirer disclosed financing that retires the unsecured notes with equity and leaves secured debt unchanged, if leverage fell below 9.00x on reported earnings, or if cash interest cover rose above 1.50x on Adjusted EBITDA. It would worsen if revenue or margin turned down so that Adjusted EBITDA fell by 15.48% or more, if the Merger failed with the 2028 notes still unrefinanced, or if the acquirer funded redemption with debt ranking ahead of the notes it replaced.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C5; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Basis: calculation C5; calculation C6; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Strengths and weaknesses

The main credit strengths are a regulated franchise, rising earnings and enough near-term liquidity. Barriers to new billboard inventory and exclusive airport and street furniture contracts protect pricing, so revenue grew 6.57% in 2025; digital conversion lifted the digital share to 44.1% and the Adjusted EBITDA margin to 32.41%; and cash and undrawn facilities of about USD 398 million cover cash interest for the rest of 2026 twice over with no maturity before 2028. Creditors also benefit from a record of no dividends, disposal proceeds applied to debt and, until the Merger closes, a bar on new borrowing and distributions.
Basis: calculation C14; calculation C7; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 57; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The main weaknesses are leverage, concentrated maturities and a fixed cost base. Total debt is 9.38x Adjusted EBITDA with only 1.40x cover, so a fall of 15.48% in Adjusted EBITDA removes free cash flow; USD 2.2 billion falls due in 2028 and 2029 with the buyer's financing undisclosed; and site leases cost 38.91% of revenue, partly in fixed minimums, with operating lease liabilities of 26.66% of debt outside the ratios. On the nine dimensions of the financial profile, scale and competitive advantage are average, diversification is weak, cost flexibility and free cash flow stability are weak, margin stability is average, refinancing access and liquidity are average, and financial policy and governance are average pending the change of control.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C8; calculation C18; calculation C15; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Company and ownership

The company sells advertising on roadside billboards, street furniture and airport displays, almost entirely in the United States, and about 75% of revenue comes from the America segment and about 25% from Airports. Most advertising is sold in four-week periods, so revenue reprices quickly in both directions, while contracts for sites, street furniture and airports run for years with minimum fees, so costs are slow to move. The mix gives short revenue visibility and a long fixed commitment, which is why modest revenue declines hit free cash flow quickly.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 16; calculation C18; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Ownership is dispersed today and passes to an acquirer vehicle on completion of the Merger, with funds advised by Mubadala Capital named as the consortium and TWG Global named in earlier documents but not in the latest. Creditors consented to amendments that make the Merger not a change of control, so they hold no takeover put, and the sponsor's willingness to support the credit is not evidenced; no sponsor history, equity cure or parent guarantee is supplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-06-30_cco-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-06-30_cco-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17

### Revenue, costs and cash conversion

Revenue is recurring in character but not contractual in the short run: advertising spend follows economic conditions, is sold mostly in four-week blocks and shows no single advertiser as material, so demand is diversified but reprices fast. Airports revenue depends on air travel demand and America on local market conditions, so a downturn reaches revenue within a quarter. The credit consequence is stable medium-term revenue with high sensitivity to the cycle in any single year.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 26

Site leases, which cost 38.91% of revenue, combine minimum payments with revenue sharing, so margin is defended only partly when volume falls; the Adjusted EBITDA margin of 32.41% would compress faster than revenue if costs are as fixed as the contracts suggest, and the two bounds of 5.02% and 15.48% for the revenue fall that removes free cash flow show how wide that uncertainty is. Capital expenditure of 3.85% of revenue in 2025 is a flexible lever, because digital conversions are discretionary, but cutting it slows the growth that supports the margin.
Basis: calculation C18; calculation C6; calculation C11; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 64; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 65

Conversion of Adjusted EBITDA into free cash flow is low: 6.34% in 2025 and 15.48% in the last twelve months, because cash interest takes 71.67% of EBITDA before capital expenditure, tax and working capital. That leaves almost nothing for debt reduction, so deleveraging must come from earnings growth or asset sales; cash flows combine continuing and discontinued operations, which could overstate the continuing business's conversion.
Basis: calculation C12; calculation C3; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Competitive forces and external factors

Rivalry is moderate: the United States outdoor industry is fragmented, with Lamar and Outfront among its other large operators and many local ones, and competition turns on audience reach, location quality and measurable results; the company can manage rates when demand rises because inventory is limited, which supports margin in an upswing but gives no protection in a downturn. New entrants face high barriers, because federal, state and local rules limit new billboard inventory and airport, transit and street furniture rights sit in exclusive contracts, which supports franchise value and recovery prospects but exposes the company to rebid risk when contracts expire. Substitutes are the main long-run threat, since outdoor advertising is only 2.3% of United States advertising spend and competes with digital media, while an outside forecast the company cites expects outdoor revenue to grow 4.0% a year from 2026 to 2030. Buyers are diversified and short-term, which lowers concentration risk and shortens visibility; suppliers, meaning landlords and authorities, hold the stronger hand because lease and concession cost is a very large expense and partly fixed.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 32; calculation C18

Political and regulatory factors matter because billboard control laws, possible taxes on outdoor revenue and review of the Merger by the interagency committee on foreign investment shape inventory, cost and the timing of the take-private; economic factors matter most, because advertising spend follows output and borrowing costs remain elevated. Social and technology factors are not primary credit drivers: digital conversion needs capital expenditure but the filings do not show it threatening the franchise.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 26

In credit terms the strengths are a protected franchise, growing digital revenue, firm recent growth and adequate near-term liquidity; the weaknesses are high leverage, thin cover, a fixed cost base and a concentrated refinancing wall. The opportunities are digital conversion, Airports growth and, if the Merger completes with equity, a lighter unsecured stack; the threats are an advertising downturn, falling air travel, failure to refinance 2028 and an acquirer that adds secured debt.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 6

### Outlook

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months the near-term outlook is supportive but unproven: first-half revenue growth of 10.17% and rising margin favour a further fall in leverage if debt stays flat, no debt matures, and the Merger caps new borrowing until it closes; headwinds are the cash cost of the Merger, a seasonally weak first quarter that leaves debt service resting on the lowest earnings of the year, and elevated interest rates. Over three to five years the outlook depends on the 2028 and 2029 refinancing and on whether the acquirer adds or removes debt; a sector tailwind is the outside forecast of 4.0% annual growth in outdoor revenue to 2030, which is the company's cited view and not tested here. The company gives no guidance, so the outlook is an analyst reading.
Basis: calculation C14; calculation C16; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-12-31_cco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 16; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Downside scenario

A realistic downside starts with an advertising slowdown that cuts revenue across both segments and a fall in air travel that hits Airports. Because most advertising is sold in four-week blocks, revenue falls within a quarter, while site lease minimums and concession fees do not, so Adjusted EBITDA falls by more than revenue; a fall of 15.48% in Adjusted EBITDA removes free cash flow, and a fall of 20% turns it negative at about USD 25 million with cover of 1.12x. The gap is funded from cash and undrawn availability of about USD 398 million, which lasts about one year of cash interest, so liquidity is not distressed in 2027 but the 2028 notes would have to be refinanced with cover near 1.12x, at a time when the cost of that debt is likely higher than the average of 7.64%. Management can cut capital expenditure, which was 3.85% of revenue in 2025, and apply Spain proceeds to debt, subject to the Merger. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress through 2027 but not sufficient to refinance 2028 on current terms without a recovery or sponsor support.
Basis: calculation C5; calculation C7; calculation C11; calculation C6; 10-Q_2026-06-30_cco-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 9

### Overall credit view

Overall, Clear Channel Outdoor Holdings presents an adequate business profile supported by a regulated United States franchise, rising digital revenue and firm recent growth, but credit quality is constrained by leverage of 9.38x Adjusted EBITDA and thin cash interest cover of 1.40x. Cash-flow durability appears weak because free cash flow is about USD 84 million, which a fall of 15.48% in Adjusted EBITDA removes. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on earnings growth, on whether the Merger completes and on how the acquirer finances the redemption of the 2028 and 2029 unsecured notes. The primary downside path is an advertising downturn that reaches earnings through fixed site costs and meets the 2028 maturities with little cushion. Further analysis would require the acquirer's financing, a management projection and covenant workings. Top risk: refinancing the 2028 and 2029 maturities after an earnings downturn.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C8; calculation C5; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)
