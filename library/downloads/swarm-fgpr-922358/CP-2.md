## Findings

### Bottom line

Fundamental credit quality is constrained: a scale propane distributor earns about USD 321.3 million of adjusted EBITDA, but leverage of 4.81x, interest cover of 2.54x and preferred payouts that absorb 87.24% of free cash flow leave little room for a warm winter.
Basis: calculation C02; calculation C03; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 64

The business profile is adequate, with the second-largest US retail propane position, tank-owning customers and margins that held in fiscal 2026. The financial profile is weak: cash interest cover fell from 3.56x to 2.54x, liquidity was USD 195.1 million against USD 259.7 million a year earlier, and USD 912.5 million of revolver and note maturities fall between October 2028 and April 2029.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C06; calculation C07; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 64

### Key findings

The business profile is adequate: scale and customer stickiness support cash-flow visibility, but weather decides the volume. The company is the second-largest US retail marketer with a national footprint, about 70% of its residential customers use company-owned tanks, and 56% of propane volume is sold in the five winter months, so a warm winter cut volumes by 3.13% and revenue by 3.83% in fiscal 2026.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C09; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 62

Margins are the main support. Adjusted EBITDA fell only 2.84% on the 3.83% revenue fall, and its margin stayed within 0.22 percentage points over three years, at 17.24% in fiscal 2026, because cost of sales fell faster than revenue. This rests on the issuer's own add-backs: reported EBITDA of fiscal 2025 was only 58.15% of adjusted EBITDA, mostly because of legal charges, so the stable margin is an adjusted view.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C16; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 59–61; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 167–168

Free cash flow is the weakest dimension. Conversion of adjusted EBITDA into free cash flow fell from 55.05% in fiscal 2024 to 17.04% and 23.45% in the next two years as cash interest paid rose 41.85% and capital expenditure took 24.06% of adjusted EBITDA. Preferred distributions took 87.24% of the year's free cash flow, and with the final Class B payout the distributions were 229.27% of it.
Basis: calculation C03; calculation C04; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 118; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Leverage is moderate and has drifted up while cover weakened. Total debt is 4.81x adjusted EBITDA against 4.44x a year earlier, and 5.07x reported EBITDA, with debt up 5.29%. The October 2025 refinancing replaced the 2026 notes with higher-coupon 2031 notes, and the fixed-rate notes are 94.20% of principal, so the rate risk is the coupon already set rather than future rate moves.
Basis: calculation C02; calculation C08; calculation C13; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 142–146; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Liquidity is adequate but seasonal and facility-dependent. At year end liquidity was USD 195.1 million, USD 64.6 million lower than a year earlier, and availability equalled the revolver commitment less borrowings and letters of credit, so the commitment and not the borrowing base was binding at that date. A further USD 20.0 million was drawn on August 27, 2026, and the borrowing base falls with receivables and inventory in a weak winter.
Basis: calculation C06; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 143–144; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 147–148; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 64

Refinancing capacity rests on a narrow window. The revolver matures in October 2028 and the 2029 notes in April 2029, which together are 58.27% of principal, the rating agencies reversed their 2025 downgrades in October 2025, and market access was shown by the 2025 refinancing, but at a coupon 387.5 bp higher. The preferred distribution rate steps up toward its cap and the preferred holders gain a forced-sale path from March 2031, so each refinancing competes with a rising senior equity claim.
Basis: calculation C07; calculation C08; calculation C12; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 143–149; 10-K_2025-07-31_fgpr-20250731.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-01-31_fgpr-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 61

Governance and financial policy are a medium risk. Business & governance finds control with a general partner owned by an employee trust, no payouts to Class A holders in two years, cash-paid preferred units and a majority of independent directors, but also the removal of the six-times Class B payout rule in March 2026 and a final Class B payout that exceeded free cash flow.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C04

On the fiscal 2026 base, adjusted EBITDA less capital expenditures covers the notes' coupons plus the preferred cash cost 1.38x, falling to 1.31x at the preferred cap. That leaves a thin amount for the revolver, leases, taxes and working capital before any debt reduction, which is why a second year of weak volume matters more than the headline leverage.
Basis: calculation C05; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 74; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 149–150

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better view is resilience. Adjusted EBITDA fell less than revenue in a winter 11% warmer than the year before, no senior notes mature before April 2029, the issuer refinanced USD 650.0 million of 2026 notes in October 2025 and regained a B2 corporate rating, distributions to Class A holders and the general partner were nil, and the issuer states that cash flow, cash and the revolver are sufficient for the foreseeable future, which is management language and not independent evidence.
Basis: calculation C01; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 62–65; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 143–145; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 154; 10-Q_2026-01-31_fgpr-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 61; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The view would improve if a normal or cold winter restored cover toward the fiscal 2024 level of 3.56x, if the revolver were extended well before October 2028, and if free cash flow after preferred distributions were positive without a revolver draw. It would worsen if cover on the cash-paid basis, 2.54x, or on the issuer's net cash interest basis, 2.79x, fell below the 2.50x level of the credit agreement test, whose definition is not supplied, if summer revolver use rose from the USD 87.5 million of year end, or if the revolver were not extended ahead of October 2028 and the 2029 notes were not addressed in the months after.
Basis: calculation C02; calculation C06; calculation C11; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 64–65; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 143–145; 10-Q_2026-01-31_fgpr-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 61

### Limitations

- The credit agreement and indenture definitions and the issuer's own covenant ratios are not supplied, so the cover and leverage ratios here are proxies; if the defined ratios differ materially, the liquidity and refinancing assessments could be too mild or too severe.
- No peer set, market price or agency report was supplied, so the adequate business profile and weak financial profile are absolute judgements and not rankings against other leveraged propane or energy-distribution issuers.
- No management forecast or guidance for fiscal 2027 is supplied, so the outlook and the downside path rest on the fiscal 2026 pattern and on stated contractual dates, and a different winter could change both.
- The first-quarter fiscal 2026 report is not supplied, so the lowest liquidity point of the year is not measured and the claim that the revolver bridges the summer rests on the year-end and April balances.
- Margin resilience is shown on the issuer's adjusted EBITDA, which excludes large legal items and adds back legal costs of the core business; on reported EBITDA the fiscal 2025 margin collapses, so the margin finding is weaker if the add-backs are rejected.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strength, scale and customer stickiness: the second-largest US retail propane marketer runs a national network of distribution locations, and about 70% of residential customers use company-owned tanks that only the owning supplier may fill in some states. That scale spreads delivery and procurement cost and the tank ownership keeps volume sticky, which supports cash-flow visibility for debt service.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 15

Strength, margin resilience: adjusted EBITDA fell 2.84% on a 3.83% revenue fall and its margin held between 17.06% and 17.28% over three years, because cost of sales fell faster than revenue. This shows the cost base and pricing spread absorb a mild volume loss, which protects interest cover in a warm year.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C16; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 60–62

Strength, refinancing completed with ratings restored: the 2026 notes were redeemed in October 2025 and Moody's and S&P reversed their 2025 downgrades, lifting the corporate rating to B2. This removed the nearest senior maturity and gives about two years before the revolver falls due, which buys time to rebuild cover.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 143–145; 10-K_2025-07-31_fgpr-20250731.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-01-31_fgpr-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 61

Strength, a cash-paid preferred and no Class A payouts: preferred distributions are paid in cash, so the claim does not compound, and no cash went to Class A holders or the general partner in fiscal 2025 or 2026. This keeps equity leakage to contractual payouts only.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 149–150; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 154

Strength, modest maintenance spending: maintenance capital expenditure was 36.22% of the total, so some spending can be deferred in stress. This is a lever that is not confirmed by the filing, since the rest of the spending is not described as discretionary.
Basis: calculation C15; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 60

Weakness, weather exposure: 56% of propane volume sells in the five winter months and the fourth quarter gave only 7.39% of adjusted EBITDA, so one warm winter cuts the year's earnings while fixed delivery and interest costs continue. This is the first break in the downside path.
Basis: calculation C09; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 62

Weakness, thin free cash flow after fixed claims: free cash flow converted 23.45% of adjusted EBITDA in fiscal 2026 and preferred distributions took 87.24% of it. Little is left to repay debt, so leverage falls only if earnings grow.
Basis: calculation C03; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 118

Weakness, higher and rising fixed charges: the 2031 notes carry a coupon 387.5 bp above the 2026 notes they replaced, and the preferred distribution rate steps up by 216.9 bp to its cap. Cash interest has risen and adjusted cover has fallen from 3.56x to 2.54x, and the preferred steps add to the claims ahead of equity.
Basis: calculation C08; calculation C12; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 145–149

Weakness, concentrated maturities: the revolver and the 2029 notes together are USD 912.5 million, 58.27% of principal, due between October 2028 and April 2029. Refinancing both inside six months in a weak winter would be costly.
Basis: calculation C07; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 143–147

Weakness, payouts and control: the general partner controls the issuer, and the final Class B payout and preferred distributions exceeded free cash flow in fiscal 2026. The removal of the six-times payout rule leaves only contract limits on later Class A payouts.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C04

### Company description

Ferrellgas Partners is a holding partnership whose operating partnership sells propane and related equipment to residential, industrial/commercial, agricultural, portable tank exchange and wholesale customers in all 50 states, the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico. Retail sales were 64.64% of fiscal 2026 revenue and wholesale sales, mainly tank exchange and bulk sales to resellers, 28.78%. ### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Volume is the driver and weather decides it: 56% of propane volume sells in the five winter months, volumes fell 3.13% in fiscal 2026 and revenue fell 3.83%, and a wholesale propane price 9% lower also cut revenue per gallon. Price per gallon is a spread over delivered cost, so pass-through of cost changes is the pricing mechanism; contracted-price commitments are hedged with derivatives, and a sudden price rise that is not hedged would compress margin. Revenue is cyclical with weather, not discretionary, and not shown to be concentrated, though no concentration measure is disclosed.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C09; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 24–25; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 56; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 62

The credit link is that revenue is predictable in structure but not in amount: a repeat of the fiscal 2026 volume fall would cut revenue by a similar rate, and a colder winter would lift it. Switching costs from rented tanks make the base sticky, which limits loss of customers, but they do not protect volume per customer.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Labour and fuel are the cost risks: fuel cost rose in fiscal 2026 and the issuer warns of rising labour cost that may not be recovered in price.

### Porter's five forces

Competitive rivalry is moderate: most retail locations compete with three or more marketers on reliability, safety and price, and competition comes from large multi-state marketers and small local ones, so the second-largest scale position helps cost but does not remove price competition. The credit link is that rivalry limits the spread per gallon and any price recovery after cost inflation.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15

The threat of new entrants is low to moderate because a national tank exchange network and technology platform need scale, and a local entrant faces the cost of tank and truck assets, but local entry into a single market is possible. The threat of substitutes is moderate: propane competes with natural gas, electricity and fuel oil, supplies about 1% of US energy, and is displaced where natural gas pipelines extend, which erodes volume slowly rather than quickly. Buyer power is low for residential customers with rented tanks and higher for large tank exchange retailers, whose concentration is not disclosed, and supplier power is moderate because ten suppliers provided about 67% of purchases, though alternatives exist and the issuer states it does not expect a near-term severe impact.

The operating event is a winter as warm as fiscal 2026 or warmer, hitting the second and third quarters. Overall, Ferrellgas presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, tank-owning customers and resilient adjusted margins, but credit quality is constrained by thin free cash flow after cash interest and preferred distributions, a higher coupon and rising preferred rate, and maturities concentrated between October 2028 and April 2029. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on cover at the time of the 2028 revolver renewal, on the borrowing base and on the rating agencies' view after the October 2025 upgrades. The primary downside path is a warm winter that cuts volume while interest and preferred claims continue.
