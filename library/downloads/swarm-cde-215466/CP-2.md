Coeur Mining's credit profile is strong at current metal prices, with net cash and no near-term maturity pressure, but earnings depend on gold and silver prices while two new mines ramp up, so creditors face price and execution risk, not leverage risk.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a strong profile is that the cushion was built by price. Net cash and 63.84% conversion of adjusted EBITDA into free cash flow rest on realized gold of USD 4,140 per ounce in the nine months, while the FY2024 average was USD 2,156 and the issuer's own guidance assumes USD 4,000 per ounce. At the FY2025 average prices the nine-month adjusted EBITDA would fall 60.31% to USD 556,043 thousand, a margin of 26.73% against 47.89% before the price effect, and the small hedge book gives little protection. A second guidance cut, a lapse of the Las Chispas permit, or debt-funded distributions would move the view toward average; a full year of the Canadian mines at planned output with stable unit costs would confirm it.

### Company description

Coeur is a precious metals producer with seven wholly-owned mines in the United States, Canada and Mexico, and gold, silver and copper supplied 62.35%, 32.54% and 5.11% of the nine-month revenue of USD 2,925,400 thousand. Revenue comes from selling bullion and concentrate at market prices, so it has no contracted price and visibility depends on production and on prices. One mine, Kensington, sells its gold concentrate under a long-term offtake, which was about 18% of metal sales in 2025. The 2025 and 2026 acquisitions of SilverCrest and New Gold took the group from five mines to seven, which raised scale and diversification at the cost of integration risk.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is price times volume with no pricing power: the company sells at market prices, and the nine-month rise of 109.66% combined a 40.01% higher realized gold price, a 105.18% higher silver price and the added mines. Silver ounces sold fell 2.59% while silver revenue nearly doubled, so earnings are exposed to a silver price reversal as well as gold. Revenue is therefore cyclical with the metal price, concentrated in two metals and spread across seven mines.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cde.htm.jsonl, page 40; calculation A5

Two streaming agreements cap the price on part of the output: Palmarejo sells 50% of its gold to a Franco-Nevada subsidiary at the lesser of USD 800 or spot per ounce, and Rainy River delivers 6.5% of gold and 60% of silver to Royal Gold at 25% of the spot price. In a rally those ounces earn less than the realized price, and in a fall the Palmarejo ounces fall less, which damps the price sensitivity shown later.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cde.htm.jsonl, pages 34–36

### Cost structure and margin resilience

### Downside scenario

The primary downside is a return of realized prices to their FY2025 averages while the two new mines are still ramping up. The first break is revenue: on nine-month volumes the lower prices remove USD 421,221 thousand from gold and USD 423,853 thousand from silver, USD 845,074 thousand in total and 28.89% of revenue, which cuts adjusted EBITDA by 60.31% to USD 556,043 thousand and the margin to 26.73%. Unit costs would then be spread over lower revenue at Rainy River, where the gap between price and cost was USD 1,193 per ounce at the third-quarter price, and the cost would rise further if silver by-product credits shrink.
