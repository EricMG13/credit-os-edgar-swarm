## Findings

### Bottom line

Asbury's diversified franchise network, owned real estate and service-led gross profit give creditors a durable business, but softening vehicle margins and debt-funded expansion leave less room for error than the scale suggests.

Parts and service earned 47.9% of FY2025 gross profit on 13.9% of revenue, which cushions weaker new vehicle margins, and 83.0% of dealership locations were owned at the end of 2025. The Herb Chambers purchase of USD 1,761.8 million in July 2025 was funded entirely by floor plan, revolver and real estate borrowings, and since then nine-month income from operations has fallen while share repurchases reached USD 277.6 million. A controls weakness at the end of 2024 was remediated, but the acquired stores were outside the 2025 controls assessment.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_abg_abg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 55, 64, 116, 161–162; 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 12, 56; calculation C1; calculation C3

### Key findings

Franchise and brand breadth limits dependence on any one manufacturer or region, although one manufacturer group is still the largest single exposure. Toyota and Lexus supplied 30% of FY2025 new vehicle revenue and Ford and Lincoln 14%, and the new vehicle mix for the nine months to September 2026 was 41% imports, 33% luxury and 26% domestic. After divestitures the group runs 34 brands in 14 states, against 36 brands in 15 states at the end of 2025.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_abg_abg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10, 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 7

Profit depends on service and finance and insurance rather than vehicle sales, which stabilises earnings but does not remove the new vehicle squeeze. Parts and service earned 47.9% of FY2025 gross profit and finance and insurance 23.4%, while new vehicles earned 20.2% on 52.8% of revenue. In the nine months to September 2026 new vehicle gross profit fell 15%, a USD 68.4 million drop, while parts and service gross profit rose 2%.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_abg_abg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 64; 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 45–46; calculation C1; calculation C4

The July 2025 Herb Chambers purchase bought scale and Northeast presence with debt secured on real estate and inventory, and management has since sold stores for cash. Of the USD 1,761.8 million price, 33.6% came from floor plan facilities, 35.4% from the revolver and 31.0% from a USD 546.5 million real estate term loan; the stores added USD 1.80 billion of revenue and USD 43.3 million of net income in nine months 2026. Dealership sales raised USD 397.2 million in the same nine months at a gain of USD 126.4 million, which indicates active pruning of the portfolio rather than expansion alone.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 12–15; calculation C2

Owned real estate gives asset backing but puts secured lenders ahead of noteholders on the financed properties. At the end of 2025, 83.0% of dealership locations were owned; the real estate term loans carry first-priority liens on the properties they financed, while the senior notes are unsecured and guaranteed by every restricted subsidiary except the TCA entities.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_abg_abg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 55, 86, 134–139; calculation C3

Governance risk is medium, not low: there is no sponsor, no dividend since 2008 and no related-party distribution disclosed in the supplied filings, but buybacks have been large, manufacturers hold change-of-control rights and a new chief executive has signed the reports since mid-2026. The buyback authorization was raised to USD 500.0 million in February 2026 with USD 322.4 million remaining at September 30, 2026, and no repurchases were made in the third quarter.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_abg_abg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 58; 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9, 56, 66; 10-Q_2026-06-30_abg_abg-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 65; Source readiness (CP-0)

Reporting quality has improved but is not clean. The material weakness in controls over the dealer management system vendor of the Koons stores was remediated during 2025, yet management's assessment excluded the Herb Chambers stores, which held USD 1.89 billion of total assets, and 47 stores moved to a new dealer system in the third quarter of 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_abg_abg-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-K_2025-12-31_abg_abg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 42, 161–162; 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 63

Legal exposure has eased but is not closed. The FTC dismissed its administrative proceeding on October 5, 2026 and keeps the ability to refile, and the company's own lawsuit against the FTC is still pending; the filings give no estimate of loss for this matter.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_abg_abg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 56; 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a durable-business view is the 2026 earnings trend. Nine-month income from operations fell 16% to USD 620.0 million, gross profit per new vehicle in the third quarter fell 12% to USD 2,949, and selling, general and administrative expense reached 68.6% of gross profit against 64.0% a year earlier, so the cost base is not flexing as fast as profit. The company's leverage target is 2.5x to 3.5x on its own definition, and the funding of the Herb Chambers purchase and of buybacks with borrowings and sale proceeds leaves less slack if margins keep falling. The view would strengthen if selling, general and administrative expense returned toward the FY2025 share of 64.7% of gross profit, new vehicle gross profit per unit stabilised, the December 2026 real estate facility were refinanced on committed terms and buybacks stayed paused while leverage is above target. It would weaken if the FTC refiled, a manufacturer exercised change-of-control or termination rights, or buybacks resumed with debt.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 37, 54, 56; 10-K_2025-12-31_abg_abg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 72; calculation C4

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The defining transaction is the Herb Chambers acquisition, agreed on February 14, 2025 and completed on July 21, 2025 for a final purchase price of USD 1,761.8 million. It added 33 dealerships, 52 franchises and three collision centers and was funded by USD 292.0 million of new vehicle floor plan financing, USD 300.0 million of used vehicle financing, USD 623.3 million of revolver borrowings and a USD 546.5 million ten-year real estate term loan. The signing note had estimated USD 1.34 billion before vehicles, parts and reimbursements, so the final price is higher than the first estimate because vehicles, parts and reimbursements were priced at closing.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_abg_abg-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 165; 10-K_2025-12-31_abg_abg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 116–117; 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13

The only new debt in the period is that purchase financing: the April 9, 2025 amendment raised the revolver commitment from USD 500.0 million to USD 925.0 million and the new vehicle floor plan facility from USD 1.93 billion to USD 2.25 billion, and the July 21, 2025 real estate loan funded the stores and their property. No new senior notes were issued in the period, and the nearest large maturity is the 2021 real estate facility, scheduled for December 2026 with refinancing expected by management.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_abg_abg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 74–75, 134; 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 56

Divestitures run the other way: the company sold 24 franchises for USD 566.5 million in FY2025 and 19 franchises for USD 397.2 million in the nine months to September 2026, of which 14 franchises were sold in the first quarter of 2026 for USD 361.5 million at a gain of USD 125.8 million. One franchise and one real estate property were held for sale at September 30, 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_abg_abg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 73; 10-Q_2026-03-31_abg_abg-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 40; 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14–16, 55
