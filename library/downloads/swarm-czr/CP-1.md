## Findings

### Bottom line

Caesars is more leveraged than its balance-sheet debt suggests: debt is 3.60x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA, but adding the VICI and GLPI financing obligations lifts that to 7.70x, and EBITDA covers all cash interest, including that rent, only 1.52x.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_czr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 15–16; calculation C65; calculation C79; calculation C67

Near-term liquidity is adequate, with cash of USD 864 million and net revolver availability of USD 2,083 million, and only USD 142 million of principal falls due before 2028, but USD 5,536 million matures in 2029 and 2030. Free cash flow after capital expenditure was USD 582 million over the last twelve months against USD 11,558 million of debt, and how the pending merger affects that debt is not established.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-27_ex991-2026q3ceiearningsrel.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_czr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6–9, 16; calculation C94; calculation C95; calculation C103

### Credit analysis by dimension

Balance-sheet leverage rose in 2025 because earnings fell faster than debt, and it has been flat since. Carrying-value debt fell from USD 12,142 million at the end of 2024 to USD 11,784 million a year later, but reported EBITDA fell from USD 3,628 million to USD 3,275 million, so debt over EBITDA rose from 3.35x to 3.60x and net debt over EBITDA from 3.11x to 3.33x. Reported EBITDA for 2024 includes a net credit of USD 331 million in transaction and other costs, which the issuer attributes in part to gains on the sale of the WSOP trademark and the LINQ Promenade, so the issuer-adjusted measure, which fell from USD 3,739 million to USD 3,624 million, is the better guide to underlying earnings. A reduction of USD 584 million in carrying-value debt since the end of 2024 has been offset by last-twelve-months EBITDA falling further to USD 3,213 million, leaving net leverage at 3.33x.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_czr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 99–100; 10-Q_2026-09-30_czr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; calculation C42; calculation C48; calculation C127

The debt line understates the fixed claims on the business. Long-term financing obligations to VICI and GLPI of USD 13,188 million sit on their own balance sheet line and are not in Total Debt, yet their payments are treated as interest, so reported EBITDA is struck before about USD 1,370 million of rent over the last twelve months. Adding the obligations to debt gives 7.70x, against 3.60x on the balance-sheet definition, which is the better measure of the fixed claims that EBITDA has to meet. Secured principal of USD 9,320 million is 2.90x last-twelve-months EBITDA and ranks ahead of USD 2,300 million of senior unsecured notes.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_czr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 5, 15–16; 10-K_2025-12-31_czr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 117–118; calculation C79; calculation C129; calculation C124; calculation C125; calculation C126

Cash interest cover has been flat at about 1.52x because falling debt interest has been matched by falling EBITDA while rent interest rises. Cash interest on debt alone fell to USD 741 million over the last twelve months from USD 1,052 million in 2024 as debt was repaid and floating rates fell, but cash interest on the lease-style obligations kept rising to USD 1,370 million. All cash interest absorbs 65.70% of last-twelve-months EBITDA, which leaves little room for a decline in earnings before free cash flow turns negative. On cash interest for debt alone, cover would be 4.34x, which is the right figure only if the landlords can be ignored.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_czr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-12-31_czr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 101; calculation C67; calculation C80; calculation C128

Cash-flow quality is acceptable but leans on lower capital spending. Operating cash flow was USD 942 million for the first nine months of 2026 against USD 998 million a year earlier, while purchases of property and equipment fell by USD 141 million, which lifted free cash flow after capital expenditure to USD 435 million from USD 350 million. Issuer-adjusted EBITDA for the nine months fell by USD 51 million, so the whole improvement in free cash flow comes from lower capital spending, not from earnings or operating cash flow, and the last-twelve-months free cash flow of USD 582 million is 5.04% of debt. Distributions to noncontrolling owners of USD 65 million and financing obligation payments of USD 29 million are further uses after that free cash flow.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_czr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; calculation C133; calculation C134; calculation C102; calculation C103; calculation C131

Liquidity is adequate for the next year and a half, but the maturity profile concentrates after that. Cash of USD 864 million plus net revolver availability of USD 2,083 million gives USD 2,947 million, against only USD 142 million of principal due from October 2026 to the end of 2027. The CEI Revolving Credit Facility and Term Loan A mature on January 31, 2028, which leaves the revolver, the source of that availability, with less than twelve months to run from the end of January 2027, and USD 5,536 million, or 47.48% of principal, matures in 2029 and 2030. The Caesars Virginia revolver of USD 25 million is counted in the issuer's liquidity but serves that joint venture only.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-27_ex991-2026q3ceiearningsrel.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_czr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 15–19; calculation C88; calculation C94; calculation C95; calculation C96

Non-debt funding float is small and shrinking. Customer deposits and other deferred revenue were USD 449 million at the end of September 2026, down USD 100 million from USD 549 million at the end of 2024; the annual report attributes the 2025 decline mainly to lower gaming deposits and states no cause for the 2026 decline, which is a modest drag on operating cash flow, not a funding stress, and the outstanding chip liability and Caesars Rewards obligations are each below USD 100 million.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_czr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 132–133; 10-Q_2026-09-30_czr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22; calculation C130

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is that debt interest and debt are falling together. Carrying-value debt is down USD 584 million since the end of 2024, cash interest on debt has fallen by nearly 30%, the covenant tests the issuer reports for the CEI Revolving Credit Facility and Term Loan A (net total leverage 6.50:1 and fixed charge coverage 2.0:1) are stated as met at September 30, 2026, and cash interest for debt alone is covered 4.34x. That view would win if the landlords' escalators stayed near the current pace while EBITDA grew; it loses if the nine-month fall in issuer-adjusted EBITDA continues, because the rent leg does not fall with earnings. Covenant headroom is not calculated here because the covenant definitions are not supplied.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_czr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 52–53; calculation C127; calculation C80

Subsequent events are flagged and kept out of period figures. The merger agreement of May 27, 2026 and the stockholder approval of September 22, 2026 describe a cash merger at USD 31.00 a share that has not closed; the filings state no financing for it and no treatment of the existing notes and loans, so pro forma net debt and leverage are not established and the post-balance-sheet table records no event. The acquisitions of Caesars Windsor on March 3, 2026 and the remaining interest in Grand Bazaar on May 1, 2026 are inside the 2026 columns, with USD 98 million of asset acquisition payments in the cash flow, and the segment perimeter changed on the first date.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_czr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6–10; EX-99.1_2026-04-28_ex991-2026q1ceiearningsrel.htm.jsonl, page 2

Data-quality and definition risks are limited and logged. Every statement foots to its printed totals, the cash flow ties to the balance sheet cash and restricted cash, and each fiscal-year ratio ties to its statement lines. Four definitions need care when figures are quoted: debt on principal against carrying value, the issuer's net debt against net debt on carrying value, reported against issuer-adjusted EBITDA (a difference of USD 349 million for 2025), and the lease-style obligations outside debt. The fourth quarter of 2025 is not reported alone and is shown as the full year less nine months. Dashes in the filings are recorded as not supplied rather than zero.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_czr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 99–102; calculation C132; calculation C92; calculation C93

## Gaps

- The issuer states no unadjusted EBITDA, so reported EBITDA is calculated as operating income plus depreciation and amortization for every income statement column; it includes impairment charges, transaction costs and stock-based compensation, and it differs from the issuer's Adjusted EBITDA by USD 349 million for 2025, which restricts comparison with the issuer's own leverage language and is a medium-severity gap closed by stating the basis beside every ratio.
- Long-term financing obligations of USD 13,188 million to VICI and GLPI are outside Total Debt, and debt over EBITDA is 3.60x without them and 7.70x with them, so any leverage quote that omits the basis misstates the claim on EBITDA; this is a high-severity definition gap closed by always showing both rows.
- Debt appears at principal, at the debt note's book value and at balance-sheet carrying value, and the issuer's net debt is on principal, so the same debt can be quoted at three figures; the differences tie to printed discounts and deferred finance charges and are logged as a medium-severity definition conflict, closed by using carrying value for ratios and naming the basis.
- The quarterly reports carry no leases note, so lease and financing obligation maturities are known only at December 31, 2025 and the later balance sheet lines and cash interest are the only evidence for 2026; this limits any forecast of rent and is a medium-severity gap closed only by a later annual report.
- The fourth quarter of 2025 is not reported alone, so revenue, reported EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA for that quarter are the full year less nine months and carry the full-year impairment of USD 182 million; this is a low-severity gap closed by the fourth-quarter release.
- Cash interest paid for other financing obligations is printed only from the second quarter of 2026 and for some 2025 comparatives, so it is kept outside cash interest paid for comparability; the amount is USD 7 million for the nine months of 2026, which makes this a low-severity gap.
