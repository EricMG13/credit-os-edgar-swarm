## Findings

### Bottom line

Cars.com is a moderately leveraged, cash-generative issuer whose creditors are well covered until the USD 400.0 million notes fall due in November 2028, so the risk is a reversal of the 2026 cost-led earnings gain while management returns most free cash flow to shareholders.
Basis: calculation LV1; calculation NT1; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Carrying-value debt is 2.73x last-twelve-months EBITDA, interest is covered 5.67x and free cash flow is 79.34% of EBITDA, but revenue is flat, visitors and OEM media are shrinking and repurchases have been 10.64x the debt repaid since the start of 2025. If EBITDA fell back to its FY2025 level, leverage would be 2.91x with the notes still to be refinanced.
Basis: calculation LV1; calculation CV1; calculation FC2; calculation BB2; calculation DS2; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Key findings

Liquidity and the maturity profile are the strongest support. Nothing falls due before November 2028, USD 305.0 million of the USD 350.0 million revolving loan is undrawn, and cash plus that availability equals 0.84x carrying-value debt, so near-term liquidity does not depend on the capital markets.
Basis: calculation LQ1; calculation LQ2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 25

Leverage is moderate and improving, but the improvement is earnings-led and rests on a calculated EBITDA. Carrying-value debt fell from 2.97x EBITDA at FY2025 to 2.73x, net leverage is 2.33x and interest cover is 5.67x; 72.76% of the nine-month EBITDA gain came from lower cash operating costs while revenue grew only 0.50%, so the gain is easier to reverse than a revenue-led one.
Basis: calculation LV1; calculation LV2; calculation LV3; calculation CV1; calculation EG2; calculation RV1; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Cash conversion is high and capital needs are small, which is what supports debt service. Free cash flow was 79.34% of EBITDA and capital expenditure only 3.20% of revenue over the last twelve months, and free cash flow has been steady across the three periods shown; the 11% workforce reduction shows cost flexibility but also that a major cost lever has been used.
Basis: calculation FC2; calculation FC3; calculation CX1; calculation CO1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Shareholder returns, not creditors, set the pace of deleveraging. Repurchases of USD 159.6 million since the start of 2025 are 10.64x the USD 15.0 million of debt repaid and took 76.39% of free cash flow in the first nine months of 2026; USD 100.2 million of the programme remains and it may be suspended at any time, so the policy is reversible, and while the debt agreements restrict distributions in general terms, their limits are not seen and nothing disclosed holds it back.
Basis: calculation BB1; calculation DP1; calculation BB2; calculation BP1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 26; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Revenue is recurring but short-dated, and its growth engine is weakening. About 80% of revenue is subscription-based and no customer exceeds 10% of revenue, yet packages are generally renewed month to month, so visibility rests on dealer behaviour and not on contracts; visitors fell 10% and traffic 7% in nine months, dealer customers slipped 1% and OEM and National revenue fell 11.18%, while a 3% rise in revenue per dealer lifted Dealer revenue 2.11%.
Basis: calculation OE1; calculation DL1; 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 58, 91; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 19, 21

The single large maturity, not near-term liquidity, is the event risk. The USD 400.0 million notes are 89.89% of principal and equal 3.11x last-twelve-months free cash flow, and the fair value of debt is 96.02% of principal against 99.80% at the end of 2025, a discount that may reflect higher market yields or more perceived risk than the leverage ratios imply; the notes' own price and yield are not stated.
Basis: calculation NT1; calculation NT2; calculation FV1; calculation FV2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 9

Reported covenant compliance is favourable but cannot be verified here. The issuer reports interest coverage of 7.33x against a floor of 3.0x and a senior secured net leverage ratio that is negative against a ceiling of 3.5x, and its Total Net Leverage Ratio of 1.77x is 0.56x below the 2.33x calculated here; the definitions sit in the credit agreement, which is not supplied, so headroom is not calculated.
Basis: calculation CV3; calculation LV6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10

Governance adds execution risk but no sponsor risk. No sponsor or controlling holder is named and the chairman is separate from the chief executive, while the chief executive who signed the 2025 annual report differs from the 2024 signatory and an 11% workforce reduction cost USD 8.5 million in March 2026, so continuity of buyback and leverage policy is not established.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious reading is that earnings, cash flow and the balance sheet have moved the right way together: leverage fell from 2.97x to 2.73x, free cash flow stayed steady and USD 305.0 million of the revolving loan is undrawn, while management attributes the audience decline to a deliberate shift towards high-intent shoppers and revenue per dealer rose 3%. If that account holds, the 2026 cost savings are a lasting margin gain and the 2028 notes can be refinanced from a lower leverage base. The strongest evidence against a comfortable reading is that debt is valued at 96.02% of principal against 99.80% at the end of 2025, with flat revenue and month-to-month dealer subscriptions behind it. The view would improve if dealer customers and revenue per dealer rose together for two quarters while repurchases slowed in favour of repayment or the notes were refinanced early, and it would worsen if EBITDA fell back to its FY2025 level, which would take leverage to 2.91x, if repurchases were funded from the revolving loan or if dealer customers fell by more than the 1% seen now.
Basis: calculation LV1; calculation LV3; calculation DS2; calculation FV1; calculation FV2; 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 58; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19–21

### Company description and ownership

Cars.com runs an online automotive marketplace with dealer websites, trade and appraisal tools and media products. About 80% of revenue is subscription-based and Dealer revenue is 90.36% of the nine-month 2026 total, so recurring dealer billing supports cash-flow visibility and debt service, while the OEM and National media line and the audience that feeds every product are the exposed parts.
Basis: calculation MX1; 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 4–8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 2

Cars.com is a listed company with no sponsor or controlling holder named, a chairman separate from the chief executive and charter provisions that can discourage a takeover. Creditor alignment therefore depends on board policy and not on an owner's willingness to support the company, and no such support is assumed. The notes are titled senior unsecured and the annual report states that the guarantors granted liens on substantially all of their assets for the debt agreements, so legal ranking is not verified here.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 83

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is predictable in pattern but no longer grows on volume. Nine-month 2026 revenue grew 0.50%, Dealer revenue grew 2.11% on a 3% rise in revenue per dealer from higher-tier marketplace packages, dealer customers fell 1% and OEM and National revenue fell 11.18%. Packages are generally renewed month to month, so a dealer that sees fewer leads can leave quickly, and Dealer revenue, 90.36% of the total, would show that in EBITDA first. Management attributes the 10% fall in unique visitors to a deliberate shift towards high-intent shoppers; the filings report no total lead volume or retention rate, so the claim is not tested.
Basis: calculation RV1; calculation DL1; calculation OE1; calculation MX1; 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 58; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 19–22

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Costs are mainly people, marketing and technology, and management has shown it can cut them: cash operating costs fell 1.70% in nine months 2026, which delivered 72.76% of the EBITDA gain, after an 11% workforce reduction that cost USD 8.5 million in March 2026. EBITDA margin was 22.34% in FY2024, 21.03% in FY2025 and 22.32% over the last twelve months, so margin has recovered to its FY2024 level on flat revenue. Margins can be defended under moderate volume pressure through further cost action, but a major lever has been used and a revenue decline would meet a smaller cushion; the filings do not split fixed from variable costs.
Basis: calculation CO1; calculation EG2; calculation MG1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3, 19

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital needs are small: capital expenditure is 3.20% of revenue and the filings do not split maintenance from growth spending. Free cash flow was USD 128.143 million in FY2024, USD 125.734 million in FY2025 and USD 128.547 million over the last twelve months, 79.34% of EBITDA and 2.24% above FY2025, after cash interest of USD 28.553 million. Cash is seasonal inside the year: it fell to USD 33.287 million at 30 June 2026 after bonus payments, the May coupon and repurchases, before recovering to USD 64.553 million at 30 September 2026.
Basis: calculation FC1; calculation FC2; calculation FC3; calculation CX1; 10-Q_2026-06-30_cars_cars-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 4–5; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is high and the contest is for audience: the direct competitors named are CarGurus, AutoTrader and TrueCar, dealer software providers such as Cox Automotive compete for dealer budgets, and search engines, social platforms and generative AI chat engines compete for the same shoppers. Substitutes matter most through AI answer engines that can give vehicle information without sending shoppers to Cars.com, which would cut organic traffic and the value of a dealer subscription. Dealer buyer power is limited because no customer exceeds 10% of revenue, but OEM and National customers are few and can change media strategy independent of the product, which explains the volatility of that line. Search engines and app stores hold the most supplier power because most traffic arrives organically and ranking changes can lower visits.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9, 17, 19, 24, 91

### Macro and technology factors

Technology is the factor that changes the credit view: AI answer engines and search changes could lower traffic and lead volumes, and the issuer lists them among its risks. Economic factors act through vehicle sales volumes, affordability, tariffs and interest rates, which the issuer says influence OEM and dealer marketing spend; the floating-rate revolving loan is 10.11% of principal, so direct interest-rate exposure is small. Regulatory and political factors are not presented as a primary credit driver in the supplied filings.
Basis: calculation FL1; 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 19, 41; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21

### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale and market position are average: the issuer describes industry-leading brand recognition, but revenue of USD 725.943 million over the last twelve months cannot be ranked because no market share or peer data are supplied. Competitive advantage is average: brand and audience scale support pricing, but traffic is falling and search and AI platforms can intermediate. Business diversification is average: dealers are many and none exceeds 10% of revenue, but Dealer revenue is 90.36% of the total. Cost and capex flexibility is strong: capital expenditure is 3.20% of revenue and cash costs fell 1.70% in nine months. Margin stability is average: margin moved 1.31 percentage points between FY2024 and FY2025 and recovered through cost cuts. Free cash flow stability is strong: free cash flow stayed between USD 125.734 million and USD 128.547 million across three periods. Ability to refinance is average: the notes are 89.89% of principal and the fair value of debt is 96.02% of principal. Liquidity is strong: it equals 0.84x carrying-value debt with no maturity before November 2028. Financial policy and governance are weak from a creditor's view: repurchases are 10.64x debt repaid and the debt agreements are not seen. Credit supports are liquidity, cash conversion and recurring dealer revenue; constraints are the audience, the bullet maturity and shareholder-first policy; the trend in leverage is down; the missing data are the debt documents, peer data and any management projection.
Basis: calculation LQ2; calculation FC1; calculation CX1; calculation MG1; calculation NT1; calculation FV1; calculation BB2; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next twelve to 24 months the supports are the remaining annualisation of the March 2026 cost reduction, price and mix gains from higher-tier packages, a fixed coupon of USD 25.500 million on the notes and no maturity; the pressures are the audience decline, the 1% fall in dealer customers, OEM media weakness and the DealerClub earnout of up to USD 88.0 million, which may be paid in cash or in stock if mutually agreed through 2028 and has not been expensed to date. Over three to five years the central event is the November 2028 refinancing of USD 400.0 million, priced on the leverage and growth then shown, with the shift of search towards AI as the structural headwind to audience and dealer value. The sector factors the issuer names are vehicle sales volumes, affordability, tariffs, interest rates and electric vehicle adoption.
Basis: calculation CP1; calculation NT1; 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19–21, 26

### Downside scenario

The most plausible downside is audience-led. Search and AI platforms divert organic traffic and unique visitors keep falling beyond the 10% seen in nine months 2026; the first operational break is at the dealer, where higher-tier package adoption stalls, dealer customers fall faster than the 1% now seen and Dealer revenue, 90.36% of the total, stops growing while OEM and National revenue keeps falling. With the March 2026 cost reduction already taken, little cost cushion is left, so EBITDA drifts back towards its FY2025 level, 6.13% below the last twelve months. At that level carrying-value debt is 2.91x EBITDA, net debt 2.48x and cash interest cover 5.33x, so the transmission is moderate leverage rising from 2.73x and not a covenant event. Liquidity holds: free cash flow at the FY2025 level of USD 125.734 million still funds interest and capital expenditure, and USD 305.0 million of the revolving loan is undrawn. The pressure arrives at refinancing: even if all free cash flow and cash were kept for the 25 months to October 2028, USD 67.641 million of the USD 400.0 million notes would still need other funding, and any repurchases add to that gap. Management's levers are to suspend the repurchase programme, cut costs again, settle the DealerClub earnout in stock where agreed or draw the revolving loan. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress; whether the notes can be refinanced on acceptable terms against a weaker audience is the open question, and the signals to watch are in the monitoring triggers.
Basis: calculation DS1; calculation DS2; calculation DS3; calculation DS4; calculation RP1; calculation FC1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10, 26

### Overall credit view

Overall, Cars.com presents an adequate business profile supported by recurring dealer subscriptions, low customer concentration and small capital needs, but credit quality is constrained by a shrinking audience, a cost-led earnings recovery and a shareholder-first financial policy. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow has been steady across three periods and converts 79.34% of EBITDA. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on EBITDA holding near its current level, the revolving loan staying available and repurchases slowing before the November 2028 maturity. The primary downside path is audience erosion that reaches Dealer revenue. A more definitive opinion would require the credit agreement and indenture, the proxy statement, the issuer's reconciliation of EBITDA, and peer and market-price data.
Basis: calculation FC2; calculation LV1; calculation NT1; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths are ranked from the most to the least important for creditors.

The first strength is liquidity with no near-term maturity: nothing falls due before November 2028 and USD 305.0 million of the revolving loan is undrawn, so interest, capital expenditure and repurchases are funded from operations and a backstop exists if the capital markets are shut. That lowers near-term default probability and gives time to refinance.
Basis: calculation LQ1; calculation LQ2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 25

The second is cash conversion: free cash flow is 79.34% of EBITDA on capital expenditure of 3.20% of revenue, so debt service does not rely on asset sales and the cash is there to repay or refinance the notes.
Basis: calculation FC2; calculation CX1; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The third is recurring dealer revenue with no customer above 10% of revenue, which spreads the loss from any one customer and supports predictable cash receipts, subject to the month-to-month term of the packages.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 58, 91

The fourth is moderate, falling leverage with wide cover: 2.73x carrying-value debt to EBITDA and 5.67x interest cover leave room for a decline in EBITDA before debt service is strained.
Basis: calculation LV1; calculation CV1; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Weaknesses are ranked from the most to the least important for creditors.

The first weakness is a shareholder-first policy: repurchases are 10.64x the debt repaid and 76.39% of nine-month free cash flow, so cash that could cut debt leaves the company and the 2028 notes are left to refinance.
Basis: calculation BB2; calculation BP1; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The second is audience and OEM media erosion: visitors are down 10%, dealer customers 1% and OEM and National revenue 11.18%, which weakens the value offered to dealers and advertisers and, if it reaches Dealer revenue, EBITDA and cover.
Basis: calculation OE1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 19

The third is a single bullet maturity: the USD 400.0 million notes are 89.89% of principal and 3.11x annual free cash flow, so they must be refinanced or funded from the revolving loan, and the fair value of debt at 96.02% of principal may reflect market pricing of that risk or higher yields.
Basis: calculation NT1; calculation NT2; calculation FV1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 9

The fourth is a cost-led earnings gain: 72.76% of the nine-month EBITDA gain came from lower cash costs, so a return to the FY2025 EBITDA level would take leverage to 2.91x.
Basis: calculation EG2; calculation DS2

The fifth is untested debt terms and leadership change: the covenant ratios are reported as met but the agreements are not seen, and the chief executive signatory changed between the 2024 and 2025 annual reports.
Basis: calculation CV3; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10

Opportunities that could improve credit quality are repaying the revolving loan from retained free cash flow if repurchases slow, an early refinancing of the notes while leverage is at its lowest in the periods shown, and DealerClub growth that earns the earnout from its own cash flow. Threats that could weaken it are AI-led loss of search traffic, the loss of one of the few OEM media buyers and a cash settlement of the DealerClub earnout alongside continued repurchases.
Basis: calculation LV1; 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 19, 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26
