## Findings

### Bottom line

Emergent presents a weak credit profile: earnings are deteriorating and trailing reported EBITDA is negative, so creditors rely on USD 225.7 million of liquidity and government orders to reach the USD 364.7 million notes due in 2028, and refinancing is the decisive risk.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C03; calculation C08; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Cash generation is better than earnings, with USD 200.1 million of trailing operating cash flow, but it was helped by a USD 62.6 million inventory release in nine months 2026. Net debt of USD 332.0 million is 3.92x trailing EBITDA before the impairment, an analyst measure, and the issuer's reported fair value puts the 2028 notes at 91.47% of principal, which shows stress rather than distress.
Basis: calculation C02; calculation C03; calculation C04; calculation C09; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ebs.htm.jsonl, page 18

### Key findings

- The earnings base is the weakest dimension and is still deteriorating. Reported trailing EBITDA is USD -106.6 million after the USD 191.3 million NARCAN impairment, the issuer's own Adjusted EBITDA fell to USD 127.5 million for nine months 2026 from USD 204.8 million and was USD -4.6 million in Q3 2026, and the November forecast implies Q4 2026 Adjusted EBITDA between USD -17.5 million and USD 12.5 million. Leverage multiples are therefore not meaningful on reported numbers, and cover turns positive only after the impairment is excluded.
Basis: calculation C03; calculation C06; Financial foundation (CP-1); EX99.1_2026-09-30_ebs_ebs202609-30ex99er-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 1–9
- Cash flow is a real support but is flattered by working capital. Trailing operating cash flow is USD 200.1 million and free cash flow USD 188.4 million, with trailing cash interest of USD 37.6 million, but the net inventory release over the period was USD 76.8 million; without it free cash flow is USD 111.6 million, still 2.97x cash interest, which is adequate cover only while capital spending stays near USD 11.7 million a year.
Basis: calculation C04; calculation C05; Financial foundation (CP-1)
- Liquidity is adequate for the next twelve months but thinner than a year ago. Cash of USD 175.7 million and the undrawn USD 50.0 million revolver give USD 225.7 million, down 26.10% from USD 305.4 million at 2025-12-31 because the revolver commitment was halved and cash was spent on notes, a milestone payment and refinancing costs; the issuer states its liquidity is adequate for at least twelve months, and no principal falls due before 2028 on stated maturities.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C07; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ebs.htm.jsonl, page 55
- Refinancing is the decisive risk and it is concentrated in 2028. The USD 364.7 million notes become current on 2027-08-15 and fall due on 2028-08-15, the revolver springs to 2028-05-17 while they remain outstanding, and the term loan could spring forward if more than USD 75.0 million of notes remain and a liquidity test in the agreement is unmet, which would put all USD 514.3 million of principal in 2028; liquidity covers 61.89% of the notes and 43.88% of all principal today.
Basis: calculation C08; calculation C09; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ebs.htm.jsonl, pages 19–23; Financial foundation (CP-1)
- The business is concentrated in one customer and one declining franchise. The U.S. government supplied 55.36% of nine-month 2026 revenue, naloxone has fallen to 27.74% of revenue after a 43.32% decline in FY2025, and ACAM2000 option exercises fell from USD 120.0 million in 2023 to USD 56.0 million in 2025, so revenue depends on option timing that management does not control.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_ebs.htm.jsonl, page 14
- Financial policy helps creditors on debt but gives some of it back. Principal fell USD 185.7 million since 2024-12-31 and the term loan margin is 200 basis points lower, worth about USD 3.0 million a year on USD 150.0 million, yet share repurchases continued and the issuer says the new terms are less restrictive with more incremental debt capacity; covenant compliance is asserted but headroom cannot be calculated from the documents supplied.
Basis: calculation C10; Business & governance (CP-1A); EX99.1_2026-03-31_ebs_a04162026refinancingpr-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ebs.htm.jsonl, pages 20–24

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a weak view is cash and the government order book. Trailing operating cash flow of USD 200.1 million is 5.32x cash interest, remaining performance obligations are USD 241.9 million, principal debt is USD 185.7 million lower than at 2024-12-31, the August 2026 plan targets about USD 40.0 million of annualized savings, and the November forecast raises adjusted gross margin to 45% to 47% from 42% to 44% even as revenue is cut.
Basis: calculation C04; calculation C10; Business & governance (CP-1A); EX99.1_2026-09-30_ebs_ebs202609-30ex99er-htm.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ebs.htm.jsonl, page 28

The view would improve if Q4 2026 results land near the top of the implied range, with revenue near USD 144.6 million and Adjusted EBITDA near USD 12.5 million, if naloxone revenue stops falling, or if note repurchases bring the outstanding notes below USD 75.0 million, which would remove the term loan's springing clause. It would worsen if Q4 revenue falls toward USD 99.6 million, if operating cash flow turns negative once the inventory release ends, or if the notes are still above USD 75.0 million as the springing dates approach without a refinancing plan.
Basis: calculation C06; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ebs.htm.jsonl, pages 21–23

### Strengths and weaknesses

The first strength is cash generation that exceeds earnings. Trailing operating cash flow of USD 200.1 million against capital spending of USD 11.7 million means debt service does not depend on EBITDA that is depressed by a non-cash impairment, which supports the next twelve months of liquidity and the credit's ability to keep repurchasing notes.
Basis: calculation C04; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The second strength is a government order book and a lower debt load. Remaining performance obligations of USD 241.9 million and principal debt USD 185.7 million lower than at 2024-12-31 reduce near-term refinancing need and give some visibility, although option timing remains management's weakest control.
Basis: calculation C10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ebs.htm.jsonl, page 28; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The first weakness is the earnings collapse in the commercial franchise. Naloxone revenue is down 43.32% in FY2025 and 26.05% in nine months 2026 on generic competition and price and volume mix, which produced the USD 191.3 million impairment and turns trailing reported EBITDA negative, so creditors cannot lean on leverage multiples.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1)

The second weakness is the 2028 maturity cluster. The USD 364.7 million notes, the revolver's springing date and the term loan's conditional springing clause converge in 2028 while liquidity covers 61.89% of the notes, so any further earnings slippage turns a manageable wall into a market-access problem.
Basis: calculation C08; calculation C09; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ebs.htm.jsonl, pages 19–23

The third weakness is concentration and loosened protection. Government customers are 55.36% of nine-month revenue, buyback authority of USD 37.3 million remains, and the issuer describes less restrictive covenants and more incremental debt capacity, which weakens the position of noteholders behind secured lenders.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ebs.htm.jsonl, page 26

### Company description

Emergent supplies government-procured medical countermeasures (anthrax, smallpox, botulism and Ebola products), naloxone nasal sprays sold over the counter and to public and first-responder buyers, and a shrinking contract manufacturing service. Revenue visibility is mixed: the countermeasure lines run on multi-year contracts with annual options, whereas naloxone competes in open markets. This supports cash-flow visibility for the government lines and weakens it for naloxone, so debt service capacity depends on option timing and on holding the naloxone decline.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_ebs.htm.jsonl, pages 7–9

### Ownership and group structure

Emergent is a listed company with no sponsor and no identified controlling holder, so creditors face no sponsor extraction but also no sponsor equity backstop; the ownership detail sits in a proxy statement that was not supplied. The only lender link to the equity is the warrants issued with the 2024 term loan. Debt sits at the registrant with unnamed subsidiary guarantors, so structural priority cannot be assessed here.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ebs.htm.jsonl, pages 21–25

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is driven by government option exercises and by naloxone price and volume. The government is a monopsony buyer with unilateral termination rights, and its ACAM2000 option exercises fell from USD 120.0 million in 2023 to USD 99.9 million in 2024 and USD 56.0 million in 2025, which shows declining order value rather than pricing power. Naloxone has no pricing power against generic and branded entrants, so it is cyclical in neither direction but structurally declining in price.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ebs.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15; 10-K_2025-12-31_ebs.htm.jsonl, page 156; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Manufacturing cost is largely fixed, so lower volumes cut margin directly: nine-month 2026 gross margin was 39% against 48% and the third quarter was 14% against 54%. The August 2026 plan and asset sales lower fixed cost, but plant shutdown costs and absorption losses show margins are not defended under volume pressure, so cost flexibility is limited in the near term.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ebs.htm.jsonl, pages 44–46; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ebs.htm.jsonl, page 52

### Capital intensity and FCF conversion

Capital intensity is low, with capital spending of USD 13.8 million in FY2025, and trailing free cash flow of USD 188.4 million exceeded cash interest of USD 37.6 million several times over, but restructuring cash, milestone payments and an inventory release make the conversion lumpy. EBITDA does not convert to cash predictably, which supports debt service in the next twelve months more than it supports deleveraging.
Basis: calculation C04; calculation C05; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Porter's five forces

Rivalry and entry are the forces that matter. Generic and branded naloxone entrants (Teva, Padagis, Amneal and prescription competitors) cut price and volume, and the government vaccine and antiviral lines face substitutes such as JYNNEOS and TPOXX, so competitive pressure is the main driver of the naloxone decline. Buyer power sits with the U.S. government, which holds unilateral termination rights, and supplier power is high because several products depend on sole-source third-party manufacturers.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ebs.htm.jsonl, pages 24–26; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ebs.htm.jsonl, pages 62–64

### PEST analysis

Political and regulatory factors are material and the other categories are not. The U.S. government may prefer U.S.-made products and reduce purchases of items made in the Winnipeg facility, and government funding appropriations drive option timing, so policy changes feed directly into revenue; interest-rate exposure is small, with a one percentage point rise in the reference rate adding about USD 1.5 million of annual interest.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ebs.htm.jsonl, pages 59–60; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ebs.htm.jsonl, page 63

### Credit-translated SWOT

Strengths are the licensed government products with few competitors, positive operating cash flow and falling debt. Weaknesses are the negative trailing EBITDA, the naloxone decline, customer concentration and the 2028 cluster. Opportunities that could lift credit quality are the restructuring savings, Canadian and international orders and note repurchases below the springing threshold; threats are further generic erosion, deferred government options and a refinancing window that closes.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale and diversification are average to weak, with eleven products but two revenue engines and one dominant customer. Margin stability and free cash flow stability are weak and average respectively, cost and capex flexibility is average, liquidity is average, refinancing access is weak until the 2028 cluster is addressed, and financial policy and governance is average because debt reduction is offset by buybacks and looser terms. Trend is down on earnings and flat on liquidity, and the key missing data are the financing agreements and trailing issuer Adjusted EBITDA.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over twelve to twenty-four months the forecast points to a weak Q4 2026, with implied Q4 revenue of USD 99.6 million to USD 144.6 million and Adjusted EBITDA of USD -17.5 million to USD 12.5 million, then a year in which restructuring savings and government orders must offset naloxone erosion. Tailwinds are preparedness orders, the Canadian standing offer for NARCAN and lower interest cost; headwinds are generic competition, option timing and the 2027 current-maturity date for the notes. Over three to five years the 2028 and 2031 maturities frame the refinancing question.
Basis: calculation C06; EX99.1_2026-09-30_ebs_ebs202609-30ex99er-htm.htm.jsonl, page 2; EX99.1_2026-09-30_ebs_ebs202609-30ex99er-htm.htm.jsonl, page 9

### Qualitative downside scenario

The downside starts with a further fall in NARCAN price or volume in U.S. public-interest channels and a deferred government option, which push Q4 2026 revenue toward USD 99.6 million and keep Adjusted EBITDA negative. Fixed manufacturing cost then deepens the margin loss, operating cash flow turns negative once the inventory release ends, cash falls from USD 175.7 million, and the notes become current on 2027-08-15 while trailing EBITDA is still weak. Management levers are the restructuring, asset sales, curtailing buybacks and further note repurchases, but resilience looks sufficient to avoid a liquidity crisis only through 2027; the observable triggers are quarterly naloxone revenue, option exercises and the notes' reported fair value.
Basis: calculation C06; calculation C07; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ebs.htm.jsonl, pages 19–23

### Overall credit view

Overall, Emergent presents a weak business profile supported by licensed government products and positive cash flow, but credit quality is constrained by negative trailing reported EBITDA, a declining naloxone franchise and customer concentration. Cash-flow durability appears adequate for twelve months but not established beyond that. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on Q4 2026 and 2027 order timing, naloxone stabilisation and progress retiring notes. The primary downside path is naloxone erosion plus deferred government orders ahead of the 2028 cluster. Further analysis would require the financing agreements and trailing issuer Adjusted EBITDA. Top risk: refinancing of the 2028 notes.
Basis: calculation C03; calculation C08; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)
