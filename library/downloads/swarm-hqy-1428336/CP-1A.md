## Findings

### Bottom line

HealthEquity is a scaled, listed HSA custodian with no sponsor or controlling holder named, so creditors face little ownership-driven extraction risk, but nearly half of revenue and most gross profit come from interest-linked custodial fees, and latest-half buybacks exceeded free cash flow.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-31_hqy-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-07-31_hqy-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2026-01-31_hqy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 122; calculation C5; calculation C8; calculation C11

The franchise is large, with HSA Assets of USD 37.9 billion at July 31, 2026, and no customer provides more than 10% of revenue. The board added USD 1.0 billion to the repurchase authorization in May 2026, and first-half repurchases were 114.60% of free cash flow while cash fell. Sponsor and governance risk is Medium: it would rise if buybacks were funded from the revolver and fall if the proxy statement confirms independent oversight.

### Key findings

- The business is a recurring-fee franchise rather than a transaction-driven one, which supports revenue visibility. The company holds HSA Assets of USD 37.9 billion for its members, reports about 20% of the HSA market by assets, says most accounts open before each fiscal year starts, and has no customer above 10% of revenue. The market share figure is a company statement citing an industry report.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-31_hqy-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-07-31_hqy-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 28; 10-K_2026-01-31_hqy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2026-01-31_hqy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 84
- Earnings depend on interest rates and a small set of partners. Custodial revenue is 49.66% of first-half revenue and 63.49% of gross profit, and fees from insurance company and depository partners were about 48% of fiscal 2026 revenue, up from 39% in fiscal 2024. USD 2.3 billion of HSA cash earning 1.5% reprices in the rest of fiscal 2027, against a book average of 3.9%, so custodial income follows the rates at which cash reprices.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-31_hqy-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-07-31_hqy-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-K_2026-01-31_hqy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 25; calculation C5; calculation C8; calculation C23

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a low-risk reading is that capital return has outrun cash generation. First-half repurchases were 114.60% of free cash flow, the new authorization is 2.17x last-twelve-months free cash flow, and the revolver that funded the 2024 acquisition is also the stated source for further acquisitions. The view would move to High if repurchases were funded by revolver draws, if a dividend were introduced, or if the credit agreement proved to allow wide distributions.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-31_hqy-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-07-31_hqy-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-07-31_hqy-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 18; calculation C11; calculation C14

The Medium level also rests on facts that could improve. The proxy statement could confirm independent directors, no related-party dealings and no large holder, and the cybersecurity case could be dismissed or capped. Partner dependence is a business risk rather than a governance one, but no partner is named and no single-partner share is stated, so a loss of a large partner would change the custodial view and cannot be tested here.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_hqy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-K_2026-01-31_hqy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 122; 10-Q_2026-07-31_hqy-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11; Source readiness (CP-0)
