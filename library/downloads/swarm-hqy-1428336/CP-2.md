### Company description and credit linkage

HealthEquity administers consumer health savings accounts and other consumer-directed benefits and earns service fees, custodial yield on HSA cash and card interchange. Its credit relevance is that most accounts are opened before each fiscal year begins, which gives revenue visibility, while custodial yield makes about half of revenue sensitive to interest rates. At July 31, 2026 it held 10,739 thousand HSAs with USD 37.9 billion of HSA Assets, HSAs grew 8% in the year, and it describes itself as the largest provider by accounts and second by assets, citing an industry report. It is one operating segment with no customer above 10% of revenue, which limits single-customer loss but not the reliance on a few insurance and bank partners that hold the cash and set the yield.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is largely recurring and account-driven: service fees are fixed tiered fees paid monthly for the term of the client agreement, custodial revenue is yield on HSA cash and client-held funds, and interchange follows card spending and is the only transactional line at 15.27% of first-half revenue. Volume is rising, with HSAs up 8% and new HSAs from sales up 20% in the first half of fiscal 2027, while other benefit accounts fell 2%. Pricing power is mixed: the average yield on HSA cash rose from 3.51% to 3.83% as contracts repriced to higher yields, but average service fees per account and the average interchange rate both fell. Switching costs are high because accounts belong to the member and closure fees apply, retention is described as strong and no churn rate is disclosed. Revenue is therefore predictable in the service and custodial lines, rate-driven in custodial, regulated throughout because tax law creates the product, and not structurally declining.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Cost of revenue was 27.1% of revenue in the first half of fiscal 2027 against 30.4% a year earlier, because service costs fell 7% on technology efficiencies and lower fraud reimbursements, medical claims and consulting spend while revenue rose 7%; service gross margin rose by 7.41 percentage points to 38.76%. Custodial costs are small at 6.78% of custodial revenue, so custodial income reaches EBITDA almost in full. Operating expenses before amortization and integration rose 13.21% against revenue growth of 7.42%, led by technology and general and administrative spend, so part of the gross margin gain is being spent. Margins can be defended against volume pressure, but not against a custodial yield fall, because custodial costs are small and do not fall with the revenue, so the loss reaches EBITDA directly.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense and asymmetric: banks such as Optum Bank and Webster Bank and retail investment companies such as Fidelity Investments compete for HSAs with greater resources, and Fidelity has gained significant share, so competition can limit service pricing and obliges the company to offer competitive yields to keep members. New entrants need Treasury approval as a non-bank custodian or a bank charter, which has not stopped well-resourced entrants. Substitution comes from network partners that decide to offer HSAs directly, which the company says would reduce channel opportunities and cause attrition, and from policy change such as single payer proposals. Buyer power is moderate because no customer exceeds 10% of revenue and switching an HSA takes effort and may cost closure fees. Supplier power is the force that matters most for credit: insurance company and depository partners paid fees equal to about 48% of fiscal 2026 revenue and set the yield, one bank identification number sponsor supports the card programme, and one custodial investment partner holds invested balances, none of them named.

### Downside scenario

The downside is a falling-rate and falling-equity cycle. The first break is custodial revenue: new and repriced HSA cash earns less, client-held funds reprice immediately, and invested balances, now 54.20% of HSA Assets (calculation C24), fall in value and cut service fees. Because custodial costs are only 6.78% of custodial revenue, the loss reaches EBITDA almost in full; each 10% fall in custodial revenue removes USD 67,073.9 thousand, or 13.16% of EBITDA, and takes net leverage to 1.53x and cover to 9.37x with no cost saving. Free cash flow falls by a similar amount before tax while buybacks of USD 231,054 thousand in a half-year continue unless the board stops them, and the Treasury bond forwards settle in cash from August 2026 with a net liability of USD 85,198 thousand. Liquidity of USD 921,003 thousand before letters of credit and no maturity before fiscal 2030 mean there is no refinancing or liquidity event in the next two years; EBITDA would have to fall 43.39%, a custodial revenue fall of 32.96%, before leverage returned to the fiscal 2025 level. Management levers are suspending buybacks, trimming capital expenditure and the undrawn revolver, and resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress; the observable triggers are the yield on repricing HSA cash, HSA cash growth, repurchases against free cash flow and the revolver balance.
