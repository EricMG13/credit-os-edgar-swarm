## Findings

Magnera is a thinly covered, slowly deleveraging credit with no maturity before 2029 and workable liquidity, so creditors face limited near-term default risk but a refinancing that depends on cash generation the company has not yet shown.

### Company description

Magnera makes specialty nonwoven and film materials for hygiene, wipes, healthcare, apparel, filtration and food and beverage customers, from 45 plants split between the Americas, 57.21% of fiscal 2025 net sales, and the Rest of World, 42.79%. Personal Care was 48% and Consumer Solutions 52% of fiscal 2025 net sales, and consumer demand gives steadier volume than industrial end markets. The credit consequence is limited by two facts: organic volume still fell 2% in fiscal 2025, and the top ten customers are about 42% of net sales with no long-term arrangement for many of them, so revenue visibility is order flow rather than contract. That supports debt service in the near term but does not underwrite a refinancing on its own.

### Ownership and group structure

Magnera is a listed company created on November 4, 2024 when Treasure Holdco, a Berry subsidiary, merged with Glatfelter; pre-merger Treasure holders received 90% and Glatfelter holders kept 10%. No sponsor, current holder, board right or fee is disclosed, and the financing paid USD 1,111 million of cash to Berry at closing, 71.40% of the borrowing proceeds. Subsidiary guarantors support the secured notes but are not named, and no restricted-group or non-guarantor figures are given, so structural subordination is not established. The distribution is a historical one-off; since closing the company repaid debt, shows no further distribution and states an intent to pursue acquisitions, so the financial-policy risk is Medium rather than low.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Reported sales move with raw-material prices more than with volume, so sales are not a margin measure. In the first nine months of fiscal 2026 net sales rose 3.38% to USD 2,445 million, helped by USD 105 million of foreign currency and USD 112 million of merger revenue, and held back by a USD 117 million cut in selling prices and a 1% organic volume decline. Pricing power is limited: costs pass through contractually only over time, at renewals and by other means, and customers can switch suppliers. ### Cost structure and margin resilience
