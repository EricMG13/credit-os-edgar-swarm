## Findings

### Bottom line

Magnera has no controlling sponsor, so creditors depend on management's own financial policy, which has been creditor-friendly since the merger: debt repaid, no shareholder distributions and a lower capital spending plan, set against material weaknesses in financial reporting controls.

The business is a large but thin-margin supplier of nonwoven materials to consumer end markets, with USD 3,204 million of net sales in fiscal 2025, ten customers making up 42% of sales and prices that follow raw material costs with a lag. Detail on the board, the shareholders and related-party terms sits in a proxy statement that was not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 20; calculation C7

### Key findings

- The owners are dispersed, so there is no sponsor whose willingness to support the debt can be assessed. At the merger in November 2024 Berry's former shareholders received 90% of the combined company and Glatfelter's shareholders kept 10%, and fewer than 62 holders of record are reported; no holder with a controlling stake is named in the documents read.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 15
- Financial policy since the merger is creditor-friendly on the evidence available. The company repaid USD 65 million of term loan and 2031 notes in the nine months to June 2026, its cash flow statements show no dividends or share repurchases, and its capital spending outlook for the year fell to USD 60 million from USD 80 million. The USD 1,111 million cash distribution to the former parent at the merger was paid out of new borrowings, so today's USD 1,901 million of debt reflects that transaction rather than later behaviour.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 15; calculation C9; calculation C10
- Management's synergy and cost reduction add-back is shrinking, which indicates the merger savings are reaching reported results. The figure fell from USD 60 million at the first quarter release to USD 48 million at the second and USD 35 million at the third, though the add-back is management's own measure and the savings it counts are not audited.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-02-05_ef20064605_ex99-1.htm.jsonl, page 6; EX-99.1_2026-05-07_ef20072634_ex99-1.htm.jsonl, page 6; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_ef20079553_ex99-1.htm.jsonl, page 6; calculation C8
- The Rest of World segment is the weaker earner and the volume risk: its adjusted EBITDA margin was 8.70% over nine months against 13.82% in the Americas, and its organic volume fell 3% on soft European demand. Customer concentration adds to volume risk, because the largest customer was 14% of fiscal 2025 sales and the top ten were 42%, and many customers have no long-term arrangement and can change suppliers quickly.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; calculation C3
- Reporting quality is the main governance weakness. The auditor gave an adverse opinion on internal control over financial reporting at fiscal year end 2025, and management reports its disclosure controls as not effective at each 2026 quarter end, with remediation tied to moving off legacy U.S. systems inherited from Berry that remain under a transition services agreement. Creditors cannot yet rely on the controls to catch an error, and a restatement would be the event that matters; none is reported.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 44; 10-Q_2025-12-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-03-28_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 20
- Acquisition appetite is stated but not yet used. Management says it intends to pursue additional acquisitions at attractive post-synergy multiples, which would add integration risk and could add debt, but the documents describe no pending transaction.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, pages 13–14

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a creditor-friendly reading is the stated intention to pursue further acquisitions while debt stands at USD 1,901 million, together with controls that make reported progress harder to verify. The view would turn adverse on a debt-funded acquisition, any shareholder distribution or share repurchase, or a restatement, and would firm on remediation of the control weaknesses and further debt repayment.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, pages 13–14; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 20

### Limitations

- The proxy statement was not supplied, so board independence, holder concentration and related-party terms are not established, and the finding of no controlling sponsor rests on the merger share split and the count of record holders alone.
- No credit agreement or indenture was supplied, so legal capacity for distributions, investments and further borrowing is not assessed and willingness cannot be separated from capacity.
- The fiscal year ended 2026-09-26 is not reported, so any repayment, acquisition or distribution after June 27, 2026 is not captured in the view of financial policy.
- Customer names, contract terms and order backlog are not disclosed beyond the two concentration shares, so revenue durability is read from volume and price trends only.
- The share of sales under raw material pass-through contracts and the energy cost exposure are not stated, so the protection from pass-through is not quantified.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The only transaction the documents describe is the merger completed on November 4, 2024, in which Treasure Holdco, then a wholly owned subsidiary of Berry Global Group, merged with Glatfelter, which was renamed Magnera; Treasure was the accounting acquirer and Berry's former shareholders received 90% of the combined company. At closing the company arranged a USD 785 million term loan, issued USD 800 million of 7.25% first priority senior secured notes and put in place a USD 350 million revolving credit facility, and Glatfelter's USD 500 million of 4.75% first priority senior secured notes stayed in place. The proceeds retired part of Glatfelter's debt and funded a USD 1,111 million cash distribution to Berry.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, pages 24–25

No new debt issue, acquisition or business disposal is stated after the merger. The company repaid USD 65 million of the term loan and the 2031 notes in the nine months to June 27, 2026 and sold one Rest of World facility in the quarter ended June 27, 2026, recording a USD 7 million loss and receiving USD 7 million of proceeds.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 8; calculation C9
