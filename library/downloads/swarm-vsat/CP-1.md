## Findings

### Bottom line

Reported leverage improved to 4.41x total debt to EBITDA at March 31, 2026 from 5.57x a year earlier, then edged up to 4.49x on a last-twelve-months basis at June 30, 2026, so deleveraging has paused while the 2027 secured notes move into current liabilities.
Basis: calculation C05; 10-K_2026-03-31_vsat-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 173; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vsat-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 2

Cash interest cover improved to 4.73x on the same twelve months, but falls to 2.43x once interest capitalized on satellites under construction is counted, and EBITDA is built from statement lines because the issuer states none, so covenant measures may differ. Cash plus undrawn revolvers cover the current portion of debt 4.36 times, although the Inmarsat revolver expires within five months of the as-of date.
Basis: calculation C06; calculation C10; calculation C14; 10-K_2026-03-31_vsat-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 129–131; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vsat-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 34, 70

### Financial interpretation

Leverage fell in fiscal 2026 for two reasons that deserve different weight. Carrying-value debt dropped from USD 7,036.109 million to USD 6,448.605 million as the 2025 notes were redeemed and the original Inmarsat term loan was repaid early, which is real deleveraging, but EBITDA also rose from USD 1,263.327 million to USD 1,462.590 million partly because the prior year carried USD 169.400 million of impairment and related charges from exiting EMEA locations. With that charge added back, an Analyst Judgement that is not applied to any ratio, fiscal 2025 leverage would be 4.91x rather than 5.57x, so the move to 4.41x overstates the underlying improvement. Over the last twelve months to June 30, 2026 leverage is 4.49x because EBITDA slipped to USD 1,435.586 million while debt stayed near USD 6,442.399 million, so further progress now depends on EBITDA growth as the ViaSat-3 satellites enter service or on repayment from cash.
