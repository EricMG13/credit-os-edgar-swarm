## Findings

### Bottom line

Creditors rest on cash rather than earnings: net debt is only USD 303 million, but total debt is 6.67x last-twelve-month reported EBITDA, so the credit depends on the USD 3,085 million cash balance staying accessible.

Reported EBITDA, calculated as operating income plus depreciation and amortisation, was USD 508 million for the twelve months to 2026-10-10 against USD 200 million of cash interest, a cover of 2.54x. The company's own adjusted measure, which also adds back rent, shows 2.2x and is a different perimeter. About USD 2,300 million of the cash sits in accounts under springing control agreements, and the only committed backstop is USD 894 million of undrawn asset-based facility. The next maturity is USD 350 million of notes due in October 2027. No credit agreement, indenture or rating was supplied, so covenant headroom is not established.

### Key findings

Gross leverage is the weak point of the credit, and it improved only because earnings recovered. Total debt of USD 3,388 million is 6.67x last-twelve-month reported EBITDA, against 14.90x at the 2025 year end, when reported EBITDA was USD 229 million after USD 204 million of restructuring and related expenses. For 2024 the ratio is not calculable because reported EBITDA was USD -421 million. Debt rose because the company issued USD 1,950 million of senior unsecured notes in August 2025 and repaid USD 300 million of 2026 notes, and it kept the rest as cash, so net debt is USD 303 million, or 0.60x. Bondholders are therefore exposed to how that cash is used: it is both the cushion and the reason net leverage looks low.
Basis: calculation C19; calculation C20; calculation C15; calculation C01; calculation C02; 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 66–68, 86–87

Interest cover is adequate on cash interest and weaker on interest actually accrued. Last-twelve-month reported EBITDA covers cash interest of USD 200 million 2.54x and interest expense of USD 213 million 2.38x. Cash interest understated the burden in 2025: USD 76 million was paid against USD 139 million of expense, because the first coupons on the new notes fell in 2026 and accrued interest rose from USD 23 million to USD 79 million. The last twelve months carry two coupons on the new notes, and the contractual annual coupon on year-end principal is USD 195.88 million, so USD 200 million is a fair run-rate. Other income, net of USD 106 million over the last twelve months, which the company attributes mainly to interest on invested cash, offsets part of the cost.
Basis: calculation C07; calculation C09; calculation C21; calculation C37; calculation C33; 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 66, 68, 87, 94; 10-Q_2026-10-10_aap-20261010.htm.jsonl, pages 5–7, 24

Cash generation has turned, driven by payables as much as by earnings. Operating cash flow was USD 311 million in the forty weeks to 2026-10-10 against USD -118 million a year earlier, and after USD 202 million of capital expenditure free cash flow was USD 109 million against USD -277 million. Accounts payable moved from a USD 270 million outflow to a USD 94 million inflow, and fiscal 2025 operating cash flow of USD -46 million reflected a USD 469 million payables outflow. Payables are largely supplier-financed: confirmed obligations were USD 2,600 million against accounts payable of USD 3,065 million. These obligations sit outside debt, but a withdrawal by the participating banks, which the annual report links to the company's credit rating, would shorten payment terms and consume cash, so this float is the main swing factor in liquidity.
Basis: calculation C36; 10-Q_2026-10-10_aap-20261010.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 6, 14; 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 33, 67–68, 103

Liquidity is USD 3,979 million, made of USD 3,085 million of cash and USD 894 million of undrawn asset-based facility after USD 106 million of letters of credit. About USD 2,300 million of the cash is designated qualified cash in accounts that become restricted on a default or covenant breach, and the facility requires minimum availability of the greater of 12.5% of the line cap and USD 125 million and excess availability of at least USD 400 million. The company reports compliance, but headroom is not established without the credit agreement. The next maturities are the 2027 notes of USD 350 million and the 2028 notes of USD 300 million at year-end principal, both within reach of cash.
Basis: calculation C29; calculation C30; 10-Q_2026-10-10_aap-20261010.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 12; 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 89–92

Funded debt is entirely senior unsecured notes: USD 3,450 million of principal at the 2025 year end in six series, with USD 350 million due in FY2027, USD 300 million in FY2028, USD 1,475 million in FY2030 and USD 1,325 million in FY2032 and FY2033. The undrawn USD 1,000 million first-lien asset-based facility ranks ahead of the notes against receivables, inventory and cash, so a drawing would be senior to the notes in those assets; the subsidiary guarantor list was not supplied, so structural priority beyond that is not established. Claims outside debt are large: operating lease liabilities of USD 2,189 million, USD 549 million of cash paid on operating leases in fiscal 2025, supplier finance of USD 2,600 million, USD 57 million of guaranteed bank loans to independent stores, and a final USD 55 million Worldpac working-capital payment made in 2026.

Three definitions drive the numbers. Reported EBITDA here is operating income plus depreciation and amortisation, which leaves out other income, net and so excludes interest income. The company's adjusted EBITDAR of USD 1,157 million also adds back rent expense of USD 524 million, share-based compensation of USD 37 million and USD 89 million of transformation and other charges, so it is not comparable with USD 508 million, and the 2.2x ratio built on it adds lease liabilities of USD 2,189 million to debt. The company also deducts all cash although about USD 2,300 million is under springing control. Periods differ: fiscal 2025 has fifty-three weeks, the interim periods are forty weeks and are labelled nine months in the tables, and the last twelve months span fifty-three weeks.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious reading is operating recovery. Forty-week reported EBITDA was USD 406 million against USD 127 million, operating income was USD 218 million against USD -87 million, restructuring expense fell from USD 180 million to USD 84 million, gross margin rose to 45.29% from 43.21%, and free cash flow was USD 109 million, with guidance raised to about USD 150 million for fiscal 2026. The company's own net leverage has been 2.4x, 2.1x and 2.2x across the three quarters of 2026. This view would win if earnings keep rising while the cash is held and the maturities to 2030 are met from cash. The view would turn weaker if confirmed supplier-finance obligations fall sharply from USD 2,600 million, or if cash is used in a way that lifts net debt well above USD 303 million without a matching rise in EBITDA.

### Limitations

No credit agreement, indenture, rating, market price or peer set was supplied, so covenant headroom, guarantor coverage and relative value are not established. Principal by note after the 2026 repurchases is not stated in the quarterly reports, so the maturity ladder, debt stack and debt reconciliation are drawn at the 2025 year end. Adjusted EBITDA is not stated by the company, which reports adjusted EBITDAR, so no adjusted leverage or cover row is given. Cash interest is paid twice a year on most notes, so any single interim period is lumpy and only the last twelve months are used.
