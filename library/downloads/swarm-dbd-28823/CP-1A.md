## Findings

### Bottom line

Diebold Nixdorf’s public operating model combines an installed service base with cyclical banking and retail hardware; for creditors, that mix diversifies revenue but leaves cash generation exposed to project timing and discretionary capital returns. Nine-month 2026 revenue grew 4.3%, led by Retail growth as Banking declined, while the company reduced its full-year earnings and free-cash-flow outlook. Services remained the larger revenue stream, but reported cash conversion was weak and share repurchases continued. The filings identify no current controlling shareholder in this evidence set, so sponsor alignment and shareholder control cannot be assessed from issuer identity or historic restructuring alone.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 5–10, 99, 105; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 19, 25; EX99.1_2026-09-30_dbd_ex-991q32026erlive-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 1–3, 7, 13; calculation C01; calculation C02

### Key findings

Retail growth shifted the revenue mix toward a lower-margin segment: Retail contributed 30.4% of nine-month 2026 sales and grew 21.9% year over year, while Banking contributed 69.6% and declined 1.9%. The mix supports growth but can pressure consolidated gross margin if lower Retail margins persist.
Basis: EX99.1_2026-09-30_dbd_ex-991q32026erlive-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9, 13; calculation C01

The company’s disclosed nine-month 2026 free-cash-flow use was USD 68.5 million, and it repurchased USD 148.6 million of shares during the period, followed by USD 19.3 million of additional purchases through October 23. Those cash uses reduce the liquidity retained for working capital and investment while management has lowered its full-year free-cash-flow outlook.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 19, 25; EX99.1_2026-09-30_dbd_ex-991q32026erlive-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 7

The 2023 restructuring changed the equity base and accounting perimeter: the former shareholders received less than half of the successor’s new common shares, and fresh-start accounting made the reorganized company a new reporting entity. Current beneficial holders, their percentages and any sponsor rights are not identified in the supplied filings.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 64–66, 105

Q3 customer wins across Banking and Retail support a constructive demand case, including orders for cash recyclers, ATMs, point-of-sale lanes and self-checkout refreshes. The release gives units and some contract values but not a complete delivery schedule, contribution margin or conversion into cash; these wins therefore support activity rather than a quantified near-term earnings bridge.
Basis: EX99.1_2026-09-30_dbd_ex-991q32026erlive-htm.htm.jsonl, page 2

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The company has a global installed base, no customer above 10% of annual sales in FY2025, a majority of revenue from services, and Q3-reported customer wins in both segments. This moderates single-customer and hardware-only concentration concerns. The outlook reduction, negative nine-month cash generation and capital returns still make cash conversion the key creditor test. A current proxy, detailed order-to-revenue conversion, margin delivery and post-quarter cash-flow data would clarify whether the service base and recent wins offset mix and working-capital pressure.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 5–10, 99, 105; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 12; EX99.1_2026-09-30_dbd_ex-991q32026erlive-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 1–3, 7, 13; calculation C02

Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18, 25

### Governance view

The filings support an issuer-level view of a listed company that has refinanced its post-reorganization debt and continues to return capital, but they do not identify a current controlling shareholder or sponsor. The documented buybacks occur alongside weak nine-month cash conversion, so they reduce liquidity available for working capital and investment; the evidence does not establish that a sponsor directed them or that they breached debt protections. The company’s periodic filings and effective disclosure-control statement support lender visibility, while the absent proxy and current compliance information limit assessment of control rights and covenant capacity. The sponsor and governance risk level is therefore insufficiently established; the actionable focus is the board’s future capital-return policy, post-quarter cash conversion and the use of the remaining authorization.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 79, 105; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18, 24–25; EX99.1_2026-09-30_dbd_ex-991q32026erlive-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The company and certain U.S. and Canadian subsidiaries filed prepackaged Chapter 11 cases on June 1, 2023; a Dutch scheme proceeded alongside them. The U.S. court confirmed the plan on July 13, the Dutch court sanctioned its plan on August 2, and both plans became effective on August 11, 2023. The company emerged using fresh-start accounting; the former common shareholders received less than half of the successor’s new common shares. This reset separated the predecessor and successor financial reporting, but the filing does not provide current beneficial-holder percentages.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 64–66

On December 18, 2024, Diebold Nixdorf issued USD 950 million of 7.75% senior secured notes due March 2030 and entered a USD 310 million revolving facility due December 18, 2029. The company used the note proceeds, a USD 70 million revolver borrowing and cash on hand to repurchase the emergence term loans, repay the prior revolver and pay related fees and premiums. The new notes and revolver share first-priority collateral; specified subsidiaries guarantee both facilities. The Q3 2026 filing reports that USD 37 million was drawn and repaid on the revolver during the first nine months of 2026, with no revolver balance at quarter-end.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 79–80; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18
