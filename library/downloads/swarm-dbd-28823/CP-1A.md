## Findings

### Bottom line

Diebold Nixdorf is a scaled, services-led self-service technology supplier with no named sponsor, so creditor risk lies in discretionary shareholder returns and thinning product margins rather than buyout leverage; governance risk is medium because covenant limits and legacy litigation status are not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 7–13; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 42; 10-K_2024-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 40

Services are 56.99% of revenue and no customer exceeds 10%, which supports cash-flow visibility, but product backlog fell 7.21% in 2025 and Banking sales are shrinking while Retail grows. Funds associated with two holders own 46.9% of the stock, and share repurchases of USD 148.6 million in nine months of 2026 ran ahead of free cash flow, which was an outflow of USD 68.5 million.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C6; calculation C8; calculation C9; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 42; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 99; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 19

### Key findings

- Business risk is moderate and moving from Banking to Retail. Services, the largest revenue area at 56.99% of FY2025 revenue, grew 3.69% in nine months of 2026, while Banking product sales fell 6.9% and Retail product sales rose 37.6%; product gross margin fell to 25.5% from 26.9% over nine months and to 22.2% from 27.3% in the third quarter alone, on memory costs, regional mix and more electronic point-of-sale sales, partly offset by USD 10.0 million of tariff recoveries. The only order measure stated in amounts, product backlog, was USD 733.1 million at 2025-12-31 against USD 790.1 million a year earlier, so hardware demand was lower entering 2026.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C5; calculation C6; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 7–13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 6–7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 13

- Shareholder returns, not acquisitions, are the creditor-relevant use of cash. FY2025 repurchases of USD 128.0 million were covered 1.87x by free cash flow of USD 239.0 million, but nine-month 2026 repurchases of USD 148.6 million came with a free cash outflow of USD 68.5 million and were paid from the cash balance, with USD 19.3 million more bought through 2026-10-23 and a new USD 250.0 million authorisation on 2026-10-28 that the board may suspend at any time. The annual report says repurchases are limited by covenants, but the covenant terms were not supplied, so capacity cannot be separated from willingness.
Basis: calculation C9; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 42; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 19

- Ownership is concentrated without a holder stated to control the company. Funds associated with Capital World Investors and Millstreet Capital Management own about 32.4% and 14.5%, or 46.9% together, against 31.7% and 16.0%, or 47.7%, a year earlier, and shares outstanding fell 10.66% between February 2025 and October 2026, which lifts each remaining holder's percentage mechanically. The company itself warns that these holders may press for transactions, including share issuance or new debt, but no board seats, consent rights or related-party dealings were supplied, and none is documented as extraction.
Basis: calculation C7; calculation C8; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 42; 10-K_2024-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2024-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 37; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 2

- The December 2024 refinancing was creditor-aligned. The USD 1,250.0 million Exit Facility was repaid with USD 950.0 million of senior secured notes due 2030, a new USD 310.0 million revolver maturing in 2029 and cash, so principal outstanding fell by USD 300.0 million and interest expense fell by USD 69.6 million, or 44.8%, in 2025; the notes and the revolver share first-priority liens on substantially all assets, so no existing creditor was primed, but little unencumbered value remains for any later creditor.
Basis: calculation C10; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 78–80; 10-K_2024-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 110

- Disclosure is adequate but incomplete. The annual accounts carry an unqualified opinion on internal control and a critical audit matter on the geographic dispersion of sales, but the proxy statement and executed debt documents were not supplied. The 2024 annual report also disclosed two German appraisal proceedings by former minority holders of Diebold Nixdorf AG over cash compensation of EUR 55.02 and EUR 54.80 per share, dismissed at first instance in 2022 and 2023 and appealed; the 2025 annual report and the 2026 quarterly reports do not mention them, so their status and any extra compensation are not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 51–52; 10-K_2024-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 40–41; 10-K_2024-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 133

- Strategy is in transition and its cash cost is still running. The Operational Evolution Program expects USD 125 million of restructuring cost, of which USD 105.1 million has been recognised, the non-core Turkey operation is being wound down by the end of 2026, an investment in a Chinese subsidiary was impaired by USD 16.9 million while strategic options are explored, and a possible strategic collaboration with Euronet Worldwide, Inc. is mentioned only among the factors that could change forward-looking statements, with no terms, funding or date.
Basis: calculation C11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 20; EX99.1_2026-09-30_dbd_ex-991q32026erlive-htm.htm.jsonl, page 14

- Overall governance risk is medium. The risk level is driven by repurchases that exceeded free cash flow in 2026, because they draw down the cash that supports liquidity and push net leverage up, which implies a thinner cushion for creditors if earnings weaken; countervailing evidence is the creditor-aligned refinancing, the unqualified audit opinions and the absence of any documented extraction beyond open-market repurchases. The level is not High because no extraction, priming or weak disclosure is documented, and it is not left unassessed because the filings document the repurchases, the refinancing terms and the ownership facts.
Basis: calculation C9; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 42; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 12

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a medium risk level is that nothing in the sources documents extraction: FY2025 repurchases were covered 1.87x by free cash flow, the refinancing cut interest cost by 44.8%, leverage is moderate, and the board can stop buybacks at any time. The view would soften if fourth-quarter 2026 cash flow rebuilds the cash used in nine months and the covenant documents show tight limits on restricted payments, and it would harden if repurchases continue under the new USD 250.0 million authorisation while free cash flow stays negative, if an appraisal ruling adds material compensation, or if the Euronet collaboration is financed with debt.
Basis: calculation C9; calculation C10; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 42; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 12

### Limitations

- Board composition, control rights, related-party dealings and executive pay sit in a proxy statement that was not supplied, so the governance conclusion rests on disclosed capital-allocation behaviour and audit quality alone and a higher or lower risk level cannot be ruled out.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 104–105
- The credit agreement and indenture were not supplied, so what covenants let shareholders take out through repurchases is not established, and the assessment of buybacks as a creditor risk cannot say whether they are near a limit.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 42; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 101–102
- The status of the German appraisal proceedings after the 2024 annual report is not established, so potential extra compensation to former minority holders is not quantified and is not part of the claims assessment.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 40–41; 10-K_2024-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 133

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The only new debt issue in the sources is the refinancing of 2024-12-18, when the company issued USD 950.0 million of 7.75% senior secured notes due 2030 at par and entered into a USD 310.0 million revolver maturing 2029-12-18, using the proceeds with revolver borrowings and cash on hand to repurchase all Exit Facility term loans, repay the prior revolver and pay premiums, fees and expenses. No debt was issued in 2025 or in the first nine months of 2026, and the revolver was borrowed and repaid within the period for USD 37.0 million. A sources-and-uses table for the refinancing is not stated.
