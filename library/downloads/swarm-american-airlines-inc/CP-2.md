## Findings

### Bottom line
American's fundamental credit profile is adequate but strained: a large hub-and-loyalty business and USD 11,763 million of liquidity support creditors, while fuel-driven earnings pressure has lifted total debt to 8.87x last-twelve-month reported EBITDA and left interest cover at 2.06x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C01; calculation C03

The primary downside path is another fuel spike or demand slowdown that cuts EBITDA while customer-funded cash unwinds, drawing on liquidity that covers principal due through 2028 only 0.98x. Refinancing capacity rests on aircraft collateral and loyalty assets, and the parent relationship adds support through guarantees and risk through USD 12,512 million of cash lent upstream.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C03; calculation C07

### Key findings
- Leverage is high because earnings fell, not because debt grew. Reported EBITDA fell from USD 4,573 million in FY2024 to USD 2,916 million over the last twelve months while debt stayed near USD 25,876 million, taking total debt to 8.87x; unhedged fuel and contract-bound labour costs pass price shocks straight into EBITDA, so creditors carry fuel risk without a cushion.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C04
- Interest cover is thin. Interest paid took 48.56% of last-twelve-month EBITDA, so cover of 2.06x leaves little room before cash interest competes with maturities and aircraft deliveries for liquidity.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C02
- Liquidity is the main protection but is finite. USD 11,763 million of available liquidity covers the USD 4,181 million of principal due before the end of 2027 by 2.81x, yet it only matches the USD 12,042 million due through 2028, so access to refinancing, not cash on hand, decides the 2028 outcome.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C03
- Recent cash flow is flattered by customer-funded balances. Free cash flow of USD 1,780 million in the first nine months of 2026 came with a USD 3,206 million rise in air traffic and loyalty liabilities, which unwind if bookings slow.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C05; calculation C06
- Support from the parent is real but runs both ways. The parent guarantees the issuer's credit facilities and the AAdvantage financing and no dividend is evidenced, yet the issuer has lent USD 12,512 million net to the parent, so the issuer's liquidity is partly a claim on the parent group.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C07

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest case that the view is too cautious is that last-twelve-month EBITDA is depressed by a fuel spike: fuel cost rose 55.22% in nine months while revenue grew 15.63%, the parent guides to continued growth in fourth-quarter revenue, and 2026 financing pushed the revolving facilities to 2031 and a term loan to 2033. A fall in fuel prices that restores EBITDA toward the FY2025 level would cut leverage from 8.87x toward 7.44x without any debt reduction. The view would turn more negative if liquidity fell below the principal due before the end of 2027, if the receivable from the parent grew while earnings weakened, or if refinancing of the 2028 maturities needed additional collateral beyond aircraft.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.2_2026-10-22_earningspresentation.htm.jsonl, page 6; calculation C01; calculation C03; calculation C04; calculation C11

### Limitations
- Leverage is measured on reported EBITDA after aircraft rent, on debt that excludes operating lease liabilities, and over a single trailing window, so the finding that leverage is high and cover thin could look different on a lease-adjusted or fuel-normalised basis that this analysis does not build.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1)
- No peer or sector benchmark was run, so the adequate rating of the business profile is relative to the issuer's own history and sources, not to other airlines.
Basis: Source readiness (CP-0)
- The outlook rests on parent-level management guidance for the fourth quarter and full year, so the direction of earnings is indicative and carries no issuer-level forecast.
Basis: EX-99.2_2026-10-22_earningspresentation.htm.jsonl, page 6
- Covenant headroom and executed financing terms are not available, so the view that liquidity protects creditors excludes any minimum-liquidity covenant that could restrict use of cash.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 61; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Strengths and weaknesses
Ranked weaknesses. First, high leverage driven by fuel: reported EBITDA of USD 2,916 million over the last twelve months against debt of USD 25,876 million gives 8.87x, and unhedged fuel passes price shocks directly to earnings, which leaves little capacity to absorb another shock. Second, thin cover of 2.06x with interest paid at 48.56% of EBITDA, which raises the cost of any shortfall. Third, a 2028 maturity of USD 7,861 million that exceeds what liquidity covers alone. Fourth, debt that is entirely secured, with 55.75% linked to aircraft and engines, so more borrowing adds claims on the same collateral. Fifth, a net receivable from the parent of USD 12,512 million, which makes the issuer's cash partly dependent on the parent's credit.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C03; calculation C07; calculation C09

Ranked strengths. First, liquidity of USD 11,763 million including USD 7,159 million of short-term investments, which covers principal due before the end of 2027 by 2.81x. Second, a network and loyalty business in which loyalty revenue was 13.42% of revenue and partners paid USD 6.2 billion of cash to the group in 2025, a recurring source that supports financing against loyalty assets. Third, demonstrated refinancing access in 2026, when revolvers were extended to 2031, a term loan to 2033 and three aircraft certificate programs were placed. Fourth, a parent that guarantees key debt and paid no dividend.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C03; calculation C08

### Business profile and ownership
American is a network carrier that sells scheduled passenger and cargo service through nine hubs, with regional carriers flying under capacity purchase agreements, and it is wholly owned by its parent. Domestic routes were 70.89% of passenger revenue in the first nine months of 2026 and loyalty revenue 13.42% of operating revenues, so the business draws on passenger demand across its network and on a loyalty program whose partner cash and mileage balances act as recurring funding. No customer concentration is stated. The group structure matters for creditors: the parent guarantees American's credit facilities and the AAdvantage financing, but it carries its own debt and has received USD 12,512 million of net cash from the issuer, so the issuer is both supported by and a funder of the group.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C07; calculation C08

### Revenue, costs and cash conversion
Revenue is cyclical and partly discretionary but backed by loyalty income: revenue grew 15.63% in the nine months to September 30, 2026, so demand has held, yet pricing power did not offset fuel, whose cost rose 55.22%. Costs are largely fixed in the short run because about 86% of the group's employees are covered by labour contracts and fuel is unhedged by policy, which means margins shrink first when fuel rises, and creditors rather than management cost actions carry the gap. Capital intensity is high, with USD 24,056 million of aircraft and engine commitments against nine-month capital expenditures of USD 2,660 million, and free cash flow of USD 1,780 million in the nine months was helped by a USD 3,206 million rise in customer-funded balances, so durable conversion of EBITDA to debt service cash is not yet shown.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 110; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 65–66; calculation C04; calculation C05; calculation C06; calculation C10

### Industry forces and framework read
Competition is intense and the filings list competitive, regulatory, labour and fuel risks as the main pressures; the credit translation is that sector pricing and cost shocks reach earnings faster than debt can be reduced. Rivalry and fuel are the forces that move margins, while supplier concentration in aircraft and engines delays deliveries and raises financing needs. Regulatory and policy factors matter through antitrust litigation at the parent group and aviation regulation, and macroeconomic demand matters through leisure and corporate travel; none of these has a quantified effect in the sources. In credit terms the supportive items are network scale, loyalty cash and a parent that guarantees debt, and the constraining items are leverage, fuel exposure and secured-debt concentration.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3–6; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 110; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 42; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 65

### Financial profile and credit quality
Scale and market position are adequate: the network is broad but no market share is stated. Competitive advantage rests on the loyalty program and hubs and is adequate. Business diversification is weak to adequate, with 70.89% of passenger revenue domestic and no customer data. Cost and capex flexibility are weak, because labour is contracted, fuel is unhedged and commitments are large. Margin stability is weak: the EBITDA margin on the nine months fell with fuel. Free cash flow stability is weak to adequate because recent strength depends on customer-funded balances. Ability to refinance is adequate, evidenced by 2026 transactions but limited by secured-only debt and the group's non-investment-grade corporate credit rating. Liquidity is adequate for the next five quarters and only matches principal through 2028. Financial policy and governance are adequate: no dividend and parent guarantees, offset by cash lent to the parent. The trend is down on leverage and cover and flat on liquidity, and the missing data are executed agreements, covenant headroom and the terms of the receivable from the parent.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 33; calculation C01; calculation C03; calculation C07; calculation C08

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds
Over twelve to twenty-four months the parent guides to fourth-quarter revenue growth of 19.5% to 22.5% and a full-year 2026 adjusted loss per share of USD (1.60) to USD (1.00), on an assumed fuel price of about USD 4.70 per gallon, so earnings stay under pressure through year-end and leverage on trailing EBITDA is unlikely to improve before 2027. Tailwinds are revenue growth, loyalty cash, extended maturities and loyalty cash; headwinds are fuel, contract wage increases and the USD 7,861 million 2028 maturity. Over three to five years the question is whether fleet renewal funded by secured debt lifts margins enough to reduce leverage; the sources give no issuer-level forecast, so that is not established.
Basis: EX-99.2_2026-10-22_earningspresentation.htm.jsonl, page 6; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C03; calculation C11

### Downside scenario
The supported downside begins with fuel staying near the parent's assumed level or rising, or with a booking slowdown. Revenue growth then fails to cover fuel, and because labour is contracted and fuel is unhedged, EBITDA falls below the USD 2,916 million last-twelve-month level and cover below 2.06x. Free cash flow turns negative as customer-funded balances stop rising, drawing on the USD 7,159 million of short-term investments, while aircraft commitments continue. Liquidity of USD 11,763 million is enough to avoid distress through 2027, but it only equals the principal due through 2028, so the first financial break would be refinancing of the 2028 maturities on secured terms against aircraft and loyalty collateral. Possible management levers are drawing the undrawn facilities and slowing lending to the parent, while deferral of aircraft deliveries is not established by the sources; resilience looks sufficient to avoid a liquidity crisis in 2027 and uncertain for 2028. No numerical stress result is established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C02; calculation C03; calculation C05

### Overall credit view
Overall, American presents an adequate business profile supported by a broad network, loyalty cash and liquidity, but credit quality is constrained by leverage of 8.87x reported EBITDA, thin cover of 2.06x and unhedged fuel exposure. Cash-flow durability appears adequate but flattered by customer-funded balances. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on aircraft and loyalty collateral, market access for secured debt and the parent's credit, since the parent guarantees key debt and holds USD 12,512 million of the issuer's cash. The primary downside path is fuel-driven earnings compression that consumes liquidity before the 2028 maturities. Further analysis would require executed agreements, covenant headroom, the terms of the parent receivable and an issuer-level outlook. Top risk: fuel-driven earnings compression.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C01; calculation C03; calculation C07
