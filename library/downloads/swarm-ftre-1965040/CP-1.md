## Findings

### Bottom line

Fortrea carries leverage that its earnings base supports only thinly, so creditors rely on liquidity and on maturities that begin to bite only in 2028 rather than on interest cover. At September 30, 2026 net debt was USD 834.7 million, which is 9.12x reported EBITDA for the last twelve months of USD 91.5 million, and that EBITDA covered cash interest 1.18x. The October 19, 2026 acquisition raises net debt to USD 873.7 million, or 9.55x, before any EBITDA the acquired business adds.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25; calculation C3; calculation C8; calculation C15; calculation C16; calculation C21

Reported EBITDA carries restructuring charges, and a house-adjusted measure that adds them back, an Analyst Judgement, gives EBITDA of USD 124.0 million and net leverage of 6.73x. The issuer states no EBITDA, covenant ratio or maturity schedule for September 30, 2026, so covenant headroom is not established and the lien ranking of the 2030 notes rests on conflicting labels. Free cash flow of USD 169.0 million over the last twelve months leans on USD 145.5 million of working capital inflow.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ftre-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 128–129; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-K_2025-12-31_ftre-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 188; calculation C14; calculation C17; calculation C18; calculation C26

### Leverage and coverage

Reported EBITDA was negative in 2024 and 2025, at USD 76.6 million and USD 794.6 million below zero, so leverage on a reported basis has no meaning for either year; the 2025 figure carries a USD 797.9 million goodwill impairment. The recovery shows in the last twelve months: EBITDA rose from USD 67.5 million at June 30, 2026 to USD 91.5 million at September 30, 2026, while gross debt stayed at USD 1,055.3 million because no principal was repaid in nine months 2026. Leverage therefore moves only with earnings, and 11.53x total and 9.12x net remain high for a business whose cash interest was USD 77.8 million over the same twelve months.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ftre-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 128–129; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2–4; calculation C1; calculation C8; calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C15

Cover of 1.18x at September 30, 2026 compares with 0.87x at June 30, 2026 and a negative cover in 2024 and 2025, so reported EBITDA now pays the interest but with little margin. Cash interest paid fell to USD 77.8 million over the last twelve months from USD 91.2 million in 2025, which the quarterly report attributes to the partial repurchase of the 2030 notes in 2025, no revolver borrowing and lower effective rates on the term loans; the rest of the improvement in cover is EBITDA.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-K_2025-12-31_ftre-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 183; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23; calculation C16; calculation C8; calculation C11

### Cash flow quality and funding float

Operating cash flow of USD 72.7 million in nine months 2026 exceeded the net loss of USD 36.2 million mainly because of non-cash charges, USD 59.4 million of depreciation and amortization and USD 36.0 million of stock compensation, while working capital added a net USD 12.7 million: unearned revenue added USD 51.0 million and payables USD 23.7 million, against USD 55.3 million absorbed by receivables. Unearned revenue stood at USD 525.5 million at September 30, 2026, a customer-funded float that reverses if new bookings slow, and a further USD 300.0 million of receivables are sold and kept off the balance sheet, so the debt lines understate the funding the business draws from outside them.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12; calculation C26

Free cash flow of USD 169.0 million over the last twelve months, after USD 32.8 million of capital spending and after cash interest, is well above reported EBITDA of USD 91.5 million only because working capital contributed USD 145.5 million of the operating cash flow, USD 132.8 million of it in the fourth quarter of 2025; before working capital, free cash flow was USD 23.5 million. Operating cash flow of USD 262.8 million in 2024 was lifted in the same way by the sale of receivables under the receivables facility, which the 2025 annual report gives, with higher payments to suppliers, as the reason operating cash flow fell in 2025.

### Liquidity and the October 2026 acquisition

Liquidity at September 30, 2026 was USD 220.6 million of cash plus USD 447.7 million available under the undrawn revolver, equal to 0.63x of total debt. The revolver and term loan A mature on June 30, 2028, when USD 387.5 million of term loan A falls due on the schedule at year-end 2025, and USD 649.0 million of notes and term loan B follow in 2030; the receivables facility is fully used and ends in February 2029. About USD 39.0 million of the cash left on October 19, 2026 for the acquisition, which is kept out of the period figures.

### Data quality and definitions

The issuer states no EBITDA, so every EBITDA here is operating income plus depreciation and amortization, which includes restructuring and impairment charges; what the issuer's own definition would give is not established. A dash in a statement is carried as nil only where the printed totals confirm it. Depreciation and amortization in the 2024 cash flow statement of USD 86.9 million differs from USD 85.3 million in the income statement because the cash flow statement includes discontinued operations. Debt is the balance-sheet carrying value of USD 1,055.3 million, USD 11.0 million below contractual principal of USD 1,066.3 million, and the 2030 notes are labelled senior in the debt note and senior secured in the exhibit list.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ftre-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 129–130; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-K_2025-12-31_ftre-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 188; calculation C4

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a stressed reading is cash and time: cash rose by USD 46.0 million in nine months 2026 with nothing drawn on the revolver, and the first principal payment above USD 25.0 million falls in 2028. The evidence for it is that last-twelve-month free cash flow rests on working capital and on USD 51.0 million of unearned revenue growth in nine months, and that restructuring charges have recurred every year, so a slowdown in new business would remove the cash cushion before it lifted earnings.
