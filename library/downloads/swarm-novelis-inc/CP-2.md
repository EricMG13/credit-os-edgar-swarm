## Findings

### Bottom line

Novelis is a scaled, recycling-integrated aluminum roller whose credit is stretched but not distressed: negative free cash flow from the Bay Minette build has lifted leverage and drained revolver availability, so creditors rely on the revolver and parent equity until cash flow turns.

Scheduled maturities are small until April 2029, but management's return to positive free cash flow in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2027 is an expectation, and the parent has made no commitment to repeat its USD 950 million equity injection.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a stretched view is that the cash burn is front-loaded by timing and choice. Capital expenditure guidance implies USD 557 million to USD 857 million in the second half against USD 1,543 million in the first, the Oswego hot mill is back in operation, USD 519 million of insurance recoveries has been recognized, Adjusted EBITDA per tonne is up 23.44%, the issuer states compliance with the covenants of the term loan facility, the revolver and the senior notes, and it guides to positive free cash flow in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2027. A parent that injected USD 950 million after the fires began also signals support. The view would improve if adjusted free cash flow turns positive in that quarter, revolver availability stops falling and the issuer's net leverage ratio moves down from 4.7x. It would worsen if Bay Minette commercial shipments slip beyond the first quarter of fiscal 2028, working capital keeps absorbing cash as metal prices rise, revolver availability approaches the USD 300 million that the annual report's formula gives for a USD 3,000 million commitment, at which a fixed-charge test applies, or distributions resume while leverage is above 3.5x.

Adjusted EBITDA per tonne was USD 553 in the second quarter, up 23.44%, and recycled metal was about 62.41% of the metal the issuer purchased or tolled in fiscal 2026, which both lowers energy use and reduces dependence on primary metal. Pricing that passes the metal cost to customers means a rise in the aluminum price does not squeeze the conversion margin, so earnings power should return to debt service once capital spending falls.

### Company description and credit linkage

Novelis rolls and recycles aluminum into sheet, plate and light gauge products for beverage packaging, automotive, aerospace and specialty customers. It operates 29 facilities in nine countries, 15 of them with recycling, and reported fiscal 2026 net sales of USD 18,434 million and rolled product shipments of 3,557 kilotonnes. Beverage packaging was 54.45% of fiscal 2026 net sales, specialty 21.48%, automotive 20.11% and aerospace and industrial plate 3.96%. The company was formed in Canada in 2004, was acquired by Hindalco in 2007 and shows no acquisition or large disposal in the period reviewed; the large strategic events are the Bay Minette greenfield plant and the two Oswego fires.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_nvl-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 188; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The model supports cash-flow visibility because most products are priced as an aluminum base plus a local premium plus a conversion premium, so metal price changes pass through, and large customers qualify their suppliers over many months, which makes volumes sticky. It weakens debt service capacity in two ways: the pass-through lifts working capital when metal prices rise, and some customer contracts carry no minimum purchase volume. Seasonality lowers third-quarter shipments, so the December quarter carries the lowest volumes.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_nvl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 49; 10-K_2026-03-31_nvl-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 14, 33

### Ownership and group structure

All 606,333,333 common shares are held directly by AV Minerals (Netherlands) N.V. and indirectly by Hindalco, and Hindalco appointed all eight directors. ### Revenue drivers and pricing power

### Porter's five forces

Rivalry is intense but structured by scale and qualification. The annual report lists competitors in each region, including Arconic, Constellium, Kaiser, Speira and several Chinese producers, states that the market is characterized by economies of scale and heavy capital investment, and says that customer qualification can take many months. Credit implication: a large installed base protects volumes, but low-priced exports from emerging markets and tariffs can move trade flows and press conversion premiums, as in the competition for scrap and the Section 232 effects the issuer reports.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_nvl-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 11–13

The threat of new entrants is low for flat-rolled sheet because a hot-mill plant costs billions of dollars, as Bay Minette shows at about USD 5 billion, and continuous casting is cheaper but serves a narrower product range. The threat of substitutes is moderate: aluminum has gained from glass, PET and steel on sustainability and weight, but the issuer notes that high aluminum prices can reverse that. Buyer power is high: the ten largest customers took 57% of fiscal 2026 net sales, Ball took 18%, some contracts carry no minimum volumes, and consolidation among customers increases their leverage. Supplier power is moderate: Rio Tinto, the primary metal supplier, was 6% of purchases in fiscal 2026, scrap is bought in a market where competition has risen, and gas is bought at market prices. Together, the forces cap pricing at the conversion premium and leave customer concentration as the main force that can move credit quality.

Political and regulatory factors are material, because trade policy changes cost and trade flows directly. The US antidumping dispute over Brazilian shipments holds a cash deposit of about USD 127 million that the issuer considers remote to lose. Credit implication: tariffs depress North American margin and add freight and working-capital cost, and Kingston curtailment shows management will trade volume for margin.

### Downside scenario

The most plausible downside path is a slow Bay Minette ramp combined with metal prices that stay high, and it runs through working capital and the revolver, not through earnings collapse. The initiating condition is commercial shipments slipping beyond the first quarter of fiscal 2028 or ramping slowly while aluminum stays near USD 3,204 per tonne and the local premium near USD 1,242. The first operational break is that start-up costs, which Adjusted EBITDA excludes, and capital spending at the top of the USD 2.1 billion to USD 2.4 billion range persist. The financial transmission is that operating cash flow stays negative as inventories absorb cash, as they did in the first half when they took USD 733 million, so adjusted free cash flow stays negative and the revolver funds it.
