## Findings

### Bottom line

Live Nation's fundamental credit profile is adequate but weakening at the margin: leverage on reported earnings rose to 6.07x as borrowing for venues grew and a litigation charge cut earnings, so creditors depend on venue returns, the antitrust outcome and continued market access.

### Limitations

- The covenant definitions of consolidated net debt and consolidated EBITDA, and any compliance certificate, are not supplied, so headroom under the net debt covenant that steps down from 6.75x to 5.25x cannot be calculated and the refinancing conclusion rests on the issuer's statement that it is in compliance.
- Debt, cash and leverage after the October 2026 refinancing and after the surrender window for the 2029 convertible notes are not stated, so the leverage and liquidity conclusions describe September 30, 2026 only and could move either way once those are known.
- Free cash is an issuer-defined measure whose components cannot be checked beyond the reconciliation printed in the release, so the liquidity conclusion would weaken if event-related prepaid expenses did not convert into shows.
- Management's outlook is the only forward evidence, and no external forecast, rating or peer benchmark is supplied, so the outlook and the comparison of this business profile with typical leveraged-credit issuers are not established.
- Cash interest and cash taxes are printed only for full years, so the cash conversion conclusion for the last twelve months stops at operating cash flow less capital spending and cannot be taken to a cash cover ratio.

### Company description

Live Nation is a listed operator that promotes concerts and festivals, operates and books venues, manages artists, sells tickets for third-party clients through Ticketmaster and sells sponsorship, with Concerts producing 82.8% of FY2025 revenue and Ticketing and Sponsorship & Advertising producing most segment profit. Fans and sponsors pay ahead, so revenue is visible: event-related deferred revenue was USD 4.0 billion at year-end 2025 and USD 3.8 billion at September 30, 2026, and contracted sponsorship revenue for later periods was USD 1.7 billion. Concerts revenue is recognised on the day of the show, so deposits are liabilities until then, which supports cash flow but exposes it to refunds on cancellation. International operations produced about 43% of FY2025 revenue, which diversifies demand but leaves USD 4.9 billion of cash in foreign subsidiaries that the issuer generally does not repatriate.

### Ownership and group structure

The issuer is a listed company with no sponsor or parent, Liberty Live holds a significant interest of unstated size, and no dividend, repurchase programme or owner fee is evidenced, so owner behaviour is not a credit driver on current evidence. The group is a holding company whose operations sit in subsidiaries: the bank facilities and secured notes are guaranteed by certain domestic subsidiaries and secured on substantially all their personal property and a pledge of 65% of first-tier foreign subsidiary shares, the 2027 senior notes are described as unsecured, and the guarantors of the convertible, VenueCo and 2032 notes are not stated. Four venues sit behind non-recourse VenueCo notes whose cash is released only after reserve and coverage tests, and noncontrolling partners hold puts and receive distributions equal to 10.61% of FY2025 adjusted operating income, so cash that reaches group creditors is lower than consolidated earnings. Sponsor and governance risk is medium because of revolver-funded acquisitions rather than owner action.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue comes from events, attendance and ticket and fee pricing, and it is discretionary, cyclical and heavily concentrated in Concerts. Visibility into the next quarters is high: third-quarter 2026 revenue grew 19%, 2026 event ticket sales were pacing 13% ahead at October 19, 2026, event-related deferred revenue was 8% higher, and 95% of 2026 sponsorship commitments were booked by mid-year. Pricing power is limited: the annual report says artist influence is increasing and barriers to entry in promotion are low, venues keep most ticketing service fees, and the issuer pays clients upfront advances to win ticketing contracts. Ticketing contracts run three to five years with about 10,500 clients, so renewals rather than churn drive volume, and about 23 million net new tickets were signed in 2026, mostly outside North America. Revenue is therefore predictable for the next year and cyclical in a downturn, but not structurally declining.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Costs are largely variable with the number of shows but contain a large fixed element in artist guarantees and venue costs. Direct operating expenses were 74.45% of FY2025 revenue and 73.96% in the first nine months of 2026, and Concerts earned a 3.3% adjusted margin, so a small shortfall in attendance or price takes most of a show's profit. Festival guarantees are fixed, so festival revenue changes move operating income by similar amounts, while venue operation earns higher margins than promotion. Mix defends the consolidated margin: Ticketing and Sponsorship & Advertising earn 36.8% and 63.6%, and the adjusted operating margin was 9.27% in FY2024, 9.39% in FY2025 and 9.19% for the twelve months to September 2026, while the reported EBITDA margin fell to 5.76% on the litigation charge. Margins can be defended against modest volume pressure and cost inflation, but not against a broad attendance shortfall or cancellations, where guarantees and venue costs are already committed.

### Qualitative downside scenario

The issuer-specific downside begins with a demand or cancellation shock in the summer touring season, when Concerts is at its peak share of segment profit: it was 47.51% of segment adjusted operating income in the third quarter of 2026 against 25.76% for FY2025. The first operational break is that artist guarantees are committed four to eight months ahead and venue leases and new-venue costs run far longer, while shows are cancelled or under-attended and cancellation insurance covers only part of the loss, which leaves a 3.3% margin little room. The financial transmission follows through deposits: cash held for cancelled events is typically refunded within 30 days, so part of the USD 3.8 billion of event-related deferred revenue turns from a float into refunds, while reported EBITDA, already reduced by the litigation charge, falls further and total debt over reported EBITDA rises above 6.07x.

The liquidity and refinancing consequence is that free cash of USD 2,306.6 million and undrawn revolver commitments cover the identified claims of the litigation accrual and the 2027 senior notes 2.53x, and that coverage falls to 1.17x if the 2029 convertible notes were also settled wholly in cash at their September 30, 2026 fair value. A weaker earnings base would also tighten the net debt covenant that steps down from 2027-03-31, and the revolving facilities would mature early if more than USD 500 million of 2027 notes remain outstanding 91 days before maturity while free cash is below the outstanding principal plus USD 500 million. At September 30, 2026 free cash was above the USD 1,450.0 million that the 4.750% senior notes plus USD 500 million would require, so the early-maturity test is not close today. Management levers are to slow revenue-generating capital spending, which was 88.07% of FY2025 capital expenditure, to pause acquisitions, to settle the convertible notes in shares, to draw the USD 700 million delayed draw term loan, and to rely on the absence of dividends. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress in a one-season shock, but a shock combined with damages above the accrual and a cash-settled 2029 conversion would consume most of the buffer. Observable triggers are falling ticket sales pacing and event deposits, rising cancellations, free cash below the identified claims and further revolver draws.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Live Nation presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, contracted revenue visibility, free cash of USD 2,306.6 million and proven market access, but credit quality is constrained by leverage of 6.07x on reported EBITDA, thin concert margins, an open antitrust and consumer litigation and a revolver-funded acquisition policy. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because deposits, sponsorship contracts and discretionary capital spending support it, although working capital moves cash sharply. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on continued access to bank, convertible and euro secured markets, the outcome of the 2029 convertible notes and the 2027 senior notes. The primary downside path is a demand or cancellation shock that hits thin concert margins and unwinds event deposits while the litigation accrual and the 2029 notes call on cash. Compared with typical leveraged-credit issuers in the sector, the business profile cannot be benchmarked because no peer data is supplied. Further analysis would require the covenant definitions, debt after the October 2026 refinancing, ownership percentages, the federal settlement terms and the cash timing of the accrual. Top risk: the Ticketmaster remedy and damages combined with a concert shock.
