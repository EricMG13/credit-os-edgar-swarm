## Findings

### Bottom line

Live Nation is a listed, scaled live-entertainment operator with no evidenced shareholder extraction, so creditor risk lies in antitrust remedies, debt-funded venue buying and thin concert margins rather than in owner behaviour. Concerts bring 82.8% of revenue but only 25.76% of segment adjusted operating income in FY2025, so profit sits in Ticketing and Sponsorship & Advertising, which earn the other 74.24%.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 142; 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation C02; calculation C03

The issuer agreed a settlement with the US Department of Justice that still needs court approval, lost a jury verdict to the remaining states in April 2026, and accrued USD 450 million, equal to 19.02% of FY2025 adjusted operating income; the remedies phase is open.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20; 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 68; calculation C08

Acquisition funding is the behaviour to watch: the September 2026 purchase of Plenitude Arena for USD 532.5 million was financed in part with a USD 453.8 million revolver draw, after a USD 775.0 million draw in 2025 to buy a further 24% of OCESA.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 114; calculation C09

### Key findings

Owner behaviour is not a source of creditor risk on the evidence supplied, although the evidence is thin. No private-equity sponsor appears, there is no announced share repurchase programme, the cash flow statement shows no common dividend, and the one significant holder, Liberty Live, holds an interest whose size the filings do not state. Liberty Media split Liberty Live off in December 2025, two directors were originally nominated by Liberty Media under a stockholder agreement, and the related-party note says certain of the relationships it describes involved no significant transactions.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 130; 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 61–62; 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 92–93; 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 152; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 54

Group profit depends on Ticketing and Sponsorship & Advertising far more than the revenue split suggests, which makes the Ticketmaster antitrust remedy the most consequential legal risk. Concerts earned a 3.3% adjusted operating margin in FY2025 against 36.8% in Ticketing and 63.6% in Sponsorship & Advertising. Concerts rose to 35.35% of segment adjusted operating income in the first nine months of 2026 and 47.51% in the third quarter partly because outdoor shows cluster in the summer months, yet Ticketing and Sponsorship & Advertising still earned 64.65% over nine months.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 71–72; 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 142; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 24; calculation C03; calculation C05; calculation C06; calculation C07

The antitrust and consumer-protection cases are an unresolved claim on cash and, through the remaining states, on the business perimeter. The original complaint sought divestiture of Ticketmaster and cancellation of certain ticketing contracts, and the remedies phase may still consider those remedies for the states that did not settle. The settlement with the United States and six further states carried an initial USD 280 million fund, the states that settled took about USD 18.6 million of it, and the USD 450 million accrual is the issuer's best estimate for those states plus the jury damages. A separate Federal Trade Commission suit survived a motion to dismiss in September 2026 and a consumer class was certified in December 2025.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 47–48; 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 127; 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 129; 10-Q_2026-03-31_lyv-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation C08

Financial policy is acquisitive and partly revolver-funded, and the pattern has now repeated. The 2025 purchase of a further 24% of OCESA from CIE was financed mainly by a USD 775.0 million draw that was repaid in October 2025 when the credit agreement was refinanced, and the 2026 purchase of Plenitude Arena was financed in part with a USD 453.8 million draw, which is 85.22% of the stated consideration. Nine-month acquisition spending in 2026 was USD 572.3 million, revenue-generating capital expenditure rose 85.41% in FY2025, and total capital expenditure was 44.41% of FY2025 adjusted operating income. Management plans to open or acquire 10 large venues by year-end, so the same funding choice may recur.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 77–78; 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 113–114; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 49–50; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_lyv-2026q3xex991er.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C09; calculation C12; calculation C13

A growing share of cash flow sits outside the direct reach of group creditors. Distributions to noncontrolling interest partners equalled 10.61% of FY2025 adjusted operating income, minority partners hold put options that the issuer estimates at USD 160.6 million for 2027, the four VenueCo venues are ring-fenced for EUR 610 million of non-recourse notes with release tests on cash, and foreign subsidiaries hold USD 4.9 billion of cash excluding client cash that the issuer generally does not repatriate. Only 8.70% of venues are owned, so most venues are held under lease, operating or booking rights rather than as owned property.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 93; 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 136; 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 44; calculation C14; calculation C16

Disclosure is adequate for monitoring financial results but incomplete for governance. The auditor gave an unqualified opinion on FY2025 and reported on internal control, management found internal control effective, and the quarterly report states that disclosure controls are effective. Board composition, director independence and holdings sit in a proxy statement that is not supplied, and the issuer's adjusted operating income excludes the USD 450 million litigation accrual, so adjusted earnings do not reflect it.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 87–88; 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 148; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 53; 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 151; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 24

Overall sponsor and governance risk is medium. The level is driven by acquisitive, revolver-funded financial policy and by legal exposure at the Ticketmaster franchise, because debt-funded purchases raise drawn debt before acquired earnings are shown and a divestiture remedy would remove the largest contributor to segment profit. Countervailing evidence is the absence of dividends, buybacks beyond employee share settlement and sponsor fees, and the issuer's record third-quarter results.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20; 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 62; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_lyv-2026q3xex991er.htm.jsonl, page 1; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_lyv-2026q3xex991er.htm.jsonl, page 3; calculation C03

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is operating momentum and the absence of owner leakage. Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 19% and adjusted operating income rose 14% to a record, every segment grew adjusted operating income by double digits, nine-month operating cash flow was USD 2.1 billion and management reports adjusted free cash flow conversion of 69% of adjusted operating income. The settlement with the United States, if approved, resolves the federal claims through relief whose terms are not described, and no dividend or sponsor fee appears in the cash flow statement. These are management statements for the near term and do not cap a damages award or a remedy.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-11-05_lyv-2026q3xex991er.htm.jsonl, page 1; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_lyv-2026q3xex991er.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 30; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32

Four developments would change the view. Court approval of the settlement, a post-trial ruling that sets damages within the USD 450 million accrual and a remedy limited to conduct terms would lower the legal weight on the Ticketing franchise. A remedy that requires a Ticketmaster divestiture or cancels ticketing contracts, or damages above the accrual, would raise it sharply. A repayment of the revolver draws after the Plenitude Arena purchase, or a funding mix with more cash, would soften the financial-policy concern, while another debt-funded purchase of a minority stake or venue would harden it. Disclosure that Liberty Live holds a controlling or influential stake, or any dividend, fee or buyback funded from group cash, would raise the ownership risk.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lyv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_lyv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 130

### Limitations

- Ownership percentages, board composition and director independence are not stated in the supplied documents because Part III is incorporated from a proxy statement that is not supplied, so the conclusion that no owner extraction is evidenced would weaken if a single holder proved able to direct distributions or board decisions.
- The terms of the injunctive and structural relief in the federal settlement are not described in any supplied document, so the effect on the Ticketing franchise cannot be sized and the legal-exposure conclusion stops at the USD 450 million accrual, with no range for further loss.
- Revenue, earnings and funding terms of Plenitude Arena and the other 2026 venue purchases are not stated, so the conclusion on acquisition funding cannot test whether acquired earnings cover the cost of the borrowing used.
- The credit agreement, indentures and VenueCo documents are known only through the issuer's summaries, so legal capacity for restricted payments, asset transfers and further ring-fencing cannot be separated from willingness, and the sponsor-behaviour conclusion rests on observed actions alone.
- Every document in the supplied set is issuer-authored and no rating, market price or third-party commentary is supplied, so the characterisation of financial policy and of management's outlook rests on the issuer's own language.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The October 2025 refinancing replaced the prior credit agreement with a USD 1.3 billion multicurrency revolving facility, a USD 400 million venue expansion revolving facility, a USD 700 million delayed draw term loan A and a fully drawn USD 1.3 billion term loan B. The revolving facilities and the delayed draw loan mature on 2030-10-21 as first written, with an earlier springing date if more than USD 500 million of the 2027 senior secured or senior notes remain outstanding 91 days before their maturity and consolidated free cash is below the outstanding principal plus USD 500 million, and the term loan B matures on 2032-10-21. In the same month the issuer sold USD 1.4 billion of 2.875% convertible senior notes due 2031 and redeemed USD 300.0 million of its 5.625% notes due 2026.
