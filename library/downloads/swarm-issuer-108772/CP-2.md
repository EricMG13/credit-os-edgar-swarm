## Findings

### Bottom line

Xerox Corp presents weak fundamental credit quality: a shrinking print business supports USD 4,220 million of debt at 6.80x last-twelve-months EBITDA, and about USD 2,931 million falls due in 2028 to 2030, so creditors rely on refinancing, not free cash flow.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 100–101; calculation C1

That EBITDA of USD 621 million includes about USD 205 million of one-off gains; without them leverage is 10.14x, interest on the debt stack takes at least USD 350 million a year and free cash flow was USD 37 million. Net debt of USD 3,810 million at September 30, 2026 would be USD 3,756 million pro forma for the exchange expected on or about November 13, 2026, or 6.05x against 6.14x, but closing is not confirmed. Cash of USD 410 million, with pro forma cash not established, and net revolver availability of USD 225 million cover the 2027 maturities, not 2028.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C6; calculation C8

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a weak assessment is operating momentum and a near-term cushion: adjusted operating income grew year over year for three consecutive quarters to USD 399 million in the first nine months of 2026 against USD 243 million pro forma, cost savings of more than USD 500 million were reached by the end of 2025, the Lexmark synergy target is more than USD 300 million by the end of 2027, no maturity above USD 197 million falls due before 2028, and the dividend suspension, discounted repurchases and the exchange all reduce debt. If those synergies land on revenue that stabilizes, EBITDA could move well above the underlying USD 416 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 59–60; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 112; 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 6–7; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

The view would improve to adequate if pro forma revenue stabilizes, underlying EBITDA rises toward the reported USD 621 million with operating cash flow above the USD 195 million top of the outlook, and the maturities due from 2028 are refinanced or exchanged at lower cost without further priority shifting. It would worsen if the outlook is cut again, receivable sale proceeds keep falling, net revolver availability falls below USD 225 million, or the exchange does not close and the 2028 notes stay at USD 556 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 56; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 97; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Company description and credit linkage

Xerox sells printing equipment, supplies and managed print services, finances equipment through Xerox Financial Services, and since the 2024 purchase of ITsavvy sells IT infrastructure solutions; Print and Other is 91% of nine-month 2026 revenue and IT Solutions 9%, and 43% of 2025 revenue came from outside the United States. The model supports cash flow visibility because 70% of revenue is post sale and customers are diverse, but it weakens refinancing confidence because reported growth of 12.86% in the first nine months of 2026 comes from adding Lexmark for six more months while pro forma revenue fell 5.0%, so the base that services the debt is shrinking.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9–12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 66–67; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C14

### Ownership and group structure

Xerox Corporation is wholly owned by Xerox Holdings Corporation, a Nasdaq-listed parent with no sponsor or controlling holder identified, and it carries all group debt because it guarantees the parent's notes and receives the proceeds in full. Creditors therefore depend on parent choices: distributions fell to USD 45 million in the first nine months of 2026 and dividends are being suspended, but the Joint Venture and the exchange show a willingness to move assets and priority, which keeps governance risk at Medium; board and holder detail is not supplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 124; 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 255

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is recurring in structure and declining in direction: post sale revenue of USD 3,955 million fell 4.1% pro forma in the first nine months of 2026, equipment sales rose 2.6% in the third quarter with installations up 9.0% but at a gross margin of 5.0%, IT Solutions fell 18.9% on the timing of large prior-year public sector placements, and financing revenue fell 22.5% as receivables are sold. Pricing power is limited because the company competes on price with Brother, Canon, FUJIFILM Business Innovation, HP, Konica Minolta, Kyocera and Ricoh and cites product cost increases and mix against gross margin, so revenue is best described as recurring, cyclical and structurally declining.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 59; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 65–66; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 68–69; 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 18

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins are being rebuilt through cost actions rather than pricing: selling, administrative and general expense fell to 20.0% of third quarter revenue from 24.3%, research and development to 3.0% from 3.8%, and total gross margin rose to 29.2% from 22.7%. About 1.8 percentage points of the nine-month gross margin of 31.6% came from tariff receivables that will not repeat, and equipment gross margin of 5.0%, which the company also attributes in part to non-recurring prior-year purchase accounting adjustments, shows exposure to input costs, since the company lowered its Print and Other projections on sustained memory price increases. Margins can be defended against volume pressure only if the savings continue, because restructuring cost USD 82 million in the first nine months and the workforce reduction announced in October 2025 is still being carried out.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 59–60; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 68–69; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 112; 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 14

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital intensity is low and conversion is poor: capital expenditure was USD 94 million over the last twelve months, 1.23% of revenue, but operating cash flow of USD 131 million after interest, restructuring payments of USD 60 million and retirement plan contributions of USD 118 million in the first nine months of 2026 left USD 37 million, and the operating cash flow includes USD 265 million of net finance receivable sale proceeds. EBITDA therefore does not translate into free cash flow for deleveraging, and capital expenditure is too small a lever to change that over the maturity wall.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34; calculation C6

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is the force that matters: seven named print competitors compete on technology, price, service and brand, so equipment pricing cannot absorb product cost increases, which shows in the 5.0% equipment gross margin and caps margin recovery. Supplier power is concentrated in FUJIFILM Business Innovation, the largest sourcing partner, with purchases of USD 811 million in 2025 or 11.55% of revenue, so a supply dispute would interrupt equipment and parts at once. Buyer power is moderate because no customer is significant, and entry and substitution threats come mainly from digital workflows and hybrid work reducing print volumes, which the company addresses by expanding into IT Solutions.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 129; 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C16

### Macro, regulatory and technology factors

Tariffs and component costs are material to cash flow: tariff costs rose in 2025, the 2026 gross margin includes a one-time benefit of USD 105 million from tariff refunds whose receivable was sold for about USD 80 million, and the company tied the goodwill impairment to memory price increases and macro uncertainty, so input costs and trade policy move margins and liquidity together. Other political, social and technology factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver in the supplied materials beyond the structural decline in print volumes.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 40–41; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 59

### Credit-translated SWOT

Strengths are a recurring post sale base, proven cost delivery, lower payouts and liquidity through 2027. Weaknesses are the 2028 to 2030 maturity wall, thin underlying EBITDA against heavy interest, weak free cash flow and an equity deficit. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are delivery of the synergy target and an exchange that reduces debt and extends maturities; threats that could weaken it are further input cost increases, a lowered cash flow outlook and more priority-shifting financings.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Financial profile and credit quality

The main credit supports are recurring revenue, cost delivery and near-term liquidity; the constraints are leverage, interest burden, weak free cash flow and a narrow window to refinance. The trend is mixed: adjusted operating income is improving while the cash flow outlook, receivable sale funding and equity are deteriorating. Missing datapoints are market prices, ratings, covenant headroom and a 2027 cash flow outlook.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 97

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over 12 to 24 months the tailwinds are further synergies, savings already reached, lower dividends and an exchange that extends USD 120 million to 2030, and the headwinds are pro forma revenue declines, product cost increases, shrinking receivable sale funding and the lowered operating cash flow outlook of USD 145 million to USD 195 million. Over three to five years the deciding issue is whether EBITDA can be rebuilt toward the level the 2028 to 2030 maturities need before the revolver and the 2028 notes mature; the issuer's own leverage target needs about USD 1,407 million of EBITDA.

### Qualitative downside scenario

The initiating condition is continued pro forma revenue decline of about 5% a year with product cost increases that keep equipment gross margin near the 5.0% of the third quarter. The first operational break is that savings stop covering the decline, so underlying EBITDA stays near USD 416 million or lower instead of rising. The financial transmission is that interest of about USD 357 million leaves little cash, receivable sale proceeds keep falling from USD 122 million in nine months, and operating cash flow stays at or below the low end of the outlook of USD 145 million, so free cash flow is near zero after capital expenditure of USD 94 million.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 68–69; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 97; calculation C3; calculation C6

The liquidity consequence is gradual rather than abrupt: USD 635 million of cash and revolver availability covers the USD 217 million due through 2027, but revolver availability depends on a borrowing base that shrinks with receivables and inventory, and the revolver matures no later than May 22, 2028, before the USD 556 million of 5.50% notes due in August 2028. The refinancing consequence is that USD 2,931 million due in 2028 to 2030 would have to be refinanced at 10.25% to 13.50% or through more exchanges and asset financings, so unsecured creditors would be asked to accept lower priority. Management levers are the dividend suspension worth about USD 28 million a year, further discounted repurchases, receivable sales and asset-backed financings, and the observable triggers are a further cut to the outlook, falling revolver availability and a failed or delayed exchange. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress before 2028 but not sufficient to refinance the 2028 wall on current earnings without exchanges or asset-backed financing.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 42; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 56; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C2

### Overall credit view

Overall, Xerox Corp presents a weak business profile supported by recurring post sale revenue, cost delivery and scale, but credit quality is constrained by a shrinking revenue base, thin one-off-flattered EBITDA, interest that absorbs most of it and USD 2,931 million of maturities in 2028 to 2030. Cash flow durability appears weak because operating cash flow depends on falling receivable sales and the outlook was cut. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on EBITDA recovery, the secured debt capacity under the indentures and further asset-backed or priority-shifting transactions. The primary downside path is a refinancing failure at the 2028 wall after the revolver matures. Further analysis would require market prices, ratings, covenant headroom, the amended Term Loan B terms and a 2027 cash flow outlook. Top risk: refinancing of the 2028 to 2030 maturities.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C1; calculation C2
