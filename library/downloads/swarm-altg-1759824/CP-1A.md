## Findings

### Bottom line

Alta is a diversified, OEM-anchored equipment dealer in which the supplied filings identify no controlling holder or sponsor, so creditors face operating and financial-policy risk more than extraction risk, and governance risk is medium.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 10–11, 111; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 25

Parts and service are 29.83% of FY2025 revenue but 53.39% of gross profit, and no customer exceeds 1% of revenue, which supports cash-flow durability. Against that, distributions of USD 41.6 million over FY2023 to FY2025 exceeded the cash left after investment in every year, so they leaned on borrowing, and preferred dividends continue with a stockholders' deficit of USD 47.1 million. The chief executive officer and chief financial officer hold an indirect minority interest in OneH2, which Alta paid USD 5.3 million to build a plant and which went into creditor assignment in June 2026; ownership, board and compensation detail is not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 40, 71–74; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 14; calculation C3; calculation C5; calculation C11; calculation C12; calculation C18

### Key findings

The operating model is durable at its core and dependent on suppliers at its edges. Parts and service were 29.83% of FY2025 revenue but 53.39% of gross profit after rental depreciation, while new and used equipment sales, 54.43% of revenue, earned a 14.07% margin. Alta is the exclusive distributor of new equipment and replacement parts in substantially all of its territories, no customer exceeds 1% of revenue and the top ten are about 6%, so cash flow is spread across many buyers. The dependency is on the manufacturers: five of them supplied 49% of equipment and parts bought in FY2025, a supplier may terminate a distribution agreement, and OEM finance arms provide floor plan lines that renew each year after a credit review, so one supplier's decision can reach both earnings and liquidity.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11, 13, 19, 30, 71–72; calculation C1; calculation C5

Earnings rest on Construction Equipment, and Material Handling is the weaker segment in 2026. Construction Equipment produced 60.83% of FY2025 revenue and 59.52% of segment Adjusted EBITDA before corporate costs, at a 9.04% margin. In the first nine months of 2026 Material Handling revenue fell 5.94% and its Adjusted EBITDA fell 5.01%, as new and used equipment sales dropped 11.67%, while Construction Equipment revenue rose 1.02% but its Adjusted EBITDA fell 1.74%. Reported group revenue fell 1.76%, but the issuer's organic revenue, which removes acquired and divested revenue, fell only 1.0%, to USD 1,302.5 million from USD 1,315.0 million, so nearly half of the reported decline comes from sold businesses. Group Adjusted EBITDA was USD 121.6 million against USD 123.8 million a year earlier, a margin of 9.33% in both periods, so the earnings base is flat to slightly lower and gives creditors no growth cushion.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 105–107; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26–27, 35; calculation C4; calculation C6; calculation C7

The rental fleet is being shrunk on purpose, which turns fleet assets into cash at the cost of rental income. Management began a fleet optimization in mid-2024 aimed at excess rent-to-sell assets and weak-return classes, cutting rental fleet gross cost by USD 84.3 million from 2024-06-30 to 2025-12-31 and new equipment inventory by USD 51.5 million in FY2025. Rental revenue fell 11.60% in FY2025 and a further 4.38% in the first nine months of 2026, while rental equipment sales rose 18.42%, so part of the cash generated has come from selling fleet, not from earnings growth. Management describes rental rates as stable to slightly positive, but utilization is not quantified, so how far the fleet cuts have outrun demand is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 44, 71–72; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26–27; calculation C4; calculation C8

No controlling holder or sponsor is identified, which removes sponsor-extraction risk but leaves no named party committed to support the equity. Alta's shares are listed on the New York Stock Exchange, ownership, board and compensation detail is incorporated from a proxy statement that was not supplied, and the market value of shares held by non-affiliates implies that affiliates held about 20% of the common shares in mid-2025. An investor, Mill Road Capital III, signed a board observer agreement and a cooperation agreement with Alta on 2026-01-21, but the filings give neither its holding nor the terms. Stockholders' equity is a deficit of USD 47.1 million at September 30, 2026, so equity offers no loss-absorbing cushion, and no guarantee, cure right or equity injection by a holder is described in any of the filings.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 111, 113; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 5–6; calculation C10; calculation C18

Shareholder distributions exceeded the cash left after investment in each of the last three years, so they leaned on borrowing; they have been cut back since, but not ended. Common dividends, preferred dividends and repurchases totalled USD 41.6 million over FY2023 to FY2025, 28.03% of operating cash flow, while operating cash flow less capital expenditure plus asset-sale proceeds was negative in two years and only USD 3.7 million in the third. The common dividend was halved in 2025 and none is printed for 2026, and repurchases of USD 5.8 million in FY2024 and USD 7.5 million in FY2025 have stopped, with USD 16.7 million of authorization still open. The preferred dividend continues, USD 2.2 million in the first nine months of 2026, despite the deficit. The filings give neither a reason for the changes nor a funding source, so the cut-back is observed, not committed.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 40–41, 73–74; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6–8, 58; calculation C11; calculation C12; calculation C13; calculation C14

Corporate activity has shifted from buying to selling, which helps creditors modestly. Alta states that it completed 17 acquisitions since 2020 and describes its growth strategy as historically built on acquisitions, but cash spent on acquisitions was USD 45.6 million in FY2023 and USD 2.7 million in FY2025, with no acquisition cash printed for FY2024. The FY2023 purchases of Burris and Ault were funded, together with working capital and rental fleet growth, from net borrowing of USD 91.3 million under the ABL facility. Three disposals since May 2025 had sale prices of USD 18.0 million, USD 6.4 million and USD 1.5 million, USD 25.9 million in all, and each was allocated to reduce senior debt. The strategy has not been withdrawn, and pro forma earnings and integration results are not disclosed for any purchase, so a return to acquisitions would be a re-leveraging risk that the filings do not size.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_altg_altg-20241231.htm.jsonl, pages 61, 103; 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 74, 102–103; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 24, 30; calculation C13; calculation C15

A related-party hydrogen investment is small but now unresolved. The chief executive officer and chief financial officer together hold an indirect, non-controlling minority interest in OneH2, to which Alta has paid USD 5.3 million to build and commercialize a hydrogen production plant for Alta. OneH2 assigned its assets for the benefit of its creditors on 2026-06-24 and the chief executive officer left its board. The total paid has not risen since the FY2024 report, which expected the plant to be operational in the first half of 2025; the FY2025 report moved that to the first half of 2026, and the 2026 reports give no start-up date. Management reports no indication of impairment and is considering commercialization opportunities, but the recoverable value of the plant and any further payments owed are not stated. Hydrogen fuel purchases from OneH2 were USD 0.6 million in FY2025, USD 1.6 million in FY2024 and USD 0.4 million in FY2023.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_altg_altg-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 89; 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 88; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 14; calculation C19

Reporting quality is adequate for monitoring, with one past correction and gaps in creditor-specific terms. The auditor's report on the FY2025 statements is dated 2026-02-26, management concluded that internal control over financial reporting was effective, and the FY2024 annual report corrected an error that had put rent-to-rent equipment sale proceeds in operating rather than investing cash flow, overstating operating cash flow by USD 7.5 million in FY2022 and USD 5.4 million in FY2023. The credit agreements, the indenture and the Series A preferred terms are not in the filings, so creditor protections must be taken from documents not supplied. Separately, 23.64% of employees are covered by collective bargaining agreements, two of which expire in 2027, and one multiemployer pension plan is in the red zone with a rehabilitation plan and a surcharge, a USD 1.7 million annual contribution whose withdrawal liability is not quantified.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_altg_altg-20241231.htm.jsonl, pages 74–75; 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 68, 104–105, 108–109; calculation C9; calculation C17

Overall, Alta presents medium sponsor and governance risk. The key supported behaviour is borrowing-funded distributions that are now curtailed, which matters because every dollar paid out while cash after investment was negative added to the claims that mature together in 2029 and implies a refinancing that depends on cash flow, not on outside support. Countervailing evidence is the stop to common dividends and repurchases, the use of disposal proceeds to repay senior debt and the cut to capital expenditure. Further analysis requires the proxy statement, the terms of the 2026 investor agreements and the credit agreements.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 41, 74, 92–93, 103; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6–8, 18, 24; calculation C11; calculation C12; calculation C16

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case that governance risk is higher than medium is a pattern of paying out cash the business had not generated: distributions of USD 41.6 million over three years, repurchases in loss years, a preferred dividend still paid with a stockholders' deficit of USD 47.1 million, and two senior officers holding an interest in a related party that has failed. The strongest case that it is lower is behaviour since mid-2025: common dividends and repurchases stopped, capital expenditure fell 51.96% in the first nine months of 2026, and USD 25.9 million of disposal proceeds went to senior debt. The view would worsen on evidence of a holder with control of the vote or a cooperation agreement that favours distributions or a sale, wide restricted-payment capacity in the credit agreements combined with resumed dividends or repurchases, or a further payment to OneH2. It would improve if the proxy statement shows an independent board with no controlling holder, the credit documents block distributions while debt is high, and further quarters pass with no payment beyond the preferred dividend.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 40–41, 73–74, 103; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6–8, 14, 24, 58; calculation C8; calculation C11; calculation C15; calculation C18

### Limitations

- Without the proxy statement, who holds the common shares and whether any holder can steer distributions or a sale is not established, so the medium rating rests on the absence of a controlling holder in the filings, not on a confirmed share register.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 111; calculation C10
- Without the terms of the January 2026 board observer and cooperation agreements, whether an outside investor has gained influence over capital allocation is not established; terms that commit Alta to a sale process or repurchases would raise the risk level.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 113
- The credit agreements and indenture were not supplied, so capacity to pay dividends, make investments or add liens is unknown and willingness to distribute is read only from what Alta actually paid; the distribution findings carry no capacity test.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 92–93, 113–114
- The hydrogen plant's carrying value and any commitment to OneH2 are not stated, so whether USD 5.3 million is recoverable is not established and the related-party finding is sized only by amounts paid.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 14
- Pro forma earnings and integration results for acquisitions, and the liquidation preference and redemption terms of the Series A preferred, are not printed, so acquisition discipline is judged from spending and funding alone and the preferred claim cannot be sized.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

No new debt was issued in FY2025 or in the first nine months of 2026. The last issue was the USD 500.0 million senior secured second lien notes due 2029-06-01, completed on 2024-06-05 to refinance earlier second lien notes due April 2026, with a USD 6.7 million loss on extinguishment, to reduce variable-rate exposure and to provide liquidity and growth funding. On the same day the ABL facility was amended to raise its capacity to USD 520.0 million, including a USD 45.0 million Canadian sublimit, and to extend its maturity to 2029-06-01, and the ABL, floor plan and second lien agents signed an intercreditor agreement. The notes are guaranteed by each domestic subsidiary and secured by second-priority liens on substantially all assets, behind the first-priority liens that secure the ABL and the first lien floor plan facility. At September 30, 2026 USD 692.9 million of ABL and note principal falls due in 2029, and the floor plan and ABL facilities together are limited to USD 1,064.5 million.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 92–93, 114; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; calculation C16

Other transactions are small. Alta bought the assets of a Quebec Yale dealer for USD 2.7 million on 2025-03-14 and sold three businesses with sale prices of USD 18.0 million (2025-05-01), USD 6.4 million (2025-08-29) and USD 1.5 million (2026-03-01), allocating each set of proceeds to reduce senior debt. The 2023 purchases of Burris, for USD 16.7 million in cash, and Ault, for USD 35.7 million including USD 5.3 million of Alta stock and a USD 3.2 million seller note, were the last large acquisitions, and net ABL borrowing of USD 91.3 million in FY2023 funded them together with working capital and rental fleet growth.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_altg_altg-20241231.htm.jsonl, pages 61, 103; 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 102–103; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 24; calculation C13; calculation C15

No equity offering, sponsor funding or dividend recapitalization since 2023 is described in the filings. Alta raised its repurchase authorization twice, to USD 20.0 million in October 2024 and to USD 30.0 million in May 2025, and signed board observer and cooperation agreements with Mill Road Capital III on 2026-01-21, whose terms are not supplied. The June 2026 increase in the incentive plan share reserve dilutes equity holders but costs creditors no cash.
