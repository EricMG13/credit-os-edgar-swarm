## Findings

### Bottom line

Creditors rely on a shrinking print business with no identified controlling shareholder, two debt-funded acquisitions, negative book equity and a brand now held in a joint venture whose lenders have first call on it, so structure and priority matter most.

Management's capital allocation has moved toward creditors: common dividends declared fell 81.51% from 2023 to 2025 and are suspended from January 2027, and 2026 repurchases retired about USD 200 million of notes for about USD 101 million. The 2023 buyback of about USD 542 million of shares from one holder group was debt-funded, ownership is not established, and governance risk is medium.

### Key findings

Recurring post sale revenue gives the business cash visibility, but the installed base is shrinking and the 2026 recovery is cost-led. Post sale revenue was 79% of FY2025 revenue and equipment sales 21%, management says the business does not depend on a single customer or a few customers, and about 43% of revenue is earned outside the United States. Committed multi-year service obligations were USD 636 million at 2026-09-30 against USD 644 million at 2025-12-31, which is small beside post sale revenue because most service revenue is invoiced as rendered. On a pro forma basis that treats the acquired businesses as owned throughout, revenue fell 7.6% in 2025 and 5.0% in the first nine months of 2026, while adjusted operating income rose to USD 399 million from USD 243 million, which the issuer attributes to integration synergies and cost discipline. A licensing agreement added 1.1 percentage points to third quarter 2026 revenue, so the decline before it was 5.8% rather than 4.7%.

Xerox Corporation consolidates the entity, and the royalty of 2.0% of specified revenue that the licensees pay it is held in a reserve account for the lenders, with certain subsidiaries of Holdings guaranteeing and securing the licence obligations. The joint venture can end the licence on certain defaults by Holdings, including insolvency or a change of control, after which the licensees keep only a 90-day sell-off period for inventory, so a default would put the use of the brand itself at stake. The term loans cost more than 11% a year even at the reference rate floor and the instruments amortize USD 81 million a year from the fourth quarter of 2026, and 10.37% of principal debt now sits in subsidiaries below Xerox Corporation, almost all of it in this joint venture. Whether the contributed assets had been collateral for the existing first and second lien notes is not established.

Ownership and control are not established, so shareholder willingness cannot be assessed and the medium governance risk rests on issuer behaviour alone. The annual report places beneficial ownership, directors, compensation and related-party items in a proxy statement that is not supplied, no controlling holder or financial sponsor is named, and the holder of the Series A preferred stock is not named. Four of the six executive officers took their present roles in 2025, including a chief financial officer who joined with Lexmark. Reporting is audited with effective internal control, but Lexmark was left out of the 2025 internal control audit, representing 13% of assets and 14% of revenue, and its purchase accounting needed corrections in both the fourth quarter of 2025 and the second quarter of 2026.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious reading is operating and liquidity. Adjusted operating income was USD 399 million in the first nine months of 2026 against USD 243 million on a pro forma basis a year earlier, the adjusted operating margin of 6.6% in the third quarter was 3.3 percentage points higher than a year before, and management states that cash on hand, cash from operations and ABL availability cover at least twelve months of needs without capital markets. The debt-funded buyback is three years old, dividends are being cut and suspended, 2026 repurchases were made at about half of par, and the joint venture raised USD 404 million of net proceeds without issuing common equity.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 42, 60, 95, 112; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 39; calculation C8; calculation C14; calculation C27

The view would turn better if the October 2026 exchange closes on the stated terms, repurchases below par continue, the next annual report names the large holders and an independent board, and the joint venture documents show that existing lenders' collateral did not include the contributed assets. It would turn worse with another transfer of assets into a financing vehicle, more secured debt ahead of the unsecured notes, further purchase accounting corrections, a repeat goodwill impairment, or operating cash flow below the management range of USD 145 million to USD 195 million for 2026, which needs USD 222 million to USD 272 million in the fourth quarter.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 56, 97; 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 57; calculation C31

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Two acquisitions explain most of the change in the structure since 2024. ITsavvy, bought on 2024-11-20, created the IT Solutions segment for USD 404 million, of which USD 194 million was cash and USD 210 million was seller notes due in October 2025 and January 2026. Lexmark, bought from Ninestar Group Company Limited on 2025-07-01, cost USD 768 million at closing including USD 93 million of cash acquired, and USD 749 million after a USD 19 million working capital adjustment finalized in the first quarter of 2026. The 2024 annual report described the pending Lexmark deal as USD 1.5 billion inclusive of net debt and other assumed liabilities, and the bridge from that figure to the equity price is not itemised.
