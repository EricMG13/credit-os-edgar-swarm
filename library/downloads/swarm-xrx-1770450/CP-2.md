## Findings

### Bottom line

Xerox is a weak credit: a shrinking print business carrying total debt of USD 4,220 million with negative reported EBITDA, negative book equity and USD 1,889 million due in 2028 and 2029, so creditors rely on cost savings, receivable sales and refinancing access.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5, 100–101; calculation C1

Liquidity covers the next twelve months on management's account and adjusted earnings are recovering, so a default before 2028 is not the central risk. The central risk is a further fall in operating cash flow that leaves the 2028 and 2029 maturities to be refinanced at coupons above 10%, with a growing share of debt secured ahead of the unsecured notes. Governance risk is medium.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 95, 97; 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 218–219; calculation C18; calculation C9

### Key findings

Business risk is weak because a mostly recurring revenue base is shrinking. Post sale revenue was 79% of FY2025 revenue and committed multi-year service obligations were USD 636 million at 2026-09-30, but on a pro forma basis revenue fell 7.6% in 2025 and 5.0% in the first nine months of 2026, and the IT Solutions line fell 12.1%. Equipment installations rose 9.0% in the third quarter of 2026 and the pro forma decline narrowed from the second quarter, which is a first sign of stabilisation rather than a trend.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 84; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 59, 62, 66

Leverage is high and has fallen only because earnings recovered, not because debt fell. Net debt of USD 3,808 million at 2026-09-30 is USD 3,754 million pro forma for the exchange of 2028 notes expected on 2026-11-13, and pro forma net leverage is not established. Net debt is 4.46x house-adjusted EBITDA of USD 854 million for the last twelve months against 6.42x at FY2025, a fall of 1.96x in which net debt rose USD 73 million and EBITDA rose 46.74%. Reported EBITDA is negative USD 275 million for the same twelve months after the USD 959 million goodwill impairment, the issuer states no EBITDA, and its own target of total debt below 3.0x EBITDA compares with 4.94x on the house measure.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; calculation C7; calculation C19

Interest absorbs a growing share of earnings, so coverage is thin and falling. Adjusted operating income plus depreciation covered non-financing interest expense 2.18x over the last twelve months against 4.23x in FY2024, because non-financing interest expense rose 64.88% in the first nine months of 2026 and total interest expense rose 41.45%, on higher rates on the Lexmark borrowings and the brand financing, and the 2025 secured issues carry coupons of 10.25% and 13.5%. The 13.00% notes due 2030 step up toward 16.75% by July 2030, and reported EBITDA covered FY2025 cash interest only 0.65x; cash interest is not printed for the 2026 interim periods.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 96, 219–220, 227; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 43; calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C18; calculation C14

Cash generation is the weakest dimension. Net cash used in operating activities was USD 77 million in the first nine months of 2026, free cash flow for the last twelve months was USD 37 million, or 4.33% of house-adjusted EBITDA against 76.68% in FY2024, and management cut its 2026 operating cash flow guidance to USD 145 million to USD 195 million from about USD 340 million on lower profitability, lower proceeds from sales of finance receivables and lower collections of tariff refunds. The range needs USD 222 million to USD 272 million in the fourth quarter against USD 208 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, and a repeat of last year's quarter would leave the year at USD 131 million, below the range.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 97; 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 71; calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C6

Liquidity is adequate to the end of 2027 but the refinancing wall behind it is large. Cash of USD 412 million and net ABL availability of about USD 225 million cover the USD 217 million of principal due to the end of 2027 by 2.94x, and management says liquidity is sufficient for at least twelve months, but principal due is USD 752 million in 2028, USD 1,137 million in 2029 and USD 1,042 million in 2030, USD 2,931 million in all, and the ABL matures in May 2028. Pro forma cash for the exchange is not established, because accrued interest on the exchanged notes is paid in cash and not stated. Management expects to address the maturities through cash from operations, refinancings and liability management whose timing and terms are uncertain.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 42, 56, 95, 100–101; calculation C1; calculation C12

Creditors are increasingly ranked by security and by entity, which raises class-specific risk for unsecured holders. About 47.15% of principal debt is now secured, USD 451 million sits in subsidiaries below Xerox Corporation, USD 450 million of it the brand financing, and the exchange would add about USD 120 million of first lien notes, while management states that further transactions may be pari passu with or effectively senior to existing debt. The company's own estimate puts the fair value of Holdings long-term debt at 49.10% of carrying amount against 76.02% for Xerox Corporation, which is an estimate and not a traded price.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 46–47, 56, 98–99, 101; 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 216–217; calculation C9; calculation C8

Financial policy is moving toward creditors but equity no longer protects them, and governance risk is medium. Dividends on common and preferred stock are suspended from January 2027, USD 200 million of notes were bought back at 50.50% of principal and capital expenditure is 1.23% of revenue, yet Holdings reports a shareholders' deficit of USD 683 million and ownership, board and related-party disclosure sits in a proxy statement that is not supplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 40, 56; calculation C11; calculation C17

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a weak reading is that earnings have turned. Adjusted operating income was USD 399 million in the first nine months of 2026 against USD 243 million on a pro forma basis a year earlier, house-adjusted EBITDA rose to USD 854 million for the last twelve months from USD 582 million in FY2025, the issuer targets more than USD 300 million of synergies by the end of 2027 and cumulative run-rate gross savings already exceed USD 500 million. The company has also retired debt at about half of par, raised USD 404 million of net proceeds from the brand financing without issuing common equity, and suspended dividends, so it has shown that it can create liquidity when it needs to.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 6–7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 39, 40, 112; calculation C20

The view would improve if the fourth quarter delivers the USD 222 million to USD 272 million of operating cash flow that the guidance implies, the exchange closes on the stated terms, the 2028 maturities are refinanced before they become current, and the next proxy statement shows a settled ownership and board. It would worsen with fourth quarter operating cash flow below USD 208 million, further asset-backed or secured financings or exchanges that move claims ahead of the unsecured notes, ABL borrowings rising above USD 75 million, or another goodwill impairment.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 42, 56, 97; calculation C5

### Company description

Xerox sells print equipment, supplies, managed print and digital services and lease financing through Print and Other, which was 89% of FY2025 revenue, and integrated IT infrastructure services through IT Solutions, which was 11%. The business earned revenue of USD 7,022 million in FY2025 with a workforce of about twenty-three thousand, 43.25% of revenue outside the United States, and no single customer or small group of customers on which management says it depends. Lexmark, bought on 2025-07-01, added USD 962 million of 2025 revenue, and ITsavvy, bought in 2024, created the IT Solutions segment.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 12, 14, 84, 189, 192

The model supports cash flow visibility through the recurring post sale base and committed multi-year obligations of USD 636 million, but the base is tied to an installed base that is shrinking and the finance arm needs USD 1,296 million of allocated debt and regular receivable sales, so the model supports debt service less than its recurring revenue suggests and makes refinancing confidence depend on cost savings rather than growth.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 100; calculation C4

### Ownership and group structure

Xerox Holdings is a listed parent with no financial sponsor or controlling holder named, and Xerox Corporation, wholly owned, holds nearly all operations. The parent carries USD 1,698 million of principal debt guaranteed by Xerox Corporation, Xerox Corporation carries USD 2,199 million, much of it secured, and subsidiaries below it carry USD 451 million, USD 450 million of it the brand financing, so unsecured parent debt is structurally behind secured and subsidiary debt except through the guarantees.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 99, 101; 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 124

Financial policy has been mixed: a debt-funded repurchase in 2023 was followed by dividend cuts in 2025, a suspension from January 2027 and discounted repurchases of notes in 2026, while a brand financing, a warrant distribution and a pending exchange show a willingness to restructure claims. Governance risk is medium because behaviour is supportive in cash terms but ownership and board control are not established.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 40, 56

### Revenue drivers and visibility

Revenue is recurring in form but declining in volume. Post sale revenue, which includes supplies, service, rentals, financing and IT products, was 79% of FY2025 revenue, committed multi-year obligations were USD 636 million, and no single customer is said to matter, yet pro forma revenue fell 7.6% in 2025 and 5.0% in nine months 2026 on lower equipment service, managed print, supplies and rental revenue, and renewal rates are not disclosed. IT Solutions fell 18.9% in the third quarter on the timing of large public sector placements, so it adds diversification but not yet stability. Pricing actions have offset part of tariff costs, but supplier cost increases may not be passed on to customers.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 28, 84; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 59, 62, 66

The credit implication is that revenue visibility supports debt service for the next one to five years of committed contracts but not the 2028 to 2030 refinancing window, because lower page volumes, reduced print usage and customer non-renewal are the mechanisms through which cash flow before interest shrinks.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 85, 90; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 59

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins are being rebuilt by cost action rather than pricing power. Total gross margin was 27.1% in FY2025 against 31.5% in FY2024, adjusted operating margin was 3.5% in FY2025 and 6.6% in the third quarter of 2026, and run-rate gross savings above USD 500 million were achieved through 2025 with more than USD 300 million of synergies targeted by the end of 2027. Fixed commitments limit flexibility, since outsourced shared services and technology commitments total USD 1,000 million, and purchases from FUJIFILM Business Innovation, a supplier that is also a listed competitor, were 11.55% of revenue, with memory price increases and tariffs adding input cost.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 6–7, 89–90, 129–130; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 59–60; calculation C16

Margins can be defended against modest volume pressure through further cost action, but operating leverage is negative and a further 5.0% revenue fall at the last twelve months' margins would remove about USD 118 million of gross profit, 23.55% of last twelve months' adjusted operating income, which is an arithmetic illustration and not a forecast.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C10

### Free cash flow conversion and capital intensity

Capital intensity is low, at 1.30% of revenue in FY2025, so capital expenditure offers little further room to cut. Free cash flow fell from USD 467 million in FY2024 to USD 133 million in FY2025 and USD 37 million for the last twelve months because operating cash flow fell with lower receivable sales, restructuring payments, pension contributions of USD 118 million in nine months 2026 and cash interest of USD 272 million in FY2025.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 227; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 47; calculation C11; calculation C4

EBITDA therefore does not translate into durable free cash flow today: conversion fell from 76.68% to 4.33% of house-adjusted EBITDA, which leaves little for debt reduction except asset-backed funding, so the maturity wall depends on earnings growth and market access.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C4

### Porter five forces, credit-translated

Rivalry is intense: Brother, Canon, FUJIFILM Business Innovation, HP, Konica Minolta, Kyocera and Ricoh compete in print on price, technology and service, and no market share data are supplied, so price pressure and equipment placement losses reduce post sale revenue and the cash that supports debt. The filings do not discuss new entrants, so that force is not assessed. Substitutes matter because the annual report lists customers selecting alternative technologies and reduced print usage among the factors that can end or shrink contracts, and lower page volumes are cited in the revenue decline.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 27, 85, 90; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 59

Buyers include commercial clients and government entities whose agreements are generally renewable at the customer's option or cancellable, so buyer power limits pricing, and supplier power is high because FUJIFILM Business Innovation, a competitor, also supplies products, parts and supplies and purchases from it were USD 811 million in 2025, which exposes gross margin to supplier terms.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 26–27, 31, 129; calculation C16

Policy and macro factors are material to credit. Social and technological factors matter because the annual report lists reduced print usage and customers selecting alternative technologies among the reasons contracts end or shrink, and lower page volumes are cited in the revenue decline.

### SWOT, credit-translated

Strengths are a recurring post sale base, a diversified customer base, low capital intensity and a cost programme that has lifted adjusted operating income. Weaknesses are a shrinking installed base, thin interest coverage, weak cash generation and negative equity. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are synergy delivery above USD 300 million, growth in IT and digital services, and discounted debt retirement. Threats that could weaken it are the 2028 to 2030 maturities, further priming of unsecured claims, supplier and tariff costs, and further goodwill impairments.

### Qualitative downside scenario

The realistic downside, an analyst scenario and not a management projection, begins with continued mid single digit revenue decline, a fall in sales of finance receivables and slower cost savings. Revenue falling another 5.0% at current margins would remove about USD 118 million of gross profit, which is 23.55% of adjusted operating income, and a fourth quarter that repeats last year would leave 2026 operating cash flow at USD 131 million, below management's range. Free cash flow would stay near zero after restructuring, pension and interest payments, so cash and the ABL would be drawn, and the borrowing base would fall if receivables and inventory fell.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_xrx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 224; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 42, 97; calculation C10; calculation C5

The first operational break would be the 2027 and 2028 cash needs rather than a payment default: principal due is USD 217 million to the end of 2027, then USD 752 million in 2028, with the ABL maturing in May 2028. Management levers are further asset sales, more asset-backed financing and liability management, each of which would move claims ahead of unsecured holders, while capital expenditure and dividends are already low. Resilience looks sufficient to avoid liquidity distress through 2027 on management's statement but not sufficient to refinance 2028 and 2029 without further priming; the observable triggers are fourth quarter operating cash flow, ABL borrowings, the closing of the exchange and any new secured issue.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_xrx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 42, 95–98, 100–101; calculation C1

### Overall credit view

Overall, Xerox presents a weak business profile supported by a recurring revenue base, a cost programme and liquidity to the end of 2027, but credit quality is constrained by high leverage, thin interest cover, weak cash generation and negative equity. Cash-flow durability appears weak because operating cash flow turned negative and guidance was cut.
