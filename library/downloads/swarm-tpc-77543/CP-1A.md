## Findings

### Bottom line

Tutor Perini is a public-company contractor whose creditors depend on project execution and public customers, not on a sponsor, and the filings document no creditor-adverse conduct, though the debt documents that would show what owners may take out are not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tpc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tpc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 69

Public agencies were 74.69% of FY2025 revenue and fixed-price work is 79% of backlog, so margins turn on execution and on settling change orders. One holder, the executive chairman with three trusts, owns about 12% and may nominate a director; distributions began in late 2025 and were 6.20% of nine-month 2026 operating cash flow, and the 2026 refinancing cut interest cost.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tpc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 27, 85; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tpc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 15; calculation C3; calculation C8

### Key findings

- Revenue visibility is strong but rests on a few large public projects. Backlog of USD 20,353.3 million at 2026-09-30 is about 49% Civil, 35% Building and 16% Specialty Contractors, public agencies made up 86% of backlog at the end of 2025, and a single customer was 10.8% of nine-month 2026 revenue, so a funding delay or a dispute on one project moves earnings. Backlog is down 5.77% on a year earlier, so the cushion is shrinking rather than growing.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tpc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 44, 49–50; 10-K_2025-12-31_tpc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11; calculation C6; calculation C7; calculation C11
- Margins depend on fixed-price execution and on collecting claims. Fixed-price work is 69.39% of FY2025 revenue and 79% of backlog, customers may terminate government contracts for convenience, and unapproved change orders and claims sit in costs and estimated earnings in excess of billings of USD 767,432 thousand, so a lost claim becomes a cash and earnings shock rather than a delay.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tpc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 13, 87; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tpc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 9; calculation C5
- Customer advances and bonding are the funding model, not debt. Billings in excess of costs of USD 2,044,207 thousand fund projects ahead of spending, and surety bonds are needed to win work, so a slowdown in awards or a loss of bonding capacity would hit liquidity before it hits leverage.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tpc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-K_2025-12-31_tpc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 26
- Control is a listed-company board with one influential holder, not a sponsor. The Tutor Group holds about 12% and may nominate one director while it holds at least 11.25%, ten directors signed the annual report, and no fund, fund vintage or exit horizon is described to infer pressure from, so alignment has to be judged from conduct rather than identity.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tpc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 65
- Financial policy has moved from covenant relief to returns, and the evidence so far is mixed rather than adverse. Two covenant amendments were needed in 2022 and 2023, the term loan was repaid early in 2025, dividends began in December 2025, USD 30,000 thousand of shares were repurchased in nine months of 2026 with USD 170 million of authorisation left, and the 2026 refinancing cost a USD 35.6 million premium that the issuer-stated USD 21.0 million annual saving repays in 1.70 years.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tpc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 26, 52; 10-Q_2026-06-30_tpc-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 67; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tpc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 15, 62, 67; calculation C8; calculation C10; calculation C12
- The Philadelphia hotel dispute is the one live item that could change the picture. The court awarded the developer approximately USD 175 million, the company has appealed, and management describes a material adverse impact as neither probable nor remote.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tpc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25–26

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a moderate governance risk reading is the covenant record and the dispute record: the first lien leverage covenant needed two amendments within five months in 2022 and 2023, FY2024 ended in a loss with legacy-dispute charges, and a court awarded approximately USD 175 million, equal to 34.71% of the USD 504,218 thousand of general-purpose cash. The strongest evidence for the opposite, a lower-risk reading, is that distributions were only 6.20% of nine-month 2026 operating cash flow and that the 2026 refinancing lowered interest cost and extended maturity without any documented priming.
The view would worsen if the appeal fails and the award is paid from general-purpose cash, if the indenture or credit agreement prove to allow large restricted payments, or if repurchases accelerate while backlog keeps falling. It would improve if the documents show tight restricted-payment limits and the appeal reduces the award.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tpc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tpc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 10, 25–26; calculation C7; calculation C8; calculation C13

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The only new debt issue in the period is the refinancing completed on 2026-07-02. The company issued USD 400.0 million of senior unsecured notes due 2033, used the proceeds with cash to redeem in full the USD 400.0 million of senior notes due 2029 at 108.906% of principal, and amended and restated its credit agreement so that the revolver grew from USD 170.0 million to USD 350.0 million with maturity extended to 2031. The old first lien net leverage covenant was replaced by a maximum total net leverage ratio and a minimum interest coverage ratio that apply only to the revolver.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tpc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 15–17

The refinancing cost a loss on extinguishment of USD 51.4 million, of which USD 35.6 million was cash premium and USD 15.8 million was non-cash write-off of unamortised costs, and the company states annualised cash interest savings of USD 21.0 million, so the premium is repaid in 1.70 years if the saving holds. The new notes carry the same subsidiary guarantees as the credit agreement and the revolver stays secured by substantially all assets; the filings describe no change in lien priority and no priming or non-pro-rata step.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tpc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 15–18, 62; calculation C10

Other capital events in the period are not new debt: the remaining term loan was repaid early in the first quarter of 2025, the defined benefit pension plan was settled in the third quarter of 2026 with a USD 33.8 million non-cash charge, and no acquisition, disposal or sponsor transaction is described in any of the five documents.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tpc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 62
