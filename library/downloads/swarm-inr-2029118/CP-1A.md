## Findings

### Bottom line

Business risk is moderate and governance risk is medium: Infinity is a larger Appalachian producer after the Antero purchase, but four sponsors held 68.8% of the voting power at 2026-03-05 and the documents showing creditor protection were not supplied, so protection is unverified. Operating evidence is solid, with nine-month 2026 net production up 88%, so the creditor risk lies in structure rather than operations.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 38–39, 55–56; 10-K_2025-12-31_inr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 78; Source readiness (CP-0)

The notes are issued by INR Holdings, of which the listed parent owns 31.8%, and USD 350.0 million of 8.0% cumulative preferred stock now ranks ahead of the common equity at the parent. The filings supplied document no sponsor extraction, but material weaknesses in internal control are not reported as remediated.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27–28, 37–39, 72; 10-K_2025-12-31_inr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 191–192

### Key findings

Operating scale has stepped up, and revenue leans toward oil while reserves lean toward gas. Net production for the nine months of 2026 rose 88%, and oil supplied 48.46% of revenue against 33.91% for natural gas and 16.23% for NGLs, yet proved reserves at 2025-12-31 were 67.90% natural gas by energy content. The reserve value behind the borrowing base is therefore likely to be more sensitive to gas prices than current cash flow is, which follows oil prices; the filings do not split reserve value by product, so this is an interpretation and not a measurement.
Basis: calculation calc-mix-9m26; calculation calc-reserve-mix; 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 55–56; 10-K_2025-12-31_inr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18

Customer concentration is high, although the products are fungible. Three purchasers took 84% of net revenue in FY2025 (BP America 35%, Marathon Oil 33% and Ergon 16%) and owed 95% of oil and gas sales receivables at year-end, and the 2026 reports say three customers exceeded 10% of revenue without naming them. Management states that losing any one purchaser would not materially affect results because crude oil and natural gas have established markets; that is management's view, and the filings give no contract terms to test it.
Basis: calculation calc-customers; 10-K_2025-12-31_inr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 24–25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 71

The Antero acquisition is the largest change to the credit, and reported figures show only part of its effect. Infinity paid USD 681.7 million for a 60% undivided interest in upstream and midstream assets in Ohio, funded by USD 334.0 million of net preferred proceeds and revolver borrowings that the notes later refinanced, and the assets added USD 72.0 million of revenue from 2026-02-23. Assuming ownership from 2025-01-01, nine-month revenue grew 65.41% rather than the 118.69% reported, so reported growth overstates the underlying rate. The filings give no pro forma EBITDA, no reserves net to Infinity for the acquired assets and no itemised bridge from the USD 720.0 million that 60% of the headline price implies to the USD 681.7 million paid.
Basis: calculation calc-antero; calculation calc-pf-growth; 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21–22, 48, 61; 10-Q_2026-03-31_inr-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-12-31_inr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 183

Creditors face a holding-company structure with a preferred claim above the common equity. INR Holdings is the borrower and notes issuer, the listed parent owns 31.8% of it and the Legacy Owners hold 68.2% as exchangeable units. The notes are senior unsecured, rank equally with the Credit Facility in right of payment, sit effectively behind its first-priority liens on substantially all assets and structurally behind any future subsidiary that does not guarantee them; the filings do not name the current guarantors or say that the parent guarantees either debt. The USD 350.0 million Series A preferred stock sits at the parent and carries an 8.0% cumulative dividend, about USD 28.0 million a year, which the parent can pay only from distributions that the credit agreement restricts and that a June 2026 amendment widened.
Basis: calculation calc-fixed; 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26–28, 37–39; 10-K_2025-12-31_inr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 77–78, 84

Control is concentrated with four financial sponsors, so any change of control is a decision for a few holders. Pearl, NGP, Quantum and Carnelian held 68.8% of the voting power at 2026-03-05 (36.2%, 12.1%, 16.1% and 4.4%), Pearl may nominate four directors, Carnelian appoints one and NGP's right to nominate one is unused; the filings describe them as acting separately. Series A holders also hold consent rights over dividends, over debt above a threshold the filings do not state and over deviations from set hedging requirements. Three change-of-control terms stack: the notes carry a 101% repurchase right, the preferred holders can require repurchase at the greater of a 13.0% annual return and 1.30x the initial USD 350.0 million, and the tax receivable agreement accelerates, with payments to the Legacy Owners estimated at approximately USD 134.3 million at 2025-02-03.

Documented behaviour to date adds to the equity base rather than removing cash, with three items to watch. INR Holdings raised USD 222.3 million of new Class B equity for the 2023 Ohio Utica purchase from an owner group that was 98.1% Pearl and NGP affiliates at the 2024 year-end and paid no distributions to members in 2023 or 2024, the IPO's USD 286.5 million of net proceeds repaid USD 285.0 million of revolver debt, Quantum and Carnelian put USD 350.0 million of new capital into the preferred stock in 2026 and the Chase acquisition was paid in shares. The items to watch are the Series A dividend moving from payment in kind to cash from the second quarter (USD 14.1 million paid for two quarters), the June 2026 widening of restricted-payment capacity, and a USD 75.0 million repurchase program with USD 72.3 million unused at 2026-06-30. No dividend on the Class A common stock and no sponsor fee is reported in the filings supplied, but the FY2025 related-party disclosure sits in a proxy statement that was not supplied.

Reporting quality is the weakest governance evidence and limits how far any figure can be relied on. Management concluded that disclosure controls were not effective at each reporting date from 2024-12-31 to 2026-09-30 because five material weaknesses (segregation of duties, risk assessment, control activities, formal accounting policies and accounting resources) are not reported as remediated, and the auditor need not attest to internal control until the company ceases to be an emerging growth company, which could be as late as the FY2030 annual report. The chief financial officer who signed the first two 2026 quarterly reports entered a transition and severance agreement dated 2026-08-09 and a different officer signed the third quarterly report; the filings give no terms and no successor details beyond the signature.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a medium rating is the record so far: new equity was raised in 2023 and no distributions were paid to members in 2023 or 2024, the IPO proceeds repaid revolver debt, the 2026 acquisitions were funded with new preferred capital, shares and notes that refinanced the revolver, no Class A dividend is reported, and the revolver was undrawn at 2026-09-30 with USD 875.0 million of capacity. Sponsor board rights also fall with ownership: Pearl's voting power was 36.2% at 2026-03-05 against 47.5% of Class A and Class B votes at 2025-03-21, although the two measures use different bases. The view would improve to low if the financing documents confirmed tight restricted-payment and debt limits with full guarantee coverage, the weaknesses in internal control were reported as remediated and the FY2025 proxy statement showed a majority of independent directors. It would worsen to high if the widened restricted-payment capacity were used for a common dividend, a sponsor fee or buybacks while borrowings rose, if the 2.0% a year step-up on the Series A dividend applied because the credit agreement blocked cash payment, or if a change of control triggered the preferred repurchase and the tax receivable acceleration together.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_inr-20241231.htm.jsonl, pages 177, 211; 10-K_2025-12-31_inr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 78, 114; 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 37

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The Antero acquisition closed on 2026-02-23. INR Holdings and Northern Oil and Gas bought upstream assets for about USD 800 million and midstream assets for about USD 400 million at the 100% level, Infinity taking an undivided 60% interest and Northern 40%. Infinity's aggregate cash consideration was USD 681.7 million after the post-closing adjustment process completed on 2026-08-07, down from a preliminary USD 683.9 million; of that, USD 61.2 million was an escrow deposit paid in 2025 and USD 620.5 million was paid in the nine months of 2026. It was funded with cash on hand, the preferred stock proceeds and Credit Facility borrowings. The purchase was accounted for as an asset acquisition, transaction costs reached USD 16.9 million by 2026-09-30, and Infinity operates substantially all of the assets under joint development and cooperation agreements with Northern.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_inr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 183; 10-Q_2026-03-31_inr-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21–22, 59

The USD 350.0 million Series A convertible preferred stock was sold on 2026-02-23 to affiliates of Quantum (275,000 shares) and Carnelian (75,000 shares) under an agreement signed on 2026-02-18. It carries a cumulative dividend of 8.0% a year for five years and 12.0% after, convertible at USD 21.39 a share, with a Company right to force conversion after three years if the share price holds above 140% of that price and a Company redemption option from the fifth anniversary at a 15.0% annual return. The first quarter's dividend was paid in kind and the second and third quarters in cash. The preferred stock is shown as temporary equity because holders can require repurchase on a change of control; the filings give two net proceeds figures, about USD 337.1 million and about USD 334.0 million.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_inr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 82; 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 37–38, 48, 61

INR Holdings issued USD 550.0 million of senior notes due 2031 at par on 2026-03-20, guaranteed on a senior unsecured basis by certain subsidiaries, with interest payable each April 1 and October 1 from 2026-10-01 and maturity on 2031-04-01. The notes can be redeemed from 2028-04-01, or earlier with a make-whole premium, and up to 40% can be redeemed with equity proceeds before then; holders can require repurchase at 101% on certain changes of control. The covenants limit debt, restricted payments, asset sales, investments, liens, affiliate transactions and unrestricted subsidiaries. Net proceeds of about USD 537.6 million repaid Credit Facility borrowings, and the coupon and redemption prices are in the transaction register.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27–29, 60

The Credit Facility, led by Citibank, had its commitments and borrowing base raised from USD 375.0 million to USD 875.0 million by the Fourth Amendment on 2026-02-23 and no borrowings were outstanding at 2026-09-30. It is guaranteed by subsidiaries of INR Holdings and secured by first-priority liens on substantially all consolidated assets, with financial tests of a current ratio of at least 1.0x and a total net leverage ratio of no more than 3.0x. The Fifth Amendment on 2026-06-22 expanded the circumstances in which certain restricted payments may be made; the filings do not say which. The facility's maturity is stated only in the 2025 annual report and not after the 2026 amendments.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26–27; 10-Q_2026-06-30_inr-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-K_2025-12-31_inr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 119, 164

The Chase acquisition closed on 2026-01-20 for 2,517,194 Class A shares recorded at USD 35.1 million in equity, adding non-operated Pennsylvania interests in the South Bend Field. The IPO on 2025-02-03 sold 15,237,500 Class A shares at USD 20.00 and its net proceeds repaid USD 285.0 million of Credit Facility borrowings.
