## Findings

### Bottom line

Governance risk is medium for PENN creditors: no controlling holder is disclosed and no liability management is documented, but $992.5 million of trailing cash rent, a $750.0 million buyback authorisation and an activist settlement running to 2028 leave creditors reliant on board restraint.
Basis: calculation C8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_penn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-K_2025-12-31_penn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 29–30; 10-K_2025-12-31_penn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 64–65

Retail casinos produced 81.32% of 2025 revenue and their segment earnings covered trailing cash rent only 1.96x, while Interactive still lost $38.7 million of Adjusted EBITDAR in nine months of 2026. Projects financed by landlord sale-leasebacks carry rent yields of 7.73% to 7.80%, so growth capital becomes fixed rent rather than debt.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C9; calculation C10; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_pennex991-q32026.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4

### Key findings

- The business is a retail casino portfolio that depends on landlords, and that dependence is the largest creditor exposure. The four retail segments produced 81.32% of 2025 revenue and 80.62% of nine-month 2026 revenue, most of their real estate is leased from two REIT landlords, and the credit facilities carry events of default that cross-refer to the master leases. Segment earnings before rent covered trailing cash rent of $992.5 million only 1.96x, so 50.98% of those earnings is committed to landlords before interest, tax or capital spending.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C8; calculation C9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_penn-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20–22; 10-K_2025-12-31_penn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 7
- Ownership is dispersed and the live governance risk is shareholder activism, not a sponsor. An activist contested the 2025 board election, and in February 2026 the company settled with a cooperation agreement that added three directors, bound the activist to a standstill and non-disparagement until the run-up to the 2028 meeting, and ended the lawsuit with prejudice. The contest cost $22.4 million of legal and advisory fees in 2025, and the activist’s holding and the board’s independence are not stated, so creditors cannot judge how much influence over capital allocation the settlement leaves with it.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_penn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 29–30; 10-K_2025-12-31_penn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 176; 10-K_2025-12-31_penn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 200
- Shareholder returns are the behaviour to monitor: the company bought back 13.20% of its opening share count in 2025, in a year it also drew a net $570.0 million on its revolver. The documents do not tie the buybacks to that borrowing, no shares were repurchased in the first nine months of 2026, no cash dividend has been paid since the 1994 listing, and a new $750.0 million authorisation runs to the end of 2028. The credit agreement’s restricted payment capacity is not supplied, so creditors can see willingness to return cash but not the limit on it.
Basis: calculation C13; 10-K_2025-12-31_penn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 89; 10-K_2025-12-31_penn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 64–65; 10-Q_2026-09-30_penn-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 31–32

The segment’s Adjusted EBITDAR loss narrowed by $188.9 million to $38.7 million over nine months, mainly because media and advertising spend fell $133.9 million to $101.1 million after the ESPN arrangement, which carried $212.7 million of advertising cost in 2025, ended on 1 December 2025. Reported Interactive revenue grew 16.83%, but gaming-tax reimbursement from market-access partners explains all of it: excluding the reimbursement, revenue fell 1.00% to $493.3 million. Group earnings therefore still rest on the retail segments, and Interactive still loses money at the segment earnings line.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a lower risk reading is that the documents show no dividend, priming, exchange or other liability management: PENN has paid no cash dividend since its 1994 listing, repurchased no shares in the first nine months of 2026, repaid its convertible notes in cash and extended all three bank facilities without a priming or exchange step. The retail segments also improved, with segment margin rising from 33.25% to 33.76% over the nine months, and the Interactive loss is shrinking. The reading would move to low if the proxy statement showed a board majority independent of any single holder, if the credit agreement’s restricted payment limits were tight and if the company published a capital return policy tied to leverage. It would move to high if repurchases resumed under the $750.0 million authorisation while retail earnings cover of cash rent stayed near 2.00x, if development beyond the three announced projects were funded by further sale-leasebacks, if the activist standstill lapsed into a new contest, or if Interactive revenue excluding tax reimbursement kept falling while losses persisted.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

PENN completed four financing steps in 2026. On March 16 it issued $600.0 million of 6.75% senior unsecured notes due April 2031 at par, callable from April 2028, and used the proceeds to repay revolver borrowings; the issue cost $10.3 million. On April 16 the Third Amendment extended the $1,000.0 million revolver and the $446.9 million Term Loan A to April 2031 at margins of 1.50% to 2.25% over Term SOFR and removed the credit spread adjustment. In May the remaining $106.7 million of 2.75% convertible notes was repaid in cash. On May 28 the Fourth Amendment extended the $962.5 million Term Loan B to May 2033 and cut its margin by 50 bp to 2.00%. The amendments cost $16.3 million of issuance costs and a $2.4 million discount.
