## Findings

### Bottom line

NCL Corporation has an adequate business profile and stretched finances: leverage is 5.51x adjusted EBITDA and not falling, free cash flow is negative while newbuilds are delivered and yields are falling, so creditors depend on refinancing access and export-credit funding rather than deleveraging.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation L1; calculation F1; calculation Y1

Total debt was 5.51x last-twelve-months adjusted EBITDA at 30 September 2026, up from 5.34x at 31 December 2025, and free cash flow was a deficit of USD 560.7 million; pro forma leverage after the 15 October 2026 notes issue is not established. Liquidity of USD 1,350.7 million about equals the USD 1,344.3 million of principal due by the end of 2027, and USD 4,134.3 million falls due in 2030. The primary downside is further yield erosion, which at the parent's stated sensitivity would lift leverage to 6.42x if Net Yield fell a further 5%.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is that the business is beating its own guidance and funding is committed. The parent reports third-quarter adjusted EBITDA of USD 937 million against guidance of USD 874 million, expects 2027 Net Yield growth, and has announced savings of USD 125 million plus about USD 100 million twice; the issuer's own unit cost excluding fuel fell 0.66% while yield fell, committed undrawn export-credit facilities of about USD 10.8 billion fund about 80% of effective ship contracts, and the October 2026 financing raised revolver commitments to about USD 2.8 billion. If Net Yield stabilises, savings reach the margin and last-twelve-months leverage falls below the FY2025 level of 5.34x, the profile would move from stretched to adequate. If instead the annual report's revolver trigger survived the October amendment, Net Yield falls further than guided, or leverage rises above 5.51x without refinancing of the 2030 maturities, the view would weaken.

Revenue is passenger ticket revenue, 68.0% of FY2025 revenue of USD 9,827.6 million, plus onboard and other revenue, 32.0%, and guests prepay, so bookings give visibility but also create refundable obligations. ### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a further fall in Caribbean pricing as new capacity arrives and the Middle East conflict keeps Europe and Asia-Pacific demand weak. The first operational break is the booking curve, which management already describes as below its optimal range, so ships sail full at lower prices; at the parent's stated sensitivity a further 5% fall in Net Yield removes about USD 380 million of adjusted EBITDA, taking last-twelve-months adjusted EBITDA to USD 2,320.1 million, leverage to 6.42x, interest cover to 3.08x and free cash flow to a deficit of USD 940.7 million. The financing consequence is that liquidity of USD 1,350.7 million, about equal to principal due through 2027, would be drawn to fund the gap while export-credit financing still covers about 80% of ship contracts, and the 2030 maturities of USD 4,134.3 million would have to be refinanced at leverage of 6.42x. Management levers are the savings programmes, the sale or charter of older ships and the reset of delivery dates; the parent has contributed equity before but is not committed to do so. The observable triggers are adjusted EBITDA below guidance, a fourth-quarter Net Yield decline worse than the guided 6.5%, and any sign that the contingent revolver maturity applies. Management states that liquidity is sufficient for at least the next 12 months, and in the stress case resilience rests on export-credit draws and the revolver rather than on internally generated cash, so it is enough to avoid distress but not to deleverage.

### Overall credit view

Overall, NCL Corporation presents an adequate business profile supported by scale across three brands, prepaid bookings and demonstrated market access, but credit quality is constrained by leverage of 5.51x adjusted EBITDA, negative free cash flow and falling yields. Cash-flow durability appears weak to adequate because earnings are price-sensitive while ship payments are fixed. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on stabilising Net Yield, the unverified revolver terms and the 2030 maturities. The primary downside path is yield erosion on growing capacity that raises leverage and widens the funding gap before 2030. Further analysis would require the credit agreement and amendment, an issuer-level pro forma position after October 2026, interim cash interest and peer or market evidence. Top risk: yield erosion.
