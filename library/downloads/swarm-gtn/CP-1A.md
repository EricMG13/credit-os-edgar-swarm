## Findings

### Bottom line

Governance risk is medium: control sits with ten-vote Class A shares and an insider family group, not a financial sponsor, and recent policy pairs maturity extension and debt repurchases with a large acquisition programme, a debt-funded preferred repurchase and continued dividends. The holders of the votes and their percentages are not established, so creditors cannot yet test how far insider restraint protects them.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, pages 37-38, 45; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 15, 18; calculation A1

The business is cyclical: revenue splits almost evenly between advertising and retransmission fees, and political advertising fell from USD 497 million in 2024 to USD 42 million in 2025 while adjusted EBITDA fell 42.3%, so debt service is easiest in election years.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, pages 49, 89; EX-99.1_2026-02-26_ex_924265.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation A3; calculation A7

### Key findings

- Voting control sits with Class A holders whose identity is not established. Each Class A share carries ten votes, so the 9.58% of shares outstanding that are Class A carried 51.45% of all votes on 2026-07-31, and the filing says an insider family group can exert significant influence, with the Executive Chairman and chief executive married to a director. Creditors therefore face shareholder decisions on mergers, acquisitions and distributions that the Class A holders can carry if they vote together, and the sources give no holding percentage or board independence to test who those holders are.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, pages 37-38, 44-45; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation A1
- Financial policy is mixed rather than extractive. Gray repurchased USD 120 million of its own notes at par in July 2026, authorised up to USD 250 million more through 2027 and, in 2025, refinanced so that no principal falls due before December 2028; against that, it paid USD 85 million of dividends in 2025, equal to 47.0% of operating cash flow after capital expenditure, and cash dividends continued in 2026.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 25-26; EX-99.1_2026-05-07_ex_956963.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, pages 54, 75; calculation A12; calculation A16
- The June 2026 preferred repurchase is the clearest case of senior secured debt paying a junior claim in cash. Gray raised USD 70 million of additional first lien notes and used USD 30 million to retire preferred stock with a USD 50 million liquidation value, a price of 60% of face; annual cash dividends fall by USD 4 million and the new interest is smaller, but first lien debt rose while a junior claim was paid, and the other USD 40 million went to buy stations.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 18; EX-99.1_2026-08-07_ex_998938.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation A15; calculation A17
- The acquisition programme adds scale, but its credit return cannot yet be measured. Closings in the first half of 2026 cost USD 280 million, with USD 54 million more agreed, and were paid from cash on hand; the 2026 acquisitions added USD 41 million of second-quarter revenue, but purchased earnings, purchase multiples and synergies are not disclosed, and the remaining station closings need regulatory approval.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for lower risk is what the sources do not show: no financial sponsor, no covenant waiver or forbearance, no exchange offer or priming transaction, no fees paid to insiders and no related-party leakage appear in them, and the 2025 refinancing removed near-term maturities while the repurchases at par and the USD 250 million authorisation reduce debt. The auditor's unqualified opinions and quarterly covenant reporting add to that. These absences are not proof, because the proxy statement, the credit agreement and the indentures were not supplied. The view would move to low if the proxy statement showed a limited insider stake and a majority of independent directors and the indentures showed tight restricted-payment and unrestricted-subsidiary limits. It would move to high if the Class A votes proved to sit with one insider group and distributions, preferred repurchases or par buybacks of selected notes continued while the first lien test was tightening, or if a later filing disclosed an uptier, drop-down or non-pro-rata exchange.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, pages 37-38, 68-70; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, pages 25-26, 35; calculation A18

### Limitations

- The proxy statement is not supplied, so Class A ownership, family holdings, board independence and related-party dealings are not established; any of them could move the governance risk level by itself and the view on who can force a shareholder decision.
- The executed credit agreement and indentures are not supplied, so capacity for restricted payments, new debt, liens and unrestricted-subsidiary transfers is not established; the creditor-alignment reading therefore rests on observed behaviour, not on what documents permit.
- Acquired earnings, purchase multiples and synergies are not disclosed, so the acquisition finding is directional only and cannot say whether the programme lowers or raises leverage.
- Evidence ends with the filings of 2026-08-07, 90 days before the as-of date, so later closings, borrowings and repurchases are not established and could change the view on financial policy.
- No market prices for the notes bought back at par are supplied, so whether any seller was paid above market, a non-pro-rata benefit, cannot be judged.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Most of the 2026 acquisition programme closed in a short window. Agreements with Scripps, Sagamore Hill, Block Communications and Allen Media were signed in the third quarter of 2025 and the WBBJ purchase was announced in December 2025. Closings between 2026-01-01 and 2026-05-15 cost USD 280 million in consideration, split between WBBJ at USD 27 million, three Allen Media stations at USD 56 million, seven Allen Media markets at USD 115 million, Block at USD 80 million and Sagamore Hill at USD 2 million; the Scripps exchange moved stations without cash and produced a USD 22 million non-cash loss. A further USD 54 million is agreed for American Spirit Media and the WHPM station, of which USD 43 million was paid on the first closings on 2026-07-01 and USD 11 million awaits regulatory approval.
