## Findings

### Bottom line

Versant's leverage is low in absolute terms at 1.37x total debt to last-twelve-month reported EBITDA at 2026-09-30, but earnings are falling and the 2025 base is a Comcast carve-out, so creditors should read the direction of EBITDA rather than the level of leverage.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_vsnt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 61; 10-K_2025-12-31_vsnt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 83–84; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vsnt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vsnt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; calculation CALC-52; calculation CALC-27

Net debt was USD 1,876 million, or 0.88x, after USD 1,051 million of cash, and cash plus the undrawn USD 750 million revolver gives USD 1,801 million, which covers scheduled amortization of USD 30 million for the rest of 2026 and USD 120 million a year to 2030 many times over. Issuer-stated Adjusted EBITDA fell to USD 1,681 million for nine months 2026 from USD 1,888 million, and net debt rose by USD 400 million in the third quarter. The first lien leverage covenant is stated at a maximum of 3.50x on a definition that is not printed, so headroom is not established.

### Leverage trajectory and drivers

Total debt rose from USD 983 million at 2025-12-31 to USD 2,927 million at 2026-09-30 because the two USD 1,000 million term loans were drawn on 2026-01-02, so total debt to reported EBITDA moved from 0.43x on the year-end notes alone to 1.32x at 2026-03-31 and 1.37x at 2026-09-30. The rise is set by the financing, not by earnings: last-twelve-month reported EBITDA fell from USD 2,282 million for FY2025 to USD 2,141 million, so each further acquisition or buyback adds to net debt without an earnings cushion. In the third quarter cash fell by USD 427 million while total debt fell by USD 27 million, so net debt rose by USD 400 million to USD 1,876 million; the nine-month cash flow statement shows USD 696 million spent on acquisitions, USD 300 million on repurchases and USD 106 million on dividends.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_vsnt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 82; 10-K_2025-12-31_vsnt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 100; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vsnt-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vsnt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 14; calculation CALC-49; calculation CALC-50; calculation CALC-52; calculation CALC-58; calculation CALC-59; calculation CALC-60; calculation CALC-27

The strongest contrary evidence is that last-twelve-month reported EBITDA of USD 2,141 million is large against cash interest of USD 149 million for nine months and scheduled amortization of USD 120 million a year, and free cash flow was USD 1,210 million for nine months; the cautious reading would give way if EBITDA stabilised and acquisitions stopped outrunning cash generation.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_vsnt-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; calculation CALC-38; calculation CALC-27

### Coverage and cash-flow quality

Cash interest paid was USD 149 million for nine months 2026 against accrued interest expense of USD 157 million, with USD 87 million paid in the third quarter against USD 31 million in each of the first two, which includes the first semi-annual coupon on the notes due 2026-07-30. Reported EBITDA covered cash interest 22.52x for three months, 21.34x for six months and 11.25x for nine months, so the nine-month figure is the better guide to run-rate cover and the earlier ratios flatter it.

### Liquidity adequacy

Cash of USD 1,051 million and the undrawn USD 750 million revolver give USD 1,801 million of liquidity at 2026-09-30, against scheduled term loan amortization of USD 30 million for the rest of 2026 and USD 120 million a year to 2030, so near-term repayment is not the constraint; the first large maturity is the 2031 amount of USD 2,460 million, which includes the USD 1,000 million notes. The binding use of liquidity is discretionary: the company repurchased USD 300 million of stock and paid USD 106 million of dividends in nine months and announced a further USD 100 million accelerated repurchase from 2026-11-04. No letters of credit drawn on the revolver are stated in any source, and the revolver's availability at 2025-12-31 is not stated.

### Earnings trend and revenue mix

Adjusted EBITDA fell from USD 3,060 million in FY2023 to USD 2,837 million in FY2024 and USD 2,425 million in FY2025, and was USD 1,681 million for nine months 2026 against USD 1,888 million a year earlier, a fall of USD 207 million; third-quarter Adjusted EBITDA was USD 353 million against USD 445 million, a fall of USD 92 million. Linear distribution revenue fell to USD 2,894 million for nine months from USD 3,095 million and advertising revenue to USD 1,164 million from USD 1,207 million, only partly offset by platforms revenue of USD 662 million against USD 622 million, so the shrinking pay-television networks are not yet replaced by digital platforms. Subscribers to multichannel video providers fell 8% in each of 2024 and 2025, and programming and production obligations of USD 5,726 million are fixed, so continued subscriber declines compress EBITDA faster than costs can fall.

Operating lease commitments are also outside debt, including USD 243 million of future minimum payments on the New York office lease net of lease incentives.

### Data quality and definition risks

The 2025 comparatives are carve-out statements with costs allocated by Comcast, so every last-twelve-month figure here mixes a combined 2025 stub with standalone 2026 periods. Reported EBITDA is calculated because the issuer prints only an adjusted figure; the adjustments were USD 142 million in FY2025 and USD 5 million for nine months 2026, so the two measures converge as separation costs end. The filings' own rounding leaves four cash flow totals that cannot be supported by the other printed totals, written as not supplied, and the covenant measure cannot be compared with any ratio here because its definition is not printed.
