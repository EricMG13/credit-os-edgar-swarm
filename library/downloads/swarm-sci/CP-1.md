## Findings

### Bottom line
Derived reported EBITDA covers cash interest 4.87x and net debt stands at 4.01x EBITDA for the last twelve months to 2026-09-30, so creditors face stable rather than falling leverage.

Net debt to EBITDA was 4.00x at the end of 2024 and 4.03x at the end of 2025. Net debt rose over the nine months because repurchases, dividends, acquisitions and headquarters construction together outran free cash flow, and EBITDA growth only held the ratio steady. The issuer states no EBITDA and prints a covenant ratio of 3.69x against a 5.00x maximum on a definition that is not supplied, so these figures cannot be rebuilt into it.

### Financial interpretation

Leverage has been flat, not falling. Total debt to derived EBITDA ran between 4.15x and 4.35x and net debt to EBITDA between 4.00x and 4.14x across the five balance-sheet dates, peaking at the end of June 2026 when the revolver balance rose to USD 365.000 million, and easing at the end of September as the revolver fell to USD 235.000 million and cash fell to USD 172.653 million. EBITDA growth is the only thing holding the ratio: last-twelve-months EBITDA is USD 1,246.945 million against USD 1,214.111 million for 2025, while net debt rose USD 104.059 million since the 2025 year end, so a flat or falling EBITDA would raise leverage immediately.
Basis: calculation C02; calculation C04; calculation C05; calculation C15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18

Cash interest is covered comfortably and the cover is improving. Derived EBITDA covers cash interest paid 4.87x on the last twelve months against 4.80x for 2025, because last-twelve-months EBITDA is up 2.70% on 2025 while cash interest is up 1.34%. After capital expenditures the cover is the 3.30x shown in the register, which is the more relevant measure for an issuer that spends about 9% of revenue on capital expenditures.
Basis: calculation C04; calculation C05; calculation C06

Cash-flow quality improved but part of the gain is not structural. Free cash flow, operating cash flow less capital expenditures, was USD 594.241 million in the first nine months of 2026 against USD 467.042 million a year earlier, and its conversion of EBITDA is 54.65% on the last twelve months against 45.65% for 2025. The quarterly report lists a USD 49.0 million fall in cash tax payments among the drivers of the nine-month increase, and the earnings release ties a USD 29.9 million working-capital benefit in the third quarter to the timing of an extra payroll cycle in the prior-year period, so the run rate is lower than the latest figure suggests.
Basis: calculation C06; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_exhibit991q326earningsrele.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27

Shareholder payments, not debt reduction, are the main use of free cash flow. Dividends and share repurchases took USD 464.363 million in the nine months, about 78.1% of free cash flow, and the same period spent a further USD 73.262 million on acquisitions, USD 72.419 million on the new headquarters, USD 16.506 million on real estate and USD 40.737 million on tax credit equity investments. Together these exceeded free cash flow, so debt and cash absorbed the gap; the remaining repurchase authorization was USD 498.8 million at the quarter end and a further USD 18.2 million was spent after it. A creditor should expect leverage to stay near four times while this policy continues.
Basis: calculation C06; calculation C08; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20

Liquidity is adequate against near-term maturities but thinner in cash than before.
