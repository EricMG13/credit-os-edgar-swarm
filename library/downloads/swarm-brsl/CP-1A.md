## Findings

### Bottom line
Brightstar is a pure-play lottery operator whose long-dated government contracts support steady cash flow, but its largest customer takes 37% of revenue and a controlling shareholder holding 63.28% of the votes can decide payouts, so creditors carry concentration and distribution risk.
The July 2025 sale of the gaming and digital business and the Italian Lotto award reshaped the credit within a year: sale proceeds repaid debt and funded a special dividend and repurchase of USD 859 million within USD 1,041 million of shareholder returns in 2025, then the license took USD 2,601 million of upfront cash across 2025 and 2026, with partners funding 38.5% of it.
Basis: 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 35, 58, 90, 163, 189; 6-K_2026-06-30_brightstar-6302026xform6k.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 17; calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C03; calculation C06

### Key findings
- Revenue is contract-based and long-dated but concentrated in two government licenses. About 92% of revenue comes from long-term operating contracts, and the two Italian licenses that one regulator awards supply 37% of revenue, so a loss or non-renewal there would cut cash flow far more than any single United States contract.
Basis: 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 10, 40; calculation C04
- The Scratch & Win license, 19% of revenue, expires in September 2028 and is stated to be non-renewable, so its replacement is decided by a new award whose conditions the law will set. That date falls in the same year as the first large note maturity, so refinancing and license renewal risk coincide.
Basis: 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 40
- The Lotto license, effective December 2025 for nine years, is now secured, but its USD 2,601 million upfront cost was paid in cash and amortizes against revenue for years. Partners funded 38.5% of the final installment through a capital increase, which limits the group's own cash call but means minority holders take a large share of the license's profit.
Basis: 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 163; 6-K_2026-03-31_brightstar-3312026xform6k.htm.jsonl, page 11; 6-K_2026-06-30_brightstar-6302026xform6k.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 12; calculation C02; calculation C05
- Control sits with one shareholder: De Agostini holds 46.29% of the ordinary shares but 63.28% of the votes through the loyalty voting structure, appoints an observer to the board and has executives on it, so payout, financing and merger decisions are effectively its to approve. No sponsor-style willingness evidence exists beyond the documented actions in the registers below, and legal capacity for distributions is not established.
Basis: 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 35, 63, 79, 90, 91
- Capital allocation has tilted toward shareholders: the 2025 special dividend and accelerated repurchase alone were USD 859 million, the regular dividend rose from USD 0.20 to USD 0.23 per share, and USD 186 million of the repurchase program remains, while the company states no formal dividend policy. Creditors are protected mainly by contractual limits and the debt reduction that followed the sale, which this analysis cannot size without the financing documents.
Basis: 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 92, 171, 182–183; 6-K_2026-06-30_brightstar-6302026xform6k.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19; calculation C06; calculation C07
- Structure leaves creditors secured by the shares of the Italian operating subsidiary, with the parent, the Dutch holding company and a US co-issuer as issuers and guarantors of different instruments, and consortium partners owning part of the licensed operating companies. Cash held in the joint ventures is shared with minority partners through dividends of USD 163 million in each of the first halves of 2025 and 2026.
Basis: 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 40, 169–171; 6-K_2026-06-30_brightstar-6302026xform6k.htm.jsonl, page 5
- Overall sponsor and governance risk is medium: control and payout discretion are high, but disclosure is detailed, eight of thirteen directors are independent, related-party dealings with the controlling holder are small and the outstanding distribution limits are tied to credit ratings.
Basis: 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 79, 171, 190

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest evidence against concern is the durability of the contracts: the revenue-weighted remaining term of the main contracts is more than five years, the Italian Lotto license now runs to 2034, 15 platform customers and nearly 90 lottery customers diversify the rest, and the debt reduction and covenant-protected distribution limits after the sale reduce the chance of debt-funded payouts. The view would soften if the Scratch & Win license is extended or replaced on similar terms before 2028, or if the 2026 revolving agreement is shown to carry tight payout limits. It would harden if shareholder returns resume above free cash flow after minority distributions, if a license is lost or renewed on worse terms, or if the controlling holder uses its voting power to approve a debt-funded distribution.
Basis: 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 38–41, 171; 6-K_2026-06-30_brightstar-6302026xform6k.htm.jsonl, page 15

### Limitations
- The terms of the revolving credit agreement signed in March 2026 on distributions, guarantor coverage and security are not stated, so the conclusion that shareholder payouts are contractually limited rests on the earlier agreements' terms and may be wrong for today's facilities.
Basis: 6-K_2026-03-31_brightstar-3312026xform6k.htm.jsonl, page 13; 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 175
- The significant subsidiaries exhibit and the organization chart were not in the supplied text, so ownership percentages below the parent for most operating companies are not established, which limits the conclusion about where creditor collateral and guarantees reach.
Basis: 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 48
- Contract-level data such as expiry dates for the largest United States contracts, renewal options and termination compensation are given only as weighted averages, so the durability conclusion is an average and a single large expiry could change it.
Basis: 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 41
- The customer behind the 37% share is not named in the passage on concentration; the match to the Italian regulator rests on the two licenses' shares adding to the same figure, an analyst reading that would be wrong if another customer is meant.
Basis: 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 189; calculation C04

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The company sold its gaming and digital business to a holding company owned by Apollo funds on July 1, 2025 for USD 4.1 billion net of cash transferred, repaid USD 2,936 million of principal during 2025, and returned USD 1,041 million to shareholders in 2025. It won the nine-year Italian Lotto license, paid the EUR 2.23 billion fee in three installments ending April 24, 2026, and had partners fund 38.5% of the final installment.
New debt in the period was a EUR 1 billion term loan due 2030 (March 2025), USD 750 million of 5.750% senior secured notes due 2033 (December 2025) that refinanced the 6.250% notes due 2027, and new revolving facilities of USD 650 million and EUR 1.0 billion due March 2031 (agreement March 2026, effective April 2026). The 2026 notes were redeemed in September 2025.
