## Findings

### Bottom line
Flutter's credit has weakened since the 2025 year-end: scale and geographic spread still support creditors, but net leverage on adjusted earnings rose to 4.55x from 3.67x as earnings fell, and repair depends on a fourth quarter that guidance assumes will recover strongly.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C05; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Near-term refinancing is not the constraint, with USD 2,490 million of liquidity against USD 65 million of maturities to 2027, buybacks paused and the revolver and term A loans extended to 2032. The exposure sits in earnings taxed at higher rates, a USD 3,692 million maturity in 2030, a Fox option priced at USD 4,800 million and tax and legal claims outside the debt.
Basis: calculation C12; calculation C14; calculation C15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_flut-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 42

### Key findings

- Leverage has risen because earnings fell, not because borrowing grew. Total debt was USD 12,313 million at 2026-09-30 against USD 12,266 million at the 2025 year-end, a rise of USD 47 million, while adjusted EBITDA fell 18.07% from USD 2,845 million in 2025 to USD 2,331 million over the last twelve months; adjusted net leverage is therefore 4.55x against 3.67x, and the company's own ratio of 4.6x sits far above its medium-term target of 2.0x to 2.5x.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C18; calculation C17; Financial foundation (CP-1)
- The earnings decline is concentrated where the profit is made. International earned 86.14% of nine-month 2026 segment adjusted EBITDA against 72.51% a year earlier, and its Q3 margin fell 280 basis points as the UK remote gaming duty rose from 21% to 40% in April 2026; the United States, 39.20% of nine-month revenue, saw adjusted EBITDA fall 60.62% as higher tax rates, player incentives and spending on prediction markets, new states and the World Cup took its nine-month margin to 4.9% from 12.7%. The UK betting duty rises again from 15% to 25% in April 2027, a step not yet in the numbers.
Basis: calculation C06; calculation C19; calculation C16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_flut-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 48, 55, 59, 66
- Guidance needs a recovery that the third quarter does not show. Q3 adjusted EBITDA fell 24.69% while revenue grew 2.11%, yet the guided midpoint of USD 2,495 million implies USD 996 million in the fourth quarter, 19.71% above the USD 832 million of the fourth quarter of 2025, and even the low end implies 1.68% growth.
Basis: calculation C04; calculation C05; calculation C17
- Cash conversion is thin and was helped by working capital. Free cash flow was USD 377 million over nine months, 25.15% of adjusted EBITDA, but a working-capital inflow of USD 343 million, against an outflow of USD 559 million a year earlier, accounts for almost all of it: before that movement free cash flow was USD 34 million against USD 828 million. Cash interest of USD 636 million over the last twelve months and USD 293 million of tax paid in nine months come out of the same cash flow.
Basis: calculation C08; calculation C09
- Deleveraging to the company's target is a multi-year task. At unchanged net debt the guided midpoint gives 4.25x, and reaching 2.5x on guided earnings needs net debt to fall by about USD 4,367.50 million, which is 11.58 times the nine-month free cash flow; the pause in buybacks helps, but it does not close that distance alone.
Basis: calculation C11; calculation C15; EX-99.1_2026-11-04_d141532dex991.htm.jsonl, page 12
- Near-term refinancing risk is low, but the next wall is 2030, part of the debt floats and the market has marked the notes down. Liquidity of USD 2,490 million is 38.31 times the USD 65 million due to the end of 2027, and the October 2026 amendment moves the term A loans and revolver from 2028 to 2032; the USD 3,692 million due in 2030 is 1.48 times liquidity. The quoted fair value of the USD notes fell 5.30% in 2026 to 96.47% of principal, and about USD 5,520 million of term loans is not covered by the disclosed swaps, so each 1% on rates costs about USD 55.20 million a year, 8.68% of last twelve months cash interest.
Basis: calculation C12; calculation C23; calculation C13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_flut-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26, 28, 32, 42; 10-K_2025-12-31_flut-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 203
- Claims outside the debt are large against liquidity. The Fox option was priced at USD 4,800 million at the 2025 year-end, 1.93 times liquidity, though carried at USD 100 million; the Indian tax demand of USD 527 million has a USD 54 million provision, the IRS proposes USD 181 million with no provision, and a USD 109 million provision covers a class action awaiting judgment.
Basis: calculation C14; calculation C20; 10-K_2025-12-31_flut-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-Q_2026-09-30_flut-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 34, 39–41
- Financial policy has turned supportive but is untested. Repurchases reached USD 1,372 million of a USD 5,000 million authorisation before the pause, no dividend has been paid since May 2020, no shareholder is named as a controller and the chief executive changed on 2026-10-01; if Fox exercises, its consent rights over FanDuel are not determined, which could limit how much FanDuel cash is available to the rest of the group.
Basis: calculation C15; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_flut-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 29, 49, 85; EX-99.1_2026-08-05_d127369dex991.htm.jsonl, page 1

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest case against a weaker view is that the business is large, still earns USD 2,331 million of adjusted EBITDA over twelve months that covers cash interest 3.67 times, and has no maturity of consequence before 2029 once the term A loans and revolver move to 2032. The company reports compliance with its debt covenants and the covenant level was increased to 5.70x in the October amendment, although headroom cannot be measured without the definition. The USD notes are quoted at 96.47% of principal, which is below par but not distressed, and the company's own 2025 goodwill test found the fair value of the UKI unit USD 3,802 million above its carrying value after allowing for the new UK taxes. Management targets cost benefits above USD 300 million and guides to USD 2,495 million of adjusted EBITDA. The view would improve if fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA reaches the USD 996 million that guidance implies and net debt starts to fall; it would worsen if the quarter lands near the low end, if the UK betting duty rise or a Fox exercise arrives before cash is built, or if the Brazilian ban becomes permanent.
Basis: calculation C10; calculation C21; calculation C17; calculation C18; calculation C23; calculation C05; 10-Q_2026-09-30_flut-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 32, 42

### Limitations
- Guidance carries no quarterly or segment phasing, so the conclusion that it needs a fourth-quarter recovery rests on a residual and would soften if the company's own seasonality puts a larger share of annual earnings in the final quarter.
Basis: calculation C05; EX-99.1_2026-11-04_d141532dex991.htm.jsonl, page 12
- Leverage is shown on the issuer's adjusted EBITDA; if the restructuring and legal loss add-backs recur, last twelve months earnings are USD 1,800 million and net leverage 5.89x, so the conclusion that leverage is 4.55x would be too low, and the filings do not let a reader test the add-backs.
Basis: calculation C10; Financial foundation (CP-1)
- No agency rating, credit spread or peer figure is supplied and only the senior secured notes carry quoted prices, so the view that refinancing capacity is adequate rests on the maturity ladder, issuance history and those note prices and would change if spreads on the bank debt were wider than its carrying value implies.
Basis: calculation C23; Source readiness (CP-0)
- Deleveraging distance is measured against nine months of free cash flow in a sports calendar the company calls seasonal, so the multi-year conclusion would change if the full-year cash flow differs materially from nine months.
Basis: calculation C11; 10-K_2025-12-31_flut-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 125
- The size of claims outside the debt is as the company states it at one date, and recourse from the Indian tax and the Austrian and German player matters to the borrower group is not established, so the conclusion that these claims are large against liquidity may overstate the cash exposure of the group.
Basis: calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_flut-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 39–41

### Strengths and weaknesses
Scale and geographic spread are the main credit strengths. The company reports USD 16,383 million of 2025 revenue, calls itself the industry leader by size, and no region exceeds 39.20% of nine-month 2026 revenue, so a tax or licensing change in one market is absorbed by the others, although two shocks in 2026 struck the UK and Brazil together. That spread supports the stability of cash flow available for debt service.
Basis: calculation C16; calculation C22; 10-K_2025-12-31_flut-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9

Liquidity and the extended maturities are the second strength. Cash and the undrawn revolver of USD 2,490 million are 38.31 times the USD 65 million of maturities to the end of 2027, and the term A loans and revolver that fell due in 2028 now run to 2032, so lenders have agreed a longer tenor even as earnings fell. The remaining exposure is the 2029 and 2030 maturities and a rating-linked margin of 1.40% to 2.00%.
Basis: calculation C12; calculation C18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_flut-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 42

The third strength is a clearer financial policy. The company has paused buybacks to delever, set a target of 2.0x to 2.5x, and targets cost benefits above USD 300 million alongside a UK restructuring; this protects cash, though the savings so far have only partly offset the UK duty.
Basis: calculation C17; calculation C20; calculation C15

The first weakness is falling earnings with rising leverage. Nine-month adjusted EBITDA fell 25.53% to USD 1,499 million with margin down to 11.99% from 17.28%, because cost of sales and marketing rose to 59.47% and 25.16% of revenue, and adjusted net leverage reached 4.55x; margin is therefore the pressure point for debt service.
Basis: calculation C03; calculation C07; calculation C01

The second weakness is reliance on a recovery and on working capital. Guidance implies a 19.71% rise in fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA, and nine-month free cash flow of USD 377 million was only USD 34 million before working capital.
Basis: calculation C05; calculation C08

The third weakness is contingent claims and rate exposure. The Fox option at USD 4,800 million is 1.93 times liquidity, tax and legal claims of USD 527 million, USD 181 million and USD 109 million sit outside the debt, and about USD 5,520 million of term loans is not covered by the disclosed swaps.
Basis: calculation C14; calculation C13

### Company and operating model
Flutter is a global operator of sports betting and iGaming brands that earns revenue from customer wagers, with 53% of 2025 revenue from sportsbook, 44% from iGaming and 3% from other products, and 88% from online businesses. This model supports cash-flow visibility through diversified brands and markets, but each market's cash flow depends on a gambling licence and a duty rate that governments can change within months, as the UK in 2025 and Brazil in 2026 showed; liquidity preservation therefore rests on the group's spread of markets, not on any contract, and refinancing confidence rests on the continued access to those licences.
Basis: calculation C25; 10-K_2025-12-31_flut-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 12; calculation C16; calculation C19; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Ownership and group structure
Flutter is an Irish public company listed in New York with no controlling shareholder named in the supplied filings; it is a holding company that relies on subsidiary distributions, and its senior secured notes and credit facilities are guaranteed on a first-ranking basis by the group and certain subsidiaries. The Fox option is the structural issue, because exercise would give Fox a minority stake in FanDuel, whose earlier investor agreement limited distributions, new debt and large acquisitions without minority consent, and what rights Fox would receive is not determined; creditors would face a subsidiary holding the US growth business and a new party with consent rights. Leverage tolerance has been shown by the debt-funded Snai and Boyd transactions of 2025, and the pause in buybacks signals a change in financial policy.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_flut-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 29, 49, 85, 189; calculation C15

### Revenue drivers and pricing power
Revenue is discretionary consumer spending priced by the company's net revenue margin, not by contract, so it is neither recurring nor protected by switching costs: the annual report says barriers to customers switching between operators are low, and Q3 2026 revenue grew only 2.11% against 7.37% over nine months as International revenue was flat after the India closure. Pricing power over tax is limited: the company's mitigation plan for the UK duty is cost reduction first and market-share growth second, so revenue growth cannot be relied on to offset the UK duty step in April 2027, and customer retention and cohort data are not disclosed.
Basis: calculation C04; calculation C03; calculation C19; 10-K_2025-12-31_flut-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 37, 134; 10-Q_2026-09-30_flut-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 50, 57

### Cost structure and margin resilience
Costs move largely with revenue: cost of sales, which includes gaming taxes and platform costs, rose to 59.47% of nine-month 2026 revenue from 54.54%, and sales and marketing to 25.16% from 22.28%, so operating leverage worked against the company as taxes and customer acquisition rose faster than revenue. Margin can be defended mainly through the cost programmes the company sizes at more than USD 300 million, the UK restructuring and the closure of Betfair Brazil; cost savings in the UK and Brazil partly offset the duty in the third quarter, yet International margin still fell 280 basis points, so margin resilience is weaker than the scale of the group suggests.
Basis: calculation C07; calculation C19; calculation C20; calculation C17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_flut-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 42, 59; EX-99.1_2026-11-04_d141532dex991.htm.jsonl, page 1

### Capital intensity and cash conversion
Capital expenditure is mostly capitalised software and intangibles, USD 527 million over nine months against USD 487 million, and the issuer does not separate maintenance from growth spending, so flexibility is judged by how fast it could be cut, not by a stated split. Free cash flow of USD 377 million after USD 636 million of last twelve months cash interest and USD 293 million of tax is 25.15% of adjusted EBITDA and was USD 34 million before a working-capital inflow of USD 343 million, so the conversion available for debt service is lower than it looks; the company also guides to about USD 620 million of transaction, restructuring and integration costs in 2026, which include legal loss contingencies.
Basis: calculation C08; calculation C09; calculation C17; calculation C20; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Competitive forces
Rivalry is intense and switching is cheap, so promotion and product spending is the competitive currency; this explains why marketing took a larger share of revenue in 2026 and why margin recovery is not assured. New entrants face licensing barriers, but prediction markets regulated by the US derivatives regulator offer similar products under a different cost structure and may be offered in states where Flutter does not offer them, and illegal operators avoid tax and compliance costs; these threaten volume and margin in the largest region. Suppliers hold moderate power: Flutter depends on sports leagues, media partners and data and payment providers, and the non-cancelable purchase obligations of USD 4,998 million, of which USD 2,054 million falls in 2027, are fixed claims on cash that cannot be cut quickly. Buyers hold power through low switching costs and the company's promotional generosity, which raised cost of sales in the US.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_flut-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 37, 41, 47; 10-Q_2026-09-30_flut-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 38, 55; calculation C20

### External factors
Regulation and tax are the material external factors, and they act on the lines that matter most: the UK duty on remote gaming from 21% to 40% and on betting from 15% to 25% in 2027, state tax increases in the United States, the Brazilian ban of 2026-09-25, the Australian online gambling reforms from 2027-01-01 and a rating-linked margin on the bank debt. Economic exposure is currency, because revenue arises in sterling, euro and Australian dollar while debt is mostly dollar and the company hedges part with cross-currency swaps, and social pressure appears as a class action over gambling-related harm in Brazil and one over a betting product in Australia. Technology matters through prediction markets, which are both a threat to the sportsbook and a new revenue line with USD 50 million of market-making revenue expected in 2026.
Basis: calculation C19; calculation C25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_flut-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 28, 40, 47–49; EX-99.1_2026-08-05_d127369dex991.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 5

### SWOT in credit terms
Strengths are scale, the spread of markets, liquidity of USD 2,490 million, the maturity extension to 2032 and the buyback pause; weaknesses are falling margin, leverage above target, thin cash conversion and reliance on working capital. The opportunities that could improve credit quality are the cost programmes, market-making and prediction-market revenue, and deleveraging toward 2.0x to 2.5x; the threats are the next UK duty step, a Fox exercise, Brazilian and Indian claims, further US state taxes and competition from prediction markets.
Basis: calculation C12; calculation C17; calculation C14; calculation C03

### Financial profile and credit quality
The financial profile is weaker than the business profile. Trend is negative: adjusted net leverage rose from 3.67x to 4.55x, interest cover on adjusted earnings fell to 3.67x from 5.36x and fell to 1.03x on reported earnings after USD 873 million of Brazil impairments. The main supports are liquidity, maturity extension and low near-term maturities; the constraints are margin, leverage, contingent claims and the floating debt not covered by swaps. Datapoints still missing for a definitive opinion are the covenant definition and headroom, agency ratings, the post-amendment maturity ladder and the guidance phasing.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C10; calculation C12; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds
Over 12 to 24 months the tailwinds are the cost programmes, the UK restructuring and lower marketing if the World Cup and launch spending does not recur; the headwinds are the UK betting duty step in April 2027, the Brazil closure, the expiry in June 2027 of swaps covering USD 1,489 million of term loan B, and the 2029 maturities of USD 1,143 million. Over three to five years the Fox option window to December 2030, the USD 3,692 million of debt maturing in 2030, mostly the term loan B, and the company's target of 2.0x to 2.5x define the test; the company must cut net debt by about USD 4,367.50 million, so deleveraging depends on earnings recovering, not on the ladder alone.
Basis: calculation C13; calculation C11; calculation C12; calculation C17; 10-K_2025-12-31_flut-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-Q_2026-09-30_flut-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 28, 42, 48

### Downside scenario
The downside starts with a fourth quarter at the low end of guidance: adjusted EBITDA of USD 846 million would make full-year earnings USD 2,345 million, and with net debt unchanged leverage would be 4.52x. The first operational break is margin, because the UK betting duty step in April 2027 would compress International margin again while US tax rates and marketing keep US earnings low, and if the restructuring and legal add-backs recur net leverage on last twelve months earnings is already 5.89x. Free cash flow of USD 377 million has little cushion, because USD 34 million remains before working capital, so a reversal of the USD 343 million inflow would cut cash available for debt service while interest on about USD 5,520 million of term loans not covered by swaps moves with rates. Management has levers in the buyback pause, the cost programmes and capital expenditure, and liquidity of USD 2,490 million is 38.31 times the maturities to 2027, so distress within the next 12 months is not indicated; the scenario becomes serious if a Fox exercise, an adverse Indian or IRS outcome and a weak 2027 coincide, because the Fox price alone is 1.93 times liquidity. The observable signs are 2026 annual results below the guided range and a company leverage ratio above 4.6x.
Basis: calculation C05; calculation C24; calculation C10; calculation C08; calculation C13; calculation C12; calculation C14; calculation C18

### Overall credit view
Overall, Flutter presents a strong business profile on scale and spread of markets, supported by liquidity, but credit quality is constrained by falling margin, leverage of 4.55x on adjusted earnings and claims outside the debt. Cash-flow durability appears adequate but not strong, because nine-month free cash flow was only USD 34 million before working capital. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on fourth-quarter delivery, on the 2029 and 2030 maturities and on the covenant definitions that are not supplied. The primary downside path is a further fall in International margin on the UK duty step combined with a Fox exercise before net debt falls, and the top risk is that earnings do not recover to the guided level. A more definitive opinion would need the executed credit agreement, agency ratings, market prices and the post-amendment maturity ladder.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C08; calculation C05; calculation C14; Source readiness (CP-0)
