## Findings

### Bottom line

BellRing's credit profile is adequate but weakening: a cash-generative, asset-light brand franchise still supports the debt, yet margin compression, repurchases above free cash flow and dependence on three retailers have lifted leverage and left little room for error.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-09-30_brbr_brbr-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12, 31; calculation C1; calculation C2

Total debt was 3.42x last-twelve-month EBITDA at 30 June 2026 against 2.88x at the 2025 year end, and net leverage was 3.27x, or 3.53x pro forma for the USD 86.0 million legal payment due on 10 August 2026. Liquidity of USD 248.0 million and no debt maturity before December 2029 contain the near-term risk. The primary downside path is a retailer cutting distribution while margins stay compressed.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-09-30_brbr_brbr-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 93; 10-Q_2026-06-30_brbr_brbr-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 10–13, 16; calculation C9; calculation C10

### Key findings

The business profile is adequate: scale and brand strength support cash flow, but concentration limits its durability. Net sales were USD 2,354.6 million over the last twelve months, up 1.64% on fiscal 2025, with Premier Protein 85.9% of sales and ready-to-drink shakes 81.7%; three retailers took 74.0% of fiscal 2025 net sales with no material long-term supply agreements. Growth has slowed from 16.05% in fiscal 2025 to 2.28% in the nine months, so the business now depends on holding shelf space more than adding it.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-09-30_brbr_brbr-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12, 31; calculation C2

Margin resilience is weak at present. Gross margin fell to 28.59% over the last twelve months from 33.26% in fiscal 2025 and EBITDA margin to 14.10% from 16.23%, as cost of goods sold rose to 71.53% of net sales in the nine months with a USD 11.3 million ingredient charge and a USD 10.0 million inventory reserve. Premier Protein's average net selling price fell because of promotion and mix, so cost inflation is not being recovered through price on the main brand: EBITDA fell 11.73% while net sales rose 1.64%.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-06-30_brbr_brbr-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 22; calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C4

Free cash flow conversion is solid, carried by low capital needs more than by earnings. Free cash flow was USD 225.0 million over the last twelve months, 67.79% of EBITDA against 68.06% in fiscal 2025, because additions to property were only 0.20% of fiscal 2025 net sales. Two items weigh on the next period: inventory absorbed USD 150.7 million of operating cash in the nine months, and the USD 86.0 million legal payment equals 38.22% of last-twelve-month free cash flow. Fiscal 2025 free cash flow does not yet carry the cash cost of the USD 69.0 million legal provision, which was accrued and not paid.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-09-30_brbr_brbr-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 90, 98; 10-Q_2026-06-30_brbr_brbr-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 16; calculation C3; calculation C6

Leverage is moderate but rising, and covenant headroom cannot be established. Total debt was 3.42x last-twelve-month EBITDA at 30 June 2026 against 2.88x at the 2025 year end, and net leverage was 3.27x, or 3.53x pro forma for the USD 86.0 million payment due on 10 August 2026, with net debt of USD 1,084.9 million rising to USD 1,170.9 million pro forma. EBITDA covered interest expense 4.20x over the last twelve months against 5.50x in fiscal 2025. The financial covenant stated is a maximum total net leverage ratio of 6.00 times under a definition that is not supplied, so the ratios here are not the covenant measure and headroom is not calculable.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-09-30_brbr_brbr-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 95, 101; 10-Q_2026-06-30_brbr_brbr-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 19; calculation C5; calculation C10

Liquidity and refinancing risk are contained for about three years. Liquidity of USD 248.0 million at 30 June 2026 is down 22.69% from USD 320.8 million at the 2024 year end, and the effect of the legal payment on liquidity is not established in Financial foundation. Both debt instruments fall due within fiscal 2030: the 7.00% senior notes of USD 840.0 million on 15 March 2030, and the revolver, USD 300.0 million drawn, on 22 August 2030 or 14 December 2029 if the notes are not refinanced, which is 3.07 years after the as-of date. The interest run-rate on principal debt is USD 75.7 million a year, so a refinancing at a higher rate or on lower EBITDA would add to the burden.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-09-30_brbr_brbr-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 93–95; 10-Q_2026-06-30_brbr_brbr-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 10–13, 32; calculation C8; calculation C9; calculation C11

Financial policy and governance are the swing factor, and the risk level is Medium. Business & governance finds repurchases of USD 474.9 million in fiscal 2025, or 185.58% of free cash flow, with USD 300.0 million of net revolver borrowing over fiscal 2025 and the nine months and USD 506.9 million of authorisation remaining at 30 June 2026, while the chairman holds a role at Post, the chief executive gave notice of retirement and securities suits are pending. There is no sponsor extracting value and no dividend, and repurchases paused in May and June 2026.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-09-30_brbr_brbr-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 53, 76–77; 10-Q_2026-06-30_brbr_brbr-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 33

The primary downside path runs from retailer concentration through volume to fixed costs. A retailer reducing distribution or moving volume to a competing brand would cut net sales while take-or-pay commitments of USD 1,362.9 million, of which USD 602.7 million fall due in fiscal 2026 and equal 38.98% of fiscal 2025 cost of goods sold, and contract manufacturer minimums stay in place; with margin already compressed, EBITDA and free cash flow would fall faster than sales, leverage above 3.42x would follow and the revolver would fund the gap. No stress figure is given because the filings do not disclose customer-level terms.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-09-30_brbr_brbr-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 14, 31, 66–67; calculation C7

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest contrary evidence is that cash generation has held while earnings fell: free cash flow was 67.79% of EBITDA over the last twelve months, above 46.63% in fiscal 2024, net sales still grew 2.28% in the nine months with Dymatize sales up 14%, and the company paused repurchases in May and June 2026. Liquidity of USD 248.0 million and a first maturity more than three years away give time to repair margin.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-06-30_brbr_brbr-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 22, 33; calculation C3; calculation C9

The view would improve if EBITDA margin recovers toward 16.23% and total debt to EBITDA returns toward 2.88x while repurchases stay below free cash flow. It would worsen if last-twelve-month EBITDA falls further below USD 331.9 million, if the legal payment is funded by revolver borrowing, if a retailer reduces distribution, or if repurchases resume while the revolver is drawn.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C1

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths, in rank order. First, asset-light cash generation: additions to property were 0.20% of fiscal 2025 net sales and free cash flow was 67.79% of EBITDA over the last twelve months, so cash is available for debt service even as earnings fall, which supports debt service capacity. Second, liquidity and maturity runway: liquidity of USD 248.0 million and a first maturity 3.07 years away limit refinancing and liquidity risk in the near term. Third, brand scale: Premier Protein is 85.9% of sales and net sales grew 16.05% in fiscal 2025 and 2.28% in the nine months, which supports revenue durability while distribution holds. Fourth, no sponsor extraction and no dividend, so cash is not leaving through fees or distributions other than repurchases.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-09-30_brbr_brbr-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 53, 76; calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C9

Weaknesses, in rank order. First, customer concentration: three retailers took 74.0% of fiscal 2025 net sales with no material long-term agreements, so one decision can remove a large share of revenue. Second, margin compression: EBITDA margin fell to 14.10% from 16.23% and cost of goods sold rose to 71.53% of net sales in the nine months, which cuts cash flow available for debt service. Third, rising leverage with shareholder returns above free cash flow: total debt to EBITDA rose to 3.42x from 2.88x while repurchases were 185.58% of free cash flow in fiscal 2025. Fourth, supply concentration and related-party reliance: one manufacturer made 46.3% of shake supply and shakes bought from a Post subsidiary reached 6.60% of nine-month cost of goods sold. Fifth, event risk from litigation, a USD 86.0 million legal payment and a leadership transition.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-09-30_brbr_brbr-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 31; 10-Q_2026-06-30_brbr_brbr-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 16–19; calculation C1

### Company and ownership

BellRing sells ready-to-drink protein shakes and protein powders under Premier Protein and Dymatize, made by third-party contract manufacturers, which produce all shakes and most other products, and sold mainly through club, food, drug, mass and online retailers; it has one operating segment and about 530 employees, so the credit rests on brand pull and supply arrangements rather than on owned assets. The company was spun off from Post in March 2022 and Post sold its last shares in November 2022, so there is no sponsor or controlling holder identified, but the chairman holds a role at Post and Post's subsidiary supplies shakes, which links the borrower to its former parent through contracts.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-09-30_brbr_brbr-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–12, 14, 17, 45–46, 87

### Revenue durability and pricing power

Revenue is likely to recur because the products are repeat-purchase staples, an analyst inference as no repeat-rate data is supplied, but it is concentrated and price-sensitive: three customers took 74.0% of fiscal 2025 net sales, the category is highly sensitive to pricing and promotion, and private label competes. Fiscal 2025 growth of 16.05% came from distribution gains and promotion; in the nine months Premier Protein volume rose 5% but its price fell, while Dymatize raised price, which shows pricing power in the smaller brand and not in the main one. The consequence for creditors is that revenue is durable only while the retailers keep the products listed, and that price support is weaker where most of the revenue sits.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-09-30_brbr_brbr-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 31, 64; 10-Q_2026-06-30_brbr_brbr-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 22; calculation C2; calculation C5

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Product costs are mostly per-unit tolling and ingredient charges but are held semi-fixed by minimum volume and take-or-pay commitments of USD 1,362.9 million, and inputs are protein, packaging and freight that are exposed to inflation and tariffs. Cost of goods sold rose to 71.53% of net sales in the nine months from 65.06%, and advertising rose to 4.21% of net sales from 3.54%, so the issuer is spending more to defend volume while input costs rise. Margins can be defended against modest volume pressure through pricing in powders and cost actions, but not against both input inflation and promotion at once; the evidence is that the second has already happened.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-09-30_brbr_brbr-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 13–14, 63, 66–67; 10-Q_2026-06-30_brbr_brbr-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 19–22; calculation C1; calculation C4; calculation C7

### Capital intensity and free cash flow conversion

Capital intensity is very low, with additions to property at 0.20% of fiscal 2025 net sales and 0.47% in the nine months, and management states that no significant capital expenditure is planned for twelve months, so EBITDA converts to free cash flow at 67.79% before debt repayment. Working capital is the swing item: inventories absorbed USD 150.7 million of cash in the nine months against USD 128.3 million a year earlier. Cash taxes and interest are inside operating cash flow, and the legal payment will add a one-time outflow in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026. Free cash flow is therefore available for debt service, but it has been directed to repurchases, not to deleveraging.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-06-30_brbr_brbr-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4, 25; calculation C3; calculation C6

### Competitive forces and sector factors

Rivalry is intense, with private label and competitors with greater resources; the main substitutes are private label and, as the issuer notes, weight-management medication that could reduce demand; buyer power is high because three customers take 74.0% of sales without long-term agreements; supplier power is meaningful because one manufacturer makes 46.3% of shake supply and a sole supplier provides the aseptic packaging; barriers to entry are not evidenced in the filings beyond brand and distribution, so the threat of new entrants is not established. Tariffs and trade policy are the policy factor that matters for credit, because they raise packaging and ingredient costs, and the other PEST factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver on the provided materials.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-09-30_brbr_brbr-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 22–26, 28, 31; 10-Q_2026-06-30_brbr_brbr-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 20

In credit terms the strengths are asset-light cash generation, brand scale, liquidity and a long maturity runway, and the absence of sponsor extraction; the weaknesses are customer concentration, compressed margin, rising leverage with shareholder returns above free cash flow, and supply concentration; the opportunities are Dymatize international growth and pricing and powder growth; the threats are tariffs, weight-management medication, private label and the securities litigation.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C3

### Financial profile and credit quality

The main credit supports are scale, with USD 2,354.6 million of net sales over the last twelve months, low capital needs that keep free cash flow conversion at a steady level, liquidity of USD 248.0 million and a maturity runway of more than three years. The main constraints are diversification, which is weak by customer, product form and supplier, margin, which is weak at present, and financial policy, where repurchases above free cash flow have added debt. The trend is down on margin and leverage and steady on cash conversion. Ability to refinance cannot be tested because no rating or market data is supplied, and the missing data are the fiscal 2026 results, the covenant definitions and customer-level terms.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0); calculation C1; calculation C3; calculation C9

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months the headwinds are input-cost inflation including tariffs, promotional intensity on Premier Protein, the USD 86.0 million legal payment, litigation cost and a leadership transition; the tailwinds are distribution gains, Dymatize pricing and international volume, a pause in repurchases and a cost base with low capital needs. Management gives no guidance in the supplied filings, so the direction is inferred from trend: earnings pressure persists until price realisation or input costs turn. Over three to five years the issue is refinancing of USD 1,140.0 million of principal in fiscal 2030, which will be priced on the leverage and margin the company shows by then; the category is described by management as rapidly growing, which is management language, not evidence of credit strength.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-09-30_brbr_brbr-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 63; 10-Q_2026-06-30_brbr_brbr-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 20–22, 33; calculation C9

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is one of the three large retailers reducing shelf space or moving volume to a competing or private-label shake, which the company cannot contract against because it has no material long-term supply agreements with customers. The first operational break is volume falling below the minimum quantities committed to contract manufacturers and suppliers, which can bring penalties or payments for product not taken; the financial transmission is lower gross profit on a margin that is already compressed, a larger inventory build, and operating cash flow that falls faster than sales. The consequence is higher leverage than the 3.42x total debt to EBITDA reported at 30 June 2026, drawing on the USD 500.0 million revolver (USD 197.6 million undrawn after letters of credit) to fund the USD 86.0 million legal payment and any resumed repurchases, and a weaker position ahead of the refinancing in fiscal 2030. Management levers are the repurchase programme, advertising and inventory; resilience appears sufficient to avoid a liquidity shortfall in the near term but not to keep leverage from rising, and the observable triggers are in the monitoring table.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-09-30_brbr_brbr-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 14, 31, 66–67, 93–95; 10-Q_2026-06-30_brbr_brbr-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 10–13, 16

### Overall credit view

Overall, BellRing presents an adequate business profile supported by asset-light cash generation, brand scale and a long maturity runway, but credit quality is constrained by customer and supplier concentration, compressed margin and rising leverage. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow conversion has held at 67.79% of EBITDA. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on leverage and margin in fiscal 2030, with no rating or market data to test it. The primary downside path is a retailer cutting distribution against fixed purchase commitments. Further analysis would require the fiscal 2026 results, the executed credit documents and customer-level terms. Top risk: customer concentration combined with compressed margin.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0); calculation C1; calculation C3; calculation C9
