## Findings

### Bottom line
BWX Technologies, Inc. gives creditors unusually durable demand from a limited-competition supplier to the US naval nuclear programme, but its recent and pending portfolio moves make financial policy, not the business, the main creditor risk.

The US Government was 68% of FY2025 revenue and backlog of USD 8,556.2 million covers 2.38x last-twelve-months revenue. There is no sponsor and no controlling holder is disclosed, but the proxy statement that would name large holders and directors was not supplied. Three acquisitions since January 2025 and a pending sale of the medical business for up to USD 800 million are reshaping the group, and no document supplied establishes the limits on that policy.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 130; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; calculation C03; calculation C04

### Key findings
Demand is durable but concentrated and appropriation-dependent. Government Operations carried 76% of backlog at FY2025, the US Government was 68% of revenue against 76% a year earlier, and USD 1,970.9 million of backlog, 23.03% of the total, is unfunded US Government work that depends on future appropriations. The issuer describes competition for naval nuclear components and fuel as limited and its Navy fuel plant as the sole provider, which supports renewal odds but ties creditor cash flow to one customer's budget cycle.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 53; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; calculation C01

The mix is moving toward Commercial Operations and Canada, which earn lower margins and carry more fixed-price risk. Commercial Operations rose to 26.63% of segment revenue in FY2025 from 19.36% in FY2024 as Kinectrics joined, its operating margin was 6.77% against 16.80% for Government Operations, 74.81% of nine-month 2026 revenue is fixed-price or fixed-price incentive work, and Canada holds 41.26% of net property, plant and equipment. A cost overrun on a fixed-price utility contract would reach cash flow before any covenant moves.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 135; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 24; calculation C07; calculation C08; calculation C10; calculation C11; calculation C12; calculation C13

Ownership is public and widely held, so no sponsor can extract value, but nothing supplied limits financial policy. The annual report defers directors, executive pay and large holders to a proxy statement that was not supplied. The signatories for chief financial officer and chief accounting officer changed between the 2024 and 2025 annual reports and one fewer director signed, with no reason stated, so succession and board change are noted but not assessed.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 137; 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 142–143; 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 147–148

Shareholder distributions are moderate against cash flow, and acquisitions are the larger call on cash. Dividends and buybacks took 25.52% of FY2025 operating cash flow, the quarterly dividend was raised by 8.00% in 2026, no shares were repurchased under the buyback programme in the nine months of 2026 and USD 347.6 million of buyback authorisation remains, whereas acquisitions used USD 535.1 million of cash in FY2025 and USD 187.0 million in the nine months of 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 82; 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 124; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6; calculation C15; calculation C16; calculation C17; calculation C21; calculation C22

Acquisition appetite is explicit and integration is unfinished. Management says it is exploring further acquisitions and would fund them with cash, debt, equity or a mix. Kinectrics, bought in May 2025, is still being brought into internal controls and the auditor flagged its fair value as a critical audit matter, the Precision Components Group price allocation is preliminary, and unallocated corporate costs rose by USD 19.7 million in the nine months of 2026 on acquisition, restructuring and healthcare costs. Integration cost or overpayment would reduce the cash available for debt service.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 78; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 46

The medical sale helps creditors only if proceeds reduce debt, which is not established. The agreement values the business at up to USD 800 million, BWXT keeps a minority interest, closing is expected in 2026 or the first quarter of 2027, no significant gain or loss is expected, and the filings do not say what the proceeds will fund. Property, plant and equipment of USD 482.1 million sits in the held-for-sale assets.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16

Joint-venture earnings are cash in some periods and retained in others, and the contract base is turning over. Dividends received from joint ventures of USD 94.3 million exceeded equity income of USD 74.9 million in FY2025, but in the nine months of 2026 equity income, 20.40% of operating income, exceeded dividends. The Portsmouth, SACOM and West Valley Phase 1 contracts ended in 2025 and the Strategic Petroleum Reserve and Canadian National Laboratory contracts began in late 2025, so income depends on renewals and performance scores.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 12–14; 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 108; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 6; calculation C18; calculation C23; calculation C24

Legacy and contingent exposures are small in the filings but not capped by them. Asbestos liabilities of former subsidiaries were assumed by Babcock and Wilcox Enterprises at the 2015 spin-off and are channelled to a trust, although the filings concede possible underinsured exposure; the Department of Energy covers substantially all decommissioning costs of the two licensed US facilities, and the latest quarterly report states no material contingencies.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 128–130; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20

Disclosure is adequate for monitoring operations but not for assessing creditor protections. The statements are audited by Deloitte and Touche LLP, controls are reported effective and covenant compliance is stated at every date, but the credit agreement, the indentures and the August 2026 supplemental indentures are not supplied, so the capacity to pay dividends, add debt or release liens is not established.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest case for a more positive view is that the conduct on record is conservative: the 2025 acquisitions were accompanied by borrowings that the zero-coupon convertible notes later refinanced, the former revolver and term loan were replaced by a larger revolver maturing in 2030, covenant compliance was reported at every date, and dividends are small against operating cash flow. The view would improve if the executed documents showed tight limits on restricted payments and added debt, and if sale proceeds were applied to debt. It would worsen if further acquisitions were funded by revolver borrowings before the medical sale closes, if integration of Kinectrics or Precision Components Group slipped, or if appropriations delays hit the unfunded backlog.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 112–116; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 36–40; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 45

### Limitations
- The proxy statement is not supplied, so the conclusion that no holder controls the company and the description of the board rest on the filings' silence and signature pages.
- The credit agreement and indentures are not supplied, so the conclusion on financial policy rests on documented conduct and not on what the documents permit.
- The sale agreement and the Precision Components Group allocation are known only from summaries, so the view of the portfolio reshaping may change once proceeds, the retained stake and the final goodwill are known.
- Joint-venture data is combined for all ventures, so the quality of equity income is judged from totals and not from individual contracts.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues
In November 2025 the company entered a second amended and restated credit agreement with a USD 1,250 million senior secured revolver maturing in 2030, issued USD 1,250 million of 0% convertible senior notes due 2030, used the proceeds to repay the former revolving credit facility and term loan, and paid USD 131.9 million for capped call transactions. The notes settle principal in cash, carry an initial conversion price of about USD 262.51 a share, and are guaranteed by the domestic subsidiaries that guarantee the credit facility. The USD 400 million senior notes due 2028 and 2029 carry 4.125% coupons and remain outstanding.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 112–117; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 36–42

The group bought Aerojet Ordnance Tennessee, Inc. for about USD 101.1 million in January 2025, Kinectrics for a headline price of CAD 782.7 million in May 2025 (purchase consideration of USD 440.6 million after assumed pension liabilities and debt), and Precision Components Group, LLC for about USD 216.8 million on 2026-07-01 under an agreement dated 2026-04-20. In the first nine months of 2025 net borrowings of USD 450.6 million accompanied the first two.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bwxt_bwxt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 101–102; 10-Q_2026-06-30_bwxt_bwxt-63026x10qdocument.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 45; calculation C20

On 2026-07-31 the company agreed to sell its medical business, BWXT Medical and the stable medical isotope business of Kinectrics, to Nordic Capital in a transaction valued at up to USD 800 million, keeping a minority interest and continuing to supply isotope and radiochemical expertise. Completion is expected in 2026 or the first quarter of 2027 subject to regulatory approvals. Three supplemental indentures dated 2026-08-07 are listed as exhibits to the latest quarterly report, and their terms are not stated in the documents supplied.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_bwxt_bwxt-63026x10qdocument.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bwxt_bwxt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 48
