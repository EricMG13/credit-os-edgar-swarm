## Findings

### Bottom line

LGI Homes' earnings carry little of its debt: at September 30, 2026 net debt was 9.27x last-twelve-months EBITDA and EBITDA covered cash interest only 1.39x, so creditors depend on the borrowing-base revolver and on inventory turning into cash rather than on earnings.
Leverage and cover improved from FY2025, when net debt was 10.76x EBITDA and cover was 1.23x, because net debt fell and EBITDA recovered, but both remain well short of FY2024 (4.69x and 2.69x). The issuer's EBITDA also adds back capitalized interest charged to cost of sales; without that add-back, cover is 0.91x.
Basis: calculation C21; calculation C18; calculation C17; calculation C26; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 72; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 42

Orders point the wrong way: backlog fell 47.5% and the cancellation rate reached 48.9% in the nine months, so the EBITDA recovery may not hold. The issuer changed its adjusted EBITDA definition in 2026, so adjusted leverage is calculable only for FY2024 and FY2025.
Basis: calculation C33; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 71; 10-Q_2026-03-31_lgih-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 30

### Leverage and coverage

Reported leverage fell from the first quarter to the third quarter of 2026 but remains far above the FY2024 level. Net debt was USD 1475.029 million at September 30, 2026, which is 9.27x last-twelve-months EBITDA of USD 159.200 million, against 10.76x at December 31, 2025 and 4.69x at December 31, 2024. The improvement comes from lower net debt (down USD 120.527 million since December 31, 2025) and a modest EBITDA recovery (up USD 10.849 million on FY2025), not from a return to FY2024 earnings, when EBITDA was USD 304.092 million.

### Cash flow and liquidity

Operating cash flow swung from an outflow of USD 226.749 million in the first nine months of 2025 to an inflow of USD 97.956 million in 2026, because spending on real estate inventory fell from USD 318.064 million to USD 52.498 million and accounts payable rose by USD 51.202 million; the company also received USD 36.954 million from selling property and equipment. The recovery in cash therefore reflects lower land and construction spending and payables timing more than earnings, and the company repaid USD 116.459 million of notes payable net of borrowings.

### Strongest contrary evidence and triggers

The strongest evidence against a weak credit view is cash generation: nine-month operating cash flow was positive, net revolver repayments were USD 116.459 million, and USD 855.0 million of commitments now run to 2030, which lowers near-term refinancing risk. If closings keep converting inventory to cash, leverage can keep falling without EBITDA growth. The view would change toward strength if orders stabilise and cancellations fall from 48.9% in the nine months, and toward weakness if backlog conversion slows further (backlog is down 47.5%) or another inventory impairment follows the USD 4.681 million recorded in 2026.
