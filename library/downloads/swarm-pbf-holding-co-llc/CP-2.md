## Findings

### Bottom line

PBF Holding is a strong credit today but a cyclical one: net cash and 0.40x leverage rest on refining margins far above the loss-making FY2024 and FY2025 levels, so creditors are protected by liquidity more than by earnings. Net cash was USD 897.6 million at 2026-09-30, or USD 827.2 million pro forma for the redemption of the last 2030 notes on 2026-10-26.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33; calculation CB1; calculation CB2

After that redemption no drawn debt matures before 2032 and the revolver is undrawn, so the primary downside path is a return to FY2025 margins that turns cash flow negative while capital spending and parent distributions continue.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation CB5; calculation CB6; calculation CB9

After the October redemption USD 1,050.0 million of notes remain, due in 2032 and 2034.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence that the credit is stronger than a cyclical reading allows is the size of the buffer: pro forma net cash of USD 827.2 million plus revolver availability of about USD 3,100.0 million would cover about 2.21 years of cash burn at the FY2025 rate before insurance, adjusted EBITDA for the last twelve months was USD 3,505.2 million, and annual cash coupons have fallen by about USD 130.2 million. The view would move to a stronger one if gross refining margin per barrel stays well above the FY2025 level for several more quarters while the inventory-related accruals do not reverse.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation CB1; calculation CB2; calculation CB6; calculation CB7; calculation CB10

The strongest evidence for a weaker view is that two of the last three years lost money at the EBITDA level before special items, that the 2026 margin is more than two and a half times the FY2025 margin, and that at the FY2025 margin nine-month adjusted EBITDA would have been negative USD 145.4 million on the same volumes. Gross refining margin per barrel below USD 10.00 for two quarters, a revolver draw, or a distribution to the parent above its dividend need would move the view to weak.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pbfh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 113; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbfh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 123; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 41; calculation CB5

- The downside figures apply FY2025 margins to 2026 volumes and hold costs and working capital constant, so the swing in adjusted EBITDA and the 2.21-year cover are scale illustrations and not forecasts; costs, the revolver borrowing base and parent distributions would all move, and the downside conclusion could be better or worse.
  Basis: calculation CB5; calculation CB6; calculation CB7

Net cash and about USD 3,100.0 million of revolver availability sit against USD 1,050.0 million of notes due in 2032 and 2034 after the October redemption, and annual cash coupons fell by about USD 130.2 million; the issuer runs six refineries in four regions with a weighted-average Nelson Complexity Index of 12.8; interest cover was 25.09x; and insurers have paid USD 1.25 billion of the Martinez claim, which management expects to finalise before year-end 2026.

### Company, ownership and operating model

PBF Holding is a wholly owned subsidiary of a listed parent and owns six refineries in four regions, with about one million barrels a day of capacity and one reportable segment. The parent controls it through a direct owner, the three directors are the parent's executives, and no financial sponsor is named, so exit pressure does not apply; the chain of control and the parent's claim on issuer cash are the governance facts that matter to creditors.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbf-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2-3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34

Revenue follows product prices and volumes rather than contracts: most sales are spot or short-term, gasoline and distillates are 88.82% of nine-month revenue, and one customer is about 13% of FY2025 revenue. Revenue fell 23.48% from FY2023 to FY2025 and gross refining margin per barrel excluding special items fell from USD 16.07 to USD 8.77, or 45.43%, while EBITDA excluding special items fell from USD 2,357.6 million to negative USD 9.5 million, so a modest fall in revenue and a margin cut of less than half removed all of EBITDA.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pbfh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 28; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbfh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 113; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbfh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 123; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbfh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 154; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 28; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation CB14

Costs are largely fixed at the refinery level: operating expenses were USD 2,581.3 million, USD 2,487.8 million and USD 2,547.0 million in FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 while throughput fell, and operating expense per barrel was USD 8.15 for nine months of 2026. Energy is the main variable cost, about half the workforce is under collective bargaining agreements, and renewable fuel compliance and general expenses rose in 2026. Capital intensity is high, with capital and turnaround spending of USD 1,144.0 million in FY2025 and USD 933.8 million for nine months of 2026, so free cash flow depends on margin and on how much turnaround work is deferred.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pbfh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbfh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 113; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbfh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 154; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbfh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 156; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 42; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 72; calculation CB8

### Industry forces and credit SWOT

Competitive rivalry is intense and sets margins: the issuer competes with integrated oil companies and foreign refiners, some larger and with their own crude, while it buys crude and feedstocks from unaffiliated suppliers. Customers are not concentrated beyond one buyer at about 13% of revenue and most volume is sold at market prices, so neither buyer nor supplier power is the main risk; substitutes matter through renewable fuel mandates and energy transition policy, which raise compliance cost and could erode product demand over time.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pbfh-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 28-29; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 6

Regulation and geopolitics are material to credit. The March 2026 federal renewable fuel rule raised compliance cost, the issuer intends to defer settlement of its California emissions obligation until 2029, and the issuer says hostilities involving the United States, Israel, the Gulf States and Iran lifted refining margins and working capital needs while their duration is uncertain. Other macro, social and technology factors do not appear to be primary credit drivers on the supplied documents.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 45

In credit terms the strengths are liquidity, distant maturities, scale and recovered cover; the weaknesses are cyclical margins, cash flow that leans on accruals and insurance, regulatory cost and ranking behind secured debt. The opportunities that could improve credit quality are the cost programme, which management says should reach more than USD 350 million of run-rate savings by year-end 2026, and a lasting margin level; the threats are margin normalisation, penalties from the Martinez investigations and parent cash calls.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-29_a991pressreleaseq3-26.htm.jsonl, page 2; Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Financial profile and outlook

On the nine dimensions of the financial profile the assessment is: scale and market position strong on capacity and complexity but not compared with peers; competitive advantage average, from coastal complexity without proprietary crude; business diversification average, with four regions but one product slate; cost and capex flexibility average, with turnarounds deferrable within limits; margin stability weak; free cash flow stability weak, with negative operating cash flow in FY2024 and FY2025; ability to refinance average, because access came at a near double-digit coupon in 2025; liquidity strong; and financial policy and governance average, in line with the medium sponsor risk level.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_pbfh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 113; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbfh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 156; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 38

Over 12 to 24 months the near-term drivers are the margin level, the renewable fuel compliance cost, the finalisation of the Martinez claim expected before year-end 2026, the 2027 catch-up of deferred turnarounds and the parent's dividend, which management raised by 18%. The tailwinds are wide spreads, lower interest cost and no maturity before 2032; the headwinds are margin normalisation, working capital reversal and regulatory penalties. No medium-term forecast is supplied, so the three-to-five-year outlook is not established.

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a normalisation of crack spreads from the Q3 2026 level toward the FY2025 level while renewable fuel compliance cost stays high. The first operational break is gross refining margin per barrel falling to the FY2025 level of USD 8.77: on nine-month volumes that removes about USD 3,429.7 million of margin and would turn nine-month adjusted EBITDA of USD 3,284.3 million to about negative USD 145.4 million, holding costs constant. Operating cash flow turns negative as it did in FY2024 and FY2025, and the accrued liabilities that rose in 2026 would be a further call on cash if they unwind.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pbfh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 113; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 59; calculation CB5

At the FY2025 cash burn of USD 1,778.9 million before insurance, pro forma net cash of USD 827.2 million plus revolver availability of about USD 3,100.0 million would cover roughly 2.21 years, with no maturity before 2032. Management levers are deferring turnarounds, which were 32.68% of nine-month capital spending, cutting the cost programme further and limiting parent distributions, which are discretionary apart from tax distributions. Resilience therefore appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress over the next two years, but the revolver borrowing base would shrink as prices fall, and the observable trigger is gross refining margin per barrel below USD 10.00 for two quarters.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pbfh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 93; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbfh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 156; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation CB6; calculation CB7; calculation CB13

### Overall credit view

Overall, PBF Holding presents a business profile that is large and complex but fully exposed to the refining cycle, supported by net cash, a distant maturity profile and revolver availability of about USD 3,100.0 million, but credit quality is constrained by cyclical margins, cash-flow quality, regulatory cost and cash calls from the parent. Cash-flow durability is weak at FY2025 margins and strong at 2026 margins. Refinancing capacity depends on the margin cycle and on the revolver borrowing base, and the primary downside path is a margin reversal that burns cash while capital spending and distributions continue. A more definitive opinion would need the executed debt documents, current ratings, peer data and a forecast. Top risk: refining margin reversal.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0); calculation CB5; calculation CB7
