## Findings

### Bottom line

Hilton Grand Vacations has an adequate but weakening corporate credit profile: leverage is supportable on recurring fees and non-recourse receivables funding, but falling sales and debt-funded buybacks are eroding the cushion creditors rely on.

Corporate net debt was 3.88x EBITDA excluding construction timing at September 30, 2026 and would be 4.02x at the midpoint of the cut 2026 guidance. In nine months the company raised a net USD 339 million of corporate debt while spending USD 410 million on buybacks and USD 103 million on Elara, and cash plus revolver capacity fell to USD 684 million from USD 1,048 million. The USD 2,891 million of non-recourse debt is payable only from pledged receivables and is not a claim on corporate cash flow.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_hgv-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 4, 22; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_hgvq32026earningsreleaseex.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 19; calculation C4; calculation C6; calculation C17; calculation C22; calculation C23

### Key findings

Corporate leverage is moderate on the issuer's own basis, but reported figures flatter it and the guidance cut pushes it higher. Corporate debt, net was 3.41x last-twelve-months adjusted EBITDA at September 30, 2026, yet that EBITDA includes net construction recognitions of USD 218 million; measured on principal net of cash and without them, corporate leverage is 3.88x, and at the low end, midpoint and high end of the cut 2026 guidance it is 4.05x, 4.02x and 4.00x. A further USD 100 million shortfall against the midpoint would lift it to 4.40x. The company reports total net leverage of about 3.9x without printing its definition or any covenant level, so the cushion to a maintenance test cannot be measured.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_hgv-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 22; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_hgvq32026earningsreleaseex.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C4; calculation C6; calculation C7; calculation C8; calculation C9; calculation C10

The gap was met with corporate borrowing: net corporate debt proceeds were USD 339 million, revolver drawings rose by USD 360 million, and cash plus remaining revolver capacity fell 34.73% to USD 684 million.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better view is that debt service is comfortable and the pressure is largely discretionary: interest cover is 5.07x on adjusted EBITDA and 4.30x without construction recognitions, corporate maturities through 2027 are small, nine-month free cash flow of USD 273 million is far above the USD 29 million of a year earlier, buybacks can be stopped at the board's choice, and the chief executive reports initial signs of progress as the third quarter ended. The view would improve if contract sales stabilise, repurchases pause while the revolver is repaid and non-accrual receivables stop rising. It would weaken if fourth-quarter contract sales fall further, guidance is cut again, revolver drawings rise to fund buybacks, or receivable delinquency keeps climbing.

### Company description and ownership

Hilton Grand Vacations sells and finances timeshare vacation ownership intervals under a Hilton licence and earns fees from managing resorts and clubs. Sales of intervals are the largest revenue line and the most cyclical, financing and management fees are recurring, and rental of unsold inventory runs below its direct cost, so the business is moderately cyclical and the recurring lines carry the debt service in a weak sales year. Properties are concentrated in Florida, Europe, Hawaii and a few other states, which ties cash flow to leisure travel demand in those destinations.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_hgv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 16

The company is publicly listed, and the annual report describes Apollo as a significant stockholder rather than a controlling owner. Funds affiliated with Apollo hold designation and consent rights that narrow as the holding falls, Hilton owns no shares, and non-recourse securitization vehicles are consolidated but their debt is not a legal liability of the issuer. The credit consequence is that sponsor control risk is receding while the company's own buybacks and the sponsor's share sales interact, and the proxy statement is needed to test board alignment.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_hgv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 68; 10-Q_2026-09-30_hgv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20

### Revenue drivers, cost structure and cash conversion

Revenue rests on repeat owners and on financing: existing owners made most contract sales, and financing propensity rose to 70% from 66%, so each sale adds a receivable that earns interest and must be funded. The risk is that sales fell in the third quarter while more were financed, which grows the receivables to fund when volume is weakest; the implication is that funding market access matters more than usual.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_hgv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 41; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_hgvq32026earningsreleaseex.htm.jsonl, page 19

Costs are largely fixed in the short run: 99 sales distribution centers, 146 Bass Pro locations with minimum annual payments and commitments to buy inventory over 12 years give little room to cut quickly, and net sales and marketing cost rose while sales fell. Operating leverage therefore works against margins in a downturn, which is the main reason EBITDA is more volatile than revenue.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.1_2026-10-29_hgvq32026earningsreleaseex.htm.jsonl, page 19; calculation C25; calculation C26

Cash conversion is unstable because deferred revenue and net construction recognitions move earnings away from cash. Third-quarter operating cash flow was far below the earnings recognised, and last-twelve-months free cash flow is 27.75% of adjusted EBITDA, so EBITDA overstates the cash available for debt service in this period.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-10-29_hgvq32026earningsreleaseex.htm.jsonl, page 17; calculation C14

### Competitive and market forces

Competitive rivalry is high: the industry is fragmented, competitors include other branded timeshare operators and hotel, cruise and home-sharing alternatives, and consolidation may create rivals with cheaper capital. The mechanism is pressure on tour efficiency and marketing cost, which shows in falling volume per guest, and the implication is that margins have limited protection.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_hgv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 16

New entrants face the barrier of brand, financing and sales distribution, and the Hilton non-compete keeps the licensor out of timeshare, which protects the franchise; substitutes are resorts, rentals and secondary-market resales, which cap pricing power and are a risk when consumers trade down.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_hgv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 22, 33

Buyers are consumers financed at a weighted 14.2% on originated loans, and most sales go to existing owners, which supports repeat sales but exposes the issuer to credit losses; suppliers include the licensor, developers who sell inventory and the asset-backed investors who fund receivables, and the licensor and investors have the most power over fees and funding cost.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_hgv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 21, 22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_hgv-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 25, 35, 41

Regulation and economic conditions are the credit-relevant PEST categories: marketing and sales are highly regulated with purchaser cancellation rights, and demand follows consumer confidence and the cost of financing. Political and technological factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver based on the provided materials.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_hgv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 17

In credit terms the strengths are receivables funding outside corporate debt, recurring management fees, an exclusive long licence and repeat owners; the weaknesses are sales conversion, selling cost rigidity, debt-funded buybacks and receivable quality; the opportunities that could improve credit are stabilising volume per guest and the integration of acquired properties into the Hilton brands; the threats are a weaker consumer, tighter securitization markets and the fee step-up.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_hgv-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 24

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months the headwinds are weak sales conversion, the guidance cut, a fee step-up on Bluegreen revenue from 2027 and leverage near or above 4.00x; the tailwinds are the company's reported early progress, the option to stop buybacks and a sound maturity profile. The implication is that leverage depends on management's choice of capital allocation more than on near-term refinancing.

### Qualitative downside scenario

The downside starts with sales conversion staying at the third-quarter level, with volume per guest down and contract sales falling by about a tenth. Selling costs are largely fixed, so EBITDA excluding deferrals falls below the guided range; a further USD 100 million shortfall against the midpoint would lift corporate leverage to 4.40x. Free cash flow then falls short of buybacks again, the revolver absorbs the gap, and the liquidity of USD 684 million shrinks while the 2028 Term Loan A and 2029 notes approach. Management can stop buybacks, which carry USD 617 million of authorisation, cut inventory purchases where commitments allow cancellation, and slow receivable origination, and with corporate maturities of only USD 28 million through 2027 resilience appears sufficient to avoid a liquidity event, but not to keep leverage near 3.9x without a change in capital allocation.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_hgv-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 35, 59; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_hgvq32026earningsreleaseex.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C10; calculation C22

### Overall credit view

Overall, Hilton Grand Vacations presents an adequate business profile supported by non-recourse receivables funding, recurring management fees and an exclusive long licence, but credit quality is constrained by falling sales conversion, sticky selling costs and debt-funded buybacks. Cash-flow durability appears weak to adequate because free cash flow swings with construction timing and covered only part of shareholder returns. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on stabilising contract sales, the securitization markets and a pause in buybacks before the 2028 and 2029 maturities. The primary downside path is a further fall in sales conversion that lifts corporate leverage above 4.00x while the revolver funds buybacks. Further analysis would require covenant terms, securitization trigger levels, peer evidence and the proxy statement. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: falling sales conversion with debt-funded buybacks.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C6
