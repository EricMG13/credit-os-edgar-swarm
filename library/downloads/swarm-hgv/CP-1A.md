## Findings

### Bottom line

Hilton Grand Vacations has a durable licensed franchise, but sales are falling, receivables funding must keep rolling and the sponsor is selling down while the company buys back stock, so creditors carry execution risk more than franchise risk.

About 73% of contract sales come from existing owners, resort management earns cost-plus fees, and the initial term of the Hilton licence has about 90 years to run. Against that, contract sales fell to USD 807 million in the third quarter from USD 907 million, guidance was cut by 5.82% at the midpoint, the discounted Hilton fee on Bluegreen revenue steps up to 5% in the fourth year after the 2024 closing, and a second USD 600 million repurchase plan was approved in August 2026. Governance is only partly assessable because the proxy statement was not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_hgv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 21, 22, 165; 10-Q_2026-09-30_hgv-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 29, 41; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_hgvq32026earningsreleaseex.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 19; calculation C18; calculation C11

### Key findings

The franchise is long-dated and exclusive, but it rests on one licensor and the fee is a growing claim on revenue. Hilton kept no ownership after the 2017 spin-off, the initial licence term has about 90 years to run with exclusivity in vacation ownership for about 25 years, and the fee is 5% of gross revenues, with license fee expense equal to 4.24% of total revenues in 2025. Reduced fees of 2% to 4% on acquired Diamond and Bluegreen revenue climb to 5%, and if annual rebranding milestones are missed the company owes an escalated fee of up to an additional 1%, while missed cumulative targets would let Hilton bar sales of its HGV Max product. The Bluegreen discount is the nearer risk because the fourth year after the January 2024 closing begins in 2027.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_hgv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 6, 21, 22, 23, 113; calculation C9; calculation C11; calculation C12

Customers are loyal but are buying less per tour, and management has acknowledged it. Existing owners made 74% of contract sales in 2025 and 73% in the nine months to September 30, 2026, and tour flow rose 1.7% in the third quarter, but volume per guest fell 12.5%, so contract sales were USD 2,336 million for nine months against USD 2,462 million and USD 807 million in the quarter against USD 907 million. The chief executive said the sales initiatives are taking longer to ramp than expected, guidance for 2026 adjusted EBITDA excluding deferrals and recognitions was cut by 5.82% at the midpoint, and a promotion to chief sales and marketing officer was announced on the same day. The member base is also slightly smaller, down 0.78% from a year earlier.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_hgv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_hgv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 41; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_hgvq32026earningsreleaseex.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 4, 19, 20; calculation C18; calculation C17

The sponsor is exiting in steps, and its board and consent rights narrow as its stake falls. Apollo-managed holders sold shares in the fourth quarter of 2024, the third quarter of 2025 and June 2026, held 22.41% of the shares in February 2026, and in the two underwritten offerings the company bought back shares from the underwriters for USD 40 million and USD 38 million. The stockholders agreement gives Apollo two board seats, falling to one and then none as its holding drops through two thresholds, and a consent right over some charter changes and board size while it holds at least the lower threshold. In February 2026 the holding exceeded the higher threshold by only a small margin, so if all shares sold in June had counted towards it one seat would have lapsed while the consent right would remain, but the filings do not state the holding after that sale.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_hgv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 28, 69, 157; 10-Q_2026-06-30_hgv-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_hgv-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 31; calculation C19; calculation C20; calculation C21; calculation C22; calculation C23; calculation C24; calculation C25

Capital allocation favours shareholders over debt reduction. The company repurchased USD 600 million of stock in 2025 and USD 410 million in the nine months to September 30, 2026, against USD 450 million in the same months of 2025, and a second USD 600 million plan approved on August 12, 2026 starts once the USD 17 million left under the current plan is used. The filings state no plan to pay cash dividends, so buybacks, including those that absorb sponsor sales, are the shareholder-return channel and compete with creditors for the same cash. The share count fell 6.88% between February and October 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_hgv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 72, 77, 114, 155; 10-Q_2026-09-30_hgv-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 29, 59; calculation C27

The acquisitions explain the debt and the perimeter, and the latest one brought its own non-recourse funding. Bluegreen cost USD 1.6 billion including net debt in January 2024, was paid in cash and brought the Big Cedar joint venture, in which the company holds 51%, although the documents read do not detail its acquired balance sheet. Elara, bought on April 29, 2026 for USD 131 million of cash, added USD 411 million of timeshare receivables and USD 310 million of non-recourse debt, so most of its funding does not rank against corporate creditors, although the purchase accounting is still preliminary.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_hgv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 89, 96, 132; 10-Q_2026-09-30_hgv-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 9

New borrowing in 2026 is mostly receivables funding, and the one corporate change extended maturities at unchanged pricing. The company closed securitizations of about USD 500 million and USD 300 million of receivables in April and June, renewed its warehouse facility to USD 1.0 billion, opened a USD 200 million facility for the Big Cedar receivables in September, and on July 17 refinanced the Term Loan B due 2028 into a USD 850 million Term Loan B due 2033 at the same margin of SOFR plus 2.00%. Access to the receivables markets therefore carries the growth that corporate debt does not, which is the dependency creditors should watch.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-03-31_hgv-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-Q_2026-09-30_hgv-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 23, 25

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against concern is the earnings base that does not depend on sales: resort operations and club management earned USD 440 million of segment adjusted EBITDA in the nine months, level with USD 441 million a year earlier, on cost-plus fees that the company describes as highly predictable. The licence is long and exclusive, existing owners are most of the buyers, and the chief executive reports initial signs of progress as the third quarter ended. The view would weaken if contract sales fall again in the fourth quarter, if the Bluegreen fee step-up arrives before sales recover, or if the proxy statement shows board or pay arrangements that favour shareholders over creditors. It would strengthen if volume per guest recovers, repurchases slow while corporate debt is paid down, and the sponsor's remaining holding and board rights are shown to be settled.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_hgv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 27, 28; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_hgvq32026earningsreleaseex.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 18

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The acquisitions that shape the balance sheet are Diamond Resorts in August 2021, Bluegreen Vacations in January 2024 and the remaining 75% of Elara in April 2026. Diamond was paid for in HGV shares, which left funds controlled by Apollo and other former owners with 28% of the company at completion. Bluegreen was an all-cash transaction worth USD 1.6 billion including net debt, and it brought the Big Cedar joint venture, a ten-year exclusive Bass Pro marketing agreement that took effect on the same date, and the reduced Hilton fee on Bluegreen revenue. Elara, a joint venture in which the company held 25%, was bought on April 29, 2026 for USD 131 million of cash after an announced price of USD 129 million, and it added USD 411 million of timeshare receivables and USD 310 million of non-recourse debt, with goodwill of USD 2 million on preliminary accounting. In the second quarter the company also sold its interests in certain resort properties under an agreement dated April 24, 2026, booking a loss of USD 48 million and keeping a right to share in future sale proceeds.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_hgv-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 38, 132; EX-99.1_2026-04-30_hgvq12026earningsreleaseex.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-03-31_hgv-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-Q_2026-09-30_hgv-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 9, 35, 36

Debt issued or reset in 2026 is mostly non-recourse receivables funding. A securitization of USD 500 million of timeshare loans closed on April 16, 2026 at an overall weighted average rate of 5.13%, and a second of about USD 300 million followed in June. The Timeshare Facility was renewed in May at USD 1.0 billion with a commitment period to May 2028 and maturity to May 2029, and a USD 200 million warehouse facility for the Big Cedar receivables was entered into on September 14. The only corporate change was on July 17, when the Term Loan B due 2028 was refinanced into an amended USD 850 million Term Loan B due 2033 at an unchanged margin of SOFR plus 2.00%, and the documents read show no new corporate notes in 2026. Funding access for receivables is therefore the dependency creditors should watch, because it carries the growth that corporate debt does not.
