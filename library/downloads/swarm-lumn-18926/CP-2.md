### Company and ownership in credit terms

Lumen sells enterprise, public-sector and wholesale network services over a large owned and leased fiber network, and the business is now one reportable segment after the 2026-02-02 sale of its fiber-to-the-home operations. Service contracts average about 38 months for Business customers, USD 6.1 billion of performance obligations is contracted, and no customer is above 10% of revenue, so cash-flow visibility is reasonable through 2027 and concentration is low. The weakness is that Legacy revenue, 39.30% of Q3 revenue, is in decline and Mass Markets, 13.59%, is mainly copper broadband and voice.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_lumn-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 21, 36; 10-K_2025-12-31_lumn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 149; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Lumen is a widely held public holding company with no sponsor named, so the owner-extraction risk is low, and the common dividend was discontinued in 2022. Debt sits in four borrowing groups, 74.22% at Level 3 Financing, and Lumen is issuer or guarantor of 71.23% of principal, so recovery and refinancing depend on entity lines; Business & governance rates governance and sponsor risk as medium because of repeated liability management that changed rankings. The proxy statement and executed financing documents are not supplied, so concentration of holders and legal capacity are not established.

### Revenue durability

Revenue is largely recurring and contracted but structurally declining in the legacy part. In Q3 2026 Strategic revenue was USD 1,279 million, up 10%, and fell 0.78% from Q2, while Legacy revenue was USD 1,067 million, down 17%, and fell 7.62% from Q2, so the mix is shifting but the sequential Strategic line is flat. Legacy products are voice, private line and traditional data services that customers move to alternatives, and a 7.00 percentage point rise in Strategic share of Business revenue in a year means the decline in Legacy sets the pace of group revenue. The credit implication is that revenue visibility is adequate but total revenue falls until Strategic gains exceed Legacy losses, and the 2028 return to Business growth is a management statement.

### Margin resilience

Costs have fallen as fast as revenue: cost of services fell 15.76% and selling, general and administrative expense 5.43% in Q3 2026, together 12.38% against revenue down 12.05%, which kept the Adjusted EBITDA margin excluding Special Items at 26.3% against 25.5% and 28.1% against 27.7% over nine months. The sold business carried network cost, so part of the fall is the sale and not savings, and the company says it is managing vendor cost increases and dis-synergies from the divestitures. Margins can be defended under this pace of volume decline, but absolute earnings fall about as fast as revenue, and the net cash cost of Special Items, USD 912 million in nine months, is outside the headline margin.

### Cash conversion and capital intensity

Earnings convert poorly into free cash flow. Nine-month operating cash flow of USD 2,812 million is made of USD 466 million of earnings after non-cash items and USD 2,346 million of working capital and other items, led by a USD 1,832 million rise in deferred revenue and USD 875 million of accrued taxes, and capital expenditure of USD 2,717 million absorbs it. Capital expenditure is 38.05% of last-twelve-month revenue and guided at USD 3,200 million to USD 3,400 million, which is 96.97% to 109.68% of the Adjusted EBITDA outlook, partly funded by customer advance payments that are applied to network projects. The company expects the sale to cut Mass Markets fiber capital expenditure by about USD 1 billion a year, which helps, but maintenance versus growth spending is not split, so flexibility over the maturity wall is not established.

### Market forces, regulation and credit-translated SWOT

Competitive rivalry and substitution are high: competitors include global providers, hyperscalers, cable and wireless companies, and some hyperscaler customers have built infrastructure that reduced their reliance on Lumen's network, which could pressure Wholesale and Legacy revenue; buyer power is high for large enterprise and carrier customers on contracts of about 38 months; supplier power is moderate because Lumen relies on a limited number of equipment suppliers and leases part of its fiber from other carriers. New entrants are limited by capital intensity, but hyperscaler self-build is the main threat to volumes.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lumn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 16, 29; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lumn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21

Regulation matters to credit through universal service funding, which Lumen gave up by relinquishing its Rural Digital Opportunity Fund awards for USD 99 million, and through a federal investigation of Huawei equipment in its networks and legacy lead-sheathed cable suits, which are contingent and carry a litigation accrual of USD 50 million. The credit-translated strengths are liquidity, cost flexibility and a diversified customer base; the weaknesses are cash conversion, shrinking revenue and capital intensity; the opportunities are AI-driven demand for private connectivity and network-as-a-service, which management says has more than 4,000 customers; the threats are hyperscaler self-build, prepayment slowdown and a further liability management round.

### Downside scenario

The issuer-specific downside starts with a slowdown in customer prepayments while Legacy decline continues. The change in deferred revenue has already fallen 25.77% in nine months, and operating cash flow before it is USD 980 million against capital expenditure of USD 2,717 million, so a further fall in prepayments cuts operating cash flow directly while contracted network builds and the guided USD 3,200 million to USD 3,400 million of capital expenditure keep cash out. At the guidance midpoints earnings less interest and capital expenditure is negative USD 800 million a year, before working capital, prepayments, taxes and Special Items, so liquidity of USD 1,655 million would last about 2.07 years, or 1.68 years at the last-twelve-month rate, before Special Items cash costs, and a further 9.15% fall in normalized Adjusted EBITDA would take net leverage to 5.81x against a 5.25x revolver test whose definition is not printed.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_lumn-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 27, 54; EX-99.1_2026-11-04_lumnq320268-kexhibit991.htm.jsonl, page 3; calculation C8; calculation C14; calculation C15

Management can respond by deferring capital expenditure, which depends on contracted builds, and by cost cuts, which have kept margins stable, and the near-term maturity profile does not force a refinancing, so resilience looks sufficient to avoid liquidity distress through 2027 unless revolver access is lost on a covenant breach. The observable triggers are Q4 free cash flow, the change in deferred revenue, Strategic revenue against the Legacy decline, and any change in the covenant compliance statement.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Lumen presents an adequate business profile supported by liquidity, cost flexibility, a diversified customer base and a lower interest burden, but credit quality is constrained by weak cash conversion, a shrinking revenue base and capital intensity. Cash-flow durability appears weak because free cash flow depends on customer prepayments and one-off receipts. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on Q4 free cash flow, the pace of Strategic growth and ratings of B2 and B, as maturities are remote. The primary downside path is a slowdown in prepayments with Legacy decline continuing, which would turn free cash flow negative and use liquidity. Further analysis would require the covenant definition, prepayment contract terms, the maintenance share of capital expenditure and peer data.
