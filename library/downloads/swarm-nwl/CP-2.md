## Request

This is the Fundamental credit assessment for NEWELL BRANDS INC. (issuer id nwl), as of 2026-11-05, on the credit snapshot pathway. It combines the Financial foundation, Business & governance and Source readiness work with the seven supplied filings and releases into an overall credit view, a downside path, a ranked list of drivers and monitoring triggers. It assigns no rating and takes no view on security selection, legal capacity or sizing.

## Findings

### Bottom line

Fundamental credit quality is weak but not distressed: debt is 4.70x issuer-normalized EBITDA yet 14.32x reported EBITDA, free cash flow is near zero, and USD 1,250 million of notes due 2028 must be refinanced from a business that has only just stopped shrinking.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17; calculation D1; calculation D4; calculation D7

Liquidity of USD 729 million and no note maturities before 2028 give time, and two early refinancings show market access. Against that, adjusted earnings cover interest after capital expenditure only 2.43x, the profit pool is narrow, and the asset-based facility can mature ahead of the 2028 notes. Governance risk is medium and does not add to the downside.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; calculation D3; calculation D6; calculation D7

### Key findings

Leverage is acceptable only on the issuer’s own adjusted earnings, and those flatter the position. At 2026-09-30 debt is 14.32x and net debt 13.59x reported EBITDA, while issuer-normalized EBITDA of USD 1,030 million gives 4.70x and 4.46x; USD 441 million of the USD 692 million of add-backs is impairment, but the figure also carries about USD 100 million of refunded 2025 tariffs and USD 80 million of restructuring that has recurred every year. Removing both leaves USD 850 million and 5.70x, and removing only the refund leaves 5.21x, so a prudent reading is 5.21x to 5.70x rather than 4.70x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 100; EX-99.1_2026-07-31_exhibit991_q2xjunex2026.htm.jsonl, page 2; EX-99.1_2026-10-30_exhibit991_q3xseptemberx20.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 18; calculation D1; calculation D2

Cash conversion is the binding weakness: the business does not yet turn adjusted earnings into free cash flow. Free cash flow was USD 17 million in FY2025 and USD 53 million over the last twelve months, against cash interest of USD 354 million in FY2025 and dividends of USD 120 million, so free cash flow after dividends was negative USD 103 million in FY2025 and negative USD 135 million in the nine months of 2026 and total debt rose by USD 168 million in those nine months. Management has since cut its 2026 operating cash flow outlook to about USD 150 million from about USD 400 million at the second quarter, so debt can fall only through working capital release, lower dividends or asset sales rather than from operations.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 82; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 17; EX-99.1_2026-07-31_exhibit991_q2xjunex2026.htm.jsonl, page 4; EX-99.1_2026-10-30_exhibit991_q3xseptemberx20.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation D4; calculation D5; calculation D17

Liquidity is adequate for the next two years but seasonal and limited by the borrowing base. Cash of USD 249 million, of which USD 174 million is held outside the United States, plus USD 480 million of net availability gives USD 729 million, or 15.06% of debt, and the first-quarter 2026 low was USD 528 million after facility borrowings rose by USD 340 million in the first half. A repeat of that first-half draw together with a full-year cash shortfall of USD 178 million at the guided run rate would use 71.1% of today’s liquidity by mid-year, which leaves little room if the dividend continues.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-03-31_nwl-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-06-30_nwl-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 48; calculation D6; calculation D13

Refinancing risk sits in 2028 to 2030 and is tied to the bank facility. No note principal falls due before 2028, but the USD 1,250 million of notes due 2028, USD 500 million due 2029 and USD 750 million due 2030 make up 51.0% of principal, and the asset-based facility matures 91 days before any debt of USD 125 million or more that is still outstanding, so the 2028 notes and the USD 215 million drawn on the facility fall into the same window. The company showed market access by issuing USD 1,250 million of notes at a coupon 430 bp above the notes replaced and USD 600 million of notes at a coupon 12.5 bp below the 2027 notes it redeemed, and its ratings were last disclosed as B2 and B+.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 59, 107; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 20; calculation D7; calculation D18

The business profile is adequate on scale but narrow on profit and exposed to two buyers. Net sales of USD 7,313 million over the last twelve months span six businesses, yet Learning and Development earned 80.2% of segment operating income before impairments in FY2025, two retailers took 30% of sales without supply contracts, and net sales fell 11.4% over two years before rising 2.1% in the nine months of 2026, of which core growth was 0.9%. The recovery is real but small, and it rests on Learning and Development and currency.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D11

Margins have little resilience, because cost flexibility has come mainly from incentive pay and capital spending and neither is a durable saving. In FY2025 net sales fell USD 378 million and gross profit USD 116 million, 30.7% of the sales decline, and operating income fell only USD 28 million because incentive compensation fell about USD 95 million; without that, operating income would have fallen USD 123 million. Incentive pay rose again in 2026, capital expenditure was cut by USD 46 million in the nine months, and the nine-month gross margin of 36.4% would be 34.6% without the tariff refund, so a sales shortfall would again fall mainly on profit.

Financial policy and governance neither help nor hurt the credit materially, but the dividend and the secured claims on receivables matter at the margin. The company has no sponsor or controlling holder and no liability-management episode, yet it pays a dividend that free cash flow does not cover and has given a receivables purchaser a first-priority lien on certain unsold receivables, while USD 435 million of sold receivables sit outside reported debt. Business & governance assigns medium governance risk, which this view accepts.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 10; calculation D5; calculation D12

Contingent claims are large against cash and unresolved, so they limit the view rather than define it. A transfer-pricing assessment of USD 80 million of tax and USD 34 million of penalties, foreign-dividend regulation exposure of USD 180 million to USD 220 million, certified Baby class actions and Lower Passaic River costs are all open, while cash is USD 249 million; management regards most as immaterial or adequately reserved.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a better view is that cash conversion is held down by items that should fade. Adjusted EBITDA less capital expenditure and interest is USD 488 million over the last twelve months against free cash flow of USD 53 million, a gap of USD 435 million made up of taxes, working capital, restructuring cash and other items; inventory is USD 315 million above year-end for expected sales, executive-plan payments move cash into operating outflows, and tariff costs are being refunded. Net sales grew 3.0% and 3.7% in the second and third quarters, management guides a 10.0% to 10.4% normalized operating margin for 2026, and the early refinancings removed every note maturity before 2028.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-07-31_exhibit991_q2xjunex2026.htm.jsonl, page 2; EX-99.1_2026-10-30_exhibit991_q3xseptemberx20.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 49; calculation D6; calculation D17; calculation D19

The case for a worse view is that the same items keep recurring: restructuring has been announced four times since January 2023, impairments were USD 339 million, USD 345 million and USD 340 million in the last three years, and the 2026 operating cash flow outlook was cut by USD 250 million within one quarter. The view would improve if free cash flow after dividends turned positive and total debt fell by about the couple of hundred million dollars management indicates by the 2026 year-end, or if the 2028 notes were refinanced early. It would worsen if first-half 2027 borrowing on the asset-based facility exceeded the USD 340 million rise of 2026, if sales growth reverted to decline, or if the 2028 notes were still outstanding as the springing date approached.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 32, 99; EX-99.1_2026-10-30_exhibit991_q3xseptemberx20.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 4; calculation D6; calculation D16; calculation D17

### Limitations

- Covenant levels, the springing fixed charge trigger and the borrowing base are not supplied: the liquidity and refinancing conclusions take the stated availability as given and cannot test headroom, so a tighter borrowing base or an earlier trigger would lower the liquidity buffer and shorten the time to the 2028 notes.
- The maturity profile after the 2026 refinancing is derived from stated offering amounts, replaces the 2025-12-31 ladder in Financial foundation that still shows the 2027 notes, and differs from carrying value by USD 62 million: the 51.0% share of principal due in 2028 to 2030 would move if the amount or timing of the 2036 and 2046 notes differs from the derivation.
- Adjusted EBITDA is the issuer’s definition and the removal of the tariff refund and restructuring is own arithmetic: the 5.21x to 5.70x range is a prudence test, and the refund haircut is an upper bound because the twelve-month window also carries part of the 2025 tariff cost.
- Interest cover beyond FY2025 uses interest expense, not cash interest, because quarterly reports print none: cash cover over the last twelve months could differ from the 3.02x and 2.43x stated.
- No peer set, bond prices or current ratings are supplied: the weak-but-not-distressed view is absolute, not relative, and cannot be placed against comparable issuers or against how the bonds trade.
- Management’s 2026 outlook is the only forward information and no 2027 outlook is stated: the downside arithmetic runs at 2026 rates and a faster sales recovery or a new cost shock would move the liquidity result in either direction.

Strengths.

Liquidity and the removal of note maturities before 2028 give the company time. Cash of USD 249 million and net availability of USD 480 million make USD 729 million, 15.06% of debt, and the 2026 refinancings redeemed the 2027 notes and replaced the revolver due in 2027, so near-term default risk is low and the company has until 2028 to fix its cash conversion.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18–20; calculation D6; calculation D7

Learning and Development is a strong profit pool. It earned a 25.9% operating margin in the nine months of 2026 and 80.2% of segment operating income before impairments in FY2025, so while it holds, it supports debt service even though the other segments earn little.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 131; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 28, 47; calculation D11

Scale and brands cushion shocks, and sales have turned up. Net sales of USD 7,313 million span six businesses and grew 3.0% and 3.7% in the second and third quarters of 2026, which supports revenue visibility if distribution gains hold.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 44; EX-99.1_2026-07-31_exhibit991_q2xjunex2026.htm.jsonl, page 2; EX-99.1_2026-10-30_exhibit991_q3xseptemberx20.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation D11; calculation D17

Market access has been demonstrated despite B-category ratings as last disclosed. The company placed USD 1,250 million of notes in 2025 and USD 600 million in 2026, the second at a coupon 12.5 bp below the notes it replaced, so refinancing is likely to be available if sales and cash flow stabilise.

### Company description

Newell Brands sells household, commercial, writing, baby and outdoor products under well-known brands, mainly to large retailers and online sellers, and had USD 7,313 million of net sales over the last twelve months. Business & governance sets out the segments and brands; for credit, three features matter. Sales run through a few large buyers on purchase orders, the business is seasonal with most cash generated in the second half, and many costs are fixed, so a sales shortfall reaches profit and cash quickly. The company has no sponsor or controlling holder and a single registrant issues the notes, so creditors depend on operating cash flow and the company’s own financial policy.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 10, 14; calculation D11

### Ownership and group structure

The company is widely held and the parent is the issuer of the notes and a borrower under the asset-based facility, with an Irish subsidiary as co-borrower and unnamed subsidiary guarantors. A consolidated special purpose entity sells receivables and its assets are not available to other creditors, and USD 435 million of receivables sold under both programmes sit outside reported debt. The asset-based facility is secured on deposit accounts, receivables, inventory, equipment and intellectual property, and since September 2026 a receivables purchaser also holds a first-priority lien on certain unsold receivables, so the senior unsecured notes due 2028 and 2031 rank behind the most liquid assets. Whether the other notes are guaranteed, and any restricted-group boundary, are not supplied, so structural subordination is described here and not sized.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 10, 18, 19; calculation D12

Business & governance finds no sponsor behaviour to price, a dividend cut by 36.4% since 2023 and kept, no repurchase programme and no exchange or priming step, and rates governance and sponsor risk as medium. For this view the consequence is that creditors cannot expect equity support in stress but also face no owner extraction; the dividend of about USD 120 million a year is the only recurring leak and is not covered by free cash flow.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation D5; calculation D17

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is discretionary, concentrated and cyclical rather than recurring, and the evidence shows only a recent and modest turn. Net sales fell 11.4% between FY2023 and FY2025 and rose 2.1% in the nine months of 2026, with innovation launches and distribution gains behind the increase and soft international demand against it; core sales, the issuer’s own measure, rose 0.9%, so more than half of the reported gain is currency. Amazon and Walmart took 30% of net sales on purchase orders with no minimum purchase commitments, and the annual report says retailer power may limit the recovery of cost increases through pricing. The mechanism is that a destocking or delisting decision by one large buyer removes sales within a quarter, and Business & governance records one such distribution loss in 2024 that led to an impairment, so revenue visibility is short and pricing power limited.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 10, 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 41, 44; calculation D11

Net pricing in 2026 is partly an accounting effect: nine-month net sales include USD 25 million from a refinement of customer-programme estimates, which will not recur at that scale. The creditor implication is that underlying price realization is weaker than the reported net pricing suggests.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 44

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins can be defended against modest volume pressure but not against a sustained shortfall, because the flexible costs have already been used. Many costs are fixed, more than half of employees work in manufacturing and supply chain, inputs include resin, glass, steel and aluminum, and resin and transport costs rose in 2026 with oil prices; in FY2025 gross profit fell by 30.7% of the sales decline and operating income was held only by lower incentive pay. Normalized overheads were 20.0% of net sales in the nine months of 2026 against 20.2%, while advertising and promotion rose to USD 325 million from USD 306 million, so management is spending to restore volume. Tariffs add a cash cost ahead of the earnings cost, USD 174 million in 2025 against USD 114 million in cost of products sold, and the 2026 gross margin is flattered by a refund. Productivity savings from the programmes announced since January 2023 are not quantified, so the offset to input costs cannot be sized.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 10, 14, 51, 53; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 6; EX-99.1_2026-10-30_exhibit991_q3xseptemberx20.htm.jsonl, page 18; calculation D9; calculation D14

### Capital intensity and free cash flow conversion

Capital intensity is moderate and flexible, but conversion of earnings into free cash flow is poor because interest, taxes, restructuring and working capital absorb most of the adjusted EBITDA. Capital expenditure was 0.79x depreciation and amortization in FY2025 and 0.56x in the nine months of 2026, a cut of USD 46 million that supports cash flow but may defer reinvestment, and the maintenance share is not disclosed. Adjusted EBITDA less capital expenditure and interest is USD 488 million over the last twelve months while free cash flow is USD 53 million; cash taxes were USD 137 million in FY2025, restructuring and executive-plan payments continue in 2026, and the inventory build of USD 315 million absorbed cash in the nine months. The creditor implication is that adjusted EBITDA overstates debt service capacity until working capital normalizes and restructuring ends, and free cash flow before dividends is not yet positive enough to cover USD 120 million of annual dividends.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 82; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 15, 49; calculation D4; calculation D10; calculation D19

### Porter’s five forces

Buyer power is the dominant force: two customers took 30% of net sales, the company generally has no long-term supply contracts, and consolidating customers may harmonize pricing and reduce inventory. This pressures margin and revenue visibility directly, so it raises default risk in a downturn and limits recovery of cost increases.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 10, 13, 14; calculation D11

Competitive rivalry is high: the company competes with many large, well-established manufacturers and distributors and with retailers’ own private-label and directly imported generic products. The mechanism is price competition and shelf-space contests, which weigh on margin and push spending to advertising and promotion; the implication is slower deleveraging.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 13

### PEST analysis

Political and regulatory factors are a primary credit driver here, so this analysis is included. Tariffs cost about USD 174 million in cash in 2025, the Supreme Court invalidated the tariffs imposed under one statute in February 2026 and refunds of about USD 107 million were received in the third quarter, but new tariffs under other statutes now apply and refund class actions are pending; the mechanism is a cash cost that runs ahead of earnings and a refund that is non-recurring, so free cash flow is volatile and 2026 margins are flattered. Product-safety and environmental rules add smaller exposures, such as the French door oven recall and Lower Passaic River costs.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 38, 51; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33; calculation D14

Economic factors weigh on revenue and cost: management cites a soft consumer backdrop, higher resin and transport costs with oil prices, and interest rates that raised the discount rate in impairment tests; about 39% of sales are denominated in foreign currencies. Social and technological factors matter through retailer and consumer behaviour: shifting preferences, the growth of e-commerce and direct-to-consumer channels, and the use of artificial intelligence by consumers and retailers change where and how products are bought, which adds to buyer power and requires marketing spend.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 14, 19, 74; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 7

### SWOT analysis

Strengths are liquidity of USD 729 million with no note maturities before 2028, a Learning and Development profit pool with a 25.9% operating margin in the nine months of 2026, scale across six businesses, demonstrated market access in 2025 and 2026, and the absence of a sponsor that could extract value. Weaknesses are near-zero free cash flow, reported leverage of 14.32x that depends on add-backs to reach 4.70x, concentration of profit and customers, an uncovered dividend, and recurring restructuring and impairment. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are a sustained sales recovery, savings from the Productivity Plan, working capital release from the inventory build, and disposals of non-core brands that the company is reviewing. Threats that could weaken it are tariffs under other statutes, retailer destocking, the 2028 refinancing with the springing maturity of the asset-based facility, and the open legal and tax claims.

### Downside scenario

The downside that fits this issuer is a retailer-led sales shortfall in the first half of 2027 on top of the cash costs that remain. The initiating condition is a destocking or distribution loss at one or two large retailers, which together take 30% of sales, while tariffs under other statutes and resin and freight costs stay high and the refund of 2025 tariffs has mostly been collected. The first operational break is in Home and Commercial Solutions, which is 52.4% of net sales, earned a 0.3% GAAP operating margin in the nine months of 2026 and carries the fixed cost base; Learning and Development, the profit engine, would need to hold.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 27, 28; calculation D11

The financial transmission can be read from FY2025, when net sales fell 5.0%, gross profit fell USD 116 million and free cash flow fell from USD 237 million to USD 17 million. At the 2026 guided operating cash flow of about USD 150 million, last twelve months capital expenditure of USD 201 million and dividends of USD 127 million, the annual cash shortfall is USD 178 million, and adding the USD 340 million first-half seasonal rise in borrowing uses 71.1% of the USD 729 million of liquidity by mid-year; this is illustrative arithmetic at 2026 rates and not a forecast. Lower factoring would add to the draw, since the smaller programme held USD 135 million against a new limit of USD 75 million.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 52; EX-99.1_2026-10-30_exhibit991_q3xseptemberx20.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 10, 34; calculation D4; calculation D6; calculation D9; calculation D12; calculation D13

Management has levers: suspending the dividend would cut the annual shortfall to USD 51 million and the same mid-year test to 53.6% of liquidity, capital expenditure was already cut by USD 46 million, the inventory build of USD 315 million can unwind, and non-core brands can be sold. On these figures resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress before 2028, with a thin margin if the dividend continues. The refinancing consequence is that adjusted leverage, already 5.21x to 5.70x on prudent earnings, would rise with any loss of EBITDA, the 2028 notes, which carry a coupon 430 bp above the notes they replaced, would have to be refinanced from a weaker position, and the asset-based facility could mature ahead of them; the observable triggers are first-half borrowing above USD 340 million, sales growth turning negative and no progress on the 2028 notes by the springing date. No default is assumed because the evidence does not support one.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 107; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; calculation D2; calculation D6; calculation D7; calculation D13; calculation D18

### Overall credit view

Overall, Newell Brands presents an adequate business profile supported by scale across six businesses, a strong Learning and Development profit pool and demonstrated market access, but credit quality is constrained by near-zero free cash flow, reliance on add-backs to reach 4.70x leverage, and a 2028 maturity tied to the asset-based facility. Cash-flow durability appears weak because of thin conversion, retailer concentration and recurring restructuring and impairment. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on stabilising sales, free cash flow after dividends turning positive, and liquidity staying above the 2026 seasonal low of USD 528 million before the 2028 notes approach. The primary downside path is a retailer-led sales shortfall that deepens the cash shortfall and uses facility borrowing while the 2028 maturity nears. Against typical leveraged issuers in the sector the profile cannot be placed, because no peer data is supplied. Further analysis would require covenant levels and the debt agreements, current ratings and market prices, a printed maturity table and a 2027 outlook.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation D1; calculation D2; calculation D4; calculation D6; calculation D7; calculation D13
