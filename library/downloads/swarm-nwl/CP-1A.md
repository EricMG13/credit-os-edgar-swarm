## Request

This is the Business & governance assessment for NEWELL BRANDS INC. (issuer id nwl), as of 2026-11-05, on the credit snapshot pathway. It covers what the company sells and to whom, who owns and governs it, how it has treated creditors through its 2025 and 2026 refinancings and its capital allocation, and the restructuring and legal history that bear on credit. It rests on the seven supplied filings and releases and on the Source readiness assessment.

## Findings

### Bottom line

Governance risk to creditors is medium: Newell Brands is widely held with no sponsor and no extraction or liability-management episode in the filings, but it keeps paying a dividend that free cash flow does not cover while total debt rises. Management says deleveraging is a top priority and expects to reduce debt and factoring by a couple hundred million dollars in 2026, which the nine-month figures do not yet show.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 82; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 4, 17; EX-99.1_2026-10-30_exhibit991_q3xseptemberx20.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation A5; calculation A7

The profit base is narrow: Learning and Development earned more than the three segments combined in the first nine months of 2026, and two retailers took 30% of FY2025 sales. The senior unsecured notes due 2028 and 2031 rank behind the secured asset-based facility on its collateral and, since September 2026, behind a receivables purchaser’s first-priority lien on certain unsold receivables.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 19, 27, 28; calculation A3; calculation A4

### Key findings

Ownership offers creditors neither a sponsor to lean on nor an owner positioned to extract value, but holder-level facts are missing. The company has one class of common stock listed on Nasdaq, thousands of holders of record, about USD 2.2 billion of non-affiliate market value at 2025-06-30, no preferred stock issued, and nine directors who signed the annual report, among them a Chairman separate from the Chief Executive Officer. Without the proxy statement, large holders, board independence and related-party dealings are not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 48, 139, 150; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3

Group profit rests on Learning and Development, and the unit that earns it carries concentrated supply and litigation risk. In FY2025 it earned USD 464 million of segment operating income against USD 301 million for all three segments, after USD 290 million of tradename impairment in Home and Commercial Solutions; before impairments it still produced 80.2% of segment profit, and in the nine months of 2026 the figures are USD 553 million against USD 545 million. Learning and Development is 37.4% of FY2025 net sales, and its Baby business, 14.0% of group net sales, takes products that are most of its sales from a single supplier that owns much of the intellectual property and faces two certified class actions over advertising claims.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 103, 131, 133; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27–29; calculation A1; calculation A3

Pricing power is limited and the 2026 margin recovery is partly a one-off. Amazon took 17% of FY2025 net sales and Walmart 13%, the company generally has no long-term supply contracts or minimum purchase commitments with its largest customers, and the annual report says retailer power may limit cost recovery through pricing. Tariffs added about USD 174 million of cash cost in 2025 before mitigation, and the nine-month 2026 gross margin of 36.4% includes a refund of about USD 100 million of 2025 tariffs; without it the margin would be about 34.6%, and two class actions seek the refund for consumers.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 10, 14, 51; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 33, 44; calculation A4; calculation A15

Sales have stabilised only recently and the recovery rests on one segment. Net sales fell 11.4% between FY2023 and FY2025, with Outdoor and Recreation down 25.8%, and rose 2.1% in the nine months of 2026, of which core sales, management’s own measure, grew 0.9%. Management describes the second quarter as the first sales growth in over four years, but core sales in Home and Commercial Solutions were 0.1% below the prior year for the nine months while Learning and Development grew 3.3%.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 133; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 44; EX-99.1_2026-07-31_exhibit991_q2xjunex2026.htm.jsonl, page 1; EX-99.1_2026-10-30_exhibit991_q3xseptemberx20.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 16; calculation A2

Financial policy is mixed: the dividend is the only distribution and it has been cut, yet cash flow does not cover it. The dividend per share fell 36.4% from USD 0.44 for 2023 to USD 0.28 for 2024 and has been held since, and there is no repurchase programme. But FY2025 free cash flow of USD 17 million covered cash dividends of USD 120 million only 0.14x, free cash flow after dividends was negative USD 135 million in the nine months of 2026, and management cut its 2026 operating cash flow outlook to about USD 150 million from about USD 400 million at the second quarter, partly because paying out terminated executive retirement plans moves about USD 85 million into operating outflows and partly because less receivables factoring is expected.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 49, 82; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 4, 53; EX-99.1_2026-07-31_exhibit991_q2xjunex2026.htm.jsonl, page 4; EX-99.1_2026-10-30_exhibit991_q3xseptemberx20.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation A5; calculation A6; calculation A8

Refinancing behaviour was early and non-coercive, but the time it bought came at a higher cost and with a springing maturity clause. The company replaced the notes due 2026 with USD 1.25 billion of senior notes due 2028 at a coupon 430 bp higher, redeemed the 2027 notes about 13 months before maturity with USD 600 million of notes due 2031 at a coupon 12.5 bp lower, and replaced its USD 1.00 billion secured revolver with a USD 800 million asset-based facility 62 days before the revolver’s net leverage covenant was due to step down. The new facility has only a springing fixed charge test and matures 91 days before any debt of USD 125 million or more that is still outstanding, so on a plain reading its maturity moves ahead of the 2028 notes unless they are refinanced. No exchange offer, waiver request, priming step or asset transfer is described in the filings read, but the terms of three revolver amendments between March 2023 and April 2024 are not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 57, 59, 105–107, 148, 149; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18–21; calculation A9; calculation A14; calculation A20

Receivables financing is being shrunk, and part of it now carries a first-priority lien, which pulls working capital back onto the balance sheet. The purchase limit of the larger programme was cut from USD 700 million to USD 625 million in September 2026 and the purchaser received a first-priority lien and set-off right on certain unsold receivables, and the smaller programme was renewed in October 2026 with a limit of USD 75 million against USD 135 million sold at 2026-09-30. Management says it is reducing reliance on factoring after the bond refinancing, and receivables sold of USD 435 million sit outside reported debt.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 10, 34, 40; EX-99.1_2026-10-30_exhibit991_q3xseptemberx20.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation A10

Restructuring and impairment recur, so add-backs should be read as a run-rate cost rather than a one-off. Four programmes announced since January 2023 had cost USD 280 million by the end of FY2025, and the latest has already booked USD 70 million of an expected USD 75 million to USD 90 million. Impairments of goodwill and tradenames were USD 339 million, USD 345 million and USD 340 million in FY2023 to FY2025, followed by USD 101 million in the third quarter of 2026, and the company says Commercial goodwill of USD 747 million has a fair value within 10% of its carrying value.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 32, 99; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 14, 51; calculation A11; calculation A12

Legal and tax matters are large against cash and several are open-ended, although management regards most as immaterial or adequately reserved. The company disputes in Tax Court an IRS transfer-pricing assessment of USD 80 million of tax and USD 34 million of penalties for 2011 to 2015, estimates USD 180 million to USD 220 million of tax if its challenge to foreign-dividend regulations fails, faces certified Baby class actions with no estimable loss, and carries Lower Passaic River exposure it cannot estimate beyond a consent decree it does not expect to be material; cash was USD 249 million at 2026-09-30. A 2023 SEC settlement over sales practices and accounting for 2016 and 2017 cost about USD 13 million, and the annual report says material weaknesses have occurred before although controls were effective at 2025-12-31.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 32, 34–36, 135, 136, 138; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 25, 29, 31, 32; calculation A13

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for lower risk is structural. No owner can extract value, the dividend per share is already 36.4% below its 2023 level, the company refinanced both its 2027 notes and its revolver early without a creditor-adverse exchange, sales grew in the second and third quarters of 2026 by 3.0% and 3.7%, and the asset-based facility leaves USD 480 million of net availability. The case for higher risk is that two of three segments lost money on a GAAP basis in FY2025, free cash flow after dividends was negative in FY2025 and in the nine months of 2026, the 2026 operating cash flow outlook was cut by about USD 250 million, and the springing clause could pull the facility’s maturity forward to ahead of the 2028 notes.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; EX-99.1_2026-07-31_exhibit991_q2xjunex2026.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 4; EX-99.1_2026-10-30_exhibit991_q3xseptemberx20.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 4; calculation A2; calculation A5; calculation A6; calculation A8; calculation A14

The view would move toward low if free cash flow covered the dividend and total debt fell by about what management indicates by the end of 2026, without new secured or priming debt. It would move toward high if the dividend were kept while the asset-based facility is drawn harder, if debt ranking ahead of the unsecured notes were raised, if a Tax Court or Baby class-action outcome needed cash beyond reserves, or if an exchange or other liability-management step appeared.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 20; EX-99.1_2026-10-30_exhibit991_q3xseptemberx20.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 4; calculation A7

### Limitations

- The 2026 proxy statement is not supplied: the conclusion that no controlling holder exists rests on the share count, the holder-of-record count and the absence of any disclosed sponsor, so a large holder, a related-party arrangement or a board-independence weakness could move the governance risk level up or down.
- The executed indentures, the asset-based credit agreement and the revolver amendments are not supplied: the priority conclusion and the reading of the springing maturity rest on the filings’ summaries, so guarantor lists, lien scope, covenant baskets and dividend capacity could widen or narrow the structural risk and decide whether the uncovered dividend is a policy choice or a constraint.
- Current agency ratings are not supplied: the last disclosed actions are Moody’s B2 in the fourth quarter of 2025 and S&P B+ in the second quarter of 2025, so the view that the 2026 refinancings were creditor-aligned cannot be tested against any rating response.
- Management’s deleveraging target and operating cash flow outlook are forecasts and no model inputs are supplied: the financial-policy view rests on reported nine-month figures, and a fourth-quarter cash inflow could show faster debt reduction than the figures to 2026-09-30 do.
- Segment profit is on a GAAP basis and the before-impairment view is own arithmetic that ignores restructuring inside segment costs: the Learning and Development concentration would soften if issuer-normalized segment profit for the full year were supplied.
- The nature and years of the earlier material weaknesses and the detail of the 2023 SEC matter are not stated beyond a summary: the reliability discount applied to management’s add-backs is a judgement and could be larger or smaller.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

No acquisition, merger, buyout, sponsor transaction or equity raise is described in the supplied filings for FY2023 to the third quarter of 2026, so the transactions that matter to creditors are debt refinancings. Completed divestitures and product-line exits appear only as costs, with no buyer, price or proceeds stated beyond USD 22 million of sale proceeds in FY2025.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 34, 82

Two refinancing rounds changed the debt stack. In May 2025 the company issued USD 1.25 billion of senior unsecured notes due 2028 and used the proceeds to redeem its notes due 2026 at a small premium. In July 2026 it entered a five-year asset-based facility of up to USD 800 million and drew USD 490 million to repay its USD 1.00 billion secured revolver, and in August 2026 it issued USD 600 million of senior unsecured notes due 2031 with net proceeds of USD 592 million.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 59, 105, 106; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 19; calculation A9; calculation A14

The 2031 proceeds redeemed the 2027 notes for total consideration of about USD 508 million, a premium of about USD 8 million, paid fees and repaid part of the facility, and the company booked a USD 13 million loss on extinguishment. At 2026-09-30 USD 215 million was drawn on the facility, with USD 46 million of letters of credit and USD 741 million of availability before borrowings and letters of credit. The notes due 2028, 2030, 2031 and 2032 carry covenants that limit debt, liens, restricted payments, affiliate dealings and asset sales, subject to exceptions, and those covenants fall away if the notes reach investment grade.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 31, 107; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 20; calculation A9

In November 2024 the company issued USD 750 million of notes due 2030 and USD 500 million of notes due 2032. In September and October 2026 the receivables programmes were amended: the larger programme limit fell from USD 700 million to USD 625 million with a first-priority lien for the purchaser on certain unsold receivables, and the smaller programme was renewed to 2027-10-01 with a USD 75 million limit, an event dated after the 2026-09-30 balance sheet.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nwl-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nwl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 10, 34, 40
