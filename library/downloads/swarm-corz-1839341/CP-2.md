## Findings

### Bottom line

Core Scientific's fundamental credit is weak today and depends on the build-out being completed and funded: reported EBITDA is negative, nine-month free cash flow is deeply negative, and net debt of USD 3,498,234 thousand rests on one customer's leases that are still being delivered.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C10

Contracted lease payments of USD 7,484,422 thousand on commenced leases, third-quarter Adjusted EBITDA of USD 133,568 thousand and proven access to the secured bond market support debt service if delivery stays on schedule. About USD 4.4 billion of build commitments exceed the funding sources the company has identified, so the financing in the next twelve months decides the outcome.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 32; EX99.1_2026-09-30_corz_q32026corescientificinc-ea-htm.htm.jsonl, page 10; calculation C16

### Key findings

Leverage is high on every measure that can be calculated, and no trailing measure exists. Reported EBITDA is negative for the twelve months to 2026-09-30, and the issuer's Adjusted EBITDA cannot be compared over twelve months because its definition widened in 2026. Annualizing the third quarter's Adjusted EBITDA gives USD 534,272 thousand, against which net debt is 6.55x and contractual principal of USD 4,385,000 thousand is 8.21x, before the further borrowing the build-out needs; these are single-quarter run-rates on a company-defined measure, not trailing leverage.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C5

Free cash flow is deeply negative by design, and operating cash flow flatters it. Operating cash flow of USD 296,243 thousand included USD 286,765 thousand from selling bitcoin, so operations without those sales produced USD 9,478 thousand. Purchases of property, plant and equipment of USD 1,494,979 thousand left free cash flow at negative USD 1,198,736 thousand, and land and power purchases of USD 676,881 thousand took it to negative USD 1,875,617 thousand, so the build-out was paid for by new debt and by a fall of USD 965,794 thousand in unrestricted cash in the third quarter alone.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 7; calculation C9; calculation C10; calculation C11; calculation C12

Liquidity is thin against the commitments it must meet. Unrestricted cash and the undrawn revolver total USD 903,941 thousand, which is 20.54% of about USD 4.4 billion of purchase and construction commitments that fall substantially all within 24 months; adding an expected restricted-cash release, every Tranche 1 warrant being exercised and the amount passed through to CoreWeave still covers only 46.65%. The balance depends on the project-level bond financing that management expects within twelve months and whose terms are not stated.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 66; calculation C13; calculation C14; calculation C15; calculation C16

Contracted rent is the main support, but its cushion over debt service is modest before operating costs. Lease payments to be received on commenced leases total USD 7,484,422 thousand and rise from USD 350,909 thousand in 2027 to USD 639,045 thousand in 2029, with a further USD 1.8bn on CoreWeave leases not yet commenced; the 2027 amount is 1.30x the annual coupons of USD 269,550 thousand and the 2028 amount is 1.67x, before power and operating costs, capital spending and the secured notes' amortization.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation C7; calculation C19; calculation C20

The structure protects noteholders for now but moves risk to the parent. The debt service reserve account of USD 347,900 thousand is 1.36x a year of coupon on the secured notes, and those notes sit in a ring-fenced subsidiary whose five subsidiary guarantors carry the notes' collateral. The parent gave an uncapped completion guarantee, so a delay or overrun is paid from parent cash that also has to support the revolver, and the notes begin to amortize only after rent commences, so FY2029 maturities of USD 649,750 thousand rest on an assumed first installment date.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 25; calculation C18; calculation C31

Concentration is the dominant business risk and will fall only when the 2027 leases commence. One customer supplied 93% of third-quarter revenue and 84% of nine-month revenue, and the AMD and Neocloud leases that diversify it do not start before 2027. AMD signed directly for 71.27% of that new capacity and holds the right, but not the obligation, to cure Neocloud defaults, while CoreWeave moved two license agreements to a special purpose subsidiary that the company understands received customer commitments sufficient for investment-grade debt.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-03-31_corz.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-Q_2026-06-30_corz.htm.jsonl, pages 38–39; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 43; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 49; calculation C35; calculation C36

The earnings ramp is credible because power is passed through and margins are high. Third-quarter Adjusted EBITDA of USD 133,568 thousand was 3.25x the second quarter's, a margin of 57.36%, as billing capacity rose by 22.78%; colocation gross margin was 59.59% and operating margin 31.07%. The same quarter capitalized USD 17,774 thousand of interest, so reported interest expense of USD 54,066 thousand understates the USD 71,840 thousand incurred.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 50; EX99.1_2026-09-30_corz_q32026corescientificinc-ea-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1; EX99.1_2026-09-30_corz_q32026corescientificinc-ea-htm.htm.jsonl, page 10; calculation C21; calculation C22; calculation C23; calculation C24; calculation C25; calculation C26

Refinancing capacity is real but depends on the equity price. The company priced USD 3.3 billion of secured notes at 7.75% in April 2026, and at the share price of USD 20.74 when the AMD warrant was set the 2029 convertible notes were 1.89x their conversion price, so conversion could remove USD 460,000 thousand of debt, while the 2031 convertible notes were at 0.92x. Revolver borrowing needs a market capitalization of at least USD 3,000 million, which on the current share count means a price of USD 9.31, 55.11% below that reference price, so the condition binds only in a sharp fall; the minimum liquidity test of USD 150,000 thousand has headroom of USD 753,941 thousand, and the Tranche 1 warrants expire on 2027-01-23.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 28; calculation C27; calculation C28; calculation C39; calculation C40; calculation C41

Governance is neutral for cash leakage and adverse for reporting quality. There is no named sponsor, no dividend and no related-party transaction in 2024 or 2025, but a material weakness that forced a restatement is unremediated, and warrants, convertible notes and the AMD warrant could add shares equal to 62.32% of those outstanding.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 73; calculation C32

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against a weak view is that the company has funded each large step so far and the contracts behind the build are long and customer-funded. CoreWeave funds almost all of its conversion projects with the company's contribution capped at a fixed amount, deferred revenue of USD 573,571 thousand is almost all CoreWeave prepayment, the secured notes closed with a reserve account, and unrestricted cash fell but still stood at USD 803,941 thousand, with an undrawn revolver and a letter of credit facility that is expected to release restricted cash. If the 2029 convertible notes convert, USD 460,000 thousand of debt becomes equity, and if the Tranche 1 warrants are exercised USD 646,269 thousand of cash arrives; together with the project bond these would close the gap.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 67; calculation C33

The view would improve if the project-level bond is completed on stated terms, if billing capacity keeps rising toward the CoreWeave contract and the AMD and Neocloud leases commence in 2027, and if Adjusted EBITDA holds near the third-quarter run-rate for two or more quarters on a stable definition. It would worsen if financing slips while spending continues at the third-quarter pace, if CoreWeave pays late or its credit weakens, if a further restatement follows the open control weakness, or if the share price falls toward USD 9.31, where the revolver would close, or below the USD 6.81 exercise price of the Tranche 1 warrants.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 49

### Limitations

- Adjusted EBITDA is the issuer's own measure, widened in 2026, and no document gives a trailing twelve months on that definition, so the annualized leverage multiples show scale rather than a ratio that a covenant or agency would compute; a consistent definition could move them materially in either direction.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); EX99.1_2026-09-30_corz_q32026corescientificinc-ea-htm.htm.jsonl, page 10
- Neither a build schedule nor a cash flow forecast is supplied, so the funding gap is sized from the total of commitments against identified sources and not from a month-by-month profile; the liquidity conclusion could prove too severe if spending is later in the 24 months or too mild if costs rise.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 53
- Cash flow statements are printed only for nine months, so the third-quarter cash movement is inferred from balance sheets; the free cash flow conclusion for the latest quarter alone is therefore not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 7
- No peer, market price or rating information is supplied, so the business and financial profile is judged against the company's own history and statements rather than against comparable issuers, and the Average assessments could be too generous or too harsh.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_corz.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 53
- The share price after 2026-07-27 is not supplied, so whether the convertible notes, the warrants and the revolver condition stand in the money or in range today is not established, and the refinancing conclusion rests on a price about four months old.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 28

### Strengths and weaknesses

The first strength is contracted visibility: lease payments to be received on commenced leases of USD 7,484,422 thousand, on terms of ten years or more, give a floor under revenue that a mining business never had, and they rise each year to 2030, which supports debt service and lowers the chance that the secured notes depend on market sentiment alone.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 48; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The second strength is a demonstrated ability to raise secured debt at scale: USD 3.3 billion of notes priced at 7.75% in April 2026 with a reserve account holding 1.36x a year of coupon, which shortens the period in which noteholders are exposed to a missed completion milestone.
Basis: EX99.1_2026-03-31_corz_d47435dex991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 25; calculation C18

The third strength is a fast earnings ramp on a passed-through power model: third-quarter Adjusted EBITDA of USD 133,568 thousand at a margin of 57.36% shows that billing capacity converts to earnings, and the customer funds most of the CoreWeave conversion cost, which limits the company's own capital at risk there.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 52; EX99.1_2026-09-30_corz_q32026corescientificinc-ea-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1; EX99.1_2026-09-30_corz_q32026corescientificinc-ea-htm.htm.jsonl, page 10; calculation C22; calculation C23

The fourth strength is optionality in the equity-linked instruments: the 2029 convertible notes can leave the debt stack by conversion, and the Tranche 1 warrants can bring cash, which reduces the gross funding needed from lenders if the share price holds.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 27; calculation C27; calculation C33

The first weakness is single-customer dependence: 93% of third-quarter revenue and the whole colocation line come from one counterparty, so a payment or credit event at CoreWeave would reach revenue, prepayments and the secured notes' collateral at once.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 43; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 49; calculation C35

The second weakness is negative free cash flow and a funding gap: free cash flow was negative USD 1,198,736 thousand before land and power purchases, and identified sources cover 46.65% of about USD 4.4 billion of commitments, so completion depends on capital markets and the share price.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 32; calculation C10; calculation C16

The third weakness is leverage with no trailing earnings base: reported EBITDA is negative over twelve months and the run-rate multiples of 6.55x net and 8.21x on principal leave little room for a delay, since stockholders' deficit of USD 1,092,078 thousand also means there is no book equity cushion.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C34

The fourth weakness is reporting quality: the control weakness behind the restatement of 2024 and 2025 interim statements is unremediated, so property, plant and equipment and impairment figures that underpin the collateral and earnings carry further restatement risk.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 73; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The fifth weakness is structural and equity-price dependence: the revolver needs a market capitalization of at least USD 3,000 million for each borrowing, the parent's completion guarantee is uncapped, and the notes' covenants bind only the finance group, so creditors outside it rely on parent liquidity that the guarantee can consume.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 25; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Company description and ownership

Core Scientific is a data-center operator that now earns most of its revenue from long-term colocation leases instead of bitcoin: colocation was 84% of nine-month revenue against 14% a year earlier, from 11 data centers in seven states. The company emerged from Chapter 11 on 2024-01-23, is widely held with no named sponsor or controlling holder, pays no dividend, and reported no related-party transactions in 2024 or 2025, so ownership neither supports nor extracts cash and the creditor case rests on the leases and the structure. The secured notes sit in a ring-fenced finance subsidiary with five guarantors, while the parent carries the convertible notes, the revolver and the completion guarantee.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_corz.htm.jsonl, page 74; 10-K_2025-12-31_corz.htm.jsonl, page 118; 10-K_2025-12-31_corz.htm.jsonl, page 148; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 43; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 45

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is shifting from a price-driven commodity to contracted capacity, and growth now comes from converting contracted capacity into billing rather than from price. Colocation contracts run ten years or more with fixed payments on capacity plus variable payments, CoreWeave receives build credits during the initial billing period that defer cash against revenue, and billing capacity rose 22.78% in the third quarter while mining fell to 7.49% of revenue. Pricing power cannot be assessed, because the rent of the AMD and Neocloud leases and the per-unit rent of the CoreWeave leases are not disclosed, and revenue is concentrated in one customer.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 48; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 67; calculation C24; calculation C37

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins are defensible because power and fuel pass through colocation customers without markup, so power prices change colocation revenue and cost equally. Colocation gross margin was 59.59% and operating margin 31.07% in the third quarter, but those margins include a pass-through and only two quarters at scale, and the mining segments still bear their own power cost until they end. Tariffs raise equipment cost on projects the customer does not fund, which now means the AMD and Neocloud build, so cost inflation falls on the company there.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 50; 10-Q_2026-09-30_corz.htm.jsonl, page 52; calculation C25; calculation C26

### Capital intensity and free cash flow conversion

Earnings do not yet convert to free cash flow because capital intensity is at its peak. Nine-month purchases of property, plant and equipment of USD 1,494,979 thousand and land and power purchases of USD 676,881 thousand exceeded operating cash flow, interest capitalized of USD 17,774 thousand in the third quarter hides part of the coupon burden, and the annual coupon on the secured notes of USD 255,750 thousand is joined by amortization of USD 379,500 thousand a year once rent commences. Capital spending is flexible only in part: CoreWeave projects are customer-funded and capped, and vendor contracts can generally be cancelled before delivery, but the company has signed leases that require the AMD and Neocloud build.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is high and works through cost of capital and access to power: the company competes with large, well-capitalized data-center providers with a lower external cost of capital and with other former miners converting to colocation, so it cannot expect pricing power and its edge is speed of delivery and sites that already have power. Buyer power is very high because one customer supplies 93% of revenue, and the second customer group has not yet commenced. Supplier power is also high: utilities require collateral before delivering power, equipment lead times exceed 12 months and tariffs raise cost, which ties up cash and can delay lease commencement. New entrants face the same power constraint, which protects sites that already hold interconnection.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 months the company has to bill the remaining contracted CoreWeave capacity, finish the hosted mining wind-down by 2026-12-31, raise project-level bond financing for the AMD and Neocloud build and see whether the Tranche 1 warrants are exercised by 2027-01-23, and each of these supports or pressures liquidity directly, and no credit facility expires in that period because they mature on 2029-08-25. The company-specific tailwind is rent already contracted; the headwinds are tariffs, equipment lead times, utility collateral and one customer.

### Downside scenario

A plausible downside starts with a delay in equipment or construction that holds billing capacity near its present level while the AMD build proceeds on the company's own funding. The first operating break would be billing growth that stalls, delaying the cash that was meant to arrive as capacity converts; the financial transmission is that annualized Adjusted EBITDA of USD 534,272 thousand would still cover annual coupons 1.98x, but capital spending would continue, and unrestricted cash plus the undrawn revolver of USD 903,941 thousand is smaller than the USD 965,794 thousand by which unrestricted cash fell in the third quarter. The liquidity consequence is that the company would rely on the restricted-cash release, the Tranche 1 warrants and the project bond, and if the share price fell the revolver would close and the warrant cash would shrink, leaving the parent's uncapped completion guarantee as the channel through which the notes' projects would draw parent cash. Management levers are deferring AMD work not yet delivered, since vendor contracts can generally be cancelled before delivery, slowing land and power purchases, and replacing cash collateral with letters of credit; observable triggers are a missed bond window, falling billing capacity growth and cash approaching the USD 150.0 million minimum liquidity test.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Core Scientific presents an adequate but highly concentrated business profile supported by contracted lease payments, a reserve-backed secured bond and a fast earnings ramp, but credit quality is constrained by negative free cash flow, run-rate leverage of 6.55x net, reliance on one customer and an open reporting weakness. Cash-flow durability appears weak today and should improve as billing capacity and new leases arrive. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the project-level bond, the share price and the timely start of the AMD and Neocloud leases. The primary downside path is delay in delivery or financing while spending continues, which draws parent cash through the completion guarantee. Further analysis would require the executed leases, customer accounts, finance-group accounts and a cash flow forecast. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: delivery and funding of the contracted build-out.
