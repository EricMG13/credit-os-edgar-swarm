## Findings

### Bottom line

Twilio's fundamental credit profile is adequate to strong, because net cash, a distant first maturity and strong cash generation outweigh thin margins and low contract visibility, so note holders face little near-term default risk and the main danger is shareholder-friendly policy rather than operating stress.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_twlo-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 13; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C33; calculation C36

The business is a large, usage-priced messaging platform whose derived earnings are small against its cost base, and the notes' summarised terms list no limit on the heavy share repurchases of the past three years. A squeeze from carrier or cloud costs or a slowdown in customer volumes would be met first by the cash cushion, which is why liquidity and shareholder payouts are the right things to monitor.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an adequate-to-strong view is how little of Twilio's earnings is protected: derived EBITDA is 9.20% of revenue, remaining performance obligations on multi-year contracts are 2.07% of last-twelve-month revenue, the cost of revenue is mostly fees set by carriers, and free cash flow after stock-based compensation was only USD 41 million in FY2024, so a second round of carrier fee increases that customers refuse to absorb would hit earnings quickly. The cash cushion answers this, but it is also what shareholders have drawn on before: it fell by USD 1.63 billion in 2024.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_twlo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 34, 87, 135–136; 10-Q_2026-09-30_twlo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 8; calculation C13; calculation C08; calculation C25; calculation C40

The view would weaken if cash and short-term securities fell below USD 2.0 billion, if repurchases exceeded free cash flow again, if debt were issued to fund them, or if gross profit fell year on year while revenue grew. It would strengthen if the executed indenture showed limits on distributions, if the commitment schedule showed the USD 1.3 billion falling due in line with revenue, and if derived EBITDA margin kept rising above 9.20% without more pass-through revenue.

### Limitations

- Competitive position rests on the issuer's own description of its rivals because no market share, pricing or peer data was supplied, so the judgement that the platform keeps its pricing and margin position could be too generous if competitors price below it.
- Contract visibility is read from one figure for remaining performance obligations that covers only contracts longer than one year, so the conclusion that revenue visibility is low could overstate the risk if shorter usage commitments recur reliably.
- The monitoring thresholds are anchored on Twilio's own reported history rather than on any covenant, rating criterion or lender test, so crossing one is a prompt to reassess and not a default signal.
- The downside sensitivity holds operating expenses at their latest level, so it overstates the net fall in earnings that would remain after cost cuts like those of 2024, and it uses no forecast.

### Credit view

Overall, Twilio presents an adequate-to-strong business profile supported by net cash, a distant first maturity, low capital intensity and strong free cash flow, but credit quality is constrained by thin derived margins, usage-based revenue with little contractual visibility, dependence on carriers and one cloud provider, and shareholder returns that the notes' summarised terms do not limit. Cash-flow durability appears adequate due to the cash conversion of stock-based pay and the cost levers management has used before. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the cash cushion rather than on market access. The primary downside path is carrier fees or price pressure compressing gross profit against a fixed cost base. Further analysis would require the executed indenture and the commitment schedule. ### Company description and credit linkage

Twilio sells communications and customer-data software to businesses: programmable messaging, voice and email interfaces that let customers send and receive messages and calls, plus customer data, authentication and engagement products. FY2025 revenue was USD 5.07 billion, growing 14%, from hundreds of thousands of customer accounts; International was 36% of revenue. The credit link is direct: usage fees tie revenue to customers' own message and call volumes, which gives fast growth when volumes rise and little protection when they fall, while the low capital needs mean that earnings convert into cash. The business moved to one reportable segment in 2025, so there is no segment profit to read.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_twlo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 6–10, 36, 82, 104–105, 136; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Operating history matters to creditors because the business was restructured in 2023, with impairments and restructuring costs that pushed derived EBITDA negative that year, and was then run for profit. Divestitures in 2023, a wind-down in 2024 and one small cash acquisition in November 2025 changed the perimeter little, so the recovered earnings are not the product of acquisitions.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_twlo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 89, 131; 10-K_2024-12-31_twlo-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 41; Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is repeat usage rather than contracted revenue: usage fees were 74% of FY2025 revenue and 76% of third-quarter 2026 revenue, the ten largest accounts were 9%, and existing customers expanded spending at 108% in FY2025 and 114% in the latest quarter. The risk mechanic is volume: customers' own usage drives revenue, so macro weakness or product substitution lowers revenue faster than a subscription business would, and the issuer itself says usage-based revenue is more immediately affected. Contracted visibility is thin, with remaining performance obligations equal to 2.07% of annual revenue. Pricing power is mixed: gross margin held at 49% in FY2025 against 51% in FY2024 while growth was 14%, but some competitors have lower list prices and carrier fee increases were passed through at cost, so revenue growth excluding those fees was 12.55% in FY2025 and 15.84% in the latest quarter. Revenue is therefore cyclical and discretionary at the margin, concentrated in Messaging at 56.80%, and not structurally declining.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Cost of revenue is mostly fees paid to network service providers, so gross margin follows carrier and cloud pricing, and pass-through of higher carrier fees raises revenue and cost equally, which dilutes margin without changing gross profit. Operating expenses consist primarily of personnel costs: they were 45.80% of revenue in FY2025 against 52.31% in FY2024, so derived EBITDA rose with gross profit and the incremental operating margin in nine months 2026 was 31.38%. Margins can be defended against volume pressure only partly: a fall of 18.94% in gross profit would remove all derived EBITDA with costs unchanged, but management has cut costs before, reducing sales and marketing expense by 15.85% from FY2023 to FY2024, which is the lever that would be used first.

### Porter's Five Forces

Rivalry is real and rising: the issuer names CPaaS companies, regional carriers with developer tools, customer-experience and customer-data vendors as rivals, expects competition to intensify, and says some rivals have lower list prices, which if matched would lower gross profit against a fixed cost base. Entry pressure comes at the edges, since new market entrants and software vendors target parts of the product line, but breadth across messaging, voice, email and data is not matched by any one rival, which protects the core. Substitutes are the sharpest threat to authentication and messaging volumes, for example if one-time passwords are phased out or over-the-top channels such as WhatsApp and RCS shift traffic, although Twilio sells those channels itself. Buyer power is limited by diversification, with the ten largest accounts at 9% of revenue, but developers can switch and may reduce or delay spending. Supplier power is high: carriers and one cloud provider are hard to replace, many carriers have no long-term contracts, and fee rises are passed on only to the extent customers accept them.

### PEST analysis

Four factors alter credit capacity. Regulatory exposure is real but contained: Twilio ended service to Iran-linked entities in March 2025 without receiving revenue from them, and telecommunications, privacy and sanctions rules govern its channels. Economic exposure is the usage model, which the issuer says is more immediately hit by weak macro conditions, tariffs and inflation than subscription revenue. Technological change cuts both ways, since AI products and new channels such as RCS add volume while changes to authentication practice could reduce it. Political factors act through trade policy and sanctions risk, with no figure that moves the credit view.

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a further round of carrier fee increases that customers resist, combined with price competition in messaging. The first operational break is gross margin: cost of revenue, mostly network fees, rises faster than revenue, so gross profit falls. The financial transmission is fast because operating expenses are largely fixed; with operating costs unchanged, a 10% fall in last-twelve-month gross profit takes derived EBITDA from USD 537 million to USD 253 million, lifts total debt to derived EBITDA to 3.92x and cuts cover to 6.75x, and a fall of 18.94% would remove all derived EBITDA. Free cash flow would fall by the same amount before tax effects, to about USD 827 million, which still covers cash interest many times over. The liquidity and refinancing consequence is limited: cash and securities of USD 2.89 billion cover the notes 2.89x before any free cash flow, nothing falls due until 2029, and management could stop repurchases, which have USD 698.7 million of authorisation left, or cut sales and marketing cost as in 2024. Resilience therefore appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress; the observable triggers are gross profit falling while revenue grows, cash and securities falling towards USD 2.0 billion, and repurchases above free cash flow. This is an illustration labelled as analyst judgement, not a forecast.
