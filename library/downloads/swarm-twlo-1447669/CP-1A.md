## Findings

### Bottom line

Twilio is a listed issuer with one class of shares and no sponsor named, so creditor risk comes from how management allocates cash, and the filings show large cash-funded buybacks, no debt-funded distribution and no liability-management history.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_twlo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 80, 116, 135–136; 10-Q_2026-09-30_twlo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13

The operating model is usage-driven with no customer above 10% of revenue, but it depends on carriers and one cloud provider, and the board, ownership and indenture detail that would test creditor protection was not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_twlo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 34, 47, 110, 149; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

- **Capital allocation, not control, carries the governance risk.** The company spent USD 2.3 billion on buybacks in 2024, USD 854.6 million in 2025 and USD 446.7 million in the first nine months of 2026, always from cash and securities rather than debt, which cut cash and securities from USD 4.0 billion to USD 2.4 billion in 2024 while the two note series stayed unchanged. The effect on creditors is a thinner cushion, not higher leverage, and nothing in the issuer's summary of the notes limits repurchases.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_twlo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 135–136, 140; 10-Q_2026-09-30_twlo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 39; calculation C13
- **The notes have been static since 2021, which keeps refinancing risk distant but untested.** The two series total USD 1.0 billion, nothing was issued, exchanged, amended or repaid in the periods read, and the first maturity is in 2029; with no credit facility and no rating stated, there is no market or lender test of the issuer's behaviour under stress.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_twlo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 135–136; 10-Q_2026-09-30_twlo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; calculation C18
- **Demand risk is spread but volume-sensitive.** No customer exceeded 10% of revenue in 2023 to 2025, yet usage-based fees were 74% of FY2025 revenue and 76% of third-quarter 2026 revenue, so spending cuts by many customers would show in revenue quickly, with fourth-quarter seasonality adding to quarterly swings.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_twlo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 87, 110; 10-Q_2026-09-30_twlo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 29
- **Supplier dependence is the sharper operating risk.** The company outsources the substantial majority of its cloud infrastructure to one provider, many carriers and network providers have no long-term contracts, and carrier messaging fee increases are passed on at cost, which lowers gross margin without changing gross profit; an outage or terms change at a supplier would hit service and cost directly.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_twlo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 34, 47, 83
- **Messaging dominates the revenue base.** Messaging was 56.80% of FY2025 revenue and grew 18.18% on the regrouped prior year, the fastest of the product groups, so credit quality tracks one usage product that is also the most exposed to carrier fees; international revenue was 36%.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_twlo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 136; calculation C01; calculation C07; calculation C06
- **Acquisitions are small and cash-funded, and the large equity stake has been a drag.** Stytch cost USD 104.1 million in cash in November 2025, while the 2022 purchase of a minority stake in Syniverse for USD 750.0 million was impaired by USD 80.6 million in FY2025 and still reduces earnings through equity-method losses; the filings describe no Twilio guarantee of the investee's debt.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_twlo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 131–133; 10-Q_2026-09-30_twlo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23
- **Board, ownership and pay detail is the main gap.** The annual report defers Part III to a proxy statement that was not supplied, so only the signing directors and officers are known and no holder concentration, committee structure or control right can be assessed.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_twlo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 149, 152; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a benign view is the scale of the 2024 buyback: cash repurchases were 3.55x free cash flow that year and were met by running down short-term securities, so the same behaviour could be repeated, and the issuer's summary of the notes lists no covenant that would stop it. The reassurance is that cash and securities still cover the notes 2.89x and the buyback pace has since fallen to 0.92x of free cash flow in 2025 and 0.52x in the first nine months of 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_twlo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 93, 107, 140; 10-Q_2026-09-30_twlo-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 6, 13; calculation C14; calculation C15; calculation C16; calculation C17

The view would worsen if the company funded a buyback or acquisition with new debt, issued secured debt or a credit facility ahead of the notes, or amended the indenture, and it would improve if the executed indenture showed limits on distributions, liens and subsidiary guarantees or if the proxy statement showed independent oversight of capital allocation.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_twlo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 135–136

### Limitations

- The proxy statement was not supplied, so board independence, committee structure, pay design, voting rights and holder concentration are not established, which keeps the governance risk level at Medium and blocks any conclusion on ownership concentration.
- The indenture was not supplied, so whether the notes restrict repurchases, asset sales or subsidiary guarantees beyond the issuer's summary is not established, which leaves the link between legal capacity and behaviour open and keeps any covenant-based conclusion out of this module.
- No earnings release, guidance or management commentary was supplied, so management's own account of capital allocation priorities rests on the strategy section of the annual report and cannot be tested against guidance.
- Dated market data and ratings were not supplied, so market reaction to buybacks and the notes' pricing are not established, which limits how far the cash-funded repurchase record can be read as creditor-aligned.

### Governance view

Overall, Twilio presents medium governance risk for creditors. The key supported behaviour is the repeated cash-funded repurchase of shares, which matters because it reduces the cash and securities that back the notes while leaving debt unchanged, and it implies a thinner liquidity cushion rather than higher leverage. Countervailing evidence is the absence of any debt-funded distribution, dividend, amendment or exchange, the clean audit opinion on the statements and internal control, and cash and securities that still exceed the notes. Further analysis requires the proxy statement, the executed indenture and any guidance document. Governance assessment completed with limitations; risk level Medium; key gaps are board and ownership detail, indenture terms and management guidance.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_twlo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 101, 135–136, 140; calculation C17
