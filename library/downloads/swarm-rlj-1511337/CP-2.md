### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an adequate view is that operating leverage has already worn the margin down while debt stayed flat: Hotel EBITDA margin fell from 30.34% in FY2023 to 27.92% on a last-twelve-months basis, so a second decline like FY2025 would lift net leverage to 5.79x and cut cover to 2.69 times, or 2.52 times if floating rates also rose 100 bp, before any refinancing cost, and distributions were raised into the decline. The strongest evidence for a better view is that operating cash flow less capex would still cover distributions 1.21 times in that scenario with capex held at the last-twelve-months level, capex is flexible, the common dividend is set by the board, and liquidity is 1.31 times the term loans due by 2028.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rlj.htm.jsonl, pages 122–123; 10-Q_2026-09-30_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 42; calculation C2; calculation C6; calculation C11; calculation C13; calculation C21

The view would improve if the USD 90.00 million of sale cash were used to repay debt and net leverage settled below the pro forma 5.04x, if RevPAR growth held through the 2027 term loan decision, or if the executed credit agreement showed wide covenant headroom. It would weaken if RevPAR growth turned negative for two quarters, if proceeds funded repurchases under the USD 250.00 million programme, if the 2027 term loan were refinanced at or above the 5.91% of the newest term loan, or if a covenant waiver or mortgage cash trap appeared.

### Company description and ownership

RLJ Lodging Trust is a Maryland real estate investment trust that owned 92 hotels at 2026-09-30, mostly premium-branded, rooms-oriented hotels in urban markets, run by independent managers. Substantially all assets and liabilities sit in RLJ Lodging Trust, L.P., of which the Trust owns 99.5%, and 91 of the 92 hotels are leased to taxable REIT subsidiaries. Rooms produced 80.99% of FY2025 revenue, the customer base is mainly transient and group business that uses meeting space is a small part of it, so cash flow follows occupancy and room rates and no contracted revenue sits behind the debt. The spread across many hotels and travellers limits single-customer risk, but hotel-level earnings are not disclosed, so the spread is measured by revenue and not by profit.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 28; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C19

No sponsor or controlling holder is named, and the Trust is the sole general partner of the operating partnership; the annual report limits any holder to 9.8% of the common shares unless the board agrees. Nine trustees signed the annual report, an executive chairman, a chief executive who is also a trustee and seven others, and the annual report states that seven trustees are independent; this corrects the Business & governance statement that trustee independence is not established, while pay, committee roles, related-party dealings and holdings by shareholder remain unsupplied. Financial policy was shareholder-friendly into the FY2025 decline and has been restrained in 2026: the common dividend per share rose from USD 0.36 in FY2023 to USD 0.50 in FY2024 and USD 0.60 in FY2025 while operating cash flow fell 22.64%, and USD 28.59 million of shares were repurchased in FY2025 alongside USD 69.04 million of hotel sale proceeds, whereas in the first nine months of 2026 the dividend was USD 0.15 per share in each quarter and no shares were repurchased under the USD 250.00 million authorisation. This refines the Business & governance reading of capital allocation as measured, which holds for 2026 and for leverage but is too favourable for FY2024 and FY2025 cash coverage. Creditor alignment is adequate on what was done, but it is not tested against the limits on restricted payments in the debt documents, which are not supplied.

### Revenue, margin and cash conversion

Revenue is transient and cyclical, and corporate and government travel moved from drag to support between FY2025 and 2026. Room revenue at comparable hotels fell in FY2025 because international, government, corporate and group travel decreased, with RevPAR down 1.75% to USD 143.43, and rose in the first nine months of 2026 on leisure, corporate and government travel and renovated hotels returning, with RevPAR up 7.04% to USD 156.51 on occupancy of 74.4% and ADR of USD 210.32. Pricing power is present in 2026, with ADR up 4.49%, but it is not assured because competition can stop operators raising rates as fast as inflation. Northeastern hotels earn less from December to March and Florida hotels more from January to April, so one quarter-end balance is not a run rate. Revenue visibility is therefore short, and creditors rely on demand holding and not on contracts.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 62; 10-K_2025-12-31_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 64; 10-Q_2026-09-30_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 34; calculation C7

The 2026 growth leaned on the three largest markets: Northern California, Southern California and South Florida produced 37.51% of nine-month 2026 revenue against 36.23% a year earlier and 60.84% of the USD 56.17 million revenue increase, with Northern California up 14.75%. A regional shock, such as the earthquake and hurricane exposure the annual report names, therefore reaches more of the recovery than the number of hotels suggests, which matters most for the 2027 refinancing decision.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 10; calculation C8

In FY2025 property operating expenses rose 1.09% while revenue fell 1.43%, and Hotel EBITDA margin fell from 30.34% in FY2023 and 29.12% in FY2024 to 27.34% in FY2025, even though revenue rose 3.31% in FY2024; the margin is 27.92% on a last-twelve-months basis. In the first nine months of 2026 property operating expenses at comparable hotels rose USD 46.16 million against a USD 57.40 million rise in comparable room revenue, with wages and benefits cited first, while lower property insurance premiums cut property tax, insurance and other expense by USD 1.95 million. Cost flexibility sits mainly with independent managers: the Trust has limited ability to influence their staffing decisions and approval rights over key posts at many hotels, so creditors should expect the FY2025 sensitivity to return in a downturn.

### Competitive position and external factors

Rivalry is high and local: the annual report describes the industry as highly competitive and market-specific, with competition from existing and new focused-service and compact full-service hotels, and comparable occupancy slipped to 71.6% in FY2025 from 72.7% before recovering to 74.4% for nine months 2026 against 72.6%. Rivalry contests rate growth and margin market by market, so it limits how fast earnings and cover can recover.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 64; 10-Q_2026-09-30_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 34

New supply is driven by construction cost and financing availability, and tariffs on construction materials and furniture raise renovation cost for existing owners, so the barrier to entry is cost and not regulation. No supply data are supplied, so the effect of new hotels on the markets with the most revenue is not established. Substitutes are non-traditional accommodations such as online home and apartment rentals, which the annual report names as competition for the same travellers and which cap rate growth at the price-sensitive end.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-K_2025-12-31_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-K_2025-12-31_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 60

Buyer power is high on price and low on concentration: customers are mostly transient, group business is small, and no customer is named as material, so no single buyer can cut revenue, but guests and online channels compare rates freely. Supplier power is the force that matters most to creditors: independent managers run daily operations and take fees, franchisors set brand standards, fees were 7.61% of FY2025 revenue and the Trust guarantees each franchise agreement, so a dispute or termination at one large manager or brand reaches many hotels at once.

Political and regulatory factors are material through travel policy and taxes: the annual report ties demand to government spending and inbound international travel under visa policies, and FY2025 comparable room revenue fell in part because international and government travel decreased. The REIT rule to distribute at least 90% of taxable income is a financial constraint as well as a tax rule, because it pushes capital spending and maturities toward external financing. Economic factors are the main driver: inflation raises wages and renovation cost faster than rate growth can follow, and higher interest rates lift the cost of the 76.2% of debt that is variable. Technology is a secondary factor, but the Trust carries no cyber insurance and relies on managers' systems. Social factors are not a primary credit driver.

### Financial profile and credit quality

The main credit supports are liquidity, a mostly unsecured and interest-only debt stack and a 2026 operating recovery that has lifted nine-month reported EBITDA 5.26%. The main constraints are leverage that has not fallen, cover of 2.94 times, a margin that has lost about 2.42 percentage points of Hotel EBITDA margin since FY2023, and 76.2% variable-rate debt that is refinanced into higher coupons. Scale is a support, with 92 hotels, and so is the rooms-led model that keeps food and beverage and meeting exposure small. Free cash flow is stable enough to cover distributions in the base case but not strongly so: cover was 2.45 times in FY2023, 1.56 times in FY2024 and 1.00 times in FY2025, is 1.55 times in the last twelve months, and would be 1.21 times in the FY2025-type scenario with capex held low. Financial policy is average, because distributions were raised while cash flow fell and then held in 2026, and market access is adequate on the 2026 refinancing but not tested by prices or ratings.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over 12 to 24 months the tailwinds are the 2026 demand recovery (comparable RevPAR up 9.68% in the third quarter), renovated hotels still ramping, lower capex and USD 90.00 million of sale cash that can cut debt. The headwinds are the USD 500.00 million term loan due on 2027-09-24, under 10 months after the as-of date, with two one-year extension options at the issuer's discretion subject to conditions, then the USD 300.00 million loan due in April 2028, and a cost of debt that has already risen. If the 2027 term loan repriced to the 5.51% to 5.91% of the newer term loans from its 4.97% rate after hedges, annual interest would rise by USD 2.70 million to USD 4.70 million. No management outlook is supplied, so this view is the analyst's own and is not management's.

Over three to five years the 2029 stack is the issue: USD 689.40 million falls due in 2029 and extensions on the two term loans would move those maturities into 2029 and 2030, while the annual report states that operating cash flow is expected to repay only part of the debt at maturity. Sector tailwinds are an upcycle in lodging demand after a fall, and headwinds are inflation, tariffs and competition from non-traditional accommodation. Refinancing confidence rests on the 2026 completion of the USD 569.00 million and USD 150.00 million term loans, the unsecured structure and the par call on the 2029 notes from 2026-09-15, which lets the issuer refinance them early if markets allow.

### Downside scenario

The scenario repeats the FY2025 transmission on last-twelve-months figures; it is the analyst's judgement built on an observed year and is not a management case. The starting condition is a second fall in international, government, corporate and group travel. The first operating break is cost stickiness: in FY2025 property operating expenses rose while revenue fell, so reported EBITDA fell 8.23% and operating cash flow fell 14.58%. Applied to the last twelve months, EBITDA would fall to USD 301.99 million and operating cash flow to USD 232.89 million, net leverage would rise to 5.79x with cash interest unchanged and cover would fall to 2.69 times, or 2.52 times with a 100 bp rise on the unhedged floating debt, which the issuer sizes at USD 7.9 million a year.

### Overall credit view

Overall, RLJ Lodging Trust presents an adequate business profile supported by 92 brand-affiliated, rooms-led hotels, no sponsor and a mostly unsecured structure, but credit quality is constrained by net leverage of 5.31x (5.04x pro forma for the 2026-10-08 sale), interest cover of 2.94 times and earnings that fall faster than revenue. Cash-flow durability appears adequate but thinner than the last twelve months suggest, because operating cash flow less capex covers distributions 1.55 times and capex is flexible, yet it covered them only 1.00 times in FY2025. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the 2026 earnings recovery holding through the 2027 term loan decision, the cost of replacing 4.00% notes in 2029 at rates near the 5.51% to 5.91% paid on the newest term loans, and the use of the sale proceeds. The primary downside path is a second demand fall like FY2025 that lifts leverage and cuts cover before the 2027 to 2029 maturities are refinanced. Further analysis would require the executed debt documents, covenant ratio values, ratings and prices, management guidance and a post-sale balance sheet. Fundamental credit synthesis completed. Top risk: refinancing the 2027 and 2028 term loans and the 2029 notes at higher cost on earnings that are still below FY2024.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Liquidity is the strongest support. Cash and the undrawn revolver give USD 1,048.34 million, which funds the 2027 and 2028 term loans 1.31 times, and the 2026 refinancing pre-funded the notes due in 2026, so default probability over the next two years is low unless covenant compliance fails.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 42; calculation C1; calculation C2

The unsecured structure supports recovery for the unsecured debt: only eight hotels carry mortgage loans, the other hotels carry no mortgage, and the indenture and credit agreements test the unencumbered asset to debt ratio, so unsecured creditors keep access to most of the asset base, although their ranking among themselves is not stated.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_rlj.htm.jsonl, pages 11–13

The 2026 operating recovery and cash conversion support deleveraging: comparable RevPAR rose 7.04% for nine months, reported EBITDA rose 5.26%, and operating cash flow less capex covers distributions 1.55 times with capex that can be cut, so net leverage can fall if the recovery holds and the sale cash repays debt.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 34; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C7; calculation C9

Hedging and an interest-only profile bound the rate risk: 65.7% of debt is fixed or effectively fixed and a 100 bp rise on the rest costs about USD 7.9 million a year, which is 7.05% of last-twelve-months cash interest, so a rate shock would slow cover but not threaten it alone.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 42; calculation C11

Net leverage and cover are the main weakness. At 5.31x (5.04x pro forma for the 2026-10-08 sale) and 2.94 times they have not improved since FY2024, so an earnings decline moves leverage directly toward the covenant tests without a cushion that the filings let us measure.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C3; calculation C4

Earnings are geared to RevPAR. A 1.43% revenue fall produced an 8.23% fall in reported EBITDA in FY2025, and Hotel EBITDA margin fell to 27.92% on a last-twelve-months basis from 30.34% in FY2023, so downside moves faster than the revenue line suggests.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rlj.htm.jsonl, pages 122–123; calculation C5; calculation C6

The 2027 to 2029 refinancing stack is the pressure point. The 2026 refinancing replaced 3.75% notes with term loans at 5.51% and 5.91%, USD 500.00 million of 4.00% notes follows in 2029, and extension options on the 2027 term loan would stack USD 1,000.00 million in one month, so higher coupons and market access decide the credit.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rlj.htm.jsonl, pages 10–12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 42; calculation C12; calculation C16

Concentration reduces flexibility: two managers run 51.09% of hotels, three brand families cover 83 of 93 hotels and three markets supplied 60.84% of the 2026 revenue growth, so a single manager, brand or region event reaches cash flow broadly.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C8

Distribution policy outran cash flow in the downturn: the common dividend per share rose 66.67% from FY2023 to FY2025 while operating cash flow fell 22.64%, free cash flow after capex covered distributions only 1.00 times in FY2025, and Series A preferred dividends of USD 25.12 million are a fixed claim on top, so there was no cushion for a further decline until capex was cut in 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 90; 10-K_2025-12-31_rlj.htm.jsonl, page 115; calculation C14; calculation C21

Severity Material; the action is to supply the executed documents with amendments and the compliance certificates.

Severity Minor; the action is to supply ratings, quotes and the latest earnings release.

- No letters of credit, standby facilities or surety instruments were found in any of the five filings, so liquidity is not reduced for them. Severity Minor; no action closes this from filings.
- The annual report states reserve funding of 3.0% to 5.0% of hotel revenue and the quarterly report 4.0% to 5.0%, and capex is not split between maintenance and growth; each range is kept with its date. Severity Minor; the action is to confirm current terms with the issuer.
