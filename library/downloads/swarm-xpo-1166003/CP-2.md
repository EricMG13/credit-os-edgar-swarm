## Findings

### Bottom line

XPO's fundamental credit profile is solid for a leveraged issuer, with net leverage of 2.03x, interest cover of 6.54x and liquidity of USD 860 million, so creditors face cyclical earnings risk rather than near-term refinancing risk.

Last-twelve-months reported EBITDA is USD 1,412 million against cash interest of USD 216 million and free cash flow of USD 657 million. Net debt of USD 2,864 million is unchanged pro forma, at 2.03x, for the October 2026 prepayment of USD 75 million (the day is not stated). The first large maturity is the USD 830 million of secured notes due in 2028.

The risks to watch are freight-cycle earnings, fuel-inflated revenue and shareholder returns, which took 69.65% of nine-month free cash flow.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C6

### Key findings

The earnings base is strong and concentrated. North American LTL produced 90.22% of nine-month adjusted EBITDA at a 25.91% margin, up from 23.37% a year earlier, on higher yield and tonnage. The consequence is that debt service rests on one business whose pricing discipline and volume recovery matter more than the European business.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C3

Leverage is moderate and falling, and debt service is well covered. Net leverage on reported EBITDA is 2.03x against 2.55x at FY2025, and EBITDA covers cash interest 6.54x against 5.14x, because last-twelve-months EBITDA is 19.97% above FY2025 while debt fell 5.7%. On the issuer's adjusted measure total leverage is 2.09x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C4

Liquidity covers the nearest obligations comfortably and the first large maturity once. Cash and the undrawn revolver give USD 860 million against USD 75 million of term loan prepaid in October 2026, and the revolver matures in 2030. The USD 830 million 2028 secured notes are the first large claim, and liquidity is 103.61% of them.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C5

Free cash flow is solid but flattered by low capital expenditure and shared with shareholders. Nine-month free cash flow was USD 537 million after capital expenditure of USD 369 million, while the 2026 guide of USD 500 million to USD 600 million implies USD 131 million to USD 231 million in the fourth quarter. Buybacks and tax withholdings on vested awards took USD 374 million, or 69.65%, of nine-month free cash flow.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 39; calculation C6; calculation C7

Reported debt understates funding in two places. The European receivables securitisation of about USD 227 million is fully used and sits outside debt, so net debt including it would be USD 3,091 million, or 2.19x. Operating lease liabilities of USD 797 million are also outside debt; rent is already deducted from EBITDA, so they are not added to leverage here.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6–7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C8

Governance and financial policy are creditor-aligned on behaviour, but control and legal capacity are not established. There is no dividend, no liability management transaction and a refinancing that extended maturities, while the credit agreements and ownership above the registered-holder count are not in the sources.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)

The European business is the weak link. It earned a 4.79% adjusted EBITDA margin on 39.48% of nine-month revenue and carries restructuring charges, so its disposal would raise group margin but remove diversification and its funding line.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 29

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against the positive view is that much of the 2026 improvement is price and fuel pass-through rather than volume. North American LTL fuel surcharge revenue rose USD 283 million of a USD 463 million revenue increase, and revenue excluding surcharges grew only 5.78%. Receivables absorbed USD 256 million of cash in nine months, and capital expenditure is running at 5.41% of revenue against 8.05% in FY2025, so free cash flow is likely to fall if spending returns to the guide.

The view would weaken if reported net leverage rose back above 2.55x, if adjusted EBITDA margin fell below 15.59%, if buybacks or withholdings exceeded free cash flow, or if proceeds from a European sale were returned to shareholders rather than used for debt. It would strengthen if lower fuel prices left North American LTL margin intact, because that would show the margin gain is structural.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25–26; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-K_2025-12-31_xpo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 79; calculation C9; calculation C10; calculation C21; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strength: North American LTL earnings power. Segment adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,070 million on USD 4,130 million of revenue in nine months 2026, a 25.91% margin, comes from yield gains of 4.05% and tonnage gains of 2.03%, with management citing more than 30% spare door capacity. The mechanism is operating leverage on a mostly fixed network, so volume recovery lifts margin; the implication is that deleveraging can continue even at modest growth.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 31; calculation C3; calculation C12

Strength: moderate leverage, strong cover and a smoothed maturity profile. Net leverage of 2.03x and cover of 6.54x follow earnings growth and USD 1,085 million of term debt repayment against USD 885 million of new term loans; no principal falls due in 2027 and the largest single claim is the USD 830 million 2028 secured notes. The implication is a wide cushion to absorb an earnings fall before cover returns to FY2025 levels.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C11

Strength: low customer concentration and no North American union. The top five customers are about 8% of FY2025 revenue and the largest under 3%, and no North American employees were represented by a union at year-end 2025, which limits single-customer loss and stoppage risk.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_xpo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-12-31_xpo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 17

Weakness: cyclicality and fuel-inflated revenue. LTL tonnage per day fell 6.2% in 2025 before recovering, and surcharge revenue inflates revenue and cost together, so revenue growth overstates volume. The implication is that cover can compress quickly in a freight downturn.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_xpo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 58; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 31; calculation C9

Weakness: shareholder returns and an uncertain European business. Returns took 69.65% of nine-month free cash flow, and the European segment earns a thin margin while leaning on a fully used securitisation line. The implication is slower deleveraging than earnings alone would give.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C6; calculation C8

### Company and ownership

XPO is a listed freight operator. Its asset-based LTL business in North America is 59.24% of FY2025 revenue and 89.78% of FY2025 adjusted EBITDA, and the rest is European truckload, brokerage and warehousing. LTL pricing power is shown by yield growth of 6.0% in 2025, and the European business is lower margin and is subject to a divestiture authorisation with no terms. The credit linkage is that cash flow visibility is high in LTL, moderate in Europe, and liquidity preservation depends on the LTL margin.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_xpo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6; 10-K_2025-12-31_xpo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 58; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C13

Ownership is public with about 49 registered holders, no sponsor identified and no cash dividend ever paid. The chairman and chief executive roles were combined in January 2026 when the former executive chairman no longer appears among the officers (the reason is not stated), and the guarantor group is the wholly-owned restricted subsidiaries. The implication is that extraction risk is low on behaviour, but the control and capacity conclusions need the proxy statement and credit agreements.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_xpo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 51

### Revenue, cost and cash conversion

Revenue is cyclical and partly passes through fuel. The LTL tonnage fall of 6.2% in 2025 was offset by yield growth of 6.0%, which held segment adjusted EBITDA up 2.4% at USD 1,142 million; in nine months 2026 yield and tonnage both grew and fuel surcharge revenue rose 50.99%. The implication is that pricing power, not volume alone, has protected earnings, and a failure of pricing would remove that protection.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_xpo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 58; 10-K_2025-12-31_xpo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 96; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 31; calculation C9; calculation C14

Cost structure is labour-heavy and partly variable. Salaries, wages and benefits fell to 40.44% of nine-month revenue from 41.96%, and purchased transportation to 19.61% from 20.34%, because revenue grew faster and linehaul was insourced; fuel, operating expenses and supplies rose to 20.33% on fuel costs. The implication is that margins can be defended in a mild downturn through productivity but not against a sharp rise in wages or fuel.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25–27; calculation C15

Capital intensity is high but flexible. Capital expenditure was 8.05% of revenue in FY2025 and 5.41% in nine months 2026, with a fleet whose average tractor age is 3.7 years, so spending can be cut; last-twelve-months free cash flow of USD 657 million is 46.53% of reported EBITDA. The implication is that EBITDA converts to free cash flow at a high rate today, with capex the main swing factor over the 2028 maturity.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_xpo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-K_2025-12-31_xpo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 79; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C2

### Market forces and SWOT

Competition comes from local, regional and national LTL carriers, and the annual report names Old Dominion Freight Line and Saia; buyer power is limited by low customer concentration, supplier power by in-house trailer manufacturing and driver schools, and new entry, in analyst judgement, by the cost of a national network. Regulatory, tariff and fuel-price factors matter to the credit, and other political, social and technological factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver on the materials provided.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_xpo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-12-31_xpo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 26

The credit-translated SWOT is as follows. Strengths: LTL margin and yield, moderate leverage, low concentration, young fleet. Weaknesses: freight-cycle exposure, shareholder returns, thin European margin, funding outside debt. Opportunities: volume recovery on spare door capacity, further deleveraging, a European sale used for debt. Threats: a longer freight downturn, wage and fuel inflation, loss of liquidity access in 2028, a debt-funded return of capital.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25

### Outlook

Over the next 12 to 24 months the supports are the LTL yield and tonnage momentum, spare door capacity and lower interest cost, with interest expense falling to USD 155 million from USD 166 million in nine months. The pressures are the capital expenditure guide, the fourth-quarter 2026 expiry of USD 550 million of interest rate swaps that fix term loan interest, and a rate rise on USD 785 million of floating term loans, where one percentage point costs USD 7.85 million a year. Over three to five years the 2028 secured notes and the amortisation of the term loan A from its third year set the refinancing task, which liquidity and EBITDA cover comfortably.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_xpo-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 39; calculation C16; calculation C17

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a freight recession like 2024 to 2025 with weaker tonnage and a lower diesel price. The first operational break is North American LTL tonnage per day, which fell 6.2% in 2025, hitting a network with spare door capacity and rising wages, so unit cost rises before yield can respond. Fuel-price falls would cut surcharge revenue and, in analyst judgement, largely fuel cost too, so EBITDA would fall less than revenue. The financial transmission is lower EBITDA with fixed cash interest of USD 216 million and unchanged scheduled debt: if last-twelve-months reported EBITDA fell 20%, net leverage would rise to 2.54x, close to its FY2025 level of 2.55x, and cover would be 5.23x against 5.14x. Management levers are the capital expenditure range, buybacks of USD 250 million, and the USD 600 million revolver. Liquidity of USD 860 million exceeds all debt due before 2028, so the resilience appears sufficient to avoid distress, with the 2028 notes the point where market access would matter. The observable triggers are the monitoring signals in the register.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_xpo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 58; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C11; calculation C18

### Overall credit view

Overall, XPO presents a strong business profile in North American LTL and a weaker one in Europe, supported by margin gains, moderate leverage and liquidity, but credit quality is constrained by freight-cycle exposure, shareholder returns and funding outside debt. Cash-flow durability appears adequate to strong because capex is flexible and yield has held through a tonnage fall. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the 2028 secured notes, liquidity and the freight cycle. The primary downside path is a tonnage fall without yield offset while capex and buybacks continue. Further analysis would require the credit agreements, ownership details, ratings and market prices. Top risk: a freight downturn that coincides with the 2028 refinancing.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)
