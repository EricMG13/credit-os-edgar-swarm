## Findings

### Bottom line

Leverage on reported EBITDA fell from 18.72x at year-end 2025 to 5.17x for the twelve months to 30 September 2026, but the 5 October 2026 refinancing has since raised gross principal, so creditors should not read the 30 September debt balance as current.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4; 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, pages 114–116; calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C05; calculation C06

On 30 September 2026 carrying-value debt was USD 3,747.4 million against cash of USD 751.9 million, so net debt was USD 2,995.5 million, or 4.13x. Reported EBITDA covered cash interest paid 1.85x on the twelve months, against 0.53x for 2025. The refinancing issued USD 3,970.0 million of new debt and lifted principal to USD 4,086.1 million from USD 3,782.6 million; net debt after it is not established. No undrawn committed facility is described, so liquidity is cash alone.

### Financial interpretation

Gross leverage on derived reported EBITDA rose from 9.87x in 2024 to 18.72x in 2025 and then fell to 5.17x for the twelve months to 30 September 2026. The swings sit mostly in the denominator: derived EBITDA was USD 412.7 million in 2024, helped by USD 156.2 million of other income, USD 215.7 million in 2025, after a USD 196.0 million loss on debt extinguishment, and USD 724.8 million for the latest twelve months. Issuer-adjusted EBITDA gives a smoother path of 11.85x, 10.42x and 4.46x, so the 2026 improvement is real at the operating level: adjusted EBITDA for the nine months to 30 September 2026 was USD 707.2 million against USD 254.3 million a year earlier, on revenue of USD 4,353.2 million against USD 3,560.6 million. Revenue rose USD 792.6 million while rent rose only USD 4.3 million, so theatre rent is largely fixed and the extra attendance flowed through to earnings; the credit consequence is that debt capacity is being rebuilt from earnings rather than from asset sales.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 77; 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, pages 113–114; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, page 75; calculation C05; calculation C06; calculation C18

Carrying debt fell USD 291.1 million between 31 December 2025 and 30 September 2026, but free cash flow did not fund that alone. Net proceeds of equity issuances were USD 333.6 million in the nine months, the conversion of exchangeable notes into shares removed USD 192.9 million of carrying value without cash, and USD 125.5 million of subordinated notes were repaid in cash, while Odeon notes of USD 400.0 million were replaced by term loans with proceeds of USD 416.5 million. For creditors the lower balance therefore depends on continued equity-market access, which dilutes holders, as well as on a strong operating year.

Interest cover is thin even after the recovery. Derived reported EBITDA covered cash interest paid 1.85x over the twelve months to 30 September 2026 and issuer-adjusted EBITDA 2.14x, against 0.53x and 0.95x in 2025; after capital expenditure of USD 242.2 million, reported EBITDA covers cash interest only 1.23x. Interest expense of USD 405.9 million in the nine months overstates cash servicing because USD 46.7 million was paid in kind and USD 55.2 million was non-cash interest on the exhibitor services agreement, which is why cash interest paid of USD 270.9 million is the denominator. ### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against caution is how broad the recovery was: attendance rose 18.85% in the nine months and 28.33% in the third quarter, adjusted EBITDA rose 178.10% in the nine months, free cash flow turned positive, and the refinancing removed the 2029 wall. If that holds, leverage on adjusted EBITDA of 3.56x net at 30 September 2026 would keep falling without further equity. The view would turn more cautious if attendance fell back toward 2025 levels, because rent of USD 669.1 million in the nine months is largely fixed and the operating leverage would reverse, or if cash interest after the refinancing proves higher than the USD 392.3 million paid in the latest twelve months. The pricing of the new first lien term loans is not printed, which is why that test cannot be run yet.
