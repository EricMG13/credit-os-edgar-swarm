## Findings

Silgan's fundamental credit profile is adequate: contract-backed cash flows and steady free cash flow support the debt, but flat 2026 earnings, seasonal net leverage of 4.67x and an unquantified October 2026 transaction leave creditors with less cushion than year-end figures suggest.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation D8

### Strengths and weaknesses

The first strength is contract-backed earnings. About 90% of projected 2026 metal container sales sit under multi-year supply arrangements that pass metal cost through, and a majority of dispensing and custom container sales do too, so reported EBITDA margins held between 13.51% and 14.34% across 2023 to 2025. That gives creditors earnings that bend rather than break when input costs move, with resin cost recovery lagging as the exception.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_slgn.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 17, 21–22, 58–59; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D1

The second strength is cash conversion. Free cash flow after capital expenditure was about USD 423 million in 2025, 46.10% of reported EBITDA, which covered dividends and buybacks 2.63 times and left about USD 262 million to reduce debt or fund acquisitions. Operating cash flow is struck after cash interest of about USD 185 million, so that free cash flow is cash left after servicing the debt.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D3; calculation D4

The third strength is a record of early and orderly refinancing. The Credit Agreement was extended to November 2029 for the revolver and November 2030 for term loans in November 2024, EUR 600.0 million of notes were sold at par in September 2025, and the USD 500.0 million secured notes were repaid in March 2026 ahead of their April maturity; no waiver, exchange offer or covenant breach appears in the filings. That lowers the risk that a market window decides the outcome.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_slgn.htm.jsonl, pages 70–71, 119–120; 10-Q_2026-09-30_slgn.htm.jsonl, page 9; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The fourth strength is delivery on cost. The USD 50 million cost program was completed, with about USD 20 million of savings in 2024 and about USD 30 million in 2025, which supported the rise in 2025 adjusted EBIT to USD 723.3 million from USD 658.2 million and gives management a lever if volumes soften.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_slgn.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10, 66

The fifth strength is a conventional governance and audit record, with unqualified opinions, effective internal control and a payout of 29.74% of 2025 net income, which keeps shareholder claims on cash moderate.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation D14

The first weakness is that the October 2026 transaction leaves claim priority, perimeter and leverage open. The largest segment by sales would sit in a partnership that is 40% owned by an outside investor and financed by USD 4.9 billion of new secured term loans on its own assets, and the effect on consolidated net debt and net leverage is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_slgn.htm.jsonl, page 16; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The second weakness is elevated and seasonal leverage with flat 2026 earnings. Net debt over reported EBITDA reached 4.67x at September 30, 2026 against 3.56x at year-end 2025, for which the pro forma effect of the October 19, 2026 announcement is not established, unit volumes fell in all segments over the nine months, and adjusted EBIT was flat only because currency, mix in custom containers, manufacturing efficiencies and an inventory benefit offset lower volumes and a less favorable mix elsewhere.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_slgn.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 15, 18–20; calculation D6

The third weakness is a concentrated maturity profile. At December 31, 2025, 31.89% of principal fell due in 2028 and 28.25% in 2030, so two refinancing windows carry most of the principal.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D9

The fourth weakness is thin metal margins and one large customer. Metal Containers earned an adjusted EBIT margin of 8.3% and Nestlé was 14% of net sales, so a small cost lag or a repricing moves group earnings.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_slgn.htm.jsonl, pages 23, 66–68; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The fifth weakness is reliance on bank-run funding. Supplier finance invoices of USD 482.0 million and customer receivable programs covering about 16% of net sales move cash forward, and the seasonal borrowing that follows is carried on one revolver.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_slgn.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-12-31_slgn.htm.jsonl, page 74; calculation D13

### Company, ownership and revenue durability

Silgan is a listed holding company that makes dispensing systems and specialty closures, steel and aluminum food containers and custom plastic containers through three segments, with about USD 6.5 billion of 2025 net sales and 32% of sales from foreign operations. No sponsor, parent or controlling holder is named; ownership detail and the founders' stockholders agreement are not supplied, so financial policy is set by a classified board and management who state that they use leverage for growth. Creditors benefit from the simple perimeter of one consolidated registrant but must read subsidiary-level claims, because the senior notes lost their subsidiary guarantees on March 31, 2026.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_slgn.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5, 46, 51, 55, 59–60; 10-Q_2026-09-30_slgn.htm.jsonl, page 9

Revenue is predictable rather than growing. About 90% of projected 2026 metal container sales are under multi-year arrangements, Nestlé supplies 14% of group sales under agreements through 2032, metal cans are sold within about 300 miles of the plant, and customers may seek competing offers during a term, which Silgan can match at a lower price. Over the nine months of 2026 net sales rose 4.59% through cost pass-through and currency while unit volumes fell, so revenue visibility is about price pass-through, not volume.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_slgn.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 23–25, 27, 31; 10-Q_2026-09-30_slgn.htm.jsonl, pages 18–20, 23–25; calculation D2

Margins can be defended against input cost over a year because metal cost passes through by contract, but not within a quarter: the third quarter of 2026 showed a delayed pass-through of freight and manufacturing inflation, a less favorable mix and lower volumes. Fixed cost absorption is the sensitive point, as in 2024 when a large pack customer and severe weather cut the fruit and vegetable pack and Metal Containers adjusted EBIT fell USD 39.98 million, 14.16%.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_slgn.htm.jsonl, page 68; 10-Q_2026-09-30_slgn.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 23–24; calculation D15

Cash conversion is steady, with capital expenditure of about USD 307 million in 2025 and about USD 310 million expected for 2026, restructuring cash costs of USD 26.1 million in 2025 and USD 43.7 million in the first nine months of 2026, and cash taxes of about USD 150 million expected for 2026. Operating cash flow is negative in the first nine months because of the seasonal build, USD 646.363 million in 2026, which the revolver finances, so debt peaks in the summer and unwinds by year-end.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_slgn.htm.jsonl, pages 75–76, 80; 10-Q_2026-09-30_slgn.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 29; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Competitive forces, external factors and SWOT

Competitive rivalry is high and price-sensitive: the annual report names several large metal container and closure competitors, says Silgan competes on price, quality and service, and notes that customers may self-manufacture, which about 7% of the U.S. metal food container market still does. Buyer power is high because a small number of large food and consumer goods customers hold multi-year contracts with competitive-proposal rights; supplier power is moderate because steel, aluminum and resin come from a limited number of suppliers, offset by pass-through clauses. The threat of new entrants is limited by plant proximity, with metal cans sold mostly within about 300 miles, and substitution by plastic, glass, paper and composite packaging is real outside high-temperature and long-shelf-life food uses. For creditors this means pricing power is limited and earnings depend on volume, mix and contract terms.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_slgn.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 26–27, 31–33

External factors are material only where they hit pass-through or cost: steel and aluminum tariffs and quotas complicate cost recovery, bisphenol and PFAS rules have forced or may force product changes, natural gas disruption affects the European plants, about 32% of sales are earned abroad against euro debt, and a one percentage point rate move changed 2025 interest expense by about USD 15.7 million. Other political and social factors are not a primary credit driver on the evidence supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_slgn.htm.jsonl, pages 32–33, 36–37, 40, 46–47; calculation D16

In credit terms the strengths are contract-backed pass-through earnings, cash conversion and refinancing record; the weaknesses are seasonal leverage, thin metal margins, customer concentration and a transaction that is not yet sized; the opportunities are pet food and dispensing volume growth, where management points, and use of the unused buyback and distribution proceeds for debt reduction; the threats are a repeat of a short fruit and vegetable pack, delayed cost pass-through and a debt-funded use of capital.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_slgn.htm.jsonl, pages 57–58; 10-Q_2026-09-30_slgn.htm.jsonl, pages 23–24; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale and diversification are supports: a group of about USD 6.5 billion of sales across three segments, with the largest customer at 14%. Competitive advantage is partial, resting on proximity, contracts and scale rather than pricing power. Cost and capex flexibility is moderate: capital expenditure near USD 310 million a year is not easily cut, though the cost program and plant closures show a lever. Margin stability is good on the three-year record and weaker on the 2026 volume trend. Free cash flow is stable in the annual figure and negative in the seasonal quarters. Access to capital markets has been shown by the September 2025 note sale at par, with no rating supplied. Liquidity is adequate in aggregate and thin in early summer. Financial policy is leverage-for-growth with moderate payouts. The trend is flat: earnings are level, debt is seasonally high, and the transaction adds uncertainty; the key missing datapoints are covenant levels, a rating, pro forma figures and customer contract terms.

### Outlook

Over 12 to 24 months management expected higher volumes in dispensing and specialty closures and in metal containers for 2026 and flat custom container volumes, but over nine months of 2026 unit volumes were about 2% lower in dispensing and specialty closures, about 1% lower in metal containers and about 8% lower in custom containers, so the company's own expectation has not been met so far. Tailwinds are pet food growth, prescription dispensing and fragrance volumes, the Europe closure savings and lower interest expense; headwinds are weak beverage closure demand, soup volumes, delayed cost pass-through, tariffs and a higher effective tax rate. Over three to five years the question is whether volume recovery and cost savings, not pass-through pricing, drive earnings, and how the partnership changes the group's debt and ownership.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_slgn.htm.jsonl, pages 57–58; 10-Q_2026-09-30_slgn.htm.jsonl, pages 20–25

### Downside scenario

A realistic downside starts with a short fruit and vegetable pack combined with weaker pet food and beverage closure volumes in 2027, the pattern of 2024 when severe weather and a large customer's cut in pack plans lowered fixed cost absorption in Metal Containers and group reported EBITDA fell 7.88%. The first operational break is lower plant utilization and delayed pass-through of freight and resin cost; the financial effect is lower EBITDA: applying the 2024 fall to 2025 EBITDA gives about USD 845 million and net leverage of 3.87x on year-end 2025 net debt, and applying it to last-twelve-months EBITDA gives about USD 833 million, net leverage of 5.07x on September 2026 seasonal net debt and cash interest cover near 4.83x. Because a short pack also builds less inventory, operating cash flow rose in 2024 and liquidity was protected, so the strain falls on leverage and refinancing, not on liquidity, as the 2028 notes approach and while the partnership's debt is consolidated. Management levers are the cost program, capital expenditure near USD 310 million, the unused buyback and the USD 2.3 billion distribution. Liquidity appears sufficient to avoid distress; the primary downside path is volume and pass-through pressure that lifts leverage while the 2028 maturities and the transaction compete for the same refinancing window.
