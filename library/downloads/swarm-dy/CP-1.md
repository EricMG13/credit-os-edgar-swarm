### Financial interpretation

Leverage rose because acquisition debt arrived before the acquired earnings were fully in the base. Net debt on carrying values was 1.50x reported EBITDA at the January 2025 year end, 2.99x at the January 2026 year end and 2.73x on trailing earnings to August 1, 2026; total debt over reported EBITDA was 4.00x and 3.11x at the last two dates. The step from the January 2025 level came from borrowing for Power Solutions in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026, and the fall since year end is earnings growth and the part-period inclusion of the acquired earnings, not debt reduction: debt was not repaid and a further acquisition was paid in cash. Neither ratio is a covenant ratio, and the reported covenant ratios are not printed.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 83–85; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 2–5; calculation C04

Cash servicing looks strong but flatters the current debt load. Reported EBITDA covered cash interest paid 11.30x in fiscal 2026 and 10.49x on trailing earnings, yet trailing cash interest holds the acquisition debt for only part of the base year, cash interest paid in the first half of fiscal 2027 alone was nearly twice the first half of fiscal 2026, accrued net interest expense in the half exceeded cash paid by a margin shown in the definition conflict register, and interest payable on the balance sheet more than tripled, so cash interest will rise toward the accrual as it is paid. Cover on the issuer's adjusted EBITDA is higher still, at 11.10x, but the add-backs are the issuer's.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation C05

Cash-flow quality is seasonal and working-capital driven. Free cash flow was an outflow of USD 54.2 million in the first half of fiscal 2027 after receivables absorbed cash, although days sales outstanding improved to 101 days from 108 days a year earlier, and the supplier finance program, with USD 367.4 million of confirmed obligations inside accounts payable at August 1, 2026 against USD 226.4 million at year end, supplies part of the operating funding. If the program were withdrawn, payables would shorten and operating cash flow would absorb the difference before any change in earnings, so the float is working-capital funding that behaves like short-term borrowing and is not in the debt figure. Receivables sold without recourse under a customer-sponsored program also shorten collection, and their volume is not printed.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 29; EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 4; EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 8; calculation C09

Liquidity is adequate but fell. Liquidity of USD 1,086.5 million at August 1, 2026 compares with USD 1,455.5 million at the year end, a fall that follows cash paid for acquisitions, including USD 225.5 million in the second quarter, the first-half working-capital outflow and share repurchases; no revolver borrowing is outstanding and the revolver and Term Loan A mature on December 23, 2030, ahead of the 2033 Term Loan B. The 2029 notes are the first bullet maturity. The two maintenance covenants, a maximum net leverage ratio of 4.50 to 1.00 and a minimum interest cover of 2.50 to 1.00, are reported as met, but their headroom is not calculable from the printed figures.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 19–21; EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation C08

The strongest contrary evidence is the pace of earnings growth: first-half adjusted EBITDA margin was 14.6% against 13.5% a year earlier, backlog reached USD 12,242 million at August 1, 2026, and the Building Systems segment reached a margin above the Communications segment. That supports a faster fall in leverage than the debt-only view, and would win if trailing earnings keep absorbing the acquired businesses while debt stays flat. It would lose if the deferral of wireless program revenue announced with the second quarter results widens or customer concentration, with AT&T and Verizon each above 10% of revenue, leads to capital expenditure cuts, because working capital would then absorb cash before earnings fell. The observable tests are receivable days, the supplier finance balance, accrued interest payable and the next two quarterly balance sheets.
Basis: EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 3; EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 54–55
