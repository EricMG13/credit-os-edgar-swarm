## Findings

### Bottom line

The credit is stretched but financeable for a year: earnings are improving and the 2027 wall is nearly cleared, yet dependence on one drug and USD 3,766 million due in 2028 leave creditors reliant on refinancing before generics arrive.
Basis: calculation M6; calculation M11; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

Reported net leverage was 7.73x at 2026-09-30 on net debt of USD 18,152 million, or 4.81x without the non-cash goodwill impairment and 4.16x on guided adjusted EBITDA. Pro forma net debt is USD 18,227 million after the separation payment dated 2026-10-12 and USD 18,237 million after the Bausch + Lomb acquisition, whose date is not stated, giving 7.77x in each case; the effect of the redemption dated 2026-11-30 is not established.
Basis: calculation M1; calculation M2; calculation M3; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Key findings

Earnings and cash flow are improving fast, which is the main support. Adjusted EBITDA for nine months 2026 was USD 3,323 million against USD 2,565 million a year earlier and operating cash flow was USD 1,714 million against USD 905 million, and the issuer raised full-year guidance, but the margin gain rests on net price rather than volume.
Basis: calculation M9; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

Leverage is high on reported figures and moderate on adjusted ones, so the answer depends on which earnings measure holds. Reported net leverage of 7.73x, or 7.77x pro forma for the events since quarter end, falls to 4.81x without the goodwill impairment and to 4.04x for Bausch Health excluding Bausch + Lomb on guided adjusted EBITDA, but cash interest cover was only 1.73x in FY2025 and cover for the last twelve months is not established.
Basis: calculation M1; calculation M3; calculation M10; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Xifaxan is the dominant downside driver. It supplied 21.55% of FY2025 revenue and Salix earned 80.30% segment margin in nine months 2026; a loss of 37.78% of Xifaxan revenue would take Bausch Health excluding Bausch + Lomb to 5.00x net leverage on guided EBITDA, and each 10% loss costs about USD 165 million of segment profit, against Medicare pricing from 2027 and expected generic competition in 2028.
Basis: calculation M7; calculation M11; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_bhc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 26

Liquidity is adequate through 2027 but thin against 2028 once the perimeters are separated. Group liquidity, cash plus undrawn facilities, of USD 3,620 million exceeds the USD 716 million due before the end of 2027, with pro forma liquidity after the 2027 redemption not established, yet Bausch Health excluding Bausch + Lomb covers only 0.81x of the principal due through 2028 and Bausch + Lomb 0.80x, because each perimeter holds its own cash and facilities.
Basis: calculation M5; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bhc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 127

Refinancing is the central risk and the structure makes it harder for unsecured holders. USD 3,766 million falls due in 2028, or 18.67% of principal, the issuer's ratings are Caa2 at Moody's and B- at S&P, and 68.28% of the Bausch + Lomb shares are pledged to the 126NumberCo secured creditors while non-guarantor subsidiaries produce 57.40% of revenue.
Basis: calculation M6; calculation M11; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bhc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 131

Management is using cash to reduce debt rather than to distribute it, which supports creditors. It repurchased USD 602 million of 2026 notes in 2025, announced redemption of USD 643 million of 2027 notes from cash and has paid no dividend, while the USD 50 million buyback is small; against this the record of exchanges, including a 44.14% principal cut in 2022, shows willingness to re-rank claims under stress.
Basis: calculation M12; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Capital intensity is low, which gives flexibility. Capex was 3.87% and research 6.13% of FY2025 revenue, and free cash flow reached USD 1,865 million over the last twelve months, but that is 49.43% of EBITDA excluding the impairment and is before USD 183 million of premium-related interest the issuer reports in financing.
Basis: calculation M4; calculation M11; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is that the issuer's guidance looks conservative: it implies fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA of USD 992 million to USD 1,092 million against USD 1,350 million in the third quarter, Bausch Health excluding Bausch + Lomb reports fourteen consecutive quarters of growth, and leverage on guided adjusted EBITDA is between 4.04x and 4.16x in every perimeter. If Xifaxan held through 2028, or generic entry were delayed, the 2028 maturities would be refinanced from a stronger base and the view would improve. It would worsen if generic entry came earlier than expected, if Salix volumes kept falling while prices reset, or if a further exchange or release of the pledged shares re-ranked claims.
Basis: calculation M8; calculation M3; Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.1_2026-10-28_ex991-q32026.htm.jsonl, page 1; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_ex991-q32026.htm.jsonl, page 4

Basis: calculation M1; calculation M10; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Credit-relevant company description

Bausch Health is a Canadian-incorporated specialty pharmaceutical and device group whose revenue is half US-led specialty products and half Bausch + Lomb eye health, which gives scale and a branded portfolio but ties cash flow to a few franchises. Revenue was USD 10,266 million in FY2025 and USD 11,113 million over the last twelve months, the United States produced 59.79% of FY2025 revenue and three wholesalers took 48%, so cash flow visibility is good while products keep their protection and weak once one loses it.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_bhc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-31_bhc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 342

The business model supports debt service because products are prescribed and reimbursed, but it concentrates refinancing confidence on Salix, whose segment profit was 47.34% of the nine-month total, and on Bausch + Lomb, whose cash is retained within it.
Basis: calculation M11; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Ownership and group structure

No sponsor or controlling shareholder is disclosed, so the credit rests on operating cash flow and refinancing rather than owner support, and leverage tolerance is shown by behavior: no dividends, cash used to retire debt, and repeated liability management. The group holds about 87% of Bausch + Lomb through a restricted financing subsidiary, 126NumberCo, whose creditors have a first lien over the pledged shares, while the older secured and unsecured notes are not guaranteed by it, so claims on the largest subsidiary sit ahead of them. Bausch Health and Bausch + Lomb share a chief executive in an interim role, which concentrates decisions on separation and dividends in one executive.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bhc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 38–40; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bhc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 138

Creditor alignment is mixed: change-of-control puts at 101% and covenant compliance support lenders, while the unrestricted status of Bausch + Lomb keeps its earnings and net debt out of the covenant tests and the 2022 exchange cut principal by 44.14% under troubled debt restructuring accounting.
Basis: calculation M12; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is predictable in volume but exposed to price and exclusivity, because growth is price-led and the largest product loses protection from 2027. Salix revenue rose USD 343 million in nine months 2026 on USD 504 million of net price against USD 173 million of lost volume after the exit of high-rebate channels, the Medicare price for Xifaxan applies from 2027-01-01 and generic competition is expected in 2028, so the price lever that lifted margins cannot be assumed to repeat and a drop in net price falls straight to profit.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bhc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 123; 10-K_2025-12-31_bhc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 26

Outside Salix, International revenue grew 7.63% and Bausch + Lomb 8.58% in nine months 2026 and Diversified fell 1.03%, which diversifies growth but not the dependence of profit on Salix.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins are improving because revenue is growing much faster than costs: nine-month revenue rose 11.34% while cost of goods sold rose 1.34% and selling, general and administrative costs rose 1.80%, and issuer-adjusted EBITDA margin rose from 34.34% to 39.95%. The large sales and marketing base is a fixed cost, so operating leverage cuts both ways, and a Xifaxan revenue loss would reduce profit by close to its revenue at the Salix margin, about USD 165 million for each 10% lost.
Basis: calculation M7; calculation M9; calculation M14; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Margins can be defended against cost inflation by the low capital need and research flexibility, but not against loss of exclusivity.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Cash conversion is moderate and improving but flattered by items reported outside operating cash flow. Free cash flow was USD 1,003 million in FY2025 and operating cash flow of USD 1,714 million less capex reached USD 1,462 million in nine months 2026 against USD 600 million, yet it is 49.43% of EBITDA excluding the impairment over the last twelve months, excludes USD 183 million of interest the issuer allocates to debt premiums, and bears USD 167 million of legacy legal settlement payments in nine months 2026.
Basis: calculation M4; calculation M16; Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-10-28_ex991-q32026.htm.jsonl, page 27

Capex flexibility is limited to a small base of 3.87% of revenue, so deleveraging depends on EBITDA and on not paying distributions.
Basis: calculation M11

### Competitive forces

Rivalry and entry matter most through generic entrants: a generic rifaximin has final approval and exclusivity decisions are still being litigated, so entry pressure on Xifaxan is high and its timing is unconfirmed. Buyer power is high because three wholesalers took 48% of revenue and payers set prices through rebates of USD 1,377 million accrued at 2026-09-30 and Medicare negotiation; supplier power and the threat of substitutes are not established from the supplied documents.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bhc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bhc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 58

### Policy, macro and regulatory factors

Policy factors are a primary credit driver because the Medicare negotiated price takes effect in 2027 and tariff rules changed in 2026; the issuer does not expect the tariff to be material. Economic exposure is mainly interest rates, as USD 6,615 million of debt is variable-rate and each 1 percentage point adds about USD 66 million of annual interest; social and technology factors are not primary drivers on the supplied evidence.
Basis: calculation M15; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bhc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 92–93

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months the outlook is supportive for cash flow: guidance implies fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA of USD 992 million to USD 1,092 million, the 2027 notes are being redeemed and no maturity above USD 701 million falls due before 2028, but 2027 brings the Medicare price and the pledged-share sweep terms limit what a Bausch + Lomb dividend can do for unsecured holders. Tailwinds are price, channel mix, Bausch + Lomb growth and the China distribution acquisition; headwinds are the 2027 price, litigation and the 2028 expiry.
Basis: calculation M8; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.1_2026-10-28_ex991-q32026.htm.jsonl, page 4

Over three to five years the maturity profile is the dominant issue: USD 3,766 million in 2028, USD 1,667 million in 2029, USD 4,123 million in 2030, USD 3,896 million in 2031 and USD 6,000 million thereafter, so refinancing capacity depends on holding earnings after generic entry and on market windows.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Downside scenario

The downside path starts with generic rifaximin entering in or before 2028 after a lower Medicare price in 2027, which cuts Xifaxan volume and price. Salix, which earns an 80.30% margin, loses profit almost one for one, about USD 165 million for each 10% of Xifaxan revenue lost, so Bausch Health excluding Bausch + Lomb moves from 4.04x on guided adjusted EBITDA to 5.00x after a 37.78% loss, with net debt unchanged. That leverage arrives as USD 2,326 million of Bausch Health debt matures in 2028 and a further USD 1,639 million in 2029, so the first financial break is refinancing access rather than liquidity, because cash and facilities cover 2027.
Basis: calculation M3; calculation M7; calculation M11; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bhc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 127–128

Management has levers: the USD 643 million redemption shows cash can retire debt, capex of 3.87% and research of 6.13% can be cut, distributions are zero, and Bausch + Lomb could be monetized, with proceeds above USD 50 million swept to the 126NumberCo secured creditors first. Resilience looks sufficient to avoid liquidity distress through 2027 but not established through 2028 without refinancing, and the primary downside path is generic Xifaxan competition meeting the 2028 maturities. Observable triggers are a Teva launch, falling Salix volume and any exchange offer.
Basis: calculation M11; calculation M12; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Overall credit view

Overall, Bausch Health Companies Inc. presents an adequate but concentrated business profile supported by earnings momentum, a cleared 2027 wall and low capital intensity, but credit quality is constrained by dependence on Xifaxan, thin reported cover and a 2028 maturity wall. Cash-flow durability appears adequate near term and weak after 2027 because Xifaxan protection is ending. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on holding Salix earnings, the ratings, the pledged-share structure and market windows. The primary downside path is generic competition for Xifaxan meeting the 2028 maturities. Further analysis would require the generic launch date, the covenant ratios and the executed debt documents. Top risk: generic Xifaxan competition meeting the 2028 maturities.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)
