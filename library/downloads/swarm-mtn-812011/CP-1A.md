## Findings

### Bottom line

Vail Resorts is a concentrated, weather-exposed ski operator whose advance pass sales bring cash in early but whose fixed costs and shareholder payouts leave creditors exposed in a poor snow season; sponsor and governance risk is Medium because no creditor-adverse action is documented.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_mtn-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 97, 99, 101, 103; 10-K_2026-07-31_mtn-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 119, 135; calculation C02; calculation C07; calculation C16

The Mountain segment produced 96.85% of Total Reported EBITDA in fiscal 2026, and a 13.4% fall in skier visits cut Resort Reported EBITDA by 11.66% on a 4.45% fall in Resort revenue. Dividends were 2.15x net income attributable to Vail Resorts, and the supplied documents include no executed debt agreement and no proxy statement, so the legal strength of creditor protection is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_mtn-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 103, 133, 135, 183, 190; calculation C02; calculation C06; calculation C05; calculation C13; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

- Earnings rest on one weather-dependent segment with high operating leverage, so a poor season reaches creditors' cash flow quickly. The Mountain segment was 88.20% of net revenue and 96.85% of Total Reported EBITDA in fiscal 2026, and Resort Reported EBITDA fell 11.66% on a 4.45% fall in Resort revenue, a decremental margin of 74.65%, even after USD 45 million of efficiency savings. The filings describe a cost base with a significant fixed component, which is why Resort EBITDA fell more than twice as fast as Resort revenue.
  Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_mtn-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 99, 101, 149, 183; calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C05; calculation C06; calculation C07

- The pass programme steadies revenue but front-loads the cash cycle, and the first reading on the next season is weaker. Pass products were about 70% of lift revenue in fiscal 2026 against 65% a year earlier and pass revenue rose USD 38.8 million while paid lift revenue fell 17.5%; pass sales for 2026/2027 through 2026-09-18 were down about 12% in units and about 6% in dollars, so fiscal 2027 starts from less advance cash, and the Epic Coverage refund terms would cut pass revenue further if another season closes early.
  Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_mtn-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 22, 23, 53, 99, 101, 104, 150

- Operating rights rest on public-land permits, leases and concessions rather than freehold title, so enterprise value depends on renewals and fee terms. Forest Service permits run to 2029-12-31 for Breckenridge and 2031-12-01 for Vail Mountain with fees of 1.5% to 4.0% of adjusted gross revenue, the company says it is not aware of a refusal to renew an operating ski resort permit, and the Grand Teton concession moves from a flat fee of 8.01% to a tiered fee of 14% to 22% from 2027-01-01, subject to review by Congress; the sales base is not stated, but variable franchise fees for the park concessions were USD 6.7 million in fiscal 2026, which caps the annual step at about USD 5.01 million to USD 11.7 million, or 30.70% to 71.71% of fiscal 2026 Lodging Reported EBITDA.
  Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_mtn-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 30, 40, 41, 48, 152; calculation C23; calculation C24

- Structure puts debt in subsidiaries and limits the cash that can move up to the parent. The parent is a holding company, the bank borrower is a wholly owned subsidiary, the Whistler facility and the secured notes assumed with Peak Resorts restrict distributions, and noncontrolling interests equal 56.85% of total stockholders' equity, so claims and cash leakage exist below the parent that the supplied documents do not size.
  Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_mtn-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 82, 133, 158, 160, 161, 162, 163, 164; calculation C20

- Shareholder payouts, not acquisitions, are now the main use of cash that competes with creditors, and they have outrun free cash flow. Dividends and repurchases were 1.52x free cash flow over fiscal 2024 to fiscal 2026 and dividends alone were 2.15x fiscal 2026 net income attributable to Vail Resorts, while equity attributable to Vail Resorts fell 43.34% and goodwill and intangibles exceed total stockholders' equity. Repurchases were cut to USD 45 million in fiscal 2026 from USD 270 million, which shows flexibility, but the quarterly dividend of USD 2.22 per share was held and the latest one is to be funded from available liquidity rather than cash on hand.
  Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_mtn-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 94, 133, 135; 10-K_2025-07-31_mtn-20250731.htm.jsonl, page 86; calculation C16; calculation C13; calculation C18; calculation C19

The annual report is signed by a chief executive who is also chairperson, the company reports two nominations of five director candidates for the 2026 annual meeting, and two interim reports overstated cash by USD 38.8 million and USD 38.4 million because certificates of deposit were counted as cash; against that, the auditor gave unqualified opinions on the statements and internal control, the notes print no related-party transaction or management fee, and affiliates hold about 1.00% of shares on an indicative basis, so there is no disclosed sponsor to support or to extract from the issuer.

- Acquisition risk is low for now, but lease-type claims ahead of equity are large. The last acquisition was Crans-Montana in May 2024, paid in cash, and no acquisition payment followed in fiscal 2025 or fiscal 2026, while the Canyons lease obligation and the USD 97.8 million of Park City contingent consideration, which the auditor treated as a critical audit matter, are fixed and variable claims of the landlord that rank beside funded debt.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is that the shortfall is weather-driven and the network is broad. The company operates 42 resorts across North America, Europe and Australia, Destination guests rose to 58% of destination-resort visits from 56%, pass revenue rose USD 38.8 million in fiscal 2026 even as paid lift revenue fell 17.5%, efficiency savings of USD 45 million cushioned EBITDA, repurchases were cut to USD 45 million, the auditor gave unqualified opinions, and no priming, exchange or other creditor-adverse action is documented.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_mtn-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 98, 101, 104, 129, 135

The view would soften if pass sales for 2026/2027 recover toward the prior year, if payouts fall back inside free cash flow, and if the proxy statement and the executed debt agreements show no concentrated holder and tight restricted-payment limits. It would worsen if a second weak pass campaign, a dividend held above free cash flow, the loss or repricing of a permit or concession, or a contested board changes financial policy, in which case the governance level would move above Medium.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_mtn-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 94, 101, 120; calculation C16

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The company has grown by acquisition but has made no acquisition payment since the purchase of Crans-Montana on 2024-05-02 for CHF 97.2 million (USD 106.8 million), funded with cash on hand; the cash flow statements print no acquisition payments in fiscal 2025 or fiscal 2026. Earlier acquisitions built the network through the Canyons lease in 2013, Park City in 2014, Whistler Blackcomb in 2016, Peak Resorts in 2019, Seven Springs in 2021 and Andermatt-Sedrun in 2022; Peak Resorts, the Canyons lease and Andermatt-Sedrun left claims that remain outstanding, namely EPR Secured Notes of USD 114.162 million at 9.52% to 12.88%, a Canyons obligation of USD 380.533 million with contingent payments to the landlord, and an NRP Loan of USD 34.025 million.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_mtn-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 22, 116, 135, 156, 163, 164, 166, 191

New debt issues since 2024 were refinancings rather than growth funding. In May 2024 the company issued USD 600.0 million of 6.50% senior notes due 2032 at par to redeem USD 600.0 million of 6.25% notes due 2025, and in July 2025 it issued USD 500.0 million of 5.625% senior notes due 2030 at par; both are unsecured, guaranteed by certain domestic subsidiaries, redeemable from 2027, subject to a change of control offer at 101% and free of financial maintenance covenants. The 0.0% convertible notes of USD 525.0 million matured on 2026-01-02 and were repaid in cash from a USD 275.0 million delayed draw term loan drawn on 2025-12-26 and cash on hand. On 2026-02-09 the credit agreement was amended and restated with a new USD 1,275.0 million term loan, a maturity that falls to 2030-04-16 while the 2030 notes remain outstanding, and a lower margin, which the annual report prints as SOFR plus 1.50% in the debt note and SOFR plus 1.88% for new borrowings in the liquidity discussion.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_mtn-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 114, 116, 158, 159, 160, 161, 162; calculation C27

Two items sit outside the debt table or beside it and matter to creditors. The Park City contingent consideration of USD 97.8 million is a liability that the auditor treated as a critical audit matter, and a new finance lease for the Canyons Parking Garage added a liability of USD 22.2 million at commencement. The Canadian Whistler facility was amended on 2025-09-24 to a 2030-09-24 maturity and a smaller size of C$250.0 million and was undrawn at 2026-07-31.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_mtn-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 131, 162, 166, 172, 173; 10-K_2025-07-31_mtn-20250731.htm.jsonl, page 95; calculation C25

After the year end the Board approved a dividend of USD 2.22 per share on 2026-09-24, payable on 2026-10-27, the company entered net investment hedges on its Swiss operations in August 2026, and pass sales for 2026/2027 were reported below the prior year through 2026-09-18.
