## Findings

### Bottom line

Fundamental credit quality is adequate but volatile: moderate leverage, fixed-rate notes due in 2028 and liquidity of USD 203.4 million support creditors, while commodity-priced earnings and an owner-controlled payout leave little margin for a price or uptime shock.
Net debt was 1.83x last-twelve-months EBITDA at September 30, 2026 and interest cover was 6.10x, yet EBITDA fell 36.36% in FY2024 on a revenue fall of 22.91%, and distributions exceeded free cash flow in FY2023 and FY2025. The primary downside path is a price or outage shock that cuts EBITDA while distributions continue; a change of control would add a repurchase offer of about USD 555.5 million against liquidity of USD 203.4 million.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_uan.htm.jsonl, page 31; calculation B1; calculation B2; calculation B8; calculation B13

### Key findings

Earnings are the weakest dimension: revenue is exposed to commodity prices and plant uptime, and fixed plant costs turn a modest revenue move into a large EBITDA move. In FY2024 revenue fell 22.91% and EBITDA fell 36.36%, 1.59 times as fast, because direct operating expenses fell only 8.32%; EBITDA then recovered to USD 210.85 million in FY2025 and USD 224.73 million for the last twelve months. Customers contract for less than one year at fixed prices, the top two took 28% of FY2025 sales, and a turnaround or outage hits a single site.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_uan.htm.jsonl, pages 14–16; 10-K_2025-12-31_uan.htm.jsonl, page 113; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B2; calculation B4; calculation B13

Leverage is moderate and the debt is fixed rate, which is the strongest support. Total debt to EBITDA ranged from 1.95x in FY2023 to 3.18x in FY2024 and stood at 2.54x on the last-twelve-months basis, with net leverage of 1.83x; because the notes carry a fixed coupon, cover of EBITDA to cash interest moved only with EBITDA, from 5.24x at the low in FY2024 to 6.10x. Debt of USD 569.74 million includes finance lease obligations, and no principal falls due before June 2028.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_uan.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-12-31_uan.htm.jsonl, pages 104–106; calculation B1; calculation B13

Cash conversion is adequate and improving but rests on rising capital spending. Free cash flow was positive in every period, USD 219.33 million in FY2023, USD 98.84 million in FY2025 and USD 148.71 million for the last twelve months, equal to 78.03%, 46.87% and 66.17% of EBITDA. Cash capital expenditures rose from USD 50.8 million in FY2025 to USD 88.08 million over the last twelve months, and the 2026 estimate sits 66.89% to 82.7% above FY2025 spending, so conversion depends on the East Dubuque expansion and reliability projects paying back.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_uan.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-K_2025-12-31_uan.htm.jsonl, page 79; calculation B5; calculation B6; calculation B13

Liquidity is adequate for the next two quarters and the refinancing window is distant but not remote. Liquidity of USD 203.4 million at September 30, 2026 is cash of USD 158.2 million plus availability under an undrawn asset-based facility; after the declared distribution of USD 18.9 million and fourth-quarter capital spending, cash would stand at USD 101.18 million to USD 110.18 million before any operating cash flow in the quarter. The notes mature 575 days after the as-of date and become a current liability in mid-2027, so refinancing capacity will be tested at the point the nitrogen cycle is least predictable.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_uan.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-09-30_uan.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_uan.htm.jsonl, page 104; Source readiness (CP-0); calculation B7; calculation B13

Financial policy is the second weakness. Distributions of USD 281.36 million in FY2023 and USD 125.99 million in FY2025 exceeded free cash flow and were met from cash, about 39% went to CVR Energy and Icahn Enterprises, and the board can change the policy at any time. Customer prepayments lifted deferred revenue to USD 83.93 million at September 30, 2026, equal to 53.06% of the cash balance, so part of the cash buffer is owed in product and the working-capital inflow will reverse as deliveries are made.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_uan.htm.jsonl, page 6; calculation B10; calculation B11; calculation B13

Event risk is concentrated in control. The owners are considering strategic options that could involve the Partnership, with no terms stated, and a sale of the Icahn interest in CVR Energy could be a change of control under the notes with a repurchase offer of about USD 555.5 million; liquidity covers 36.61% of it. Supply and services from CVR Energy also depend on common control, so a change would reach operations as well as debt, and the executed debt documents that would settle the terms are not in the evidence.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_uan.htm.jsonl, page 47; 10-K_2025-12-31_uan.htm.jsonl, page 115; calculation B8; calculation B13

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better view is the balance sheet and the cost position. Net leverage of 1.83x is below its FY2024 and FY2025 levels, cover of 6.10x has recovered, free cash flow covered nine-month 2026 distributions, and U.S. natural gas prices keep a cost advantage for domestic producers while Middle East disruption has lifted fertilizer prices. That view would win if the East Dubuque expansion lifts output, prices stay firm through the fourth quarter and the owners' review ends without a transaction.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_uan.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21; calculation B1

The view would turn negative if EBITDA fell again by the size seen in FY2024, which on last-twelve-months EBITDA would leave USD 143.03 million and take total debt to EBITDA to 3.98x and cover to 3.89x, or on any disclosed move in control. This is an illustration of the issuer's own history, not a forecast; the Downside module sets the stress properly.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B2; calculation B9; calculation B13

### Company description

CVR Partners makes and sells nitrogen fertilizer, mainly UAN and ammonia, from two plants: Coffeyville, Kansas, which gasifies pet coke, and East Dubuque, Illinois, which runs on natural gas. Revenue is wholesale, to retailers and distributors and to agricultural and industrial buyers, under contracts that generally fix price for less than one year, so revenue visibility is short and follows market prices within each season.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_uan.htm.jsonl, pages 12–16

Cash generation follows the planting season, with higher sales in the first half, and is interrupted every few years by a planned turnaround at each plant. The business model therefore supports cash-flow visibility for a quarter or two, not for the length of the debt, and refinancing confidence depends on where the nitrogen price cycle stands when the notes become current.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_uan.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-K_2025-12-31_uan.htm.jsonl, page 79

### Ownership and group structure

The Partnership is a listed limited partnership controlled by CVR Energy through the general partner, with Icahn Enterprises owning about 71% of CVR Energy. Public unitholders hold about 60% of the units but cannot elect the general partner or its directors, and the board was reshaped in October 2025. The financial-policy implication is that distributions, transactions and debt decisions are owner-led, and creditors are protected by the debt documents rather than by governance.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_uan.htm.jsonl, page 9

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

UAN is the largest product and is priced at the gate per ton, so revenue is a function of price, volume and mix, with no pass-through of feedstock cost. In the third quarter of 2026 lower UAN prices and ammonia volumes cut net sales by USD 17.4 million against the prior-year quarter, while for the nine months higher UAN and ammonia prices added USD 64.7 million; the revenue base is therefore cyclical and not contractually recurring, and the top two customers are 28% of sales.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_uan.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-K_2025-12-31_uan.htm.jsonl, page 16

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins cannot be defended well under volume or price pressure because the plants carry high fixed operating costs: direct operating expenses excluding turnaround were 40.69% of FY2024 net sales against 34.21% in FY2023 after revenue fell. EBITDA margin was 41.25%, 34.06% and 34.79% in FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025. Feedstock is a small part of cost, so margin depends mainly on price and utilization rather than input cost.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_uan.htm.jsonl, page 113; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B3; calculation B4; calculation B13

### Capital intensity and FCF conversion

Maintenance and growth capital spending are rising and turnaround costs are expensed in the quarter incurred, so conversion of EBITDA to free cash flow was 78.03% in FY2023 and 46.87% in FY2025. Management can defer growth projects and the board can cut distributions, which are the levers that protect liquidity; the issuer says it may adjust the timing or scope of growth capital projects.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_uan.htm.jsonl, page 32; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B5; calculation B13

### Market forces, credit-translated

Rivalry is high: domestic competitors, led by a much larger nitrogen producer, and subsidised imports compete on delivered price. Buyers are sophisticated and price focused, which caps pass-through; suppliers matter because CVR Energy's refinery supplies part of the pet coke; entry and substitution threats are limited for nitrogen itself. The credit consequence is a price-taker earnings profile with no pricing power to defend cash flow in a downturn.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_uan.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_uan.htm.jsonl, page 16

Regulatory and geopolitical factors do alter credit risk here: Middle East supply disruption and import tariffs have supported U.S. prices, and litigation over an October 2025 ammonia release and a Kansas environmental class action carry unquantified liability. These items change liquidity and earnings risk and so are treated as material.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_uan.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_uan.htm.jsonl, page 14

### Credit-translated SWOT

Strengths are fixed-rate debt with no maturity before June 2028, moderate leverage, positive free cash flow in every period, an undrawn facility and domestic feedstock. Weaknesses are volatile EBITDA, high fixed costs, owner-controlled payouts and a short contract tenor. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are the East Dubuque expansion and firm prices; threats are a price or outage shock, a change of control and the unquantified lawsuits.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_uan.htm.jsonl, page 19

### Financial profile and credit quality

Assessed on the nine dimensions: scale and market position Average; competitive advantage Average; business diversification Weak, with two plants, one segment and two products; cost and capex flexibility Average, because growth projects can be deferred while direct costs are largely fixed; margin stability Weak; free cash flow stability Average; ability to refinance Average, untested and dependent on the price cycle; liquidity position Average; financial policy and governance Weak. The main supports are leverage and liquidity, the main constraints are earnings volatility and owner control, the trend in leverage is improving from FY2024, and the missing datapoints are the debt documents, ratings and peer figures.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

In the next twelve to twenty-four months the tailwinds are firm prices from supply disruption, the expansion adding about 5% of ammonia capacity and the end of the turnaround; the headwinds are a normalisation of prices, higher capital spending and the lawsuits. Over three to five years the notes mature in 2028, the Coffeyville dual-feedstock project is planned for construction in 2028 and the owners' strategic review is unresolved. Management expects fourth-quarter ammonia utilization of 88% to 93% and says nitrogen supplies remain tight, which is management language and not evidence of price direction.
Basis: EX99.1_2026-09-30_uan_d162480dex991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 7; EX99.1_2026-09-30_uan_d162480dex991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_uan.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_uan.htm.jsonl, page 20

### Qualitative downside scenario

The scenario is a nitrogen price fall or a plant outage of the kind that cut FY2024 EBITDA. The first operational break is lower realized gate prices or lost tons at a single site; because direct operating costs are largely fixed, EBITDA falls faster than revenue; free cash flow then drops below the distribution the board has declared, and cash covers the gap, as it did in FY2023 and FY2025. Leverage rises and cover falls as shown in the illustration, the facility's availability of USD 45.2 million is the next buffer, and the notes become current in mid-2027 while earnings are weak. The board can cut distributions and defer growth projects, which should avoid liquidity distress at current cash, but a change of control in the same window would add the repurchase offer.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B2; calculation B9; calculation B11

### Overall credit view

Overall, CVR Partners presents an adequate business profile supported by fixed-rate debt with no maturity before 2028, moderate leverage and positive free cash flow, but credit quality is constrained by commodity-priced and operationally concentrated earnings, an owner-controlled payout and unseen debt terms. Cash-flow durability appears adequate in the near term and weak through a full cycle. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the nitrogen price level and the owners' plans in 2027. The primary downside path is a price or outage shock with distributions continuing and a change of control in the same window. Further analysis would require the executed debt documents, ratings and market prices.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths, ranked. First, fixed-rate notes of USD 550.0 million with no maturity before June 2028 and an undrawn facility: interest cost is fixed, so cover of 6.10x moves only with EBITDA, and no refinancing is needed for 575 days. Second, moderate leverage of 1.83x net on last-twelve-months EBITDA, which leaves room to absorb a moderate decline. Third, positive free cash flow in every period, USD 148.71 million over the last twelve months, which funds capital spending and most distributions. Fourth, liquidity of USD 203.4 million, which comfortably exceeds the known calls of the fourth quarter.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_uan.htm.jsonl, page 31; calculation B1; calculation B5; calculation B13

Weaknesses, ranked. First, volatile EBITDA on high fixed costs: EBITDA fell 36.36% in FY2024 on a revenue fall of 22.91%, which is the path to a liquidity or refinancing problem. Second, owner control with a variable distribution policy that exceeded free cash flow in two of three fiscal years, so creditors rely on cash rather than retained earnings. Third, change-of-control exposure of about USD 555.5 million with owners reviewing options. Fourth, rising capital spending and a working-capital inflow from prepayments that will reverse. Fifth, unseen debt documents and no ratings or market prices.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B2; calculation B8; calculation B11; calculation B13
