## Findings

### Bottom line

Murphy's credit profile is solid on leverage and liquidity but exposed to the oil price and thin on free cash flow, so creditors are well covered through a price fall like 2025's but not if shareholder returns and exploration spending keep rising. Net debt of USD 1,134.05 million is 0.65x last-twelve-month EBITDA, liquidity of USD 2,499.9 million is 152.96% of total debt and no note falls due before 2029, yet free cash flow was only USD 240.61 million after USD 1,526.64 million of property additions and no oil is hedged.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C3; calculation C5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_mur-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 13–14, 55

The view is limited because no rating, peer data or executed debt document is supplied, so the profile is judged on its own terms and the ranking of the notes against the revolver is not tested.
Basis: Source readiness (CP-0); 10-K_2025-12-31_mur-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 84–85

### Key findings

Leverage is low and cover is wide, but both rest on an EBITDA that rose with price and not with volume. Last-twelve-month EBITDA of USD 1,740.6 million is 46.06% above FY2025 while third-quarter production was 7% lower, so net leverage of 0.65x and cash interest cover of 20.17x would read 0.95x and 13.81x if EBITDA returned to its FY2025 level of USD 1,191.7 million with debt unchanged; that is a sensitivity, not a forecast. The issuer's EBITDA excludes the minority in the Gulf of America venture while debt and cash include it, so the ratios are slightly conservative.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C2; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_mur-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 35

Margins are high but they follow price. EBITDA margin was 55.77% over the last twelve months against 44.30% in FY2025, helped by a 27.87% fall in lease operating expenses in the nine months, while exploration expenses rose 170.38% and depreciation, depletion and amortization 6.82%. A price fall would remove the revenue side of that gain at once, whereas the cost side rests on lower workover and production-related costs in the Gulf of America that may not repeat.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_mur-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 35, 37

Cash conversion is the weakest dimension. Free cash flow of USD 240.61 million is only 0.14x EBITDA because property additions take 48.92% of revenue, nine-month capital spending is already 16.98% above the whole of 2025, and adjusted free cash flow after dividends, minority distributions and acquisitions was negative USD 32.2 million for the nine months. In 2025, at lower prices, adjusted free cash flow was USD 12.1 million, USD 87.9 million short of the USD 100.0 million of repurchases, and the revolver was drawn USD 100.0 million at the year end.
Basis: calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_mur-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 45, 50; 10-K_2025-12-31_mur-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 64, 113

Liquidity and the maturity profile remove refinancing risk for several years. Liquidity of USD 2,499.9 million is 152.96% of total debt and 3.48x the notes due through 2032; the first note maturity is USD 117.58 million in 2029, then USD 600.0 million in 2032, USD 500.0 million in 2034 and USD 339.76 million in 2042, and the USD 2.00 billion revolver is undrawn to January 2031. The notes average 8.4 years to maturity and the January 2026 issue of USD 500.0 million at 6.50% shows market access, but no rating is supplied, so the cost of the next refinancing is not established.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a solid view is cash generation, not leverage: at 2025 prices adjusted free cash flow was USD 12.1 million, 2026 capital spending is running 16.98% above 2025 even at higher prices, and the 2026 EBITDA recovery rests on prices that the issuer calls volatile and exposed to a regional conflict. If WTI averaged USD 66.70 again, the illustration shows a USD 405.48 million annual gap and net leverage of 1.29x after one year, with USD 494.1 million of capitalised exploratory costs exposed to impairment, so the credit would drift weaker without any refinancing event. The view would weaken if adjusted free cash flow stays negative for another quarter while dividends rise or repurchases restart, or if capital spending guidance moves above USD 1,600 million; it would strengthen if capital spending came in below the guided range of USD 1,500 million to USD 1,600 million, if adjusted free cash flow stayed positive after dividends and minority distributions at lower prices, or if the notes proved to be guaranteed alongside the revolver.
Basis: calculation C4; calculation C7; calculation C8; calculation C9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_mur-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 51–52

### Company description

Murphy is an independent oil and gas producer whose cash flow comes mainly from oil sold at index prices from the Gulf of America, the Eagle Ford Shale and Canadian offshore fields, with Canadian onshore gas as the second source and an exploration programme in Vietnam, Côte d'Ivoire, Morocco and new countries. Oil is 86.07% of nine-month 2026 sales revenue and offshore fields 58.97%, so visibility depends on the oil price and a few hubs; reserves equal to 10.60 years of production give the longer visibility that debt service needs.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C11; 10-K_2025-12-31_mur-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 7–9

### Ownership and group structure

Murphy is a listed company with no sponsor or controlling holder named in the supplied documents. Its operating subsidiaries are wholly owned except for a 20% minority in the Gulf of America venture, whose share of nine-month net income is 13.54% and which drew more than it earned in 2024 and 2025. The revolver is guaranteed by two subsidiaries while the notes are issued by the parent, so their structural ranking is not established, and financial policy leans to shareholders, with a sponsor and governance risk level of Medium.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C11; 10-K_2025-12-31_mur-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 84–85

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is price-taking. Sales are largely index-priced, no oil is hedged and Canadian fixed-price forward sales cover only part of the gas, so the rise in WTI from USD 64.93 to USD 85.79 in the third quarter, while production fell 7%, explains the revenue gain. Two customers took 31% of 2025 sales, but the issuer sees no difficulty selling at market prices because many purchasers exist, so buyer concentration is a lesser risk than price.
Basis: calculation C11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_mur-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 38, 53; 10-K_2025-12-31_mur-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 140–141

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margin follows price, and this year's cost gains came from lower workover and production-related costs. EBITDA margin was 55.77% over the last twelve months against 44.30% in FY2025, lease operating expense was USD 8.20 per barrel of oil equivalent in the third quarter against USD 8.70 and USD 8.83 in the first two, and nine-month lease operating expenses fell 27.87% on lower workover and production-related costs in the Gulf of America, while depreciation, depletion and amortization rose 6.82%. Margins can be defended against modest volume loss, but not against a price fall to the 2025 level, where EBITDA was USD 1,191.7 million.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_mur-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 35, 37; EX-99.1_2026-05-06_mur-2026q1xex991.htm.jsonl, page 1; EX-99.1_2026-08-05_mur-2026q2xex991.htm.jsonl, page 1; EX-99.1_2026-11-04_mur-2026q3xex991.htm.jsonl, page 1

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital intensity is the main constraint on cash conversion. Property additions are 48.92% of revenue, full-year capital spending is guided at USD 1,500 million to USD 1,600 million excluding the minority, and the Vietnam lease and the drillship add fixed calls, so EBITDA converts to only 0.14x of free cash flow. The issuer states that planned capital expenditure may be adjusted during the year, which is a lever, but it is exercised against committed drilling and a capitalised exploratory balance of USD 494.1 million.
Basis: calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_mur-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 32, 52

### Industry forces

Competitive rivalry is limited because Murphy is a price taker in a commodity market; the force that matters is price, set by supply decisions that the issuer says it cannot predict. New entrants are not a credit factor given the capital needed. Substitutes and climate policy act through long-run demand, which the annual report lists as a risk, and are not a primary driver within 24 months. Buyer power is small because sales are index-priced and many purchasers exist, though two customers took 31% of 2025 sales. Supplier power shows in drilling rigs, vessels and operators: the drillship agreement and the five-year operating contract on the Pioneer vessel commit Murphy to those suppliers' rates and capacity for the contract terms.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_mur-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 32, 51; 10-K_2025-12-31_mur-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 140–141; calculation C11

### Political and economic factors

Political and economic factors are material, and social and technological factors are not a primary credit driver on the documents supplied. The regional conflict involving Iran affects prices and equipment costs through the Strait of Hormuz, new tariffs of 10% or 12.5% on imports from 60 economies add cost uncertainty, and offshore regulation in the Gulf of America covers well design, well control and subsea containment; the issuer has had no direct disruption, but lower prices or higher costs could cause material impairment charges, which links the political risk to the capitalised exploratory balance.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_mur-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 51; 10-K_2025-12-31_mur-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 30–31

### Opportunities and threats

Opportunities that could improve credit quality are the ramp-up of Lac Da Vang and Chinook #8, the Cello and Banjo discoveries expected to produce in the fourth quarter of 2027 and the Bubale appraisal programme, all of which add volume that existing cash flow can fund if prices hold. Threats that could weaken it are a fall in WTI toward USD 66.70, a storm in the Gulf of America, dry holes on new exploration positions and the restart of repurchases while adjusted free cash flow is negative. Strengths and weaknesses are set out above.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_mur-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 34, 52; calculation C9; EX-99.1_2026-11-04_mur-2026q3xex991.htm.jsonl, page 2

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 months to 24 months the tailwinds are forward prices of USD 96.46 for the rest of 2026 and USD 86.12 for 2027 and raised production guidance, and the headwinds are that spending steps up, with the drillship starting in the first quarter of 2027, a decision on Hai Su Vang expected in late 2027 and exploration expense guided at USD 195 million. Over three to five years the 2029 notes of USD 117.58 million are the first maturity, the revolver matures in January 2031 and the Vietnam lease runs to 2039, so the funding profile is long while the sector headwind is a price that the issuer cannot control.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_mur-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 32, 38, 50, 52; EX-99.1_2026-11-04_mur-2026q3xex991.htm.jsonl, page 3; calculation C6

### Downside scenario

The scenario is a sustained oil price at the 2025 level of about USD 66.70, against USD 85.79 in the third quarter of 2026, which is 22.25% lower. The first operational break is revenue, because no oil is hedged and offshore fields supply 58.97% of sales; EBITDA then moves toward its FY2025 level of USD 1,191.7 million while capital spending stays near the last-twelve-month level and the dividend near USD 200.74 million a year. On that arithmetic, which is an illustration and not a forecast, the cash gap is USD 405.48 million a year before working capital and minority distributions, net leverage reaches 1.29x after one year and liquidity of USD 2,499.9 million covers the gap for 6.17 years, so no maturity falls inside the cover. Management can pause repurchases, cut the dividend, defer exploration and adjust capital spending, which makes liquidity distress unlikely before 2029; the observable triggers are negative adjusted free cash flow for a second quarter, WTI below USD 66.70 and any revolver drawing.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C8; calculation C9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_mur-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 38, 50, 52; 10-K_2025-12-31_mur-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 64

### Overall credit view

Overall, Murphy presents an adequate business profile supported by long reserve life, two oil basins plus Canadian gas and a low debt burden, but credit quality is constrained by an unhedged oil price, concentration in offshore hubs and thin free cash flow after a rising capital programme. Cash-flow durability appears adequate at current prices and weaker at 2025 prices, because adjusted free cash flow was USD 12.1 million in 2025. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on market access for new notes before the 2029 maturity and on whether the notes rank equally with the revolver. The primary downside path is a sustained price fall into a larger capital programme and fixed distributions. Further analysis would require a credit rating, the executed indentures and credit agreement, the proxy statement and unit cost data by field.
Basis: calculation C7; calculation C8; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)
