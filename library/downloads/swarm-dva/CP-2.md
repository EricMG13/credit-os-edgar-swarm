## Findings

### Bottom line

DaVita’s fundamental credit is adequate but thinning: steady Medicare-anchored cash flow supports debt of 3.82x last-twelve-months EBITDA, yet buybacks run ahead of free cash flow and 76.95% of principal falls due in 2030 and 2031.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B06

Net of cash, leverage is 3.64x, or 3.68x pro forma for repurchases through 2026-10-27. Profit rests on a small commercial patient group, volumes are flat and unit costs are rising, so the primary downside is a commercial-mix and cost squeeze that pushes leverage toward the 4.00x level where covenant limits on buybacks return.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 183

### Key findings

The business is strong in scale and regulated revenue but narrow in profit. DaVita serves about 36% of U.S. dialysis patients, Medicare and Medicare Advantage pay 57% of U.S. dialysis patient service revenue, and no commercial payor exceeds 10% of revenue, but non-hospital commercial payors are 26% of revenue and 11% of patients and generate nearly all profits. Revenue is therefore durable in total and fragile in the part that earns the profit.

### Analytical appendix — complete canonical registers

### Credit-relevant description

DaVita is the largest U.S. dialysis provider by patients served, with about 36% of the market, and earns about 86% of revenue from U.S. dialysis through 2,657 outpatient centers at the end of 2025. The rest is integrated kidney care, other ancillary services and international dialysis, which together are about 14% of revenue and grew 27.3% in 2025, mainly abroad. Patients typically need three treatments a week for as long as they live or until a transplant, Medicare has covered dialysis since 1972 under a single bundled rate, and 89% of U.S. patients are on a government program, so volume and cash flow are visible even though price is not set by DaVita.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 116; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 117

That visibility supports debt service, but profit is less diversified than revenue: the company says commercial payors generate nearly all profits and that Medicare rates are, on average, significantly lower, so cash-flow durability rests on keeping commercial patients rather than on the number of patients served. Refinancing confidence therefore depends on commercial mix as much as on scale.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 118

### Ownership and group structure

DaVita is a widely held public company with no controlling sponsor. Berkshire Hathaway holds a stake that the filings do not size; a repurchase agreement requires DaVita to buy shares from Berkshire to hold it at 45.0% and a standstill limits its influence over control. Borrowers and guarantors are DaVita Inc. and its domestic subsidiaries, with the secured facilities ranking senior to the senior notes, and joint venture partners hold minority stakes in centers that supply about 30% of U.S. dialysis revenue.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 183; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 200; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 202

The financial-policy consequence is that creditors face a company-managed shareholder-return policy, not a sponsor: leverage tolerance is stated as a 3.0x to 3.5x target on the issuer’s own definition, buybacks have exceeded free cash flow, and no dividend, sponsor fee or creditor-adverse transaction is documented. The governance risk level is Medium on that evidence.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 108; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 114

### Revenue durability and pricing power

Revenue is predictable, regulated and flat in volume. Treatments fell 1.1% in 2025 and rose 0.5% in nine months 2026 while revenue per treatment rose 4.7% in 2025 and 2.7% in nine months 2026, with the Medicare base rate rising 2.2% for 2026 and an estimated 2.7% for 2027. Price is therefore the growth driver and is set by regulators for 57% of revenue and negotiated with commercial payors for the profit-bearing part.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 120; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 121; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37

The risk mechanism is a mix shift, not a volume collapse: payors have tried plan designs that limit commercial coverage after the Supreme Court’s Marietta decision, enhanced premium tax credits expired at the end of 2025 and reduced exchange enrollment and commercial mix, and each commercial patient who moves to Medicare is paid a lower rate. Substitution by transplantation, home therapy or drugs such as GLP-1 receptor agonists is a slower risk to volume. The implication is that revenue is durable in total and exposed in its most profitable part.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 61; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 62; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins are being squeezed. Clinical labor, supplies and fixed center costs make up patient care cost, which rose 5.9% per treatment in 2025 against revenue per treatment up 4.7%, lifting clinic cost from 65.96% to 66.74% of revenue per treatment, and the nine-month 2026 share is 67.09%. EBITDA margin fell from 21.96% to 19.90% on the house measure, and international operations earn a 6.61% operating margin against 17.67% for U.S. dialysis.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B03

Margins can be defended only partly under volume pressure or wage inflation: the company reports clinical wage rates up about 3.8% in each of 2025 and 2024, productivity gains from lower turnover and cost initiatives, and a union organizing push in California, while Medicare rate updates are below cost growth. The implication is a slow decline in EBITDA margin unless commercial mix and productivity offset cost inflation.

### Porter's five forces, credit-translated

Rivalry is moderate and mostly for acquisitions, medical directors and clinical labor: the largest competitor, Fresenius Medical Care, also manufactures supplies, and DaVita competes with large and mid-size providers, private equity-backed kidney care providers and nephrologist-owned units. The credit effect is limited pressure on rates and some pressure on acquisition prices and wages.
