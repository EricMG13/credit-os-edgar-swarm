## Findings

### Bottom line

Snap's creditors depend on founder discretion rather than contract: the two co-founders hold over 99% of the votes, the notes carry no stated limit on debt, liens, buybacks or dividends, and conduct so far has been creditor-neutral to supportive.

The founders own 19.59% of the shares, so the votes and the economics are far apart, and buybacks of USD 600,964 thousand in nine months 2026 exceeded free cash flow of USD 570,998 thousand. Advertising fell to 80.38% of nine-month revenue from 87.82% as other revenue grew 85.31%, but the filings do not show how much of that growth recurs. Litigation losses cannot be estimated and insurers dispute coverage.

### Key findings

Control sits with two people and does not depend on how much equity they own. Mr Spiegel and Mr Murphy hold 53.13% and 46.40% of the votes through ten-vote Class C shares, 99.54% together, while the non-voting Class A shares that make up most of the equity carry no say. Their combined economic interest was 19.59% at the end of 2025, down from 20.68% a year earlier as their Class A holdings fell with no loss of votes, and any future Class A stock dividend would prolong that control. Any merger or sale of the company needs only their consent, and the notes' only reaction to one is a put on a change of control, at 101% of principal for the senior notes and 100% for the convertible notes.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_snap.htm.jsonl, pages 39–40; 10-K_2025-12-31_snap.htm.jsonl, page 247; 10-K_2024-12-31_snap.htm.jsonl, page 202; 10-Q_2026-09-30_snap.htm.jsonl, pages 77–78; 10-Q_2026-09-30_snap.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_snap.htm.jsonl, page 22; calculation C04; calculation C05; calculation C06

The board is independent in form but is not a counterweight to the founders. Ten of twelve directors at the end of 2025 were non-employee directors the board judged independent, the chair is independent, Snap has not used the controlled-company exemption, and the audit, compensation and nominating committees are each made up of independent directors. Yet the 2026 directors were elected by written consent of the Class C holders, so no outside vote can remove them, and the thirteenth name elected that day does not appear in the annual report's director list and is not stated to be independent.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_snap.htm.jsonl, pages 209–213; 10-K_2025-12-31_snap.htm.jsonl, page 256; 10-K_2025-12-31_snap.htm.jsonl, page 40; 10-Q_2026-06-30_snap.htm.jsonl, page 160; calculation C14

The notes give creditors almost no contractual protection against a change in financial policy; only the revolving facility does. The annual report and each later quarterly report say the indentures do not restrict additional debt, secured debt, buybacks, investments, dividends or debt recapitalisations, while the revolving facility limits new debt, liens, intercompany transactions, dividends and buybacks and, since February 2025, requires at least USD 800.0 million of unrestricted cash and cash equivalents instead of USD 300.0 million. The size of each basket is not established because the executed indentures and credit agreement are not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_snap.htm.jsonl, page 114; 10-Q_2026-09-30_snap.htm.jsonl, page 156; 10-Q_2026-09-30_snap.htm.jsonl, page 143; 10-Q_2026-09-30_snap.htm.jsonl, page 26; Source readiness (CP-0)

Behaviour to date is creditor-neutral to supportive, with one watch point. Snap has never paid a cash dividend, ran three USD 500.0 million buyback programmes without net new borrowing, and in 2025 replaced USD 2,232.2 million of convertible notes due 2026 to 2028, bought back USD 227.8 million below carrying value, with USD 2,050.0 million of unsecured senior notes that rank equally with the convertible notes that remain, giving no one security or priority. The watch point is pace: nine-month 2026 buybacks exceeded free cash flow by 5.25%, USD 150.0 million of the latest programme remains available, and contractual interest on the notes rose 46.00% to USD 109,203 thousand in the same nine months.

Related-party leakage is small and approved by independent committees, but it is concentrated in the founders' circle. Snap paid USD 52.3 million to a law firm where the chief executive's father is a partner and USD 5.5 million to one where his stepmother is a partner in 2025, together 0.97% of FY2025 revenue; it leases aircraft from entities the founders control for USD 0 while paying their running costs, and pledged USD 3.0 million to a foundation the founders set up. The annual report states that no 2025 related-person transaction fell outside the approval policy.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_snap.htm.jsonl, pages 251–256; calculation C10

The business is shifting its mix and margin in the right direction, but advertising still funds most of it. Advertising was 80.38% of nine-month 2026 revenue against 87.82% a year earlier and grew only 5.33%; other revenue rose 85.31% and supplied 68.95% of the revenue increase, with the issuer attributing it to subscriptions. Gross margin rose to 59.13% from 53.33% as content and developer partner costs fell to 7.01% of revenue from 11.09%. The other-revenue line also holds a fixed-fee partner contract with USD 387.9 million of unsatisfied obligations at the end of 2025 running only into the first quarter of 2027, and the filings do not split subscriptions from that contract.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_snap.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_snap.htm.jsonl, pages 36–37; 10-Q_2026-09-30_snap.htm.jsonl, pages 45–46; 10-K_2025-12-31_snap.htm.jsonl, pages 171–172; calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C11; calculation C13

North America is the weak spot in a platform that depends on three outside providers. Daily active users grew 4% in the third quarter of 2026, but the growth rate printed for North America was a decline of 6% and for Europe a decline of 1%, against 9% growth in Rest of World, while North America still produced 55.84% of nine-month revenue. Snap runs most of its computing on Google Cloud and AWS, relies on Google, Apple and Amazon for operating systems and services, and carries USD 2.6 billion of non-cancelable commitments largely due within three years; from the first quarter of 2027 it will stop reporting regional daily users and regional revenue per user, which removes the clearest view of that weakness.

Litigation and regulation are the one claim on cash that the filings cannot size. Thousands of plaintiffs allege that the platform harms minors, state attorneys general trials start in August 2027, the European Commission opened a Digital Services Act proceeding on 26 March 2026, and insurers contest coverage with a hearing set for 10 December 2026; Snap says no loss can be estimated; the first four bellwether trials were settled confidentially, a school-district case also settled, and settlements to date had no material effect. Snap's own risk factor cites a competitor settlement of over USD 12 billion and states that its resources are substantially more limited.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_snap.htm.jsonl, pages 27–30; 10-Q_2026-09-30_snap.htm.jsonl, pages 131–132

Management continuity is intact at the top but unexplained in finance. The Chief Financial Officer named in the quarterly report signed on 6 May 2026 was Mr Andersen, the reports signed on 3 August and 4 November 2026 name Mr Hott, and the sources give no reason; a general counsel joined in November 2025 and the April 2026 plan to cut about 16% of staff cost USD 128,505 thousand in nine months, inside the issuer's estimated range of USD 95 million to USD 130 million. The founders' contractual salary is USD 1, and their employment terms renew automatically for five years from 1 January 2027 unless ended.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious reading is the record. No cash dividend has ever been declared, buybacks have come from cash rather than new debt, the revolving facility's liquidity covenant was raised to USD 800.0 million in 2025, the 2025 refinancing was unsecured and ranks equally with the notes it replaced, internal control over financial reporting was effective at the end of 2025, and cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities of USD 2.8 billion are large against the USD 150.0 million left on the buyback programme. Revenue grew 15.07% in nine months 2026, and the founders' disclosed holdings are Snap shares, which a credit failure would impair.

The view would turn more negative on a debt-funded buyback or distribution, any secured or priming debt, a non-pro-rata exchange, repurchases that keep exceeding free cash flow while cash and cash equivalents of USD 1,019,215 thousand move toward the facility's USD 800.0 million floor, or a litigation outcome that is large against cash and uninsured. It would turn more positive if the executed documents show tight baskets on restricted payments and liens, if subscription revenue is shown with subscriber counts and holds after the partner contract ends, and if the insurance dispute is resolved in Snap's favour.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_snap.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-Q_2026-09-30_snap.htm.jsonl, pages 49–51; 10-Q_2026-09-30_snap.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_snap.htm.jsonl, page 200; calculation C02; calculation C07; calculation C16

### Limitations

- The executed indentures, the credit agreement and the list of subsidiaries are not supplied, so the finding that contract protection is thin rests on the issuer's own risk-factor wording and could be too negative if the documents hold baskets or definitions that wording omits.
- Ownership is dated 31 December 2025 and the Tencent line is the issuer's estimate from a 2021 report by Tencent, so the founders' economic interest of 19.59% may be overstated after their 2026 trading plans, which would weaken the alignment argument but not the control finding.
- The reason for the change in Chief Financial Officer and the role of an executive hired under a September 2026 agreement are not stated, so the continuity finding cannot tell planned succession from stress.
- Subscription and partner revenue are not reported separately and no subscriber count is stated, so the diversification finding cannot say how much of the 85.31% growth in other revenue recurs once the partner contract ends.
- Regional user and revenue-per-user values appear only as charts whose growth-rate text was extracted, with the two developed-region series told apart by heading order and checked against one overlapping quarter, so the North America weakness rests on growth rates, not on user counts.
- No accrual, loss range or insurance recovery is stated for the platform and regulatory cases, so the finding that litigation is the unsized claim cannot be turned into a cash-at-risk figure.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The sources describe no sponsor buyout, pending merger or change of control; Snap's transactions are refinancings and buybacks that it funds itself. In February 2025 it sold USD 1.50 billion of unsecured senior notes due 2033 and in August 2025 USD 550.0 million due 2034, receiving net proceeds of USD 1.47 billion and USD 541.1 million, and used them for privately negotiated repurchases of convertible notes due 2026, 2027 and 2028: USD 1,642.7 million of principal in February and USD 589.5 million in August, at USD 190.1 million and USD 37.7 million below carrying value, which produced gains of USD 66.9 million and USD 29.8 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_snap.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_snap.htm.jsonl, page 23; calculation C08

The exchanges moved much of the 2026 to 2028 maturities out to 2033 and 2034 but raised the cost of the debt: contractual interest on the notes was USD 109,203 thousand in nine months 2026 against USD 74,797 thousand a year earlier, and the issuer's schedule shows USD 145.3 million of interest due within twelve months. The USD 1.05 billion revolving facility was amended in February 2025 to extend USD 800.0 million to 2030 and to raise the minimum-liquidity covenant to USD 800.0 million; USD 250.0 million of it still expires in May 2027, and the convertible notes due 2026 were repaid at maturity in August 2026. In 2026 Snap bought businesses for USD 88.1 million, of which USD 68.3 million was cash, paid partly in Class A shares, and on 4 November 2026 its equity-plan share reserve was enlarged, adding about 2.61% to the share count if fully issued.
