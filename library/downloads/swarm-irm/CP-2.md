## Findings

### Bottom line

Iron Mountain's fundamental credit is adequate but leverage-dependent: a contracted, diversified records base and a growing data center book support cash flow, yet net debt of 6.87x reported EBITDA, thin interest cover and negative free cash flow make credit quality depend on EBITDA outgrowing debt. Net debt over reported EBITDA fell from 7.88x at the end of 2025 to 6.87x at 30 September 2026 because earnings grew while debt rose; the dividend declared on 5 November 2026 is not reflected, and its pro forma effect on net debt is not established.
Basis: calculation CAL-1; calculation CAL-2; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_irm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 24

The strongest support is the records franchise, which is contracted, diversified and still produces most revenue, while data centers now supply about half of the growth in segment EBITDA. The primary downside is a slower data center build-out: spending is committed ahead of rent, so a stall would hold leverage near its current level while USD 1,232.90 million of debt due within a year and the 2027 maturities come due.
Basis: calculation CAL-41; calculation CAL-42; calculation CAL-14; Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Key findings

The business profile is stronger than the financial profile, and the gap between them is the credit. Global RIM Business produced 71.71% of nine-month revenue, storage rental was 56.73% of revenue, no customer exceeds about 3% of revenue and storage contracts run one to five years, so the cash base is recurring and diversified. Against that, net debt of 6.87x reported EBITDA and cash interest cover of 2.83x mean that the franchise's stability is already largely spent servicing debt: cash interest took 31.68% of last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against the cautious reading is that the shortfall is discretionary and the business is outgrowing its debt: growth investment is 93.07% of capital spending, operating cash flow covers maintenance and the dividend with USD 295.34 million to spare, covenant leverage of 4.8x sits 2.2x inside its 7.0x maximum and fixed charge coverage of 2.5x sits 1.0x above its 1.5x minimum, the debt terms carry no rating trigger, and the company has extended maturities at par in both bond markets. That reading would win if growth spending slowed while Adjusted EBITDA kept rising and the 2027 maturities were refinanced without a rise in cost.

### Limitations

- No credit rating, agency report or peer set was supplied, so the adequate reading is a judgement on this issuer's own figures and not a ranking against comparable borrowers.
- Reported EBITDA is derived by Financial foundation while the company's guidance and several margin figures use its own Adjusted EBITDA, so every leverage conclusion carries two measures that differ by a gap that is not bridged line by line.
- The debt maturity schedule is printed for year-end 2025 only, so the refinancing conclusion rests on a ladder that does not reflect the 2026 refinancings and cannot show the 2027 and 2028 amounts at the latest date.
- Guidance covers 2026 only and no forecast beyond it is supplied, so the medium-term outlook and the downside arithmetic are sensitivities on current figures and not projections.

### Company description

Iron Mountain is a global information management group whose cash base is physical records storage and whose growth comes from data centers, digital solutions and asset lifecycle management. Global RIM Business produced 71.71% of nine-month 2026 revenue, Global Data Center Business 13.28% and Corporate and Other, which holds asset lifecycle management, 15.01%; storage rental was 56.73% of revenue and the United States 66.27% of FY2025 revenue, with other countries outside the United Kingdom and Canada 22.51%. The recurring, contracted rental base gives cash flow visibility and debt service capacity, while the service and asset lifecycle management lines add revenue that moves with activity and component prices.
Basis: calculation CAL-42; calculation CAL-43; calculation CAL-58; calculation CAL-44; calculation CAL-56; calculation CAL-57; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Credit-relevant history is acquisitive but modest: the Regency Technologies purchase in January 2024 carries an earn-out of up to USD 200.0 million payable in 2027 if earned, and Project Matterhorn, a transformation programme of about USD 574.4 million, ended in 2025 so restructuring cost no longer weighs on 2026 cash flow. The earn-out is a contingent call on liquidity in the same year as the USD 1,000.0 million of notes due in September 2027.

### Ownership and group structure

Iron Mountain is a listed holding company with no sponsor, parent or controlling holder named; the charter caps any holder at 9.8% and the board sets dividends in its sole discretion, so creditors depend on governance and covenants rather than on a sponsor. Sponsor and governance risk is medium in Business & governance: reporting is transparent and covenants have headroom, but the payout is growing, project debt is ring-fenced and the proxy statement that would confirm holders is not among the supplied documents. The indentures treat a leveraged recapitalization as outside the change-of-control repurchase right, so the change-of-control put gives noteholders no protection against a debt-funded recapitalization.

### Competitive forces

Competitive pressure is moderate in records and higher in data centers and asset lifecycle management. In records most competitors operate in one market or region, so Iron Mountain's global scale and chain-of-custody record are a barrier that supports pricing; in data centers it competes with many developers on power availability, security, location, connectivity and rent, and in asset lifecycle management it competes with hyperscalers and corporate clients that process their own assets. ### Downside scenario

The primary downside path starts with a delay or shortfall in data center leasing or energizing while construction commitments continue, and turns into a leverage and refinancing problem rather than an immediate liquidity one. Revenue growth in the data center segment, now 39.61%, would slow first; because facility costs and the debt service on completed capacity would continue while rent was delayed, the segment margin of 53.60% would fall, and service and asset lifecycle management revenue would add to the pressure if component prices or activity weakened. ### Overall credit view

Overall, Iron Mountain presents an adequate business profile supported by a contracted, diversified records base and long-dated data center leases, but credit quality is constrained by net debt of 6.87x reported EBITDA, a free cash flow shortfall of USD 536.82 million over the last twelve months and claims that rank ahead of parent noteholders. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because maintenance spending and the dividend are self-funded and covenants have headroom, and refinancing capacity is likely to depend on continued EBITDA growth, the revolver and open bond markets, with about 14.08% of year-end 2025 principal due in 2027. The primary downside path is a data center slowdown that leaves debt-funded capacity idle while the payout keeps rising. Further analysis would require the maturity schedule after the 2026 refinancings, tenant concentration, the executed debt documents and a rating, and the dividend declared on 5 November 2026 is not reflected in the leverage figures, its pro forma effect on net debt not being established. The top risk is leverage that stays near its current level because EBITDA growth stalls.
