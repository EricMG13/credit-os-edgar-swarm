## Findings

### Bottom line

Iron Mountain is a listed real estate investment trust with no sponsor or controlling holder named in the filings, so creditor risk comes from management's debt-funded data center build and rising dividend, not from sponsor extraction. Creditors get disclosure and covenants rather than control: the board sets dividends at its sole discretion, and the charter caps any one holder at 9.8% of the capital stock, subject to exceptions.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_irm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 44; 10-K_2025-12-31_irm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 45; 10-K_2025-12-31_irm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 92; Source readiness (CP-0)

The operating base is steadier than the capital plan: storage contracts run one to five years and no customer exceeds about 3% of revenue, while data centers produced 50.44% of the nine-month rise in reportable-segment Adjusted EBITDA on 13.28% of revenue. That growth outruns internal cash: operating cash flow covered 69.81% of capital spending in the nine months to 30 September 2026, before USD 790.00 million of dividends, and net new senior-note proceeds of USD 1,485.00 million covered the USD 1,310.57 million left after dividends.

### Key findings

No sponsor stands behind the credit and none is named above it, so the creditor-adverse behaviours to test are the board's own funding and payout choices, not a sponsor's leakage. The registrant is the top company of its own group, no preferred shares are in issue, and the filings name neither a parent nor a controlling holder; the holder list itself sits in a proxy statement that is not among the supplied documents, so control is not established beyond the charter cap. Creditors have no equity cure or parent support to rely on.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_irm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 53; 10-Q_2026-09-30_irm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-K_2025-12-31_irm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 92; 10-K_2025-12-31_irm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 37; Source readiness (CP-0)

Reporting quality is sound and governance is conventional for a listed company, which supports monitoring but does not bind management. Both the annual statements and internal control carry unqualified audit opinions, each quarterly report is dated the same day as the matching earnings release, eleven directors signed the annual report including the chief executive, and the board oversees enterprise risk through a risk and safety committee. Director independence, the chair's role and director shareholdings are not established.

Dividend policy is the clearest creditor-adverse behaviour, because payouts rise while investment outruns operating cash flow. The quarterly dividend per share rose 10.06% in November 2025 and 14.93% in November 2026; in the nine months to 30 September 2026 the company paid USD 790.00 million of dividends while operating cash flow of USD 1,203.63 million covered 69.81% of capital spending of USD 1,724.21 million, and net senior-note proceeds of USD 1,485.00 million filled the gap. The real estate investment trust payout rule and a public pledge to grow dividends make the payout hard to cut, so it competes with debt reduction.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a medium reading is on the supportive side. Covenant ratios were well inside their limits at 30 September 2026 (net total lease adjusted leverage of 4.8x against a maximum of 7.0x, a headroom of 2.2x, and fixed charge coverage of 2.5x against a minimum of 1.5x, a headroom of 1.0x), the debt terms carry no rating trigger, no shares were repurchased in the latest quarter, maturities were extended through new notes, a refinanced project loan and a longer receivables programme with no waiver or covenant relief described, and the documents describe no priming, exchange offer or non-pro-rata transaction.

Against that, the dividend and the build could be more adverse than stated if the debt documents leave room for larger distributions, because the filings summarise covenants but do not show baskets. The view would turn adverse if the proxy statement showed a holder with board or veto rights, if the board funded a special distribution or buyback with debt while leverage sat near its limits, or if capital spending stayed above operating cash flow after leasing slowed. It would turn favourable if operating cash flow covered capital spending and dividends as the build tapers, or if executed debt documents showed tight restricted-payment limits.

### Limitations

- Holder names, holder percentages and board independence are not in the supplied documents, so the conclusion that no holder controls the company rests on the charter cap and on the absence of any named holder, and stays provisional until a holder list is seen.
- The documents give no tenant-level detail for data centers beyond a statement that five of the largest hyperscalers are customers, so the durability of data center cash flow cannot be tested for tenant concentration.
- The 2026 acquisitions are not named or priced, so the conclusion on acquisition appetite rests on cash paid and goodwill acquired alone.
- The executed debt documents are not supplied, so whether dividend and investment baskets leave room for a larger distribution is not established and the dividend conclusion rests on observed payments rather than on legal capacity.
