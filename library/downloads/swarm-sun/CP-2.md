## Findings

### Bottom line

Sunoco LP is an adequate, moderately leveraged credit: liquidity of USD 3,060 million and a diversified earnings base support it, while leverage that rests on acquired earnings and a controlling owner's discretion limits headroom.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C06

Net debt was USD 12,403 million at September 30, 2026, 3.51x trailing adjusted EBITDA, and USD 13,003 million pro forma for the Offen purchase of approximately USD 600 million that closed on 2026-10-27; cash interest is covered 4.87x. Distributions absorb much of free cash flow, so leverage falls only if earnings hold. The primary downside path is a fall in fuel margins to the 2025 level, which would lift leverage to 4.18x without threatening liquidity.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01; calculation C04; calculation C05

### Key findings

Scale and diversification support the profile, but earnings quality is flattered. Fuel distribution, pipelines, terminals and the refinery all contribute, with fuel distribution about half of segment Adjusted EBITDA, yet trailing reported EBITDA of USD 3,624 million holds net inventory valuation gains of USD 258 million and adjusted EBITDA holds a one-time first-quarter gain of USD 102 million; removing that gain lifts net leverage from 3.51x to 3.61x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C01; calculation C02

Leverage is moderate and falling, but the fall is earnings-driven rather than repayment-driven. Net debt moved by only USD 95 million from year-end 2025 while trailing adjusted EBITDA rose by USD 1,488 million, and free cash flow after distributions was USD 856 million over the trailing year, 6.90% of net debt, so lower leverage depends on holding the acquired earnings.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C02; calculation C03; calculation C05

Liquidity covers the nearest maturities with room to spare. Cash and unused revolver of USD 3,060 million are 1.57x the USD 1,949 million of notes due in 2027 and 2028, the revolver runs to June 2030, and the largest maturity is USD 2,723 million in 2029. The 2027 notes are classified as long-term only because management intends to refinance them, and availability after the Offen purchase is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C06; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sun-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 15

Acquisitions and distributions are the main policy risks. The Offen purchase raises net debt to USD 13,003 million, 3.59x calculated reported EBITDA, before any Offen earnings, and the partnership agreement requires distribution of available cash, so growth leans on external funding; governance risk is medium because a controlling owner holds broad discretion.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C09

The structure ranks the secured revolver ahead of equal-ranking unsecured notes, and note holders depend on subsidiaries outside the guarantee. Senior unsecured notes are 92.22% of principal and the secured revolver 0.84%, while non-guarantor subsidiaries earned 46.77% of nine-month net income and the guarantor group's liabilities exceed its assets before investments in them.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C07

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a stronger credit is that leverage on the issuer's own covenant basis fell from 4.03x at year-end 2025 to 3.5x at September 30, 2026, management expects 2026 Adjusted EBITDA to exceed its raised guidance range of USD 3.5 billion to USD 3.7 billion, and Parkland was funded partly with equity-like units. If the next quarter shows leverage at or below the current level after Offen, with distributions held within coverage, the view would move toward strong.
The strongest evidence for a weaker credit is that the improvement rests on one year of acquired earnings and inventory gains. If fuel margins revert to the 2025 level while Offen is debt funded, leverage reaches 4.39x on the calculated basis, and the view would weaken if distributions continue to grow faster than cash retained for repayment.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C04; 10-K_2025-12-31_sun-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 251; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sun-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 15; EX-99.1_2026-11-03_ex991sunocoandsunococorper.htm.jsonl, page 2

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. The first strength is earnings scale across four segments: nine-month 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of USD 2,889 million came 53.86% from fuel distribution, 18.52% from pipelines, 11.84% from terminals and 15.78% from the refinery, so no single segment decides debt service, which lowers the odds that one shock breaks leverage. The second is liquidity of USD 3,060 million, 23.25% of total debt, with no note maturing in 2026 and a revolver to 2030, which gives time to refinance the 2027 notes. The third is equity-like funding of the largest deal, with Class D units of USD 2,547 million and preferred units of USD 1.47 billion, which limited the debt added and supports the 3.5x covenant leverage.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C06; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sun-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 15, 22

Weaknesses. The first weakness is dependence on acquired and inventory-driven earnings: the trailing figures hold net inventory gains of USD 258 million and a one-time gain of USD 102 million, so the ratio can rise quickly if margins revert. The second is policy risk from the controller: distributions of USD 880 million in nine months, a distribution growth aim of at least 5%, incentive distribution rights taking 28.46% of the third-quarter payout and the Offen purchase after the quarter. The third is structure: note holders depend on subsidiaries that earned 46.77% of net income but do not guarantee, and the rank of the GoZone Bonds and lease-related debt is not stated. The fourth is a lumpy maturity profile with USD 2,723 million due in 2029 and USD 1,949 million in 2027 and 2028.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C01; calculation C06; calculation C07

### Company description

Sunoco LP distributes motor fuel to about 11,000 branded and partner locations, owns about 14,000 miles of pipeline, over 170 terminals and a refinery in British Columbia, and operates in 33 countries and territories. Contract-based dealer and commission-agent supply, fee-based pipelines and terminals and a regulated tariff base give cash-flow visibility, but most revenue is fuel sold at pass-through prices, so earnings follow the margin per gallon, not revenue. The group grew by acquisition (NuStar in 2024, Parkland in 2025, then TanQuid, Delta and Offen), which adds scale and integration risk and raises debt service needs.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_sun-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 22; 10-K_2025-12-31_sun-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 11

### Ownership and group structure

Energy Transfer owns the general partner, 20.8% of the common units and all incentive distribution rights, and appoints the board through SunocoCorp, which holds the Class D units. The general partner may favour its owner, has no cap on cost reimbursements and decides borrowing and distributions, so financial policy is the owner's to set; the independent audit committee and five independent directors out of seven are the checks. Debt is issued at Sunoco LP, Sunoco Finance Corp and NuStar Logistics with reciprocal subsidiary guarantees, and non-guarantor subsidiaries, including Parkland businesses, sit structurally ahead of the notes.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_sun-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 91, 92, 155; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sun-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 46, 47, 48

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is mainly fuel sold at market-linked prices: fuel was 95.54% of third-quarter revenue, so the revenue line says little about credit quality and the margin per gallon, 16.9 cents in nine months 2026 against 10.9 cents a year earlier, matters most. Long-term dealer contracts and branded supply agreements give volume visibility, while commodity price swings, a shift to alternative fuels and supplier dependence limit pricing power. Contractually committed revenue is USD 2,038 million at September 30, 2026, small against fuel revenue.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C08; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sun-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 37; 10-K_2025-12-31_sun-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 11

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Costs are mostly pass-through fuel cost, with operating, lease and administrative costs of USD 1,664 million in nine months 2026 against USD 644 million a year earlier after Parkland. Margin resilience therefore depends on gross profit per gallon and on volume, not on cutting costs, and the first-quarter inventory gain and net inventory valuation gains show how timing effects move reported earnings. The group can defend margins in a volume dip through branded supply contracts, but not against a sharp margin compression.
Basis: calculation C10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sun-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 12; EX-99.1_2026-05-05_ex991sunerq12026.htm.jsonl, page 2

### Capital intensity and FCF conversion

Free cash flow was USD 1,905 million over the trailing year after capital expenditure of USD 738 million, and about USD 1,049 million of distributions leave USD 856 million before acquisitions. Maintenance capital spending of USD 400 million to USD 450 million and growth capital of at least USD 600 million are planned for 2026, so growth spending is the flexible lever if liquidity tightens. Cash interest of USD 726 million takes 20.54% of trailing adjusted EBITDA, and operating cash flow benefited from a USD 316 million working-capital release in nine months 2026.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01; calculation C03; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sun-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 44

### Porter's Five Forces

Rivalry is intense and margins are narrow in wholesale and retail fuel, so a competitor price move quickly compresses the per-gallon margin. New entrants face high asset costs in pipelines and terminals, while retail fuel already faces many competitors across store formats, which caps pricing in that part of the portfolio. Substitutes, mainly alternative fuels and efficiency gains, are a long-dated volume risk the annual report flags. Buyers have contract-bound volumes through dealers, which limits switching, and suppliers, with Chevron, Exxon, Phillips 66 and Valero the largest branded sources, hold bargaining power over supply and trade credit terms.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sun-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 12, 16, 66

### PEST

Regulation and litigation are the material factors. The pipeline tariff index was reset in April 2026 to producer prices minus 0.55% for five years from July 2026, which slows regulated pipeline revenue growth; Sunoco and an affiliate face climate-change lawsuits in several states with no loss estimate; foreign currency exposure produced a loss of USD 94 million in nine months 2026; and variable-rate debt of USD 398 million is exposed to rate moves of about USD 4 million a year per 100 bp. Other political, social and technology factors do not appear to be primary credit drivers on the documents read.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sun-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 30, 48, 50

### SWOT

Strengths are the diversified segment base, the liquidity and maturity runway, and equity-like funding of Parkland. Weaknesses are acquisition-driven earnings, inventory-timing gains, the controller's policy discretion and structural subordination. Opportunities are integration benefits, with management expecting 2026 Adjusted EBITDA above its range, and refinancing the 2027 notes at longer maturity. Threats are margin compression, an Offen purchase that earns little, tariff pressure on pipelines and climate or tax disputes.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.1_2026-11-03_ex991sunocoandsunococorper.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sun-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 30

### Outlook

Over twelve to twenty-four months the supportive items are a full year of Parkland, management guidance above its USD 3.5 billion to USD 3.7 billion range and planned growth spending that can be trimmed; the pressures are the Offen purchase, 2027 and 2028 refinancing of USD 1,949 million and inventory timing that reverses. Over three to five years the pipeline tariff index, fuel demand and the integration of a larger Canadian and Caribbean footprint decide whether leverage settles near the current level. The documents do not state a leverage target, so the direction of deleveraging is not established.
Basis: calculation C02; EX-99.1_2026-11-03_ex991sunocoandsunococorper.htm.jsonl, page 2; EX-99.1_2026-08-04_ex991sunerq22026.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sun-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 30

### Downside scenario

A realistic downside starts with the fuel margin falling from 16.9 cents per gallon in nine months 2026 to the 13.2 cents of FY2025, a drop of 3.7 cents. On trailing volume of 15,432 million gallons, each cent per gallon is worth about USD 154.3 million of EBITDA, so the drop removes USD 571 million, 16.15% of adjusted EBITDA. Trailing adjusted EBITDA falls to USD 2,964 million, net debt to EBITDA rises from 3.51x to 4.18x, or 4.39x if the Offen purchase is added without earnings, and cash interest cover falls to 4.08x. Free cash flow falls to about USD 1,334 million before tax effects, still above distributions of USD 1,049 million, so liquidity of USD 3,060 million covers the 2027 and 2028 notes without distress, but deleveraging stalls and management's levers are growth spending and the pace of distributions.
Basis: calculation C04; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sun-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 37, 44; 10-K_2025-12-31_sun-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 132

### Overall credit view

Overall, Sunoco LP presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, segment diversification and liquidity, but credit quality is constrained by earnings that rely on acquisitions and inventory timing, a controlling owner's policy discretion and structural subordination. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow covers distributions, though not with a wide margin. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on holding leverage near 3.51x through the Offen integration and on market access for the 2027 notes. The primary downside path is margin compression with a debt-funded acquisition, and further analysis would need a rating, the credit agreement and Offen's earnings. Top risk: margin compression while distributions and acquisition funding continue.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C04
