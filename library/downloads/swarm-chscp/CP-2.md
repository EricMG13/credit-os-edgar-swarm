## Findings

### Bottom line

CHS has a strong year-end credit profile, with net debt at 1.14x EBITDA and USD 3,063.6 million of cash and undrawn revolver, but its earnings are cyclical and policy-dependent, and seasonal debt, rising capital spending and higher member payouts can erode that cushion.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B01; calculation B05

The main downside path is a refining-margin and farm-economy downturn that meets peak-season borrowing while payouts and capital spending rise: on FY2025-level EBITDA and the fiscal 2027 plan, net debt would reach about USD 4,604.8 million at the peak, 3.26x. Covenant levels, ratings and debt agreements are not in the filings, so headroom is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B08; calculation B09; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 163

- Year-end credit metrics are strong and improving. Net debt was 1.14x EBITDA against 1.88x a year earlier, EBITDA covered cash interest 11.79x against 9.57x, and total debt fell from USD 2,988.3 million to USD 2,501.7 million as working capital released cash. Creditors are well covered on the 2026 year-end balance sheet.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B01; calculation B02; calculation B03

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious reading is that even the downside case leaves net debt at 3.26x EBITDA at the seasonal peak, committed lines of USD 2,800.0 million exceed the USD 725.5 million of extra borrowing it needs, and member equity of USD 11,644.8 million is 57.90% of assets, so a liquidity break would need a worse outcome than a repeat of FY2025. The view would improve if covenant levels show wide headroom, if period-end debt stays well below the February level, or if Energy earnings prove less dependent on exemptions. It would worsen if the securitization and repurchase facilities are not extended beyond August 25, 2027, if equity distributions from CF Nitrogen fall while capital spending runs at USD 850.8 million, or if payouts exceed the USD 300.0 million intended.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B09; calculation B15

### Limitations

- Debt is known only at five period ends, so the true intra-quarter peak is not established and the USD 4,604.8 million downside peak may understate the borrowing need.

### Strengths and weaknesses

CHS's main strengths are financial, not competitive. Low year-end leverage and high interest cover let it absorb one weak year without refinancing pressure, as net debt of 1.14x EBITDA and cover of 11.79x show. A USD 2,800.0 million undrawn committed revolver and a long-dated unsecured note stack mean no large maturity wall, with USD 450.0 million the largest year. Member equity of 57.90% of assets and board-discretionary redemption of equity certificates give a buffer. Scale, with USD 39.2 billion of revenue across energy, grains and crop inputs, spreads exposure across three commodity chains. Finally, there is no sponsor, so value extraction by an owner is not a documented risk.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 144; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, pages 163–165; calculation B01; calculation B05; calculation B13

Its main weaknesses are earnings volatility and seasonal reliance on short-term funding. Gross margin is thin at 4.56% of revenue and operating margin only 1.51%, so a small price or volume move changes earnings sharply. Half of pre-tax earnings is equity income from joint ventures CHS does not control, led by CF Nitrogen. Energy earnings depend on refining margins, renewable energy credit costs and refinery exemptions that may not recur. Seasonal borrowing lifts net debt to 1.73 times its year-end level, funded partly by receivables facilities that need renewal by August 2027. Capital spending and member payouts are both set to rise while farm demand is weak, and covenant levels are unknown.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 60; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 64; calculation B04; calculation B11

### Company description and group structure

CHS is a farmer-owned cooperative whose three reportable segments run on commodity spreads. Energy refines about 190,000 barrels a day at Laurel and McPherson, Grains is the largest cooperative marketer and processor of grain and oilseeds, and Agronomy sells crop inputs and holds a 7.86% interest in CF Nitrogen. Revenue is price-driven and thin-margin, so the cash that services debt comes from refining spreads, processing margins and joint venture distributions, not from revenue scale. Grains is 55.02% of revenue but 11.29% of pre-tax earnings, while Energy is 28.96% of revenue and 49.13% of earnings.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 67

No sponsor, parent or restricted group sits above the issuer. Control rests with member-elected farmer directors, preferred holders and lenders have no vote, and the debt is issued by the consolidated cooperative; CHS Capital, Cofina Funding and the equity-method joint ventures sit below it. For creditors this removes sponsor extraction risk but leaves the board's discretionary payout policy as the financial-policy lever, and the structural position of unsecured notes against receivables-backed financing is not established because no debt agreement is supplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 91; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 164

### Revenue drivers, cost structure and cash conversion

Revenue is volatile through price and stable through volume. Revenue fell 9.68% in FY2025 and rose 10.63% in FY2026 on commodity prices while volumes moved less, and the company states it is exposed to weather, trade policy and the farm economy. Because gross margin is only 3.21% to 4.56% of revenue, creditors should read revenue as a driver of working capital, not of earnings.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-11-04_a4qfy26earningrelease.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation B11

Costs are largely variable commodity inputs, with fixed refinery and processing costs behind them, so margin depends on spreads. Operating margin was 0.26% in FY2025 and 1.51% in FY2026; marketing, general and administrative expense rose 14.3% in FY2026 on incentive pay and software amortisation. This is operating leverage to spreads: refining margin, crush margin and urea prices explain most year-to-year change, which makes margin resilience weak without an offset from equity income.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 72; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 66

Cash conversion swings with working capital and capital spending. Free cash flow after capital spending and major maintenance was USD 1,052.5 million in FY2026, minus USD 364.2 million in FY2025 and USD 441.4 million in FY2024, and Q4 of FY2026 delivered USD 1,382.8 million of operating cash flow as receivables unwound. Capital spending flexes, since major maintenance fell from USD 271.4 million to USD 68.3 million, but FY2027 plans USD 850.8 million, so conversion in a weak year is the credit test.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B12; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 76; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 146

### Competitive forces and policy environment

Rivalry and buyer power cap pricing. CHS competes with larger, better known companies, its members are not obliged to trade with it, and consolidation among cooperative customers and input manufacturers could remove it from supply chains. Substitution matters mainly to Energy, where lower fuel demand would hit diesel sold to farms. These forces explain why margins are thin and why creditors should not extrapolate FY2026 spreads, but they also spread risk across many customers, none above 10% of receivables.
Basis: 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, pages 28–30; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 153

Policy is a material credit driver here. The EPA's March 2026 renewable volume obligation is the highest ever, renewable credit prices rose 86% and 82%, and the small refinery exemptions that gave about USD 205.0 million of relief in FY2026 depend on petitions CHS may not win again; tax rules for cooperatives and clean fuel credits add further policy sensitivity, and China's 2025 suspension of soybean imports shows trade exposure. These factors can move Energy and Grains earnings by more than the cost of CHS's interest, so they belong in the primary downside path.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 46; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, pages 63–64; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 168

### SWOT in credit terms

Strengths: low year-end leverage, large unsecured term debt with no large maturity wall, USD 2,800.0 million of undrawn committed revolver, member equity buffer and board-controlled payouts. Weaknesses: thin margins, half of pre-tax earnings from joint ventures, policy-dependent Energy earnings, seasonal debt and unknown covenants. Opportunities: the Wisconsin soybean plant and phosphate venture could add earnings from late 2028 or 2029 if approved and funded sensibly, and lower major maintenance in fiscal 2027 helps cash. Threats: a repeat of FY2025 margins, loss of CF Nitrogen distributions, non-renewal of the receivables facilities by August 2027, farm credit losses at CHS Capital, and tax or exemption changes.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.1_2026-11-04_a4qfy26earningrelease.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 76; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, pages 163–165

### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale and market position are strong: revenue of USD 39.2 billion and a leading cooperative position in energy and grains. Competitive advantage is average, resting on member relationships and owned logistics rather than pricing power. Business diversification is average because three chains are diversified but equity income concentrates earnings in one joint venture. Cost and capex flexibility is average, since payouts and growth capital are discretionary while refineries need turnarounds. Margin stability is weak, with gross margin at 3.21% in FY2025 and 4.56% in FY2026. Free cash flow stability is average, from minus USD 364.2 million to USD 1,052.5 million. Ability to refinance is average: long-dated unsecured notes show private market access, but no rating or market data is supplied and the receivables lines renew in 2027. Liquidity is strong at the year end with USD 3,063.6 million of cash and undrawn revolver, subject to unstated letters of credit. Financial policy and governance is average: no sponsor and board control support creditors, but payouts are discretionary and covenants are unseen.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B05; calculation B11; calculation B12

The main support is year-end leverage of 1.27x total debt to EBITDA and cover of 11.79x; the main constraints are earnings concentration and seasonal debt; the trend is better in FY2026 than FY2025 but cyclical; and the key missing datapoints are covenant levels, ratings and debt agreements.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Source readiness (CP-0)

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over twelve to twenty-four months, tailwinds are the company's FY2026 operating cash flow, lower major maintenance of USD 41.8 million, higher crush margins from biofuel policy and CF Nitrogen pricing, while headwinds are weak farm demand, record renewable credit costs, a possible end to exemptions and capital spending of USD 850.8 million. Over three to five years the Wisconsin soybean plant and phosphate venture add capacity from late 2028 or 2029 but raise capital needs first; none of the filings gives a forecast, so the medium-term outlook is qualitative.
Basis: 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, pages 62–64; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 69; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 76; EX-99.1_2026-11-04_a4qfy26earningrelease.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2

### Downside scenario

The downside is a repeat of FY2025 conditions at peak-season borrowing under the fiscal 2027 plan. The initiating condition is Energy margins falling back as crack spreads and exemptions normalise while farmers defer purchases and trade restrictions persist. The first operating break is Energy and Agronomy earnings; operating cash flow falls to the FY2025 level of USD 635.8 million against USD 1,361.3 million of planned capital spending, major maintenance, preferred dividends and member payouts, a shortfall of USD 725.5 million that is funded by borrowing. Net debt would then be about USD 2,963.6 million at year end, 2.10x FY2025-level EBITDA of USD 1,412.3 million, and about USD 4,604.8 million at the seasonal peak, 3.26x. The revolver of USD 2,800.0 million exceeds the extra borrowing, so liquidity would hold, and the observable triggers are covenant tests, the August 2027 receivables renewal and the April 2028 revolver expiry. Management levers are lower payouts and capital spending, which the board has used before.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B08; calculation B09; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 76; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, pages 163–164

### Overall credit view

Overall, CHS presents a strong business profile on scale and a cooperative structure with no sponsor, but credit quality is constrained by thin and policy-dependent margins, half of pre-tax earnings from joint ventures and seasonal debt that is far above the year-end level. Cash-flow durability appears adequate: cover is high in a good year but free cash flow was negative in FY2025. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on renewing the receivables facilities by August 2027 and the revolver by April 2028 and on covenant headroom that the filings do not show. The primary downside path is a margin downturn that meets peak-season borrowing while payouts and capital spending rise. Further analysis would require the credit agreement, covenant levels, ratings and CHS Capital loan data. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: policy-dependent Energy margins meeting peak-season borrowing.
