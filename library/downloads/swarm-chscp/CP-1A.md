## Findings

### Bottom line

CHS is a member-owned cooperative with no sponsor to extract value, so creditor governance risk is moderate and comes from discretionary member payouts, USD 2,264.0 million of preferred stock and 50.4% of pre-tax earnings being equity income.
Basis: 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 91; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 144; calculation A04; Source readiness (CP-0)

Directors are farmer-elected by region, no employee may sit on the board, and preferred holders and lenders have no vote. Cash patronage and equity redemptions paid fell to USD 130.4 million in FY2026 from USD 721.9 million in FY2024, but the company intends USD 300.0 million in fiscal 2027 while capital spending is guided to USD 850.8 million.
Basis: 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, pages 91–92; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 146; EX-99.1_2026-11-04_a4qfy26earningrelease.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 76; calculation A05

### Key findings

- No sponsor controls CHS, so the usual extraction risk is absent, but control sits with member-elected directors and creditors have no vote. Voting rights arise from membership rather than from equity or debt, the bylaws bar employees from the board, and the board has seventeen seats with one vacant, so the discipline on payouts rests on the board's policy and on debt terms the filings do not show.
Basis: 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 91; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 125; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 126
- Member distributions are the financial-policy lever and the board sets them at its discretion. Cash patronage and equity redemptions paid fell from USD 721.9 million in FY2024 to USD 130.4 million in FY2026, which left cash for debt reduction, but the company intends USD 300.0 million in fiscal 2027, USD 150.0 million of patronage cash and up to USD 150.0 million of redemptions, beside USD 168.7 million of preferred dividends a year.
Basis: 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, pages 169–170; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 146; EX-99.1_2026-11-04_a4qfy26earningrelease.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 76; calculation A05
- Half of pre-tax earnings is equity income and one joint venture dominates it. Equity income was USD 569.2 million, 50.4% of FY2026 pre-tax earnings of USD 1,128.7 million, and CF Nitrogen, in which CHS holds a 7.86% interest it cannot control, supplied USD 406.3 million of earnings and USD 447.3 million of cash distributions. The agreement is expected to pay out about USD 35 million a year more than CHS's share of earnings, which runs the carrying value down to zero over the supply agreement's life.
Basis: 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 158; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 159; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 144; calculation A04; calculation A07
- Energy is the swing factor and its earnings depend on policy as well as markets. Energy earned USD 554.6 million before tax against a loss of USD 19.0 million in FY2025, helped by about USD 205.0 million of small refinery exemption relief while renewable energy credit costs reached a record, and the Laurel refinery takes 94% of its crude from Canada.
Basis: 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 67; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 64; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 5
- Grains carries most of the revenue but little of the earnings, at 55.02% of FY2026 revenue and 11.29% of pre-tax earnings, and China's May 2025 suspension of soybean imports from CHS shows its exposure to trade policy. Energy was 28.96% of revenue and 49.13% of earnings, so the revenue mix says little about where creditor cash flow comes from.
Basis: 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 67; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 46; calculation A01; calculation A03
- CHS Capital puts farm credit inside the consolidated balance sheet, with short-term notes receivable of USD 1,346.8 million at the 2026 year end, 74% of the book lent to producers, no significant past-due amounts, an immaterial allowance and USD 1.0 billion of unused credit commitments. A farm downturn would hit this book and the Agronomy volumes together.
Basis: 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 152; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, pages 153–154
- Disclosure is adequate for a snapshot but thin on creditor protections. The audit opinion is unqualified, but the filings give no covenant levels, ratings or debt agreements, no audit committee member is an audit committee financial expert, and a company owned by a director owed USD 10.26 million past its payment dates, now on a repayment plan to June 30, 2027, as that director leaves the board.
Basis: 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 98; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 124; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 163; Source readiness (CP-0)
- Capital needs are rising into a weak farm economy. Capital expenditures are expected at USD 850.8 million in FY2027, including a USD 700 million soybean plant in Wisconsin and a USD 450 million phosphate joint venture, both pending approvals and expected in late 2028 or 2029; how they are funded and CHS's share of the venture are not stated.
Basis: 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 76; EX-99.1_2026-11-04_a4qfy26earningrelease.htm.jsonl, page 2

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The case for lower governance risk is strong. Members own the business and are also its customers and suppliers, total equities of USD 11,644.8 million are 57.90% of assets, and USD 6,256.0 million of patron equity certificates can be redeemed only when the board decides, which cushions creditors in a downturn and was used in FY2026 when payouts were cut. The view would move to higher risk if fiscal 2027 redemptions exceed the USD 150.0 million intended, if CF Nitrogen distributions fall below USD 447.3 million while capital spending rises, or if losses emerge in the CHS Capital book; it would move to lower risk if the debt agreements show distribution limits that bind the board.
Basis: 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 144; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, pages 169–170; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 158; calculation A06

### Limitations

- The number of members and the split of voting power between individual and cooperative members are not stated, so how concentrated control is among members is not established.
- Executed debt agreements are not in the sources, so whether the board can raise distributions without lender consent is not established and the governance view rests on the board's stated policy alone.
- No source states CHS's share of the phosphate venture cost or how the soybean plant and the venture will be funded, so their effect on borrowing is not established.
- The CHS Capital loan book is described only by no significant past-due amounts and an immaterial allowance, so the loss exposure behind that statement is not established.
- Related-party credit to directors is disclosed for one fiscal year, so a longer pattern is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 91; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 154; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 124

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

CHS announced no merger, sponsor transaction or acquisition in the filings for FY2026. It finished its exit from the Producer Ag joint venture on December 31, 2025, taking working capital, mainly inventory and cash, for its equity interest and amounts previously owed, with no effect on the FY2026 income statement. In FY2025 it paid USD 237.2 million for business acquisitions, and the annual report ties goodwill of USD 59.5 million that year to the acquisition of West Central Ag Services.
Basis: 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 159; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 146; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 161; 10-Q_2025-11-30_chscp-20251130.htm.jsonl, page 8

No new note issue appears for FY2026. The USD 80.0 million of notes due in fiscal 2026 were repaid, leaving private placement notes at USD 1,703.0 million. The new term debt was a USD 300.0 million draw on the revolving term facility between November 30, 2025 and February 28, 2026, which reverts to a nonrevolving term loan payable October 29, 2029, and amendments on August 26, 2026 extended the securitization and repurchase facilities to August 25, 2027.
Basis: 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, pages 164–165; 10-Q_2025-11-30_chscp-20251130.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-02-28_chscp-20260228.htm.jsonl, page 13

Two growth projects are announced but not yet approved: a USD 700 million soybean plant in Wisconsin and a USD 450 million phosphate joint venture with OCP North America in Louisiana, both expected in late 2028 or 2029. The company intends USD 300.0 million of fiscal 2027 patronage cash and equity redemptions; none of these is a completed transaction, and no funding plan is stated.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-11-04_a4qfy26earningrelease.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2; 10-K_2026-08-31_chscp-20260831.htm.jsonl, page 76
