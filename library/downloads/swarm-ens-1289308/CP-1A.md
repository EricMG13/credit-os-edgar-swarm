## Findings

### Bottom line

EnerSys is a widely held industrial battery maker with no controlling owner or sponsor named, so the creditor risk is how management uses cash, not extraction by an owner: buybacks, a cash-funded Ultralife purchase and a large cell plant compete with debt reduction.
Basis: 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 53; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 59

Sales are diversified, with no customer above 10% of revenue, and defense and data center growth is offsetting weaker forklift and truck demand, but half-year profit leans on production tax credits worth 28.83% of reported EBITDA and a one-off tariff refund of about USD 29 million. Board, holder and compensation detail is not established because the proxy statement was not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, pages 40–41; EX99.1_2026-10-04_ens_ex991-earningsx2qfy27-htm.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation C1; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

Customer concentration is not a credit risk: no customer accounts for more than 10% of revenue, and sales are spread across many end markets and countries.
Basis: 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 8

The mix is moving toward defense and data center demand and away from forklift batteries: in the six months to 2026-10-04 Precision Power sales rose 22.3% and Network & Infrastructure sales rose 6.2%, while Industrial Mobility sales fell 5.6% on a 5% fall in organic volume.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, pages 28–29; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, pages 40–41; calculation C7

Reported profit growth is flattered by items outside the operating franchise: production tax credits supplied 28.83% of six-month reported EBITDA and a tariff refund added about USD 29 million, and both helped lift gross margin to 31.7% from 28.8%.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 41; EX99.1_2026-10-04_ens_ex991-earningsx2qfy27-htm.htm.jsonl, page 5; EX99.1_2026-10-04_ens_ex991-earningsx2qfy27-htm.htm.jsonl, page 16; calculation C1

Cash use is the governance risk that matters: the company bought back USD 180.0 million of shares in six months, has USD 769,996,837 of authorization left, which is 2.79 times its cash, and has announced about USD 820 million of acquisition and plant spending, so debt reduction is not the only claim on cash flow.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 59; calculation C2; calculation C3

Reporting quality supports creditor monitoring but control detail is missing: the statements are audited with effective internal control, related-party transactions are stated as none and no sponsor is named, yet board, holder and compensation detail sits in a proxy statement that was not supplied, and the chief executive changed after the 2025 annual report without a supplied explanation.
Basis: 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 50; 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 84; 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 153; EX99.1_2025-12-28_ens_ex991-earningsx3qfy26-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1; Source readiness (CP-0)

Creditor treatment looks aligned so far: the sixth amendment raised the revolver by USD 150 million, extended its maturity to 2030-09-30 and repaid the term loans, and no uptier, exchange or waiver is stated, but the legal capacity for distributions is not tested because the credit agreement was not supplied.
Basis: 10-Q_2025-12-28_ens.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 54

Management has acted on cost: a reduction in force of about 11% of non-production staff cost USD 21,651 thousand, and operating expenses fell to 15.9% of net sales from 17.7%.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 41

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against reading cash use as the main risk is the half-year record: the company repaid revolver debt out of operating cash flow while also buying back shares, no owner stands behind a leveraged distribution, and the acquisition is described as funded from cash on hand. The view would change if the Ultralife purchase or the buybacks were funded with new borrowing, if the proxy statement showed a holder with control or veto rights, or if the credits and refunds that lifted gross margin stopped, which would show how much of the profit growth is organic. It would also change if the credit agreement allowed distributions at levels that cash flow could not cover.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 41; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The pending acquisition of Ultralife is a cash tender offer at USD 16.00 per share, an enterprise value of about USD 320 million, announced on 2026-10-27 and expected to close in the first quarter of calendar 2027 if a majority of shares are tendered and regulatory approvals are received. EnerSys expects to fund it with cash on hand, which the company says would reduce its cash balance; no amount is recognised in the statements at 2026-10-04, and the effect on net debt is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, pages 30–31; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 39

No new notes or term loans were issued in the six months to 2026-10-04. The debt issues in the period covered are the 6.625% notes due 2032, issued on 2024-01-11 for USD 300 million, and the sixth amendment to the credit facility in the fiscal second quarter of 2026, which raised the revolver to USD 1.0 billion, extended its maturity to 2030-09-30 and repaid the term loans. The 4.375% notes due 2027, USD 300 million, are the next maturity.
Basis: 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 120; 10-Q_2025-12-28_ens.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 38

The company also amended its receivables purchase agreement on 2025-12-15 to a maximum of USD 250 million plus an uncommitted USD 50 million accordion, maturing 2028-12-15, and plans a lithium-ion cell plant costing about USD 650 million with a DOE grant of about USD 150 million, leaving net investment of about USD 500 million to be funded from operating cash flow.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 39
