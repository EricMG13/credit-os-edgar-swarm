## Findings

### Bottom line
Leverage on EBITDA calculated from the statements has risen from 2.24x total debt at FY2023 to 4.66x at 2026-09-30, because acquisitions and buybacks were funded with debt while earnings fell, so creditors depend on cash conversion recovering and on a revolver extended to 2031.

Net debt was USD 1,248.5 million, 4.17x EBITDA of USD 299.6 million for the last twelve months, and EBITDA covered cash interest paid 4.15x against 7.48x in FY2023. Cash plus stated revolver availability is USD 374.5 million, below the USD 541.0 million of 2028 notes that form the next large maturity, and the issuer's 2.12x senior secured covenant ratio rests on a lender definition the filings do not let anyone reproduce.

### Financial interpretation

Total debt at carrying value rose from USD 1,041.6 million at FY2023 to USD 1,396.0 million at 2026-09-30, while EBITDA calculated from the statements fell from USD 464.4 million to USD 299.6 million over the last twelve months, so total debt to EBITDA moved from 2.24x to 4.66x. Revenue fell from USD 4,450.6 million in FY2023 to USD 3,980.4 million in FY2025 and has been flat since, at USD 3,986.8 million over the last twelve months, while the calculated EBITDA margin dropped from 10.43% to 7.51% as gross margin narrowed and selling and administrative cost rose. The debt was added to buy growth and return capital rather than to fund operations: acquisitions of USD 587.7 million (TopBloc in 2025, Quinnox in 2026) and buybacks of USD 547.8 million exceeded free cash flow of USD 754.7 million by USD 380.8 million between FY2024 and the first nine months of 2026, and net debt rose USD 382.8 million. Creditors therefore hold more claims against a smaller earnings base. The one structural improvement is the revolver, which the July 2026 amendment enlarged and extended, but the drawn amount has grown so that availability of USD 227.0 million is well below the USD 500.0 million of FY2024.
