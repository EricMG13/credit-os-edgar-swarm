## Findings

### Bottom line

SunCoke's credit metrics are repairing but remain weaker than before the 2025 Phoenix Global acquisition: LTM Q3 2026 total debt is 3.71x reported EBITDA against 1.82x in FY2024, and no borrowing matures before 2029.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 91–92; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 113–115; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 17; calculation C03; calculation C04

FY2025 reported leverage of 6.28x overstates the strain because reported EBITDA carries a USD 90.3 million impairment; issuer-adjusted leverage was 3.13x and is 2.31x on an LTM basis. ### Financial interpretation

Leverage moved in two steps. Reported total debt to EBITDA was 1.82x in FY2024, rose to 6.28x in FY2025 and stands at 3.71x on a trailing-twelve-month basis at Q3 2026, with net leverage of 3.35x, or 3.42x pro forma for the USD 10.2 million dividend payable on 2026-12-01. The Phoenix Global purchase was funded with cash and revolver borrowing, taking carrying debt from USD 492.3 million to USD 685.5 million, and the USD 90.3 million Haverhill I impairment pulled FY2025 reported EBITDA down to USD 109.2 million. The trailing window at Q3 2026 still contains the negative Q4 2025 reported EBITDA of USD 39.0 million, so reported leverage will fall when that quarter leaves the window, provided Q4 2026 reported EBITDA is above that negative level.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 91–93; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 79; calculation C01; calculation C03; calculation C04; calculation C05; calculation C09

Coverage has fallen from 11.10x reported in FY2024 to 4.39x on a trailing basis at Q3 2026. Trailing cash interest paid is USD 36.9 million, and issuer-adjusted EBITDA covers it 7.04x. The notes coupon of USD 24.38 million a year is about 66% of trailing cash interest, and the 2025 annual report attributes higher net interest expense to revolver borrowings, so cover should track the revolver balance. The revolver covenants are a maximum leverage ratio of 4.50x and a minimum interest coverage of 2.50x; their definitions were not supplied, so headroom is not established, and the issuer reports compliance at Q3 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 114–115; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 75; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 18; calculation C03; calculation C04; calculation C14

Cash-flow quality is improving but depends on receivables. Operating cash flow was USD 140.3 million in the nine months to Q3 2026 against USD 52.5 million a year earlier, when payments settling liabilities assumed with Phoenix Global weighed on cash. Within 2026, however, operating cash flow was negative USD 27.2 million in Q2 and USD 94.8 million in Q3 because receivables absorbed USD 99.9 million in the nine months. Receivables stand at USD 208.9 million against USD 111.5 million at FY2025, the allowance fell from USD 11.1 million to USD 0.3 million, and the issuer attributes the build to the timing of customer payments without quantifying it. Trailing free cash flow of USD 119.3 million covers dividends of USD 41.2 million and distributions to minority holders of USD 7.4 million, but a failure of the receivables to convert would remove that cushion.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 91–93; calculation C06; calculation C07; calculation C11

Liquidity is lower than before the acquisition, but no maturity is near. Cash of USD 57.6 million and undrawn revolver of USD 218.0 million give USD 275.6 million, down from USD 539.6 million at FY2024 but up from USD 220.7 million at FY2025, and the revolver is USD 107.0 million drawn compared with USD 193.0 million at FY2025. No borrowing matures until the 2029 notes of USD 500.0 million, and the revolver commitment itself runs to 2030. Cash was USD 42.7 million at Q2 2026, the lowest of the interim dates, so day-to-day liquidity depends mainly on the undrawn revolver.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; 10-Q_2026-06-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 80; calculation C08; calculation C10

The earnings improvement is acquisition-led. Nine-month adjusted EBITDA rose USD 40.5 million to USD 203.0 million, but Industrial Services contributed a rise of USD 53.1 million, from seven more months of Phoenix Global, while Domestic Coke fell USD 5.5 million as lower volumes after the Haverhill I shutdown and weaker energy results more than offset better pricing and yields. Cliffs Steel supplied 61.7% of nine-month revenue, so the cash flows behind take-or-pay contracts depend heavily on one counterparty group. One support is contracted volume: coke sales agreements carry 16.2 million tons of unsatisfied obligations and Industrial Services holds USD 505.8 million of fixed-fee and take-or-pay revenue.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 30–32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20; calculation C12; calculation C13
