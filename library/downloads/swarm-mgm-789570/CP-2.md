## Findings

### Bottom line

MGM's fundamental credit profile is adequate and its leverage is falling, but earnings are softening against fixed rent, so creditors are protected mainly by liquidity and by management restraint on buybacks and MGM Osaka funding rather than by earnings growth.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C1; calculation C3

Net leverage fell to 1.47x adjusted EBITDA at 2026-09-30 from 1.72x at year-end; it is 1.39x on reported EBITDA, or 1.45x on reported EBITDA pro forma for the October 2026 MGM Osaka funding of about USD 123 million, and a pro forma figure on adjusted EBITDA is not established. Adjusted EBITDA for the last twelve months is 8.12% lower than in FY2025, partly because Northfield Park was sold and prior-year insurance proceeds ended, and rent-burdened fixed-charge cover slipped to 1.69x from 1.77x. Cash and the undrawn revolver cover the 2027 and 2028 maturities 2.15x, so the primary downside is weaker Las Vegas pricing or Macau hold meeting USD 1.8 billion of annual rent and about USD 2.1 billion of MGM Osaka funding.

### Limitations

- No covenant level, compliance certificate or credit agreement is supplied, so revolver availability under stress and the headroom behind the liquidity conclusion are not tested.
- The like-for-like Q3 comparison uses rounded insurance amounts and an assumed share of Northfield Park's rent, so the size of the underlying earnings decline is a range and the conclusion that earnings are softening holds only in direction.
- The issuer gives no earnings guidance, so the 12-month to 24-month outlook rests on observed trends and one management comment on October trading, and the conclusion that earnings are softening could reverse.
- No rating, market price or peer data is supplied, so the view that the profile is adequate against typical leveraged gaming issuers cannot be benchmarked.
- Fixed-charge cover mixes rent expense with cash interest paid and interim cash flows are year-to-date only, so the direction of cover is reliable but its level is not comparable with a covenant definition.
- Parent-only earnings and cash flow are not separable from MGM China, so parent coverage of the USD 180 million to USD 200 million of expected parent interest is not calculated.
- The latest document is dated 2026-10-28, so any buyback, financing or trading change after that date could alter the view.

### Company description

MGM is a Delaware holding company whose subsidiaries run nine Las Vegas Strip resorts, six regional casinos, two casino resorts in Macau through its 56%-owned MGM China and an online gaming business, and it holds 50% of BetMGM and about 40% of MGM Osaka. Because it leases the real estate of its domestic properties, the cash that supports debt is earned after rent of USD 1.8 billion a year. Revenue is mostly cash-based and not seasonal, which gives steady daily cash generation, but quarterly results move with hotel rates, hold percentages and conventions.

### Ownership and group structure

MGM is a publicly traded company with no controlling holder identified in the supplied reports; IAC Inc. and Barry Diller are parties to a voting agreement whose terms are not supplied. Parent notes are guaranteed by the wholly owned material domestic subsidiaries, while MGM China, which carries 40.89% of principal debt and USD 598 million of cash, three domestic casinos, the BetMGM holder and the LeoVegas holder are not guarantors. Noteholders therefore rank behind subsidiary creditors on non-guarantor assets, and the parent reaches MGM China cash only through dividends that other holders share. Business and governance risk is Medium.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is discretionary, cyclical and sensitive to price and hold. In Las Vegas the average room rate fell 8% in Q3 2026 with occupancy flat at 89%, so pricing power is limited, while casino revenue rose 8% on a table win percentage of 25.3% against 22.6%. In Macau revenue rose 5% although the main floor drop fell 10%, and online revenue grew 20.77% in nine months. The result is that revenue visibility is short, and cash flow depends on rates and hold that the issuer says it does not fully control.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

### Porter's five forces

Competitive rivalry is high and new capacity is arriving: Las Vegas has many casino hotels and competitors have expanded or plan to expand rooms and convention space, and MGM China competes with five other concessionaires, so rate and share pressure feeds directly into EBITDAR. Entry barriers are high because gaming licences and Macau concessions are limited, which protects existing cash flow. Substitutes matter: iGaming and online sports betting can reduce visits and spend at the properties, which is a long-run risk to land-based revenue. Buyer power is moderate: leisure customers are price sensitive, as the 8% fall in the Las Vegas room rate shows, and premium players are offered high-limit rooms and lounges. Supplier power is concentrated in labour, with about 37,000 US employees under union agreements, and in landlords, whose rent is fixed.

### PEST analysis

Regulatory and economic factors are credit drivers here. The Macau concession expires on 2032-12-31 and gaming-law and cross-border travel changes can reduce visitation; a temporary government ban on fixed-odds betting and online casino games stopped operations in Brazil in September 2026; and trade tension and tariffs could cut cross-border travel. Social and technological factors matter mainly through online competition. Yen and Hong Kong dollar exposures arise from MGM Osaka funding and MGM China, and variable-rate borrowings are about 12% of the total, so a 1 percentage point rate rise adds about USD 6.75 million of annual interest.

### Downside scenario

Initiating condition: Las Vegas room rates keep falling, as the 8% decline in Q3 2026 shows, and Macau hold returns toward the 25.0% of the prior-year nine months from 27.3%. First operating break: Las Vegas EBITDAR falls faster than revenue, as it did in Q3 2026 (11.66% against 1.53%), and the 2.30 percentage point hold reversion would remove about USD 145.94 million of Macau segment profit after gaming taxes over nine months. Financial transmission: adjusted EBITDA 10% lower would lift net leverage to 1.63x and cut fixed-charge cover to 1.61x. Liquidity, leverage and refinancing: cash and the undrawn revolver of USD 4,670.957 million still cover the USD 2,175.000 million due in 2027 and 2028 2.15x, but MGM China cash of USD 598 million covers its USD 750 million 2027 notes only 0.80x, so subsidiary refinancing or dividends matter. Management levers: cut the discretionary buyback (USD 1.4 billion authorised), slow MGM Osaka calls, sell Springfield (USD 443 million), and draw the revolver. ### Overall credit view

Overall, MGM presents an adequate business profile supported by liquidity, scale across four segments and an on-schedule refinancing record, but credit quality is constrained by earnings that are softening against fixed rent, off-debt commitments and a Macau structure outside the guarantee group. Further analysis would require covenant levels, the voting agreement terms, a reconciliation of the landlord guarantees, earnings guidance and market data.
