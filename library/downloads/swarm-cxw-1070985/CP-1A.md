## Findings

### Bottom line

Creditors are now lending to a government contractor that has sold four owned facilities to its largest customer and repaid most of its debt, so credit quality rests on federal contract renewals and on how shareholder returns are balanced against that cushion.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 10; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxw_d165428dex991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 3

Federal agencies produced 54% of 2025 revenue and ICE alone 35%, and ICE can end any contract for convenience or lack of appropriation. The four sold facilities remain under contracts that the company expects to modify, and two of them expire in August 2027. A new chief executive took over on 24 September 2026, and the board has twice raised the repurchase authorization, by USD 500.0 million each time.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 54; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxw_d165428dex991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 74

### Key findings

- Federal demand is the credit: federal agencies were 54% of 2025 revenue, up from 51% in 2024, and ICE alone was 35%, so occupancy, contract renewal and appropriations at one customer family drive cash flow. Every ICE contract can be ended for convenience or non-appropriation, which turns a strong 2026 into a revenue stream that depends on continued political demand rather than on contract protection.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 54

- Selling the four facilities to the federal government turned owned collateral into cash but left CoreCivic operating the sites for the buyer: gross proceeds were about USD 2.2 billion, USD 716.3 million of debt was repaid, and two of the four management contracts expire in August 2027. Lower debt improves solvency, while weaker asset backing and a customer that now owns the real estate reduce bargaining power at renewal.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 54; calculation B02; calculation B03

- Capital allocation has moved from debt repayment to shareholder returns within weeks: a USD 500.0 million accelerated repurchase was paid on 10 August 2026, and the board added USD 500.0 million to the authorization on 4 August and again on 3 November, for a total of USD 1,700.0 million. Whether creditors keep the post-sale cushion depends on covenant limits that the supplied documents describe but do not define.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 74; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 75; calculation B04

- Operating momentum is real: occupancy was 84.0% in the third quarter of 2026 against 76.7% a year earlier, four idle facilities were activated since early 2025, and a new five-year contract for the Appleton facility is expected to add about USD 75.0 million of annual revenue when fully activated. This supports earnings but the activation costs and the idle capacity still held are a drag until demand fills them.
  Basis: EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxw_d165428dex991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxw_d165428dex991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 11

- Governance is adequate for monitoring but incomplete for control: audited statements, an internal control opinion and quarterly reconciliations are available, the chief executive changed on 24 September 2026 after health-related resignation, and ownership concentration, board composition and related-party dealings are not established because the proxy statement was not supplied. The sponsor and governance risk level is Medium.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 153; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxw_d165428dex991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-31_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 154

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against concern is the balance sheet: carrying-value debt fell below cash plus undrawn revolver, the only maturity of size is the 8.25% notes due in 2029, and ICE demand is strong, with occupancy up and a new five-year contract awarded in August 2026. The view would improve if the revised contracts for the sold facilities keep current economics, if two of them renew before August 2027, and if sale proceeds keep going to debt reduction rather than repurchases. It would worsen if ICE terminated or re-priced contracts at the sold facilities, if further repurchases or acquisitions were funded with new debt, or if federal appropriations or detention priorities shifted.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 54; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxw_d165428dex991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxw_d165428dex991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 7

### Limitations

- The 2026 proxy statement was not supplied, so ownership concentration, board composition and related-party dealings are not established, which keeps the governance risk level at Medium rather than allowing a firmer view.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 154

- The terms of the revised management contracts for the four sold facilities are not disclosed, so margin and revenue on those facilities after the change in ownership are not established, which limits the confidence of the view on asset backing.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 54

- The executed credit agreement and notes indenture were not supplied, so whether buybacks and further asset sales are inside covenant capacity is not established, which limits how far the creditor-alignment reading of capital allocation can be pushed.
Basis: Source readiness (CP-0)

- Fiscal 2025 is not recast to the new segments, so statements about segment mix rest on the third quarter of 2026 and on the earlier basis for fiscal 2025 separately.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Four detention facilities were sold to the United States through the Department of Homeland Security in two pairs: California City and Otay Mesa on 2 July 2026 and Appleton and Midwest on 4 August 2026, for gross proceeds of about USD 2.2 billion and a gain of USD 1,770.7 million. CoreCivic continues to manage them under ICE contracts expected to be modified, and the company says its discussions with ICE about buying further facilities are preliminary.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 54; calculation B03

The proceeds repaid USD 716.3 million of debt: the revolver, the Incremental Term Loan, the Initial Term Loan and the 4.75% senior notes due 2027, which were redeemed at a make-whole price on 12 August 2026. The only debt left is the 8.25% senior notes due 2029 and the Kansas mortgage note; the only new term debt in 2026 was the USD 100.0 million Incremental Term Loan of 10 April 2026.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 18; calculation B02

A USD 500.0 million accelerated repurchase agreement was paid on 10 August 2026, with final settlement expected before the end of the second quarter of 2027, and CoreCivic acquired Clinical Solutions Pharmacy on 1 April 2026 for an estimated USD 199.6 million including an earn-out.
Basis: EX99.1_2026-06-30_cxw_d118761dex991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 75
