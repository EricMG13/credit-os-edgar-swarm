## Findings

### Bottom line

CoreCivic's credit profile has strengthened sharply on the balance sheet after the 2026 facility sales, but it remains a single-customer business whose cash flow depends on federal detention demand, so the profile is adequate rather than strong.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 54

Net debt fell to USD 75.9 million at 30 September 2026 from USD 1,122.8 million at the end of 2025, liquidity is about USD 1,098.7 million, and the only sizeable maturity is USD 500.0 million of 8.25% notes due in 2029. The primary downside is a federal shift in detention priorities or re-pricing of the sold facilities' contracts, made worse if repurchases use the cash cushion.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation M01; calculation M02; calculation M03; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 52

### Key findings

- The balance sheet is repaired, but by one-off proceeds: gross debt at carrying value fell to USD 623.1 million and net debt to USD 75.9 million at 30 September 2026, against USD 1,122.8 million of net debt at the end of 2025, because four facilities were sold for about USD 2.2 billion. Lower debt cuts interest and refinancing risk, while the repair cannot be repeated without selling more owned assets.
  Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation M02; calculation M03

- Leverage and cover are now comfortable on adjusted earnings: gross debt is 1.45x trailing adjusted EBITDA of USD 429.8 million and that earnings figure covers cash interest 5.10x, against 3.37x reported leverage and 5.03x cover at the end of 2025. The adjusted figure is built partly from a house-adjusted fiscal 2025 number, and the issuer's own net leverage claim below zero cannot be reproduced, so the ratios are estimates rather than settled facts.
  Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation M04; calculation M05

- Cash conversion is the weakest part of the financial profile: free cash flow was USD 54.0 million in fiscal 2025 on adjusted EBITDA of USD 365.6 million and an outflow of USD 397.7 million over the last twelve months because USD 518.7 million of tax on the sale gains was paid. Thin conversion means debt service depends on EBITDA rather than on retained cash flow, so lower occupancy would reach liquidity quickly.
  Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation M06

- Customer concentration is the dominant business risk: federal agencies were 54% of 2025 revenue and ICE alone 35%, and every ICE contract can be ended for convenience or non-appropriation. Revenue visibility is therefore political rather than contractual, and the federal buyer of the four facilities now also owns the assets behind the management contracts.
  Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation M07

- Liquidity and the maturity wall are favourable: cash of USD 547.2 million and USD 551.5 million undrawn on the revolver are about USD 1,098.7 million, while scheduled principal is below USD 10.0 million a year until USD 508.0 million falls due in 2029. This makes a refinancing squeeze unlikely before 2029 unless the cushion is spent.
  Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation M01

- Capital allocation is the main creditor-adverse risk: a USD 500.0 million accelerated repurchase was paid in August 2026, the authorization was raised by USD 500.0 million twice and USD 755.8 million remained available after the November increase. Repurchases compete with repaying the 2029 notes, and the executed covenant documents that limit them were not supplied.
  Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation M08

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against concern is the combination of near-zero net debt, large liquidity and rising federal demand: nationwide ICE detention populations rebounded from an April 2026 trough to a higher level by July 2026, the issuer's occupancy rose to 84.0% against 76.7% a year earlier, and management cites funding for ICE secured through September 2029. The view would improve if the revised contracts for the sold facilities kept current economics, if the 2029 notes were refinanced or repaid early, and if repurchases were held to operating cash flow. It would worsen if ICE populations fell again as in early 2026, if the sold facilities' contracts were re-priced or ended, or if buybacks or acquisitions were funded with new debt.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 52; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxw_d165428dex991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Overall credit view

Overall, CoreCivic presents an adequate business profile supported by long federal relationships, high occupancy and a pharmacy and services line that diversifies revenue, but credit quality is constrained by concentration in one federal customer family and by capital returns that can absorb the cushion created by the facility sales. Cash-flow durability appears adequate: earnings are rising on activations, while free cash flow conversion is thin. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the 8.25% notes due April 2029 and on how much of the USD 1,098.7 million of liquidity is returned to shareholders. The primary downside path is a fall in federal detention demand or a re-pricing of the sold facilities' contracts. Further analysis would require the executed credit agreement and indenture, the proxy statement, and the revised contract terms. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: federal customer concentration.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation M01

### Downside scenario

The downside is issuer-specific. The initiating condition is a repeat of early 2026, when nationwide ICE detention fell by about 10,500 individuals from a January high of about 70,800, or a contract change at the four sold facilities after the change of ownership. The first operational break is occupancy: the Residential segment margin was 22.3% in the third quarter of 2026 against 22.5% a year earlier even in a strong quarter, and margin for nine months fell to 22.8% from 24.0% when populations dipped. Because costs are largely fixed, lower occupancy cuts EBITDA faster than revenue and free cash flow, already thin, turns negative. Liquidity of about USD 1,098.7 million absorbs a long period of weak cash flow, and leverage would rise from 1.45x trailing adjusted EBITDA, with the 4.50x covenant maximum far above that level although its definition was not supplied, so resilience appears sufficient to avoid a liquidity problem before 2029 unless repurchases use the cushion. Management levers are idle-facility activation timing, capex deferral and repurchase restraint. The observable triggers are ICE population, occupancy and any change to the sold facilities' contracts.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 50; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 16; calculation M01; calculation M04

### Strengths and weaknesses

- Strength: balance sheet repair. Net debt of USD 75.9 million against USD 1,122.8 million at the end of 2025 follows the sale of four facilities and repayment of USD 716.3 million of debt; this reduces interest and removes the 2027 maturity, so default risk before 2029 is low unless the cash is distributed.
  Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation M02; calculation M03

- Strength: liquidity. Cash of USD 547.2 million and USD 551.5 million undrawn on the revolver give about USD 1,098.7 million against scheduled principal below USD 10.0 million a year until 2029, so a squeeze is unlikely.
  Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation M01

- Strength: demand and occupancy. Occupancy of 84.0% and the new Appleton contract expected to add about USD 75.0 million of annual revenue support earnings as idle capacity is activated.
  Basis: EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxw_d165428dex991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxw_d165428dex991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 7

- Weakness: customer concentration. Federal agencies were 54% of 2025 revenue and ICE terminates for convenience, so a policy change reaches revenue directly and the buyer of the sold assets is the same counterparty.
  Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation M07

- Weakness: thin cash conversion. Free cash flow was USD 54.0 million in fiscal 2025 and an outflow of USD 397.7 million for the last twelve months, so debt service relies on EBITDA and liquidity rather than retained cash.
  Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation M06

- Weakness: shareholder-friendly capital allocation. USD 500.0 million repurchased in August and USD 755.8 million of authorization left after the November increase can consume the cushion, and the limits were not supplied.
  Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation M08
