## Findings

### Bottom line

Olin is widely held with no controlling shareholder or sponsor in the sources, so creditor risk centres on the pending Huntsman merger, which would put a larger secured bank facility over the combined company before the treatment of existing notes is known.

Sales were almost flat at USD 6,833.0 million in 2023 and USD 6,780.8 million in 2025, but combined segment results fell to USD 145.3 million from USD 888.8 million, and to USD 171.0 million in the first nine months of 2026 from USD 253.6 million. Dividends took 98.0% of nine-month operating cash flow, although buybacks have stopped.

### Key findings

#### Ownership and control

Governance risk comes from board decisions, not from a controlling owner, because Olin is a listed company and the sources name no sponsor, parent or controlling holder. The annual report shows a Chairman separate from the chief executive and nine directors who signed it, but the proxy statement that would list holders of 5% or more, board independence and related-party dealings was not supplied, so dispersed ownership is an inference from the absence of any disclosed controlling holder. No sponsor fees or fund-life pressure arise from this ownership, and the sources supplied disclose no related-party payments.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_oln-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-K_2025-12-31_oln-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 142; 10-K_2025-12-31_oln-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 150

#### The Huntsman merger and its financing

The merger decides creditor outcomes, and its financing runs through secured bank debt. The all-stock merger of equals would leave existing Olin shareholders with 54.5% of the combined company and Huntsman stockholders with 45.5%, both groups approved it on 25 August 2026, and closing is expected in the first quarter of 2027. At closing a new USD 2,500.0 million senior secured facility would replace Olin's term loan of USD 650.0 million and revolver of USD 1,200.0 million, USD 650.0 million more than Olin's own commitments, and would also replace Huntsman's revolver, whose size is not stated. Certain subsidiaries of both companies would guarantee it and it would carry first-priority liens on certain assets. The sources do not say what happens to Olin's existing senior notes or receivables financing, or what Huntsman owes, so the effect on unsecured creditors and the combined leverage are not established.

#### Cost and constraints while the merger is pending

A failed or delayed merger has a price, and the pending deal narrows the board's room to act. Olin could owe a termination fee of USD 121 million if the agreement ends under specified circumstances, it booked USD 24.3 million of advisory and legal costs in the first nine months of 2026, shareholder lawsuits about the merger have been filed, and the agreement bars certain actions without Huntsman's consent before closing. These constraints limit how freely management can change financing or capital returns before closing; their exact scope is not stated.

#### Lenders hold security until the relief period ends

Bank lenders took security and payout limits in return for looser covenants in February 2026. The amendment of 19 February 2026 reduced the minimum interest coverage ratio, raised the maximum net leverage ratio and adjusted the EBITDA and debt definitions for a relief period, and at the same time required guarantees from certain material domestic subsidiaries and liens over substantially all of their and Olin's personal property. During the relief period it also limits new debt, certain asset sales and dividends or distributions above the regular dividend, and Olin prepaid part of the term loan with USD 109.7 million of revolver borrowings. The guarantees and liens are released at the latest on 30 September 2027 or earlier if Olin elects, so the banks hold security until then. The covenant levels are not stated, and the sources do not state the guarantees or security of the senior notes, so the position of noteholders is not established.

#### Shareholder returns are cut but still a call on cash

Distributions have fallen sharply but remain a call on thin cash flow. Dividends and buybacks together fell from USD 812.3 million in 2023 to USD 142.1 million in 2025 and USD 68.3 million in the first nine months of 2026, because buybacks stopped after November 2025 while the USD 0.20 quarterly dividend per share, unchanged for five years, continued. In the nine months operating cash flow of USD 69.7 million did not cover capital spending of USD 106.6 million, free cash flow was negative USD 36.9 million before dividends, and net borrowings of USD 185.3 million covered the gap. Management still expects about USD 200 million of capital spending for 2026, which implies about USD 93.4 million in the fourth quarter. A repurchase authorization of USD 1,948.9 million remains open but unused, and while the relief period lasts the credit agreement limits distributions above the regular dividend, so buybacks are constrained and the regular dividend is allowed.

#### Earnings have fallen much faster than sales

The group earns far less on stable sales: combined segment results fell 83.7% from 2023 to 2025 while sales slipped 0.8%. Chlor alkali and vinyls, 54% of 2025 sales, saw results fall from USD 664.2 million to USD 181.1 million, epoxy lost money in each of the three years, and Winchester's results fell from USD 255.6 million to USD 67.7 million, with commercial ammunition sales down 23.9% in 2025 while military and law enforcement sales rose. In the first nine months of 2026 epoxy turned to a profit of USD 13.7 million and Winchester rose to USD 77.1 million, but chlor alkali and vinyls fell to USD 80.2 million from USD 270.8 million while nine-month caustic soda sales fell 15.6% and chlorine and derivative sales 13.1%.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against this cautious reading is that the merger could strengthen creditors rather than dilute them. It is an all-stock combination with no cash paid to shareholders, both shareholder groups approved it, the US antitrust waiting period has expired, the bank group has committed a USD 2,500.0 million facility, and the agencies are split, with Fitch on positive watch, S&P on developing watch and Moody's holding a negative outlook. The recovery of epoxy and Winchester earnings in 2026 and a backlog of USD 1,331 million also show the diversification working. The view would turn more positive if Huntsman's statements, the merger agreement and the new facility's covenants show lower combined leverage and keep the ranking of existing notes; it would turn negative if closing slips beyond the first quarter of 2027, the USD 121 million termination fee becomes payable, the Lake City labour agreement is not renewed, or chlor alkali results keep falling.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

On 15 June 2026 Olin entered into a definitive all-stock merger agreement with Huntsman Corporation to form OlinHuntsman Corporation, announced the next day. Each Huntsman share converts into a fixed number of Olin shares, which leaves existing Olin shareholders with about 54.5% and Huntsman stockholders with about 45.5% of the combined company. The shareholders of both companies approved on 25 August 2026, the registration statement was declared effective on 13 July 2026, the US antitrust waiting period has expired, closing is expected in the first quarter of 2027 subject to remaining regulatory approvals, and Olin could owe a termination fee of USD 121 million if the agreement ends in specified circumstances. Acquisition-related costs were USD 24.3 million in the first nine months of 2026.
