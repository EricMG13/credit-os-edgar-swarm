## Findings

### Bottom line

Chord's fundamentals are strong on liquidity, maturities and reported leverage but depend on an oil price well above 2025's, so a price fall would reach creditors through cash and distributions long before it threatened debt service.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

Net debt was 0.22x last-twelve-months EBITDA at 2026-09-30, or 0.25x after USD 91.0 million of buybacks to 2026-11-04, with USD 2,774.4 million of liquidity and no maturity before the 2030 notes. At the FY2025 average crude price, 18.61% below the last-twelve-months average of USD 77.13 per Bbl, net leverage would be 0.30x and distributions 1.26x free cash flow, a gap that cash would cover for 5.35 years.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 46–47; calculation P2; calculation P3; calculation P5; calculation P6; calculation P8

### Key findings

Liquidity and maturities are the strongest support. Liquidity of USD 2,774.4 million combines cash with USD 1,969.7 million of undrawn revolver commitments, the two notes of USD 750.0 million each fall due in 2030 and 2033 at fixed rates, and the revolver now matures in 2031; with nothing due for almost four years, refinancing risk is low and the revolver is a second line behind cash.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 46–47; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 57; calculation K3

Reported leverage is low and the fall since FY2025 is real: net debt over EBITDA was 0.22x at 2026-09-30 against 0.77x at FY2025 and 0.36x at FY2024, and 0.58x at FY2025 even after adding back the non-cash impairment. After USD 91.0 million of buybacks to 2026-11-04 it is 0.25x; the pro forma effect of the Marcellus Shale sale is not established because closing and net proceeds are not stated. EBITDA is derived, not issuer-stated, and excludes hedge settlements.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation K1; calculation K2; calculation K4

Cash generation is strong at today's prices and entirely price-dependent. Free cash flow was USD 1,307.73 million in nine months 2026, 49.82% of EBITDA, because the average crude sales price was USD 83.54 per Bbl against USD 64.73 a year earlier and USD 62.78 in FY2025. Hedges cover 47.27% of fourth-quarter 2026 crude volumes but only 24.63% of last-twelve-months volumes for 2027 (31.15% with contracts added after the quarter), so most of the price exposure is open.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a strong reading is that FY2025 net profit was close to nil after the impairment with gross leverage of 0.89x, and that the 2026 recovery came with an oil price that management links to the Middle East conflict and the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz. The view would weaken if the crude sales price fell back to the FY2025 level while buybacks continued, if the Marcellus Shale proceeds or new borrowing funded distributions above free cash flow, or if the unsupplied covenant terms are tighter than the headline liquidity implies. It would strengthen if hedge cover for 2027 rose well above a quarter of volumes at floors near USD 65.54 per Bbl, if buybacks were kept below free cash flow, or if a compliance certificate showed wide headroom.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 126–127; calculation K1; calculation K11; calculation H2

### Limitations

- The downside scenario applies one price anchor to last-twelve-months volumes and leaves out hedge settlements and lower production taxes, so it overstates the near-term shortfall and understates exposure beyond the hedge horizon.
- Covenant ratios and the definitions behind them are not printed, so the liquidity and refinancing conclusions treat lender tests as untested, and headroom could be thinner than liquidity alone suggests.
- No production, price or cost guidance after February 2026 is supplied, so the twelve to twenty-four month outlook rests on reported run-rates and hedges rather than on management's plan.
- No agency rating or dated market price is supplied, so the market-access conclusion relies on the 2025 note issuance and not on where the notes trade.

### Company description

Chord is an independent oil and gas producer whose cash flow comes from crude oil sold to refiners and marketers from the Williston Basin, with a limited non-operated gas interest in the Marcellus Shale that is held for sale. It operates most of its portfolio, markets its own production, and has been built through the Whiting merger of 2022, the Enerplus combination of 2024 and the XTO purchase of 2025; the single basin and single product give cash-flow visibility only to the extent hedges and the oil price allow.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36

### Ownership and group structure

Chord is widely held, with no sponsor or controlling holder identified, so the credit does not carry sponsor extraction risk but also has no committed support. The borrower under the revolver is a subsidiary and certain subsidiaries guarantee the revolver and both notes, but the guarantors are not named, so structural priority cannot be assessed here; the business and governance review rates governance risk as medium because distributions have run near free cash flow and the credit agreement is unsupplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 164; 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 165

### Revenue durability and pricing power

Revenue is price-driven and not contractually recurring. Production revenue rose with the crude sales price from USD 64.73 to USD 83.54 per Bbl between nine months 2025 and nine months 2026, while the quarterly price fell 8.71% from the second to the third quarter of 2026, which shows the volatility that creditors carry. Chord is a price taker; its levers are volume and marketing, and purchased oil and gas sales add revenue at close to nil margin.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 38; calculation D1

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins can be defended at current prices but the cost trend is adverse. Lease operating expense per Boe rose from USD 9.74 to USD 10.24 and the production tax rate from 7.4% to 8.1% of oil, NGL and gas revenues, so costs absorb a share of any price gain; EBITDA margin on total revenues was 45.96% on the last twelve months against 34.19% in FY2025, a swing that is mostly price.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 44; calculation K9

### Capital intensity and cash-flow conversion

EBITDA converts to free cash flow at about half in nine months 2026 and at 41.54% in FY2025, so capital spending absorbs roughly half of EBITDA. The planned 2026 capital programme of USD 1.35 billion to USD 1.45 billion is largely discretionary on held-by-production acreage, which gives Chord the lever to protect free cash flow if prices fall.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 110; calculation K6; calculation K9

### Porter's five forces

Rivalry and substitutes matter through price: Chord is a price taker in a commodity market, and energy-transition and supply decisions by other producers set the price that drives cash flow. Buyer power is concentrated but limited on price because realised prices follow NYMEX WTI and other purchasers exist, while supplier power shows in service-cost inflation and in a limited set of midstream providers; new entrants are unlikely to match the acreage position quickly, an Analyst Judgement that supports Chord's standing without changing price exposure.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 48; 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 178; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40

### PEST analysis

Political and economic factors are the material categories. Geopolitical disruption raised oil prices in 2026, and a change in OPEC+ policy, including a member leaving the group in May 2026, could reverse them; tribal and federal regulation on the Fort Berthold Reservation and a title dispute with the MHA Nation add a regulatory and legal exposure whose size is not stated. Social and technological factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver on the materials supplied.

### SWOT analysis

Strengths are liquidity, low reported leverage and operator control; weaknesses are price dependence, payout policy and concentration. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are Marcellus Shale proceeds used to retain cash and a longer hedge book; threats are a price fall toward the FY2025 level, a pipeline outage and a debt-funded acquisition.

### Financial profile and credit quality

On the nine dimensions the profile reads strong for scale, liquidity and ability to refinance, average for competitive advantage, cost and capex flexibility, margin stability, free cash flow stability and financial policy, and weak for diversification. Margin and free cash flow stability are rated average rather than strong because FY2025 EBITDA was only 54.05% of the last-twelve-months level; liquidity is strong on USD 2,774.4 million; refinancing is strong on no maturity before 2030 though no rating or market price is supplied; financial policy is average with medium governance risk.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0); calculation K11

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months the near-term outlook is supportive but price-contingent. Tailwinds are the higher oil price, improved Williston Basin takeaway, a hedge floor on 47.27% of fourth-quarter 2026 volumes and the Marcellus Shale proceeds; headwinds are cost inflation, a rate rise in the third quarter of 2026 that tightens financial conditions, geopolitical reversal and the open 2027 hedge book. Over three to five years the question is whether payout policy and acquisitions keep leverage near its present level, which neither company guidance nor a rating in the sources settles.

### Downside scenario

The realistic downside is a return of the crude price to the FY2025 average of USD 62.78 per Bbl, 18.61% below the last-twelve-months average of USD 77.13 per Bbl, while distributions continue. On last-twelve-months volumes that price would cut revenue and EBITDA by the same amount, leaving EBITDA at 72.74% of its reported level and net leverage at 0.30x, or 0.34x after the buybacks to 2026-11-04; free cash flow would fall to 79.08% of distributions, so distributions would run at 1.26x free cash flow, although the annualised base dividend alone would still be covered. The break comes in cash, not in debt service: cash would cover 5.35 years of that shortfall, buybacks of USD 529.0 million capacity could be stopped, capital spending could be cut, and no maturity falls due before 2030. This is an Analyst Judgement anchored to the reported FY2025 price; it omits hedge settlements and lower production taxes, so it overstates the near-term shortfall.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Chord presents a strong liquidity and refinancing position and an adequate business profile, supported by low reported leverage, USD 2,774.4 million of liquidity and operator control, but credit quality is constrained by price dependence, a payout policy that has run near free cash flow and an unsupplied legal framework. Cash-flow durability appears adequate: strong at the current price and contingent on hedges and spending cuts if prices fall. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the oil price and on whether distributions stay below free cash flow, since nothing matures before 2030. The primary downside path is a crude price fall that turns free cash flow below distributions and draws down cash. Further analysis would require the credit agreement, covenant figures, an agency view and 2027 guidance.
