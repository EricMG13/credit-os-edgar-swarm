## Findings

### Bottom line

Chord is a scaled, single-basin oil producer with no sponsor or controlling holder identified, so creditors face concentration and capital-return risk, not extraction risk, and governance risk is medium. Distributions took 106.10% of free cash flow in FY2024 and 98.54% in FY2025, then 48.77% in nine months 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 111; 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 127–128; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 8; calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C6

The pattern that matters is debt-funded acquisition followed by a cash build as higher oil prices lifted cash flow: net debt rose USD 484.4 million during 2025 and the Marcellus Shale sale for USD 550.0 million is pending. The credit agreement is unsupplied and the signing chief executive changed without explanation, so capacity and continuity are not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 58; 10-Q_2026-06-30_chrd_chrd-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 56; calculation C7; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

The business is large and concentrated, which gives creditors scale but little diversification. Chord operates almost entirely in one basin with one reportable segment, crude oil is 91.73% of oil, NGL and gas revenues and 56% of proved reserves, and the company operates 89% of its reserves on acreage that is almost all held by production, so it can pace capital spending.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 130; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 38; calculation C1

Customer and takeaway concentration are the operating dependencies that matter most to cash flow. Phillips 66 took 21% and Marathon Petroleum Supply & Trading 12% of FY2025 product sales, and the annual report names a limited number of midstream providers and a possible Dakota Access Pipeline shutdown as risks; an outage would force shut-ins or wider price differentials and cut cash flow, although management says other purchasers exist.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 178; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36

No sponsor or controlling holder is identified, and no extraction behaviour is documented, but shareholder returns are policy-driven and were large against free cash flow. The board is 82% independent, the variable dividend was dropped after 2024, and the share count fell 10.81% between February 2025 and October 2026, yet returns equalled 98.54% of FY2025 free cash flow, leaving no deleveraging cushion in a year of debt-funded acquisition.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 111; 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 173; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-K_2024-12-31_chrd_chrd-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C5

Growth has come through acquisitions funded partly with debt, so leverage rose in 2025 before falling in 2026. The Enerplus combination paid USD 375.8 million in cash and issued shares, and the XTO assets cost USD 540.4 million, funded from the 2030 notes and cash; the pending Marcellus Shale sale of USD 550.0 million points the other way, towards pruning a non-core interest.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_chrd_chrd-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 114; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation C7; calculation C8

Disclosure is adequate for monitoring but thin on the points creditors rely on. The financial statements are audited and the quarterly reports are regular, but covenant compliance is stated without figures, the credit agreement was amended twice to raise baskets and thresholds without terms being supplied, and an 8.5% stake in a private specialty chemicals company with a Chord board designee is a related-party arrangement whose amounts are not stated.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 163; 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 164; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 57; Source readiness (CP-0)

Overall, Chord presents medium governance risk. The supported behaviour is large, cash-flow-funded shareholder returns with a falling share count, which matters because distributions near free cash flow leave little room to absorb a price fall or a debt-funded deal, and implies that creditor protection rests on leverage staying low rather than on structural restraint. Countervailing evidence is a base-only dividend, a declining payout share in 2026 and no documented creditor-adverse action.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 111; 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 173; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 29; calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C6

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a benign reading is that no sponsor exists, no liability-management or priming action is documented, the 2025 refinancing replaced notes with longer unsecured notes, and 2026 returns used under half of free cash flow with no new debt. The view would turn adverse if the Marcellus Shale proceeds or a new borrowing funded distributions above free cash flow, if a debt-funded acquisition followed the XTO deal, or if the unsupplied credit agreement shows wide capacity for restricted payments after the amendments. It would improve if a compliance certificate and the agreements show tight restricted-payment limits, or if the explanation of the chief executive change shows continuity of the capital-return policy.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 165; 10-K_2025-12-31_chrd_chrd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 166; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chrd_chrd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 57; calculation C6

### Limitations

- Ownership and control are limited to no controlling holder identified, because the proxy statement is not supplied and holders above 5% and director holdings are not established.
- The signing chief executive differs between the second and third quarterly reports with no explanation, so continuity of the capital-return policy is assumed rather than shown.
- The 75% return tier of the policy cannot be tested because adjusted free cash flow and the forward EBITDA basis are not printed, so the pace of future distributions is a range rather than a forecast.
- Legal capacity for restricted payments, investments and liens cannot be assessed without the credit agreement and indentures, so the governance level reflects behaviour only.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Chord's transactions follow a consistent pattern of buying scale with stock, cash and notes and then pruning. The Enerplus combination in May 2024 paid USD 375.8 million in cash and issued shares, and the XTO assets bought in October 2025 cost USD 540.4 million, funded by the 2030 notes and cash. The company issued two series of senior unsecured notes in 2025, USD 750.0 million due 2033 in March and USD 750.0 million due 2030 in September, using the first to repurchase and discharge the 2026 notes and the second to fund the XTO purchase.
