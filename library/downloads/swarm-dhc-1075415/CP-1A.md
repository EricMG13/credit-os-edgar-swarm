## Findings

### Bottom line

Business and governance risk is moderate and structural rather than behavioural: an external manager controlled by the trust's chair runs the company, and 61.5% of net operating income rests on third-party senior housing operators, yet no debt-funded payout or creditor-adverse transaction is documented.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 23, 25, 30; 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 213–215; Source readiness (CP-0)

The manager's agreements run to 2045 and renew each year, and ending them for convenience would cost a fee equal to the present value of 19 years to 20 years of future fees. The trust paid the manager a cash incentive fee of USD 17.91 million for 2025 and has recognised USD 22.00 million for the first nine months of 2026, while it pays shareholders only USD 0.01 per share a quarter and applied 2025 property sale proceeds to debt. The debt documents, covenant levels and proxy statement are not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 197, 212–213; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 23–24; calculation K1

### Key findings

- Control sits with the manager, not with creditors or an independent owner. Every officer of the trust is an officer and employee of the manager, the trust's chair controls the manager's parent through ABP Trust, and the trust has no employees; five of seven Trustees were independent at 2025-12-31. The agreements run to 2045, ending them for convenience on 60 days notice would cost a fee equal to 19 years to 20 years of future fees, or the fee for 10 years if the reason is performance, and in January 2025 the trust consented to the pledge of the manager's rights to the manager's lender and agreed to give that lender 30 days to cure a manager default before it terminates. For creditors this means that independent Trustees, not owner incentives, are the check on related-party decisions.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 34, 213, 215; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 23, 25

- Operating risk is concentrated in one segment run by outside operators. Senior housing operations produce 61.5% of net operating income and 87.01% of revenues; the largest manager holds communities worth 30.5% of gross real estate book value and the three largest hold 63.1%. Managers of roughly half the managed communities were replaced between September 2025 and December 2025 when the related operator AlerisLife wound down, and notice was given on six more communities in September 2026, so results depend on how those transitions bed in. Occupancy across all senior housing properties rose to 83.8% from 81.5% and quarterly net operating income to USD 49.66 million from USD 29.62 million, which is the evidence for the recovery.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11, 20, 30–31, 46; calculation K3; calculation K4; calculation K11

- Capital allocation has favoured creditors so far. In 2025 the trust sold 69 properties for USD 604.87 million and applied USD 402.23 million of net proceeds to redeem secured notes; it acquired no property in 2023, 2024 or 2025; it holds its distribution at USD 0.01 per share a quarter, USD 9.66 million for 2025; and it issued no debt in 2026 to 2026-09-30. No dividend recapitalisation, priming, drop-down or non-pro-rata exchange is described in the sources read.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 185, 195, 197; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 7, 20; calculation K5

- Cash leakage to the manager is moderate, but it is paid in cash on a formula that ignores credit metrics. The trust paid the manager USD 52.74 million in 2025 and USD 47.59 million in the first nine months of 2026, of which incentive fees were USD 17.91 million and USD 22.00 million. The fee is 12.0% of equity market capitalisation times the amount by which three-year total return exceeds an index, capped at 1.5% of shares, so a positive share return, not leverage or free cash flow, triggers payment; management guidance puts the 2026 fee at USD 29.30 million, payable in January 2027, which is 26.48% of cash at 2026-09-30.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 211–214; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 23–24; EX99.1_2026-09-30_dhc_tm2629915d1-ex99-1-htm.htm.jsonl, page 6; calculation K1; calculation K2; calculation K10

- No creditor-adverse behaviour is documented, but legal capacity is not established. Willingness evidence is benign: small distributions, property sales applied to debt and redemptions at principal. Capacity under restricted payment, investment, lien and guarantee-release terms cannot be assessed because the debt documents are not supplied. The 2025 refinancing added secured debt, yet the secured share of principal was 34.46% at 2026-09-30 against 35.09% at 2024-12-31.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 219–224; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 16; calculation K9

- Disclosure supports monitoring of the financial statements but not of covenants. Audit opinions on the 2025 statements and on internal control were unqualified and disclosure controls were effective at 2026-09-30, but covenant levels are not printed and governance detail is incorporated from a proxy statement that is not supplied.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 171–172, 178, 181; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 49–50, 56

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a higher risk level is the manager's contractual entrenchment: terms to 2045, a termination fee of 19 years to 20 years of fees, a cash incentive fee that is paid regardless of credit metrics, and a pledge consent that delays any termination by 30 days. The strongest evidence for a lower level is conduct: no debt-funded payout, asset sales applied to debt, redemptions at principal and a board with five independent Trustees out of seven whose Independent Trustees approved the pledge consent. The view would move higher if the debt documents give wide restricted payment, investment or guarantee-release capacity, if the trust funds a distribution or related-party transfer with debt, or if the proxy statement shows that independent Trustees do not control the committees that approve related-party matters. It would move lower if the documents restrict payments tightly and the termination fee proves small. A lender call on the manager's pledge, or an incentive fee payable in January 2027 that exceeds the guided amount, would also weigh on the view.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 116–117, 212–215; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, page 23; calculation K10

### Limitations

- The proxy statement is not supplied, so committee membership, independence determinations and the ownership table are not established; the board-independence part of the first finding rests on the annual report's count of five independent Trustees out of seven, and the other shareholders cannot be named.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 34, 171–172; Source readiness (CP-0)

- The credit agreement, indentures and mortgage loan agreements are not supplied, so legal capacity for distributions, further secured debt, investments and the release of guarantees is not established; the risk level describes documented behaviour only and would rise if the documents grant wide capacity.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 49–50, 60–62; Source readiness (CP-0)

- No statements are supplied for the manager, its parent, ABP Trust or AlerisLife, so the manager's own strength behind its lender pledge and the residual value of the AlerisLife wind-down are not established; the entrenchment finding is therefore read from contract terms alone.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 213, 215; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 26

- The 2026 incentive fee estimate is a management figure from the November 2026 presentation, which does not reconcile its guidance to GAAP; it sizes the January 2027 cash call but is not a contractual amount.
  Basis: EX99.1_2026-09-30_dhc_tm2629915d1-ex99-1-htm.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, page 23

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The trust's recent transactions are refinancings and disposals, not an acquisition or a sponsor-led deal. In 2025 it sold 69 properties for USD 604.87 million, applied USD 402.23 million of net proceeds to partly redeem the senior secured notes due 2026, and redeemed the remainder in full by 2025-12-29, having paid USD 152.87 million of accreted interest in cash during the year.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 185, 197, 221; calculation K5

The new debt of 2025 was USD 375.00 million of 7.25% senior secured notes due 2030, issued in September 2025 with net proceeds of USD 364.73 million and secured by a first-priority lien on the equity of subsidiaries that own 36 properties; four mortgage financings totalling USD 343.16 million between March and May 2025 secured on 14, 7, 4 and 2 senior housing communities; and a USD 150.00 million secured revolver obtained in June 2025 that was undrawn at 2026-09-30. The notes mature in 2030 and the mortgage loans between 2028 and 2035, which moved the nearest large maturity to 2028.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 220, 222–224; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, page 16

No debt was issued in the first nine months of 2026. The transactions of that period are the sale of 13 communities for USD 23.00 million in March 2026 and the USD 14.50 million purchase of two land parcels in April 2026 that ended two finance leases.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 7
