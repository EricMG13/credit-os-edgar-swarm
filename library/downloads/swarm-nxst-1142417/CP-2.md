## Findings

### Bottom line

Nexstar's credit is stretched rather than impaired: near-term liquidity and a back-ended maturity profile protect creditors, but leverage rests on election-year cash flow that falls away in 2027, so the 2026 repayment pace should not be extrapolated.

Net debt was USD 10,963 million at 2026-09-30, 6.86x last-twelve-month reported EBITDA; pro forma for the dividend payable on 2026-11-27 it is USD 11,020.33 million and 6.90x. The issuer's first-lien ratio of 3.03x against a 4.75x limit rests on a definition that cannot be reproduced here. Political advertising matches the whole of the legacy business's 2026 earnings growth, so USD 1,311 million repaid since the start of 2026 says little about 2027.

### Key findings

- Leverage is the central weakness, and no single measure fixes it. Net debt over last-twelve-month reported EBITDA was 6.86x at 2026-09-30 (6.90x pro forma for the dividend payable on 2026-11-27), against 3.38x at the end of FY2024 and 6.05x at the end of FY2025; excluding the USD 381 million equity-investment impairment it is 5.54x. The issuer's 4.04x total net ratio counts a full year of TEGNA, averages an election year with a non-election year and excludes The CW, so it is closer to through-cycle leverage in concept but cannot be verified from the documents.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-05-07_nxst-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 7; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_nxst-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 4

- Cash generation is strong and is the only deleveraging source for now. Nine-month 2026 free cash flow was USD 1,154 million, 62.04% of issuer Adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,860 million, with capital expenditure at 2.05% of revenue; the issuer repaid USD 786 million of debt and paid USD 171 million of dividends in the period and a further USD 525 million of facility principal after the quarter end.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against caution is the issuer's own coverage and repayment record. It reports first-lien net leverage of 3.03x and total net leverage of 4.04x, repaid USD 1,311 million of debt since the start of 2026, generated USD 1,154 million of free cash flow in nine months and issued USD 5,115 million of notes in 2026 after the antitrust suits were filed, which shows that bond investors remained open to the credit. The 6.50% secured notes were valued at 96.14% of principal at quarter end by a bank's bid, which does not point to distress pricing. These points would win if the injunction were narrowed or lifted, if the first quarters of 2027 showed legacy non-political earnings growing and distribution revenue rising faster than subscribers fall, and if repayment continued while the dividend stayed flat, because the covenant average would then fall without relying on synergies.

The view would turn more cautious if the Ninth Circuit or the 2027 trial left the injunction in force through the outside synergies date, if the first odd-year quarters on the new structure showed cover below the 2.51x illustrated here, if the issuer's first-lien ratio rose above 3.03x while the limit reverted to 4.25x, if repurchases resumed before leverage fell, or if the 2027 notes of USD 440 million had to be refinanced at materially higher cost.

### Limitations

- No post-acquisition odd-year result exists, because TEGNA has been consolidated only since 2026-03-19 and only in an election year, so the interest-cover sensitivity applies the legacy business's FY2025 cost behaviour to the combined company; if TEGNA's costs are more variable the odd-year cover would be better than the 2.51x to 2.57x shown, and if less variable, worse.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nxst-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 60; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nxst-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17; calculation C07

- The liquidity stress and the run-rate interest range are constructions from stated assumptions, not forecasts: the stress sets free cash flow to zero and the interest range applies the term-loan rate band to every floating balance, so they size the 2027 funding conclusion without predicting it.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_nxst-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 55; calculation C03; calculation C04

- The covenant conclusion rests on a ratio the issuer computes and no compliance certificate or executed definition was supplied, so headroom is stated in turns of leverage only and the fall in earnings that would breach the test is not calculable.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_nxst-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_nxst-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation C13

- Two exposures are unquantified and sit outside every leverage figure here: the class action on local advertising pricing, with trial set for 2027-11-01, and the dispute over the 2009 Chicago Cubs transactions, where the IRS has appealed a Tax Court decision that found no tax due; either could add a cash claim that the liquidity and claims conclusions do not include.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The credit's strengths start with a contractual distribution base. Distribution fees were USD 2,924 million in FY2025 against USD 2,928 million in FY2024 while advertising fell USD 456 million, because per-subscriber fees and annual escalators have outrun subscriber losses, so in the non-election year 59.08% of revenue held steady, and this is what carries fixed charges when political advertising is absent. The second strength is capital-light cash conversion: capital expenditure was 2.99% of FY2025 revenue and 2.05% in nine months of 2026, so most operating cash flow is available to repay debt, which is how USD 1,311 million was repaid in 2026. The third is liquidity and maturity shape: USD 887 million of cash and undrawn revolvers, both revolvers running to June 2030 and only 5.02% of principal due by the end of 2027 give the credit time to repay from cash flow. The fourth is demonstrated capital-markets access, with USD 5,115 million of notes issued after the antitrust suits were filed and a USD 1,750 million term loan raised at the March closing. The fifth is the absence of rating-downgrade triggers that accelerate maturities, so a rating action would raise cost and limit access rather than force repayment.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_nxst-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 59; 10-K_2025-12-31_nxst-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 60; 10-K_2025-12-31_nxst-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 119; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nxst-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nxst-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nxst-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 52; calculation C02; calculation C09; calculation C12; calculation C17

The weaknesses start with leverage after a debt-funded acquisition: carrying debt rose USD 5,076 million to USD 11,409 million, and interest expense, net rose to USD 186 million in Q3 from USD 94 million a year earlier, so net interest absorbed 24.60% of Adjusted EBITDA even in the strongest quarter of 2026. The second is political cyclicality: with fixed operating costs, nearly every dollar of lost political revenue was lost operating profit in FY2025, which makes even-year earnings a poor guide to debt service. The third is dependence on a court and regulatory outcome that blocks the integration the price assumed, with no synergy size disclosed and the covenant window for counting synergies closing on 2027-09-19. The fourth is rate and refinancing cost: 42.61% of principal floats without hedges and the 2026 notes carry coupons of 6.50% and 7.25%. The fifth is shareholder and contingent-claim policy: a maintained dividend, a USD 1.4 billion repurchase authority, a USD 110 million minority put on The CW and unquantified litigation sit beside debt that is already high.

### Industry forces

### Overall credit view

Overall, Nexstar presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, a contractual per-subscriber fee base and low capital intensity, but credit quality is constrained by leverage taken on to buy TEGNA, by earnings that swing with the political cycle and by an integration that a court has blocked. Cash-flow durability appears adequate in election years and not yet established in odd years, because FY2025 showed a near-full flow-through of lost political revenue and the combined company has no odd-year record. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the pace of repayment through the first half of 2027, the outcome of the injunction litigation and the cost at which the 2027 notes and 2028 notes can be refinanced. The primary downside path is a non-election year with the injunction unresolved. No peer set was supplied, so the comparison with typical leveraged issuers in television broadcasting is a judgement: scale and the per-subscriber fee base are the differentiators and leverage at the election-year peak is the offset. Further analysis would require the covenant definitions, any court ruling after 2026-11-17, a post-acquisition odd-year result and peer comparisons. Fundamental credit assessment complete.
