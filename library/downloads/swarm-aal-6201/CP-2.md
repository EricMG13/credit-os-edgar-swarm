## Findings

### Bottom line

American Airlines has a scaled network and USD 11,797 million of liquidity but a thin, eroding earnings base, so creditors rely on liquidity, customer-funded float and secured market access rather than cash earnings to service USD 29,321 million of principal.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C10

Total debt was 10.25x last-twelve-months EBITDA and EBITDA covered cash interest 1.81x, with free cash flow near zero after capital spending. The primary downside is persistent unhedged fuel cost against rising unit costs, and a 2028 maturity of USD 7,861 million that must be refinanced in secured markets.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C7; calculation C9

### Key findings

- The earnings and leverage profile is weak and still deteriorating: EBITDA calculated from statement lines fell to USD 2,891 million over the last twelve months from USD 4,970 million in FY2023 while revenue grew 15.52%, so the EBITDA margin fell from 9.42% to 4.74% and total debt reached 10.25x EBITDA. Net debt fell 15.70% over the same period, which shows cash building and not earnings recovery.
  Basis: calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C4; Financial foundation (CP-1)
- Fuel is the transmission channel and it is unhedged: in the nine months to September 2026 fuel cost rose by USD 4,427 million, absorbing 69.74% of the USD 6,348 million revenue gain, and each ten cents per gallon adds about USD 450 million, or 15.57% of last-twelve-months EBITDA, so fuel at the third-quarter level erodes coverage faster than fares can reprice.
  Basis: calculation C5; calculation C6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 44–45, 69
- Cash generation does not fund investment from earnings: capital spending of USD 4,358 million was 1.51x EBITDA and free cash flow was negative USD 91 million, because operating cash flow of USD 4,267 million was lifted by USD 2,970 million of working capital and other items, and customer-funded float supplied 70.60% of nine-month operating cash flow, which reverses if bookings weaken.
  Basis: calculation C7; calculation C8; Financial foundation (CP-1)
- Liquidity is the genuine support but it is not unlimited: USD 11,797 million, including USD 3,510 million of undrawn facilities, covers 0.98x of the principal due through 2028 and 60.78% of that principal plus aircraft and engine commitments, so the debt service plan depends on continued financing as well as on cash on hand.
  Basis: calculation C10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 60–61, 64–66
- Refinancing capacity is demonstrated but secured: in 2026 the company extended revolvers to 2031 and term loans to 2033 and raised USD 4,102 million of aircraft and engine debt, yet 87.22% of principal is secured, 52.24% sits in aircraft certificates and loyalty financing, and a USD 7,861 million maturity falls in 2028.
  Basis: calculation C9; calculation C13; Business & governance (CP-1A)
- Financial policy and governance are supportive but unverified: there is no sponsor, no dividend or buyback, and audited reporting, while American Airlines, Inc. holds a USD 12,512 million net receivable from the parent and board and ownership detail was not supplied, so governance risk stays medium.
  Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)
- The business model supports revenue but not margin: loyalty is 13.81% of revenue and yield rose 12.1%, yet unit cost growth of 12.5% outran unit revenue growth of 10.9%, salaries rose USD 962 million, most employees are unionised and regional capacity contracts commit USD 4,461 million of minimum payments.
  Basis: calculation C11; calculation C12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 47–48, 66; Business & governance (CP-1A)
- Claims outside the debt figure add to the burden: operating lease liabilities of USD 6,639 million equal 22.40% of total debt, aircraft and engine commitments are USD 24,056 million, and the accident and antitrust litigation is not sized, so leverage understates the fixed claims on cash.
  Basis: calculation C17; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a weak earnings reading is momentum and balance-sheet repair: third-quarter revenue grew 19.3%, yield rose 12.1% over nine months, liquidity rose to USD 11,797 million from USD 9,233 million at the 2025 year end, and net debt fell by USD 3,976 million since FY2023. If fuel prices fall back toward the FY2025 average of USD 2.39 per gallon while yield holds, EBITDA could recover quickly because most of the 2026 damage is the fuel price, not demand.
Basis: calculation C4; calculation C5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 43–48; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The view would improve if EBITDA cover of cash interest returns above the 2.28x of FY2023, if free cash flow turns positive without float growth, and if the 2028 maturity is refinanced early. It would weaken if fuel stays near the third-quarter average of USD 3.85 per gallon, if unit cost growth stays above unit revenue growth for another year, or if float reverses so that liquidity falls while the 2028 maturity approaches.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C5; calculation C11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 48

### Limitations

- EBITDA is built from statement lines because the issuer states none, and FY2025 depreciation differs by USD 329 million between the income statement and the cash flow statement, so every leverage and coverage conclusion is only as reliable as that derivation; if the same gap applied to the last twelve months, total debt would be 9.20x EBITDA rather than 10.25x.
  Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1)
- No executed debt documents, ratings or market prices were supplied, so covenant headroom, structural priority and legal capacity are not established and the view that refinancing stays open assumes no restriction on new secured debt.
  Basis: Source readiness (CP-0); 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 61
- No peer set or management guidance was supplied, so the business profile cannot be compared with other airlines and the outlook rests on filing trends, which limits the strength of the comparison with typical issuers in the sector.
  Basis: Source readiness (CP-0)
- Interim cash flow statements print operating cash flow as one total, so the float share of operating cash flow is built from balance-sheet changes and could differ from the cash flow lines, which would change how much of the improvement is recurring.
  Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 8
- Nothing dated after 2026-10-22 was supplied, so fuel, bookings and financing since the latest quarter are not reflected and the downside scenario is a sensitivity on reported trends, not a forecast.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 73

### Strengths and weaknesses

The five strongest supports all work through cash availability rather than earnings. Liquidity of USD 11,797 million, with revolvers maturing in 2031 and term loans in 2033, gives the company time to absorb a fuel shock without a near-term maturity, which lowers the probability of a liquidity-driven default over the next two years even though it does not cover the 2028 wall alone. Loyalty brings partner cash of USD 6.2 billion in 2025 and 13.81% of revenue, which both supports unit revenue and gives lenders a pledgeable cash stream, although USD 5,985 million of secured debt already draws on it. Scale and network, with nine hubs and a domestic share of 70.89% of passenger revenue, spread demand across markets and let capacity and pricing respond to local conditions. Demonstrated access to asset-backed markets, with USD 4,102 million raised in nine months, lowers refinancing risk for as long as lenders accept aircraft collateral, whose values were not supplied. No dividends or buybacks keep cash in the business, which supports debt service.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C10; calculation C11; calculation C13; Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1)

The five weaknesses all work through thin earnings against fixed claims. Leverage of 10.25x and cash interest cover of 1.81x leave little room for a further earnings decline, so a modest fall in EBITDA moves both measures materially. Unhedged fuel and rising unit cost turn price shocks directly into lower EBITDA, as the 69.74% absorption of the 2026 revenue gain shows. Free cash flow of negative USD 91 million and dependence on float for 70.60% of nine-month operating cash flow mean the cash position is partly borrowed from customers and will shrink if bookings slow. The maturity profile puts USD 12,042 million of principal, 41.07% of the total, due by 2028, and the secured share of 87.22% limits the assets that can back new debt. Fixed commitments, namely USD 24,056 million of aircraft and engine purchases, USD 4,461 million of regional capacity payments, USD 6,639 million of operating leases and labour contracts that open in 2027, reduce cost flexibility when revenue falls.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C5; calculation C7; calculation C8; calculation C9; calculation C13; calculation C17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 64–66

### Company description and credit linkage

American Airlines Group Inc. runs a major network carrier through American Airlines, Inc. and its regional subsidiaries, with nine hubs, service to more than 350 destinations and about 224 million passengers boarded in 2025. FY2025 revenue of USD 54,633 million was 83.48% passenger travel, 13.81% loyalty-related and 1.54% cargo, and 70.91% of passenger revenue was domestic, so cash flow comes mainly from advance ticket sales across a broad network and from partner payments for loyalty miles.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C20; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The business model supports cash visibility but not earnings stability. Customers and loyalty members had advanced USD 20,928 million at 30 September 2026, which funds working capital and cushions the quarter-to-quarter swings, while the fleet, labour and regional capacity commitments are fixed, so a drop in demand or a rise in fuel falls straight through to EBITDA and to the cash available for the USD 29,321 million of principal.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C17; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Ownership and group structure

The company is widely held with no disclosed sponsor or controlling holder, so the risks that matter are cash leakage between group entities and the ranking of lenders, not sponsor extraction. American Airlines, Inc. is the main borrower of secured debt, American Airlines Group Inc. guarantees the senior secured notes and the loyalty financing, the parent's own debt is the USD 3,746 million of Treasury notes guaranteed by the subsidiaries, and American Airlines, Inc. holds a USD 12,512 million net receivable from the parent, which grew when the subsidiary funded the parent's USD 1.0 billion cash settlement of convertible notes in 2025.
Basis: calculation C18; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Financial policy has been supportive: no dividends or buybacks in 2024 and 2025, voluntary prepayment of the highest-coupon secured notes, and 2026 refinancings that extended maturities. Ownership restrictions on foreign holders and a tax benefit plan to October 2027 protect USD 11.9 billion of federal tax losses and limit concentrated accumulation. The legal rank of each facility and the capacity of the debt documents to permit asset transfers were not supplied, so structural priority is not established here.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is driven by yield more than volume: over nine months 2026 capacity grew 4.3% and traffic 2.8%, load factor fell 1.2 percentage points and yield rose 12.1%, which lifted total unit revenue 10.9% and third-quarter revenue 19.3%. Revenue is discretionary, cyclical and seasonal, with a summer peak, and it is exposed to fuel, the economy and competition from network, low-cost and ultra-low-cost carriers that match fares market by market.
Basis: calculation C11; calculation C15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 43–48; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 23

Pricing power is real but partial: the 2026 revenue gain of USD 6,348 million exceeded the USD 4,427 million fuel increase, but fuel took 69.74% of the gain and other costs rose USD 2,415 million, so operating income still fell USD 494 million, and the loyalty program adds a second revenue stream of partner payments that does not depend on seats flown. No customer concentration is disclosed, and the largest partner flows are given only in aggregate, so concentration risk in the co-brand card relationships is not established.
Basis: calculation C5; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Cost structure and margin resilience

The cost base is mostly fixed or externally set: fuel was 26.78% of nine-month operating expenses with no hedge, salaries, wages and benefits rose 7.36% to USD 14,027 million, about 86% of employees are unionised with the pilot and mechanic contracts opening in 2027, and regional capacity contracts commit USD 4,461 million of minimum payments whatever the load factor. Margins can be defended only through fares and capacity, and in 2026 unit cost growth of 12.5% exceeded unit revenue growth of 10.9%, which cut the EBITDA margin to 4.74% over the last twelve months from 9.42% in FY2023.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C11; calculation C12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 47–48, 66; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 19

Cost flexibility sits in capacity and in the timing of aircraft deliveries, not in the cost lines. Management states that it is managing costs and reengineering the business, but the filings give no quantified savings, so the effect of those levers on margin is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 45

### Capital intensity and cash flow conversion

Capital spending of USD 4,358 million over the last twelve months was 1.51x EBITDA, and the issuer does not split it into maintenance and growth. Operating cash flow of USD 4,267 million was 147.60% of EBITDA only because working capital and other items added USD 2,970 million after cash interest of USD 1,594 million, and free cash flow of negative USD 91 million was negative 3.15% of EBITDA, so earnings alone do not cover interest and investment together.
Basis: calculation C7; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Conversion depends on float and on financing: air traffic and loyalty liabilities rose USD 3,206 million in nine months, 70.60% of operating cash flow, and the company raised secured debt to pre-fund investment, with cash and short-term investments up USD 2,451 million against a USD 727 million rise in principal. Float reverses if bookings fall, and card processors can hold back cash if the financial position worsens, which would reduce liquidity at the moment it is needed. Cash taxes are low because of federal tax losses of USD 11.9 billion, but cash taxes for 2025 and 2026 are not stated.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C16; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 62

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense and sets fares: on most domestic nonstop routes the company faces other network carriers and low-cost and ultra-low-cost carriers, and price cuts are matched quickly, so a fuel-driven cost increase can only be recovered if rivals also raise fares, which limits how much fuel cost fares can recover; in 2026 fuel took 69.74% of the revenue gain. Entry is limited by gates, slots, aircraft supply and capital, but ultra-low-cost carriers and long-range narrowbody entry increase competition on international routes, so entry affects yield more than market share.
Basis: calculation C5; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16

Substitutes matter on shorter segments, where ground and rail compete, and in discretionary travel, where customers can defer trips. Buyer power is high because fares are transparent and a significant share of tickets is sold through third-party distributors that charge more than direct channels, although the loyalty program and co-brand cards raise switching costs. Supplier power is concentrated: aircraft come from a few manufacturers with long lead times, fuel is bought at market prices, and unions represent most employees, so input costs are largely outside management control.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 15–17, 83, 86

### Macro and regulatory factors

Macro and regulatory factors are a primary driver of credit quality because they move fuel and demand. Oil and gas price volatility from conflict and trade policy drives the fuel price, which ranged from about USD 1.86 to about USD 4.78 per gallon in the first nine months of 2026, and a temporary U.S. Government shutdown cut fourth-quarter 2025 revenue. A 100 basis point rise in rates would raise interest expense on variable-rate debt by about USD 150 million and interest income by about USD 80 million, a net USD 70 million cost, so rate risk is secondary to fuel. Regulation by transportation and aviation authorities, foreign ownership limits and antitrust litigation shape capacity and cost but do not change the base view.
Basis: calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 43, 69; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 23, 110

### Credit-translated SWOT

Strengths that support credit are the liquidity of USD 11,797 million, the loyalty cash stream, network scale and the absence of shareholder distributions. Weaknesses that constrain credit are 10.25x leverage, 1.81x interest cover, unhedged fuel and negative free cash flow. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are a fall in fuel prices with yields holding, the change of co-brand card issuer in 2026, whose financial effect is not quantified in the filings, fleet renewal that lowers unit fuel burn, and early refinancing of the 2028 maturity. Threats that could weaken it are fuel above the third-quarter level, labour contract openings in 2027, an unsized accident litigation with trial set for April 2027, and a reversal of customer float.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C10; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale and diversification are the strongest dimensions: revenue of USD 54,633 million, nine hubs, a domestic share of 70.91% and a growing international share spread demand and give pricing options. Competitive advantage rests on the network, alliances and the loyalty program, which keep yield above unit cost but not by enough to protect margin. Cost and capex flexibility, margin stability and free cash flow stability are the weakest dimensions, as the 4.74% EBITDA margin, 1.51x capital spending to EBITDA and negative free cash flow show.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C7; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Ability to refinance and liquidity are supportive but depend on continued secured financing, since liquidity covers 0.98x of principal due through 2028 and 87.22% of principal is already secured. Financial policy and governance are supportive in behaviour but incomplete in evidence. The trend is a thinner earnings base each year since FY2023 with cash and liquidity building through new debt, and the missing datapoints are an issuer-stated EBITDA, covenant levels and headroom, legal rank, ratings and peer comparisons.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C10; calculation C13; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months the company has tailwinds in yield strength, a new co-brand card issuer in 2026 whose financial effect is not quantified, fleet renewal, extended revolvers and term loans, and USD 11,797 million of liquidity. Headwinds are fuel at the third-quarter level, unit cost growth above unit revenue growth, USD 631 million of principal in the remainder of 2026, USD 3,550 million in 2027 and USD 7,861 million in 2028, a cargo receivables facility that expires in December 2026, pilot and mechanic contract negotiations in 2027 and the April 2027 accident trial. Management gave no earnings guidance in the filings, so the outlook rests on reported trends.
Basis: calculation C9; calculation C10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 44–48, 65–66; 10-Q_2026-06-30_aal_aal-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 24

Over three to five years the tailwinds are a younger fleet with lower fuel use per seat and a loyalty program that grows with card spending, and the headwinds are 2030 and 2031 Treasury note maturities of USD 3,746 million, a USD 10,666 million block of principal from 2031 and USD 24,056 million of aircraft and engine commitments that keep demanding secured financing. Sector headwinds are competition on international routes and regulatory and labour constraints; sector tailwinds are limited by the lack of peer or industry forecasts in the inputs.
Basis: calculation C9; calculation C17; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 21–22

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is fuel staying near the third-quarter average of USD 3.85 per gallon through 2027 while yield gains fade as capacity grows. The first operational break is the unit margin: unit cost already grew 12.5% against unit revenue growth of 10.9%, so a stall in yield turns the narrowed spread negative, and with unhedged fuel each additional ten cents per gallon adds about USD 450 million of annual cost. The financial transmission is lower EBITDA from USD 2,891 million, interest cover below the 1.81x level, and free cash flow that is negative even before a float reversal, since EBITDA less cash interest less capital spending is already negative USD 3,061 million.
Basis: calculation C6; calculation C7; calculation C11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 44–48

The liquidity and refinancing consequence follows the float: if bookings weaken, air traffic and loyalty liabilities of USD 20,928 million shrink and card processors may hold back cash, so liquidity of USD 11,797 million falls while USD 12,042 million of principal falls due through 2028 and USD 7,366 million of aircraft and engine commitments fall due in the same period. Management levers are the USD 3,510 million of undrawn facilities, deferral of aircraft deliveries, capacity cuts, asset-backed financing and fares; resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress through 2027, but leverage would rise and the 2028 maturity would need refinancing from a weaker position. The observable triggers are listed in the monitoring register: fuel above the nine-month high, coverage below its current level, liquidity below its current level and delay in financing the remaining aircraft certificate escrow. No default is assumed because the sources do not support one.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C10; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 43, 59–61, 65–66

### Overall credit view

Overall, American Airlines Group Inc. presents an adequate business profile supported by network scale, the loyalty program and USD 11,797 million of liquidity, but credit quality is constrained by thin earnings, 10.25x leverage and unhedged fuel exposure. Cash-flow durability appears weak because free cash flow is negative and depends on customer float. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on continued access to aircraft and loyalty-backed secured markets and on the stability of collateral values. The primary downside path is persistent high fuel cost against rising unit costs and a 2028 maturity of USD 7,861 million. Further analysis would require executed debt documents, covenant levels, ratings, a peer set and any management guidance.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C7; calculation C9; calculation C10; Source readiness (CP-0)

Fundamental credit assessment completed. Top risk: unhedged fuel cost against a thin and eroding earnings base.
