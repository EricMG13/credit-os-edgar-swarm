## Findings

### Bottom line

American Airlines has no disclosed sponsor or controlling owner, so creditors face operating and structural risk rather than owner extraction: no dividends or buybacks were paid, but fuel-driven cost pressure and an increasingly secured, asset-backed debt stack drive the credit.
Basis: calculation C7; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 102–103

Liquidity of USD 11,797 million is the key protection against that pressure: fuel took 26.78% of nine-month operating costs with no hedges, and USD 5,985 million of secured debt depends on loyalty cash flows. Governance risk is medium because board, ownership and related-party detail was not supplied.
Basis: calculation C16; calculation C13; calculation C8; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 264

### Key findings

- Owner-extraction risk is low but unverified: the shares are widely dispersed and no sponsor, parent or controlling holder is disclosed, yet board independence, holders above 5% and related-party terms sit in a proxy statement that was not supplied. Legal limits on foreign holders (24.9% of votes and 49.0% of equity) and a plan that dilutes any holder reaching 4.9% shape who can accumulate control.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 102, 104, 264; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 2
- Shareholder behaviour has been creditor-aligned: no cash dividends or share repurchases were made in 2024 and 2025, the 2026 equity statements show only employee plan issuance equal to 0.35% of shares, and the only sizeable equity-linked cash outflow was USD 79 million to settle government warrants in 2025.
  Basis: calculation C14; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 102–103, 144; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–10
- Loyalty is a major cash engine and is already pledged to lenders: loyalty revenue was 13.81% of FY2025 revenue and partner cash payments were USD 6.2 billion in 2025, while USD 5,985 million of secured debt, 20.41% of principal, is repaid from those flows and secured on the programme's assets.
  Basis: calculation C3; calculation C8; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 167–169
- Costs, not demand, are the exposed side of the operating model: fuel was 26.78% of nine-month operating expenses against 20% in FY2025 with no hedges, each one-cent rise adds about USD 45 million to annual fuel cost, 86% of employees are unionised and regional capacity contracts commit USD 4,461 million of minimum-flying payments, so unit revenue growth of 10.9% did not stop operating income falling to USD 521 million from USD 1,015 million.
  Basis: calculation C13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 44–45, 48, 66, 69; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 22
- New debt extended maturities and funded fleet renewal but raised both the amount and the secured share: principal rose by USD 727 million to USD 29,321 million, the secured share rose from 84.70% to 87.22%, and the only unsecured debt is the U.S. Treasury's USD 3,746 million of notes.
  Basis: calculation C7; calculation C10; calculation C11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14–20
- Funding flows from the operating subsidiary up to the parent: American Airlines, Inc. holds a net receivable from the parent of USD 12,512 million, which rose by USD 1,680 million in 2025, due in part to funding the USD 1.0 billion cash settlement of the convertible notes, and by USD 574 million in the nine months to September 2026, so the subsidiary's own creditors carry the parent's repayment risk on that balance.
  Basis: calculation C15; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 171, 257; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26, 40
- Litigation adds claims the filings do not size: the accident cases seek unspecified damages with trial set for 2027-04-12, the Northeast Alliance class action is at class certification, and the latest quarterly report no longer describes the accident cases that the second-quarter report said insurance covers.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_aal_aal-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 24–25; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 112, 190–192
- Fleet renewal is a large claim on future cash: aircraft and engine commitments of USD 24,056 million remain, and 54.00% of the mainline fleet is owned, which keeps debt secured on aircraft and sustains the need for secured issuance.
  Basis: calculation C5; calculation C12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 64–66; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 100

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is the balance sheet: liquidity of USD 11,797 million, no dividends or buybacks, maturities moved out to 2031 and 2033, and revenue growth of 19.3% in the third quarter. Together they show creditors are protected by cash and market access rather than by sponsor support, and they leave little sign of value moving away from creditors apart from the intercompany funding of the parent.
Basis: calculation C16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14–17, 43–46

The view would improve if fuel falls back toward the FY2025 average of USD 2.39 per gallon, if the proxy statement confirms no controlling holder and no conflicted related-party terms, and if the accident litigation is resolved within insurance. It would weaken if fuel stays near the third-quarter average of USD 3.85 per gallon, if distributions resume while the secured share keeps rising, or if executed documents show wide capacity to move assets outside the secured group.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 113; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 48

### Limitations

- Board composition, independence, holders above 5%, executive pay and related-party dealings sit in a proxy statement that was not supplied, so the medium governance rating rests on behaviour shown in the financial statements and would move if the proxy shows a controlling holder or conflicted terms.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 264, 313–314; Source readiness (CP-0)
- No executed debt documents were supplied, so the conclusion that no value has been moved away from creditors rests on reported transactions only, and capacity for restricted payments, liens and asset transfers is not assessed.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 122; Source readiness (CP-0)
- The latest quarterly report omits the January 2025 Washington accident litigation that earlier reports described, so its status after 2026-06-10 is not established and the litigation conclusion uses the second-quarter report.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_aal_aal-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 24–25
- The group reports one operating segment, so the revenue mix shows where revenue comes from but not profit by line, and the conclusion that loyalty is a major cash engine rests on revenue and cash payment amounts, not on a stated margin.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 195
- Fleet, labour and loyalty facts are group figures while the principal subsidiary reports slightly different amounts (nine-month revenue of USD 46,979 million against USD 46,982 million for the group), so perimeter-sensitive conclusions use the group.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

No acquisition, merger, disposal or sponsor transaction appears in the five filings, so the transaction record is a funding one. In the nine months to September 2026 the group raised USD 5,933 million of long-term debt and repaid USD 5,347 million, and principal outstanding rose from USD 28,594 million at the 2025 year end to USD 29,321 million. No equity was issued for cash; the only new shares came from employee plans.
Basis: calculation C10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9, 14–15

New secured aircraft and engine debt was the largest source: USD 127 million from the last of the 2025 aircraft trusts, USD 1,141 million from the May 2026 trusts, USD 567 million of engine certificates in June, USD 692 million so far from the USD 1.3 billion August trusts, USD 870 million of junior borrowings on aircraft already delivered and USD 705 million of floating-rate equipment loans. These sum to USD 4,102 million and match the roughly USD 4.1 billion the company reports. A further USD 633 million of the August proceeds sat in escrow at 2026-09-30 and is not counted as debt.
Basis: calculation C11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–20, 45–46

Bank debt was amended rather than replaced. In March 2026 the three revolving facilities were re-established with commitments of about USD 3.1 billion, undrawn, and their maturity moved from June 2029 to March 2031; in May 2026 about USD 1.1 billion of term loans due January 2027 were refinanced into term loans maturing May 2033 at SOFR plus 3.00%, with USD 703 million of incremental borrowings; and in February 2026 the margin on the 2025 AAdvantage term loan was cut to SOFR plus 2.75%.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14–17, 45–46; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 198

Repayments were funded from new debt and cash: USD 629 million of short-term term loan, USD 1.0 billion of 8.50% senior secured notes and USD 310 million of aircraft equipment notes were prepaid, alongside USD 2.8 billion of scheduled repayments. After these transactions the aircraft certificate balance is USD 9,332 million, the AAdvantage secured debt is USD 5,985 million, and the Treasury's notes of USD 3,746 million fall due in 2030 and 2031. One subsequent event in October 2026 added purchase options on five Airbus A320 Family aircraft to commitments the company intends to finance with cash and long-term debt.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14–20, 64–65
