## Findings

### Bottom line
Kodiak's contracted compression business covers its interest and faces no material maturity before 2030, but the power build-out is cash-negative and is funded first by equity and then by debt, so creditors should expect leverage to hold near today's level, not fall.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.1_2026-08-06_kgsq22026earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 49; calculation C05; calculation C07

Net debt was 4.29x reported EBITDA at June 30, 2026; pro forma net leverage is 4.34x after the July 2026 lease buyout and 4.37x after the dividend payable on August 27, 2026, each taken on its own. Liquidity of about USD 1.7 billion makes a near-term payment problem unlikely, and remaining contract revenue of USD 2.6 billion covers 85.67% to 89.76% of guided second-half compression revenue but only 59.46% to 60.89% of a flat 2027, so renewals from 2027 are the real test.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is that the first half of 2026 absorbed an acquisition of USD 576.0 million and capital spending of USD 318.6 million while net debt rose only USD 35.3 million, because the USD 836.1 million equity issue paid for most of it, and that guided adjusted EBITDA for 2026 was raised while quarterly adjusted EBITDA rose 21.7% from a year earlier. Revenue due after 2026 also grew 2.74x in six months, and the company says it can cut growth spending or lean on the revolver if cash flow disappoints. That evidence would win if the third and fourth quarters show receivables reversing, free cash flow at the better end of guidance and signed multi-year power contracts, in which case year-end leverage would fall below 3.25x.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The first strength is fixed-fee compression contracts with investment-grade customers. Remaining contract revenue of USD 2.6 billion, utilization of 98.2% and an adjusted gross margin of 70.0% mean most compression cash flow is set by contract rather than by volumes, and customers bear fuel and property tax costs, so cash reaches debt service with little leakage. For creditors this supports debt service on the existing fleet through a downturn of moderate length.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 17; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_kgsq22026earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 6

The second strength is liquidity with a distant maturity wall. Cash and undrawn revolver commitments of about USD 1.7 billion face no material maturity until the revolver falls due in September 2030, and 86.17% of principal is fixed-rate notes due from 2031. That removes refinancing from the near-term default path and leaves time to adjust growth spending.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-08-06_kgsq22026earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation C12; calculation C21

The third strength is proven access to both debt and equity markets. The company issued USD 1.0 billion of 2031 notes in March 2026, raised USD 836.1 million of equity in May 2026 and, in the first half, paid for an acquisition of USD 576.0 million while net debt rose only USD 35.3 million. Equity funding of growth limits leverage drift and shows that investors were willing to finance the plan.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 22; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_kgsq22026earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 9; calculation C08

The fourth strength is pricing support from tight equipment supply in large-horsepower compression. Utilization rose to 98.2% from 97.2% a year earlier and the adjusted gross margin to 70.0% from 68.3%, and management describes long lead times for new large units. Tight supply slows competing fleet additions and supports renewals as contracts reach the end of their primary terms from 2027.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-08-06_kgsq22026earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 2; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_kgsq22026earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 6; EX-99.1_2026-05-11_kgsq12026earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 3

The fifth strength is low maintenance capital need. Maintenance spending was USD 76.0 million in 2025, 10.63% of adjusted EBITDA, and guidance of USD 80 million to USD 90 million for 2026 is 9.30% to 10.84%, so nearly all other spending is growth that can be slowed. That is the main lever for protecting leverage in a downturn, although USD 2.5 billion of equipment orders limit how fast it can be used.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 77; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_kgsq22026earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 29; calculation C11

Guided growth and other spending for 2026 exceeds guided discretionary cash flow by USD 125 million to USD 235 million, and issuer-defined free cash flow was negative USD 87.5 million in the second quarter of 2026. Because dividends continue, the gap is met from cash and then the revolver, so debt rises while power earnings are still ramping.

### Business profile and credit linkage

Kodiak Gas Services, Inc. provides large-horsepower contract compression in the United States, centred on the Permian Basin, and since April 1, 2026 has also leased distributed and behind-the-meter power generation after acquiring Distributed Power Solutions. The reporting entity is Kodiak Gas Services, Inc. on a consolidated basis; its senior notes are issued by the operating subsidiary Kodiak Gas Services, LLC and guaranteed by the parent and certain subsidiaries that the filings do not name, so every figure here is consolidated and there is no separate subsidiary perimeter to isolate.

### Macro, regulation and technology

Three factors matter to credit and one does not. Regulation and emissions standards favour modern large units, and electric motor drive is being deployed under long fixed-revenue contracts with select customers.
