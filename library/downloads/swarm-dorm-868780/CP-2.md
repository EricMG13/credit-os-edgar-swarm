## Findings

### Bottom line

Dorman is a conservatively financed auto-parts supplier whose 1.28x total debt to EBITDA, USD 965.9 million of liquidity and first maturity in 2031 give creditors a strong cushion, constrained mainly by customer concentration and off-balance-sheet receivable funding.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation P1; calculation P2

The two weak points are linked: two customers took about 40% of sales, and USD 970.9 million of receivables sit in customer-sponsored programs, almost equal to total liquidity. Earnings swing with tariff timing, so cash-flow durability is adequate rather than strong: free cash flow averaged USD 144.03 million over 2023 to 2025 against USD 28.13 million of annual note interest. The medium governance risk from Business & governance stands, and only covenant headroom and ranking depend on documents that were not supplied.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation P4; calculation P5; calculation P10; calculation P15

### Key findings

Leverage, liquidity and maturities are the strongest part of the credit. Total debt of USD 440.7 million is 1.28x last-twelve-months EBITDA and net debt of USD 273.7 million is 0.79x; EBITDA is derived because the issuer reports none, and excluding the USD 56.7 million Heavy Duty goodwill write-off leverage is 1.10x. Liquidity is USD 965.9 million, being cash of USD 167.0 million and USD 798.9 million of undrawn revolver, and the first maturity is the revolver in June 2031, followed by the USD 450.0 million notes in June 2034. The creditor consequence is that refinancing is not a near-term risk.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-26_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 26; calculation P1; calculation P2; calculation P3; calculation P22

Cash-flow durability is adequate, not strong, because free cash flow is volatile and the latest figure is flattered. Free cash flow was USD 191.6 million in 2024, USD 75.7 million in 2025 after a USD 250.4 million inventory build, and USD 269.2 million over the last twelve months, which includes USD 99.7 million of tariff refunds and a USD 177.6 million inventory release in nine months. Through the three years 2023 to 2025 it averaged USD 144.03 million, 44% of EBITDA, and covered the annual coupon of USD 28.13 million 5.12 times; excluding the refunds, last-twelve-months conversion is 49%.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, page 72; 10-Q_2026-09-26_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 28; calculation P5; calculation P6; calculation P7; calculation P8; calculation P9; calculation P22

Business diversification is the weakest dimension. Light Duty is 79.43% of sales and 91.57% of segment profit, two customers take about 40% of sales and the four largest owe 78% of receivables, so a price or volume decision by one account reaches the segment that carries the debt. Heavy Duty earns 1.35% of segment profit and Specialty Vehicle, bought for USD 509.8 million, adds little cushion.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 79, 80, 95; calculation P15

The receivable programs are a second dependency of the same size as liquidity. USD 970.9 million of receivables would otherwise be outstanding, which is 1.01 times liquidity, and the financial institutions that run the programs can modify or terminate them; counting the receivables as debt-like funding lifts leverage to 3.57x. The programs also cost USD 50.7 million over the last twelve months, 1.80 times the annual note coupon, so they are a larger financing cost than the notes.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-Q_2026-09-26_dorm.htm.jsonl, page 26; calculation P10; calculation P11; calculation P12; calculation P22

Margin stability is average because tariff timing moves earnings by several percentage points. The Light Duty segment margin rose from 12.80% in 2023 to 20.53% in 2025 and was 18.5% in the third quarter of 2026 against 23.7% a year earlier, while the group gross margin fell by 500 bp to 39.4%, because tariff costs are capitalised in inventory and price rises lead cost recognition. Earnings therefore depend on how fast the company passes tariff costs to customers who press for lower prices.

Refinancing capacity is strong but its documents are not in the set. Ranking, the security of the notes and covenant headroom are not established, so the view rests on maturity dates, liquidity and leverage.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a strong cushion is that the headline ratios are flattered and part of the liquidity rests on programs that third-party institutions run for customers. EBITDA is derived, the latest free cash flow includes tariff refunds and an inventory release, the receivable programs equal liquidity, and the revolver's availability of USD 798.9 million is only as good as the borrowing conditions and covenants that were not supplied. Repurchases at the 2026 pace would also use most of the underlying free cash flow, which is why the financial policy dimension is only average.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation P2; calculation P8; calculation P10; calculation P19; Source readiness (CP-0)

Evidence the other way is that every test applied here leaves headroom: leverage stays near 2x even with the 2023 margin, coupon cover is 7.61 times in that case, the first maturity is 2031 and capital spending is only 1.78% of net sales. The view would weaken if gross margin fell below 39.4% for a second quarter, if the revolver were drawn to fund repurchases or a large acquisition, if either large customer cut orders, or if receivables sold fell sharply. It would strengthen if the executed documents show wide covenant headroom and a bar on large distributions, or if the customer base broadened.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation P17; calculation P18

### Company description

Dorman supplies replacement and upgrade parts to the motor vehicle aftermarket and sells mostly through retailers, dealers and distributors in North America. About 76% of 2025 products carried owned brands, most light-duty products are made by third parties and most specialty vehicle products are made in the company's own plants, so the company carries design and sourcing risk more than factory risk. Most sales are repair parts, which the company describes as less cyclical, and demand follows vehicle age and use: vehicles in operation reached 302.7 million with an average age of 12.9 years. For creditors this means demand is steady but visibility is short, because customer agreements typically carry no purchase commitment and revenue is recognised on purchase orders of one year or less.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 7, 12, 20, 93; 10-Q_2026-09-26_dorm.htm.jsonl, page 16

### Ownership and group structure

Dorman is a public company with no sponsor or parent. The Chairman's family held about 13% of the common stock in February 2026, down from about 16% at the end of 2023, and it sells under planned sale programs, so there is no fund exit clock and no extraction is shown in the documents. The borrower group is the consolidated company: the revolver is guaranteed and secured by the material wholly owned domestic subsidiaries and the senior notes carry the same subsidiary guarantees, with the notes' security and the guarantor names not established. Financial policy shows no dividend, USD 113.8 million of repurchases in nine months 2026 and a stated appetite for acquisitions, with a leverage covenant that allows 4.00x for four quarters after certain acquisitions. Related-party rent of USD 3.59 million in 2025 is small. Business & governance rates sponsor and governance risk medium.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-26_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 16, 26

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is transactional, concentrated and partly price-led. Net sales rose 6.03% in 2025 on tariff-related pricing, early-year demand and new products, and 2.68% in nine months 2026 on pricing partly offset by lower volume, while third-quarter growth of 3.3% came from Light Duty volume and business wins net of price decreases in some categories. The company has some pass-through, because it raised prices as tariffs rose, but the pass-through lags cost recognition and its large customers push for lower prices, longer terms and higher rebates, with customer credit provisions of USD 404.2 million in 2025. The credit consequence is that revenue is predictable at the level of the repair category and unpredictable at the level of price and account.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 22, 59, 60; 10-Q_2026-09-26_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 21, 22, 23; calculation P20

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins can be defended only as fast as tariff costs are passed on. Gross margin was 42.14% in 2025 and fell 500 bp to 39.4% in the third quarter of 2026 as higher-cost inventory reached cost of goods; about 77% of purchases come from outside the United States and about 38% from China, and tariffs are capitalised in inventory, which delays the hit. Selling, general and administrative expense is 25.42% of sales including factoring costs of 2.74% of sales, which are priced off Term SOFR, so operating leverage is moderate and a fall in sales would not be offset by cost cuts quickly. The company's levers are price increases, supplier diversification and automation, which it describes as mitigation without a stated size.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 57, 58; 10-Q_2026-09-26_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 20, 21; calculation P17

### Capital intensity and FCF conversion

Capital intensity is low and conversion is driven by working capital. Capital spending was 1.78% of net sales in 2025, operating leases call for USD 30.0 million of payments within twelve months, cash taxes were USD 68.6 million in 2025 and cash interest USD 27.4 million, which will rise to the note coupon of USD 28.13 million. ### Qualitative downside scenario

The scenario starts with a tariff increase that is not passed through quickly, together with a repricing demand from a large Light Duty customer. The first operational break is gross margin: it already fell by 500 bp in the third quarter of 2026, and if the Light Duty margin returned to its 2023 level of 12.80% segment profit would fall by USD 130.74 million. EBITDA would then be USD 214.05 million, total debt to EBITDA 2.06x and net debt to EBITDA 1.28x, with the coupon covered 7.61 times, so leverage stays below the 2.14x of fiscal 2023. Free cash flow would fall, as it did to USD 75.7 million in 2025 when inventory absorbed cash, and the pressure would then fall on funding: if the institutions that run the receivable programs curtailed them, USD 970.9 million of receivables would return to the balance sheet against liquidity of USD 965.9 million. Management levers are stopping repurchases, which were USD 113.8 million in nine months 2026, releasing inventory, as USD 177.6 million was released in 2026, raising prices and cutting capital spending, which is only 1.78% of sales.
