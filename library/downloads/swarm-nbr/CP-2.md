## Findings

### Bottom line

Nabors presents an adequate but only partly assessable credit profile: scale, an undrawn revolver and no maturity before 2029 support creditors, while thin free cash flow, one-partner concentration and unestablished note priority constrain it.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F1; calculation F7

At 2026-09-30 total debt was 2.26x and net debt 1.73x last-twelve-months adjusted EBITDA of USD 893.57 million, liquidity was USD 825.07 million including cash held in the SANAD joint venture, and issuer-defined free cash flow over the last four quarters was only USD 170.44 million. The primary downside path is a cut in Saudi Arabian activity, which would reach revenue, joint venture cash and covenants together.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F1; calculation F2; calculation F4; calculation F6

A firmer opinion needs the indentures, the credit agreement, the proxy statement and the joint venture agreement, none of which is supplied.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a stronger credit is the pace of operating improvement and the deferral of maturities: quarterly adjusted EBITDA rose from USD 221.56 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 to USD 245.54 million in the third quarter of 2026, management guides about USD 930.00 million for 2026 and an annualised run rate near USD 1 billion going into 2027, no principal falls due before 2029, and the interest coverage ratio reported to lenders rose from 4.16x to 5.20x. The strongest evidence for a weaker credit is that net leverage has not fallen, free cash flow is small after growth capital spending, and about half of revenue is international with one counterparty at its centre. A fourth quarter at or above the guided adjusted free cash flow of USD 60 million to USD 70 million, a fall in capital spending after the newbuild program, and a renewed receivables purchase agreement would support the stronger reading; further SANAD rig suspensions, a Saudi payment delay, or a refinancing of the 2029 notes that needs a higher coupon would support the weaker one.

### Company description

Nabors is a Bermuda holding company that owns one of the largest land drilling fleets and offshore platform rigs, sells drilling technology and tubular running services, and makes rig equipment; its borrower is Nabors Industries, Inc. Revenue of USD 3,268.07 million over the last twelve months comes from four segments, International Drilling is the largest at 53.05% of nine-month 2026 revenue, and 58.88% of FY2025 revenue arose outside the United States. Daywork contracts at a dayrate dominate, so cash flow follows rig count and daily margin, not volume commitments; this gives visibility only for the length of a term contract, which runs from six months to five years, and no backlog is supplied.

History matters for the credit: Parker was acquired for shares on 2025-03-11 and Quail Tools was sold on 2025-08-20, so the earnings base after the sale is smaller than FY2025 and debt was reduced afterwards; the Quail Tools gain of USD 413.96 million and the Parker gain of USD 113.65 million inflate reported FY2025 EBITDA but not adjusted EBITDA.

### Ownership and group structure

Nabors is a public company with no controlling holder identified: officers, directors and holders of 5% or more together held about 4.49% of shares at 2025-06-30, former Parker holders vote with the Board under lock-up agreements, and no sponsor fee, recapitalisation or common dividend is stated. The borrower and note issuer is a wholly owned subsidiary, the notes are guaranteed by Nabors and subsidiaries that are not listed, and the SANAD joint venture is half owned by Saudi Aramco and fully consolidated, so the borrower's creditors reach SANAD cash only through what the venture distributes after meeting its own liabilities, while its partner holds USD 502.72 million of redeemable interests.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_nbr-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 48; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nbr-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, page 13; calculation F14

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is cyclical, partly concentrated and pricing-sensitive: operators award most work by bid, competition is price-led with excess rig supply historically, and US operators have kept activity flat while gaining drilling efficiency, so rig counts need not rise with the oil price. Against that, leading-edge Lower 48 pricing is rising, average rigs working rose from the second quarter to the third quarter of 2026, and International Drilling's daily adjusted gross margin rose to USD 19,060, so margin per rig day, not rig count, is the near-term driver. Saudi Aramco's per-rig contracts at about 30% of FY2025 revenue make Saudi activity the single largest revenue sensitivity.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

The cost base is heavy in assets and variable in direct costs: direct costs were 62.24% of nine-month 2026 revenue, depreciation and amortization 19.29% and general and administrative 8.90%, so a revenue decline falls through to EBITDA quickly while depreciation does not flex. Adjusted EBITDA margin moved from 27.78% to 26.14%, 27.20% and 28.15% over the last four quarters, which shows margins can be defended when rig activity and margin per day rise together; resilience to a fall in activity is not tested because no downturn quarter is in the window.

### Porter's five forces

Rivalry is intense and sets pricing: the annual report names Helmerich & Payne, Patterson-UTI, Precision and Ensign as rivals and says contracts are mostly bid, so pricing power is limited to periods of tight supply. New entry is limited by the capital needed for modern rigs, but rigs move between regions, so excess capacity can be imported; substitution takes the form of efficiency and automation, which lower the number of rigs needed per unit of output. Buyers are large and informed, and Saudi Aramco is both buyer and joint venture owner, which gives it more leverage than a normal customer; supplier power is low for rig equipment because Nabors makes its own through Canrig.

### PEST analysis

Interest rates and oil prices set customers' spending; social and technology factors are secondary to credit, apart from automation that lowers rigs needed.

### Downside scenario

The issuer-specific downside begins with a cut in Saudi Arabian activity: the Middle East conflict has already led operators to issue suspension notices and five SANAD rigs are suspended, so a wider cut would first lower rig days and daily margin in International Drilling, 53.05% of revenue. Because direct costs are 62.24% of revenue and depreciation does not flex, EBITDA would fall faster than revenue; free cash flow, already thin at USD 38.65 million over nine months, would turn negative unless capital spending falls, and receivables from Saudi Arabia, 41% of the balance, would rise as payment slows. Leverage would rise toward the covenant tests, whose definitions are not supplied, but no maturity falls due before 2029, so resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress within twelve months and the first observable triggers are further suspensions, Saudi collections and fourth-quarter free cash flow.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Nabors presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, technology breadth, an undrawn revolver and no maturity before 2029, but credit quality is constrained by thin free cash flow, concentration in Saudi Aramco and the SANAD joint venture, and note priority that is not established. Cash-flow durability appears weak to adequate because issuer-defined free cash flow was USD 170.44 million over four quarters. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on holding adjusted EBITDA near USD 930.00 million, cutting growth capital spending after the newbuild program, and renewing the receivables programme.
