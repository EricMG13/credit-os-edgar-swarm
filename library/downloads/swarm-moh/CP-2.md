## Findings

### Bottom line

Molina's fundamental credit is weak at present because earnings no longer cover its leverage: last-twelve-months EBITDA of USD 333 million against USD 3,771 million of parent notes is 11.32x, though near-term liquidity and a first maturity in 2028 give noteholders time.
The main downside path is medical cost trend outrunning premium rates while membership shrinks: an increase of 0.80 percentage points in the medical care ratio, with premiums unchanged, would remove all last-twelve-months EBITDA, and the USD 352 million of favourable reserve development that helped 2026 cannot be assumed to recur.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C5; calculation C6

Support comes from USD 8,579 million of unrestricted cash and investments, an undrawn USD 1,250 million revolver, no maturity before June 2028 and suspended buybacks, but only USD 608 million sits at the parent that owes the notes, and about USD 170 million of subsidiary dividends can be paid without regulatory approval.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_moh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 39; calculation C3; calculation C9

### Key findings

Earnings, not debt, are the weak link. EBITDA built by Financial foundation fell from USD 1,893 million in FY2024 to USD 976 million in FY2025 and USD 333 million over the last twelve months, while revenue was USD 40,650 million, USD 45,426 million and USD 44,127 million, so the damage is margin. The medical care ratio rose to 92.1% for the nine months of 2026 from 90.8%, the Medicaid ratio to 92.6% from 91.2%, and on current-year costs alone to 93.22% from 91.15%, because USD 352 million of favourable reserve development flattered the reported figure. State and federal premium rates are set annually, so the company cannot reprice quickly, and each percentage point of the ratio is worth USD 415.88 million of EBITDA.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_moh-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 32–33; calculation C1; calculation C5; calculation C6

Liquidity buys time but is held in the wrong place. Unrestricted cash and investments of USD 8,579 million exceed claims and government settlements owed of USD 6,129 million by only USD 2,450 million, and most of it sits in regulated plans; the parent held USD 608 million, which is USD 3,163 million less than the debt it owes. Dividends of about USD 170 million can be paid without approval, which is 88.83% of the annual coupons on the notes, so servicing them relies on regulators approving more. Net cash passed up to the parent covered its interest expense 2.84x in FY2025 but 1.24x in the first nine months of 2026.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_moh-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 36, 39; calculation C3; calculation C9; calculation C10

Refinancing is a 2028 question, not a 2026 one, but replacement debt would cost more. The first maturity is USD 800 million on 2028-06-15, followed by USD 650 million in 2030 and USD 2,350 million across 2031 to 2033, and the USD 1,250 million revolver is undrawn to 2030-11-20. The issuer-stated fair value of the notes is 93.84% of principal, and 86.93% for the 2032 notes; S&P rates them BB- and Moody's Ba2; replacing the 2028 notes at the coupon carried by the 2031 notes would add USD 17.00 million of annual interest.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_moh-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_moh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-K_2025-12-31_moh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 94; calculation C11; calculation C12; calculation C13

The business model concentrates risk in one program and four states. Medicaid was 74.89% of premium revenue and the Texas, California, Washington and New York contracts together 40.23%; state contracts run three to five years, can be ended by either party and are rebid, and membership at 2026-09-30 was 13.61% below a year earlier, with Marketplace down 59.24% since year-end. Management expects the federal budget law to cut premium by 2% to 3% a year through 2029, and the premium it states for the new Florida contract is hard to reconcile with its enrolment, so replacement revenue is uncertain.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_moh-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 14–16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_moh-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 28–31; calculation C8

Financial policy is a medium governance risk rather than a rating driver on its own. There is no sponsor and no dividend; the parent bought back USD 1,000 million of stock in each of 2024 and 2025, 211.86% of FY2025 net income in the latest year, in a year when it also borrowed USD 1,100 million, and nothing in 2026. The covenant interest cover test was lowered temporarily to 1.75x on 2026-02-04 for reasons not given, and headroom cannot be measured because the agreement is not supplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_moh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 132; 10-K_2025-12-31_moh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 135; calculation C15

Cash generation is a working-capital story, not an earnings story. Operating cash flow was an outflow of USD 535 million in FY2025 and an inflow of USD 629 million in the first nine months of 2026, of which USD 297 million came from working-capital movements; by quarter it was USD 1,082 million, then outflows of USD 294 million and USD 159 million. Capital spending of USD 101 million is small, so free cash flow follows the timing of settlements with governments rather than earnings.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_moh-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4; EX-99.1_2026-04-22_moh1q26_er.htm.jsonl, page 5; EX-99.1_2026-07-22_moh2q26_er.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C4

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a weak assessment is that the worst quarter is leaving the window. EBITDA for the fourth quarter of 2025 was an outflow of USD 118 million against USD 144 million in the third quarter of 2026, and 2026 guidance implies fourth-quarter net income of roughly USD 10 million, so last-twelve-months EBITDA should rise at the next report even though the new Florida contract is guided to cost USD 1.50 per share in that quarter. Before the USD 93 million impairment, last-twelve-months EBITDA would have been USD 426 million and EBITDA over accrued interest 2.00x. Management says medical cost trend appears to be plateauing and 2027 rate updates are strong, which is its own view and untested here. Nothing matures before 2028 and the revolver is undrawn.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-10-21_moh3q26_er.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2; EX-99.1_2026-10-21_moh3q26_er.htm.jsonl, page 9; calculation C14

The view would improve if the medical care ratio returns toward its FY2025 level of 91.7% without reserve releases, if parent cash keeps rising from USD 608 million, or if the credit agreement shows wide headroom under the interest cover test. It would worsen if the ratio keeps rising, if the Florida start-up costs more than guided, if buybacks resume under the USD 500 million authority that lapses on 2026-12-31, or if a large-state contract is lost.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_moh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40; calculation C3; calculation C15

### Business and ownership

Molina is a pure-play manager of government-funded health plans that serves 21 states, so its cash flow depends on state and federal budgets rather than on consumers. Medicaid is 74.89% of premium revenue, Medicare 14.48% and Marketplace 10.42% in FY2025, and Medicaid has risen to 78.30% of premium for the nine months of 2026 as Marketplace shrinks. Contracts are short and terminable, which gives visibility of a few years at most and exposes revenue to rebids: the Virginia contracts ended on 2025-06-30. The consequence for creditors is that debt service capacity is only as durable as the next procurement cycle.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_moh-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 14–16; calculation C8

The issuer is a listed public corporation with no sponsor and no controlling holder disclosed; the notes are obligations of the parent only, with no subsidiary guarantor named, and the regulated subsidiaries must keep minimum capital of about USD 3.0 billion. That structure removes sponsor extraction risk but also any sponsor backstop, and it makes noteholders structurally dependent on subsidiary dividends.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_moh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_moh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 39

### Revenue, margin and cash conversion

Revenue is visible only through contract terms. Premiums are fixed per member and actuarially sound by requirement, but they are re-set annually while cost trend moves continuously, which is why the ratio rose even though premium revenue for Medicaid was unchanged for the nine months. Membership is falling (down 11.46% since year-end), so premium revenue fell 4% in the third quarter.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_moh-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 24, 33; calculation C8

Margins cannot be defended by cost cutting alone. Medical care costs are about 92% of premium and the G&A ratio rose to 6.8% for the nine months from 6.5% as premium fell, so operating leverage is adverse: a change of less than one percentage point in the medical care ratio equals all of last-twelve-months EBITDA. The company holds some levers, notably suspending buybacks, exiting the MAPD product and repricing Marketplace, but each shrinks revenue.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_moh-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 24, 26, 30; calculation C5; calculation C6

Capital intensity is low (capital spending of USD 101 million in FY2025), so EBITDA converts to cash easily in a normal year, but the conversion has been negative in the worst periods because government settlements, tax payments and claims timing swing operating cash flow by hundreds of millions. FY2025 operating cash flow was an outflow of USD 535 million on EBITDA of USD 976 million.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C4

### Market forces

Buyer power is the dominant force: the buyers are state agencies and the federal government, which set rates and rebid contracts, so Molina has little pricing power and its margin depends on rate adequacy. Supplier power sits with providers and drug suppliers, where outpatient and high-cost drug categories drove the Medicaid ratio up. Rivalry takes the form of procurement competition and sector consolidation, and entry barriers are regulatory and procurement-based, so new entrants are a limited threat. Policy is the primary external driver: the federal budget law is expected to cut Medicaid enrolment gradually from 2027 and reduce premium by 2% to 3% a year through 2029, and Marketplace rule changes have shrunk that book. For creditors the sector translates into a slow-moving but one-way risk to revenue and a rate-lag risk to margin.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_moh-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_moh-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 28, 33; calculation C8

In credit terms the strengths are scale, low capital intensity, a maturity profile with nothing due before 2028 and a record of winning state contracts; the weaknesses are thin margins, parent dependence on regulated subsidiaries, state concentration and shrinking membership; the opportunities that matter are rate updates that catch up with cost trend and new contracts in Florida, Illinois and Wisconsin; the threats are further rate lag, loss of a large-state contract and policy-driven enrolment loss.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Outlook

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months the revenue base is expected to shrink further (Medicaid to about 4.4 million members by year-end excluding the new Florida enrolment, Marketplace to about 250,000, Medicare to about 230,000) while new contracts start between 2026-10-01 and 2027-01-01, and the first-quarter 2026 impairment and the fourth-quarter 2025 loss drop out of the reported figures. Over three to five years the federal budget law reduces Medicaid expansion enrolment gradually and limits provider tax financing from 2028, and management targets premium and earnings growth of 11% to 13% that is not supported by figures in the documents. The credit implication is that deleveraging depends on margin recovery rather than debt paydown, because nothing matures before 2028 and the notes are not prepayable from free cash flow at this scale.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_moh-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 28–30; 10-K_2025-12-31_moh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10; calculation C14

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is that state rate updates for 2027 do not keep pace with medical cost trend while the favourable prior-year reserve development seen in 2026 does not repeat. The first operational break is the medical care ratio: without the USD 352 million of reserve release the nine-month EBITDA would have been USD 99 million and last-twelve-months EBITDA an outflow of USD 19 million, before any offset from minimum medical loss ratio rules and cost corridors. That feeds through subsidiary earnings to dividends: about USD 170 million can be paid without approval against annual coupons that are higher, so the parent would draw on its USD 608 million of cash, which covers about 33.99 months of accrued interest at the 2026 pace. Leverage would be undefined on a negative EBITDA and the interest cover test, already cut to 1.75x, would be the observable trigger; if it were breached the undrawn revolver could become unavailable and a covenant default under it could in turn engage the cross-default terms of the notes, although the filings do not give the definitions or thresholds. Management levers are suspending buybacks, repricing or exiting products, and capital moves between subsidiaries. Resilience looks sufficient to avoid a liquidity event in the next twelve months, because of the USD 8,579 million of unrestricted cash and investments and no maturity before 2028, but not sufficient to hold its current credit quality if the ratio keeps rising.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_moh-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_moh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 39; calculation C7; calculation C16

### Strengths and weaknesses

The strengths are a maturity profile with nothing due before 2028 and an undrawn revolver, which means a refinancing event is not the near-term test; low capital intensity, with capital spending of USD 101 million against USD 45,426 million of revenue; the suspension of buybacks and the rise in parent cash to USD 608 million; a record of renewing and winning state contracts, in management's words; and audited, transparent reporting that lets creditors see where the weakness is.
The weaknesses are margins that depend on a ratio the company does not set and that has moved from 90.8% to 92.1% in a year; leverage of 11.32x on last-twelve-months EBITDA with accrued interest cover of 1.56x; concentration, with Medicaid at 74.89% and four states at 40.23% of premium revenue; a parent that relies on subsidiary dividends and holds a small part of group cash; and shrinking membership with policy headwinds through 2029.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C2; calculation C8; calculation C9

### Overall credit view

Overall, Molina presents a business profile of adequate scale supported by contract-based government revenue, a maturity profile with no principal due before 2028 and low capital needs, but credit quality is constrained by margins that have collapsed with the medical care ratio, concentration in Medicaid and four states, and a parent that owes all the debt while most cash sits in regulated plans. Cash-flow durability appears weak at present because operating cash flow swings with settlement timing and reported earnings include reserve releases. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on whether the medical care ratio recovers before 2028 and on the coupon needed to replace notes now carrying low rates. The primary downside path is medical costs outrunning premium rates while membership falls. Further analysis would require the credit agreement definitions, the proxy statement, market prices of the notes and management's 2027 plan. Fundamental credit review completed with limitations. Top risk: the medical care ratio.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0); calculation C2; calculation C6
