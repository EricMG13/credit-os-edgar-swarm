## Findings

### Bottom line

Creditors rest on a stable but slowly shrinking mail and shipping business with no controlling owner named in the filings and with shareholder returns running ahead of debt repayment, so protection comes from covenants and management's continued paydown, not owner support. In the first nine months of 2026 buybacks and dividends were USD 246.32 million against USD 107.78 million of debt principal repaid, and in 2025 they were USD 429.42 million against operating cash flow of USD 383.26 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 67; calculation A09; calculation A10.

Governance risk is rated medium: the documents supplied show no sponsor fees, related-party payments or creditor-adverse transactions, but a board-led shift to buybacks, a new executive team and unexplained strategic review costs leave the direction of capital allocation to board discretion. The proxy statement and the executed debt documents are not supplied, so board independence, the large holders and the limits on distributions are not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 54; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 99–100; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 31; Source readiness (CP-0).

### Key findings

Two profit pools are moving in opposite directions, so group earnings are held up by SendTech cost cuts while Presort loses margin. SendTech adjusted segment EBIT rose 17.34% in the first nine months of 2026 on revenue that fell 1.06%, helped by lower selling, general and administrative expense and a USD 5 million tariff refund, and its margin reached 37.84%. Presort adjusted segment EBIT fell 26.19% as its margin dropped from 25.86% to 19.92% on lower volumes, price actions and higher transport and fuel costs. SendTech now supplies 79.41% of segment EBIT against 70.82% a year earlier, which concentrates creditor reliance on a business whose meter population and lease portfolio are shrinking.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 30–31; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; calculation A02; calculation A04; calculation A05; calculation A06.

Both segments lean on the same postal system, so a rule, rate or volume shock would hit both profit pools together rather than diversify risk. The annual report says a significant portion of revenue depends on the USPS and other national posts, that postal authorities regulate and must approve many offerings, and that physical mail volumes are in continuing decline. The issuer describes Presort as the largest USPS workshare partner and the United States earned 84.27% of 2025 revenue. No client exceeded 10% of net sales in 2025 or 2024, which limits client concentration, but the business relies in places on single-source suppliers and on third-party transport.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 51; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 83; calculation A08.

The finance arm adds a shrinking asset base and a deposit float, so a quarter of operating cash flow is a run-off release that cannot be assumed to repeat. Net finance receivables fell 9.67% to USD 995.10 million between 2025-12-31 and 2026-09-30 as the lease portfolio shrank, with clients extending leases of existing equipment instead of buying new, and cash released from receivables supplied 25.13% of nine-month operating cash flow after 27.98% in 2025. The Pitney Bowes Bank held USD 647.95 million of customer deposits, 7.94% less than at the end of 2024, and the annual report names depositor withdrawals as a liquidity risk.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation A15; calculation A16.

Control rests with a public board and its shareholders, not a sponsor, so creditors have no equity cure or sponsor support to count on and no sponsor fee leakage to price. Directors, large holders, related-party dealings and compensation are incorporated by reference to a proxy statement that is not supplied, so board independence and the holders are not established. The filings do show that funds linked to the chief executive officer hold shares and adopted a trading arrangement in November 2025 covering up to 4.00% of shares outstanding, while buybacks cut the share count by 9.60% between January and October 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 54; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 52–53; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation A12; calculation A13.

Senior leadership is new and was drawn from the board, so the capital allocation record is short and the strategy is still being set. Three of the five executive officers have held office only since 2025 and each served as a director beforehand, the chief executive officer joined the board in May 2023 and was appointed in May 2025, and the annual report names leadership change as a continuity risk. Transaction and strategic review costs totalled USD 42.25 million from 2024 to September 2026 without any description of the review, so the chance of a leveraged transaction can be neither dismissed nor sized.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10; calculation A20; calculation A21.

Shareholder returns were set above what free cash flow supported in 2025 and still absorb most operating cash flow in 2026, so deleveraging is a board choice rather than a commitment. In 2025 returns of USD 429.42 million exceeded operating cash flow less capital expenditures by USD 112.44 million, net debt rose USD 258.17 million and cash fell USD 184.84 million, and part of the net proceeds of the 2025 convertible notes was used to repurchase shares in an amount the sources do not give. In 2026 returns are 2.29x debt principal repaid, the repurchase authorisation stands at USD 750 million with USD 165.91 million left, and the quarterly dividend is USD 0.10 per share.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 67; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 100; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 38–39; calculation A09; calculation A10; calculation A11; calculation A18.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a medium risk reading is that creditors have been paid: debt principal fell USD 107.78 million in nine months, net debt fell USD 89.24 million after rising in 2025, a tender offer retired USD 46 million of notes at a discount, the margin on the 2032 term loan was cut by 75 bp, the revolver was raised to USD 450 million and no final maturity falls before March 2029. Buybacks in 2026 were covered by operating cash flow, and USD 165.91 million of authorisation remaining is small beside the debt outstanding. Evidence that would move the view toward low risk is a stated payout policy tied to net leverage, a proxy statement showing an independent board with no holder able to force a change, and disclosed covenant headroom. Evidence that would move it toward high risk is repurchases funded by revolver draws or new notes, a distribution that uses a covenant basket, an adverse ruling on the Kingsbridge claim or a leveraged transaction arising from the strategic review.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_ex991q32026earningspress.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 39; calculation A10; calculation A11; calculation A18.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

No material acquisition, sponsor buyout or change of control appears in the documents supplied; a USD 2.2 million acquisition in the first quarter of 2025 is the only one in the cash flow statements and is not described. The transactions that matter to creditors are the August 2024 ecommerce exit, the 2025 refinancing and convertible issue, and the 2026 note add-on, term loan extension and tender offer.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 67; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 85.

In August 2024 the issuer sold 81% of the voting interests of its ecommerce logistics subsidiary to an affiliate of Hilco Commercial Industrial for de minimis consideration and kept 19% of the votes and all of the economic interest; the subsidiary then filed for Chapter 11 and its liquidation plan was confirmed on November 25, 2024.
