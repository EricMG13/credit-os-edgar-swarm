## Findings

### Bottom line

Creditors should treat B&G Foods as a shrinking-sales, customer-concentrated branded-food credit that is reshaping its portfolio and has accepted a much higher coupon to move its nearest bond maturity to September 2028, with no identified sponsor to support or drain it.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-03_bgs_bgs-20261003x10q.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-10-03_bgs_bgs-20261003x10q.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-K_2026-01-03_bgs_bgs-20260103x10k.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-10-03_bgs_bgs-20261003x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 34–35; 10-K_2026-01-03_bgs_bgs-20260103x10k.htm.jsonl, pages 81–82; 10-Q_2026-10-03_bgs_bgs-20261003x10q.htm.jsonl, page 60; 10-K_2026-01-03_bgs_bgs-20260103x10k.htm.jsonl, page 150

The portfolio changes since 2025 trade a lower-margin frozen business and small shelf-stable brands for higher-margin broth brands, and the board halved the dividend rate, which reduces cash leakage. The open questions are who leads the company after the chief executive's retirement in August 2026 and who holds the shares, because the successor and the proxy statement are not in the supplied documents.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-03_bgs_bgs-20261003x10q.htm.jsonl, page 58; 10-Q_2026-10-03_bgs_bgs-20261003x10q.htm.jsonl, page 69; 10-Q_2026-07-04_bgs_bgs-20260704x10q.htm.jsonl, page 36; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

- Customer concentration is the operating fact that matters most for cash-flow stability: one retailer, Walmart, took 31.0% of fiscal 2025 net sales and the ten largest customers took 63.6%, so one negotiation on price, promotions or shelf space can move a large part of earnings. The retailer's share eased to 28.9% in the first three quarters of 2026, and it held about the same share of trade receivables at the quarter end.

- The sales base is shrinking through disposals while the underlying brands have stopped declining. Net sales fell 5.4% in fiscal 2025 and 2.9% in the first three quarters of 2026, but base business net sales, which exclude acquired and divested brands, rose 1.8% in 2026 after falling 4.0% in fiscal 2025, helped by volume growth of 1.4%. Creditors are relying on mix and cost recovery, not growth, for cash flow.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious reading is that the underlying business has stopped shrinking and is earning more on what is left. Base business net sales rose 1.8% in the first three quarters of 2026 on volume growth of 1.4%, adjusted gross profit was 22.3% of net sales against 21.6% a year earlier as higher-margin broth brands joined, a lower-margin frozen business left and tariff expense fell, and the issuer reports compliance with every covenant at the latest quarter end. The 2031 notes also remove the 2027 maturity, so no bond matures before September 2028, and the halved dividend keeps roughly $29.8 million a year inside the business.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-03_bgs_bgs-20261003x10q.htm.jsonl, page 60; 10-Q_2026-10-03_bgs_bgs-20261003x10q.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-Q_2026-10-03_bgs_bgs-20261003x10q.htm.jsonl, page 24; calculation A8

Three developments would turn the view more constructive: a named chief executive with a stated plan for the disposal programme, a completed sale of the Green Giant Canada business with proceeds applied to the revolver, and a proxy statement showing a large holder committed to the equity. Three would turn it more negative: base business net sales falling again, a further failed or discounted disposal, and a change in terms from the largest customer. A covenant ratio close to its limit would also change the view, but the supplied filings do not print the ratios.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-03_bgs_bgs-20261003x10q.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-Q_2026-10-03_bgs_bgs-20261003x10q.htm.jsonl, page 21; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Limitations

- The conclusion that no sponsor is present rests on the absence of a named large holder in the supplied filings; the proxy statement and any ownership filings were not supplied, so a controlling or activist holder could exist and would change the ownership reading from dispersed to concentrated.
  Basis: 10-K_2026-01-03_bgs_bgs-20260103x10k.htm.jsonl, page 150
- The leadership finding covers only the retirement terms: the successor, any interim arrangement and any change of plan are not stated in the supplied text, so the execution risk could be higher or lower than described.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-04_bgs_bgs-20260704x10q.htm.jsonl, page 36
- The transaction findings rest on the issuer's own summaries; the purchase agreements, the indentures and the credit agreement were not supplied, so what the issuer may still sell, pay out or borrow is not established and the disposal and distribution conclusions are directional.
- Customer concentration is sized by share of sales and receivables only; contract terms, rebates and promotion commitments with the largest customer are not disclosed, so the severity of the single-buyer risk cannot be quantified.
- The latest quarterly filing is eight days before the as-of date, so a later sale, financing or management announcement would change the timeline and the transaction findings.
- The margin comparison in the contrary evidence uses the issuer's own adjusted measure, which is not audited, so the improvement is directional rather than exact.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-03_bgs_bgs-20261003x10q.htm.jsonl, page 60

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Since May 2025 the issuer has sold four brand groups and bought one. The Don Pepino and Sclafani brands (May 23, 2025, $10.6 million), the Le Sueur brand in the United States (August 1, 2025, $59.1 million), the Green Giant frozen business in the United States (March 2, 2026, about $61.5 million, with a continuing co-manufacturing agreement) and the B&M and Joan of Arc brands (October 13, 2026, $8.0 million, after the quarter end) were sold. The College Inn and Kitchen Basics broth brands were bought on March 19, 2026 for $109.7 million in cash, after an agreement dated January 15, 2026. The issuer says the purpose is to sharpen focus, improve margins and reduce long-term debt.
