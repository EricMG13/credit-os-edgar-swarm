## Findings

### Bottom line

Valvoline's credit profile is adequate: a cash-generative maintenance-service business now carries more debt than management's own ceiling allows, so creditors depend on free cash flow to reduce leverage rather than on any cushion in the capital structure.
Basis: calculation F6; calculation F4; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Net debt of USD 1,517.8 million is 2.79x Adjusted EBITDA against a stated ceiling of 2.5x, but liquidity of USD 554.3 million, nine-month free cash flow of USD 112.3 million and growth capital expenditure of USD 129.0 million that can be deferred make near-term liquidity distress unlikely on the evidence supplied. The primary path to deterioration is a lubricant cost squeeze or a fall in maintenance demand that arrives while the control weakness, the unknown maturity of the term loans and a floating-rate share of 67.00% remain unresolved.
Basis: calculation F6; calculation F8; calculation F10; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 19, 29–30

### Key findings

Leverage is the weakest dimension, and the credit case rests on reducing it. Net debt of USD 1,517.8 million is 2.79x Adjusted EBITDA, 0.29x above management's ceiling of 2.5x, and reported EBITDA gives 3.61x because it carries a USD 57.9 million loss on the forced sale of 45 acquired stores. Net debt is USD 125.0 million below the USD 1,642.8 million pro forma figure after the three quantified events that followed the fiscal 2025 balance sheet, so deleveraging has started; earnings include the acquired business for only seven of the twelve months, so the ratio overstates leverage by an amount the filings do not let us measure.
Basis: calculation F6; calculation F15; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 8–11, 26, 30

Cash generation is improving but thin against the debt, and it depends on discretionary capital spending. Free cash flow of USD 140.5 million over the last twelve months converts 25.85% of Adjusted EBITDA and equals 8.77% of total debt, against 10.26% and 4.46% in fiscal 2025, and net debt is 10.80x last-twelve-months free cash flow. Capital expenditure absorbs 65.87% of operating cash flow, but 74.87% of nine-month capital spending is growth spending that management can defer, so free cash flow is closer to a policy choice than to a fixed charge.
Basis: calculation F4; calculation F5; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 29

Margins have held, but the cost pressure has not yet fully arrived. Adjusted EBITDA margin was 27.34% in fiscal 2024, 27.29% in fiscal 2025 and 27.67% on the last twelve months, and gross margin fell by 1.00 percentage points to 39.46% in the third quarter of fiscal 2026, almost all of it in other service delivery costs, while the materials ratio was flat at 16.53% and management says lubricant costs rose through the quarter and will stay elevated. Franchisee product prices generally follow base oil indices, while company-operated store prices are set by management, so a pricing lag would land on company-operated margin first: 1.00 percentage points of margin on last-twelve-months revenue is USD 19.64 million of Adjusted EBITDA and would lift net leverage to 2.90x.

Liquidity is the strongest dimension, but refinancing capacity cannot be fully assessed. Cash of USD 84.2 million and USD 470.1 million undrawn on the revolver give USD 554.3 million, 11.64 times the USD 47.6 million of installments printed for fiscal 2026 and fiscal 2027, while the last printed ladder shows USD 498.0 million in fiscal 2028 and USD 535.0 million after it and predates the Term Loan B. No maturity date is printed for the revolver or the term loans, and 67.00% of principal floats with no interest rate swaps since they matured in fiscal 2024, so a rise in base rates of 1.00 percentage points adds USD 10.86 million of annual interest.

No sponsor or controlling holder is named, so no extraction channel is evidenced, but USD 325.0 million of repurchase authorization remains and the conditions for resuming it are not stated.

The filings give no market share, so the strength of the brand against dealerships and other quick-lube chains is described by management, not measured.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is that deleveraging is already visible and leverage is overstated. Nine-month Adjusted EBITDA margin was 27.37% against 26.80% a year earlier, free cash flow was USD 112.3 million against USD 19.7 million, USD 50.0 million of the Term Loan A was prepaid, and the reported ratio carries a USD 57.9 million forced-sale loss and only seven months of Breeze Autocare earnings. Reaching the 2.5x ceiling at today's earnings needs net debt USD 159.05 million lower, which compares with the USD 125.0 million of reduction since the acquisition, or Adjusted EBITDA of USD 607.12 million at today's net debt, and the filings do not quantify the acquired earnings still missing from the last twelve months.
Basis: calculation F1; calculation F4; calculation F6; calculation F15; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 12, 26

The view would improve if the fiscal 2026 annual report shows net leverage inside the 2.5x ceiling without another debt-funded acquisition, the control weakness is declared remediated, and the maturity dates show no wall inside 24 months. It would worsen if lubricant costs outrun price increases for two quarters, same-store sales growth turns negative, repurchases resume while leverage is above 2.5x, or the revolver proves to mature before the fiscal 2028 bucket.
Basis: calculation F6; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 30, 34

### Business model and credit linkage

The business model supports cash-flow visibility because revenue comes from low-ticket preventive maintenance tied to vehicle use, but it concentrates that visibility in a single service line. Oil changes and related fees were 73.60% of nine-month revenue, other maintenance services 21.26% and franchise fees 5.14%, and 96.89% of fiscal 2025 revenue came from the United States. Stores are 49.84% franchised, so about half of the network pays fees on its sales without drawing on the issuer's capital; the risk mechanic is that royalties and product margin on franchised stores depend on franchisee sales and financing, which the annual report identifies as a dependency. The credit implication is steady, fee-supported cash flow with a narrow base, and a refinancing story that leans on the record of same-store sales growth.
Basis: calculation F12; 10-K_2025-09-30_vvv-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 22–23; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16, 22

### Ownership and financial policy

Ownership is dispersed and no sponsor or controlling shareholder is named, so the credit risk is the willingness of management, not an owner's extraction. The company paid no dividend in fiscal 2024 or fiscal 2025 and does not expect to, but returned USD 1,850.2 million through repurchases and dividends across fiscal 2023 to 2025 after the Global Products sale, and then used a USD 740.0 million Term Loan B to fund a USD 637.4 million acquisition. The mechanic is that policy has moved from returning capital to adding debt for growth; the implication is that creditors should treat the paused repurchases and the lowered range of 1.5x to 2.5x as a commitment still to be proven, because USD 325.0 million of authorization remains and the resumption conditions are not set out. Guarantees of leases and franchisee debt add USD 105.8 million outside reported debt.
Basis: calculation F13; calculation F14; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 13, 29–30

### Revenue durability and pricing power

Revenue is recurring and mostly non-discretionary, but pricing power is untested at the margin. Net revenues grew 5.64% in fiscal 2025, 20.19% in the first nine months of fiscal 2026 and 24.05% in the third quarter, where USD 50.8 million of the USD 105.6 million increase came from the acquired business, while system-wide same-store sales grew 8.0% with higher volumes and favourable pricing and service mix. The mechanic is that a customer can defer an oil change in a downturn or as miles driven fall, which the annual report lists as a risk, so durability rests on the company's ability to hold volume as prices rise. The implication is that revenue is predictable in normal conditions and cyclical at the margin, with no customer concentration measured.
Basis: calculation F2; calculation F11; 10-K_2025-09-30_vvv-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 25; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 9, 18–19

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margin resilience rests on pass-through to franchisees and pricing action in company-operated stores, against a cost base of labour, materials and other service delivery costs. Labour was 24.29% of revenue and materials 17.39% in fiscal 2025, and in the third quarter of fiscal 2026 other service delivery costs rose to 20.62% of revenue from 19.68% while labour and materials were flat. The mechanic is operating leverage on store labour and a supplier whose prices follow base oil; the implication is that a sustained cost squeeze would reach Adjusted EBITDA before it reaches liquidity, and that wage legislation or tariffs, which the annual report flags, would add to it.
Basis: calculation F16; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-09-30_vvv-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 21, 26; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 19

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital intensity is high and mostly discretionary, which is the main source of flexibility in the credit. Capital expenditure was 13.81% of last-twelve-months revenue, USD 172.3 million in nine months of which USD 129.0 million was growth spending, and the company forecasts USD 250 million to USD 280 million for fiscal 2026. The mechanic is that growth spending, store development and acquisitions compete with debt reduction for the same operating cash flow. The implication is that free cash flow converts 25.85% of Adjusted EBITDA now, against 10.26% in fiscal 2025, and would rise by the amount of growth spending management chooses to defer; interim working-capital swings of USD 42.6 million in other assets and liabilities mean part of the improvement may not repeat.
Basis: calculation F3; calculation F4; calculation F5; 10-K_2025-09-30_vvv-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 56; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 28–29

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is the force that matters most for margin: the market is fragmented and Valvoline competes with dealerships, repair centers and other quick-lube chains on brand, price, speed and location, so it can pass on cost but not earn above-cost pricing everywhere. Buyer power appears high because the filings show no switching cost for a consumer and give no measure of fleet or franchisee concentration; the largest franchisees add concentration risk to the royalty stream. Supplier power is high: substantially all lubricants come from one counterparty under a long-term agreement whose terms are not supplied. Substitutes are limited today, but electric vehicles and longer drain intervals, and owners who maintain their own vehicles, reduce visits over time. New entrants face the cost of real estate and brand building, which limits their effect on credit within the horizon.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_vvv-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8, 17–18, 20, 23, 25

### Macro, regulatory and technology factors

Economic and technology factors affect credit; regulation matters mainly through the franchise and acquisition rules. A recession or higher energy prices reduce miles driven and lead customers to defer maintenance, which would lower same-store sales and operating leverage on store labour; vehicle technology and manufacturer service intervals change drain frequency over a longer horizon. Federal Trade Commission review forced the sale of 45 acquired stores at a USD 57.9 million pre-tax loss, which shows that further consolidation by acquisition is constrained in a fragmented market with limited targets remaining, and wage and tariff changes pass through to costs.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 months to 24 months the outlook is for continued revenue growth and gradual deleveraging, with margin as the swing factor. The tailwinds are same-store sales growth of 8.0%, a full year of Breeze Autocare earnings from fiscal 2027, a USD 1.85 million annual interest saving from the Term Loan B repricing, and paused repurchases; the headwinds are lubricant costs management expects to stay elevated, interest expense that rose 37.97% from fiscal 2025 to the last twelve months, and integration and control remediation work. Over three to five years the support is network growth and franchise expansion, and the pressure comes from electric vehicle adoption and longer service intervals, the supplier relationship, and the refinancing of the fiscal 2028 and fiscal 2031 buckets, which together were 95.60% of principal at September 30, 2025.

### Downside scenario

The realistic downside is a cost and demand squeeze that starts in company-operated store margin and ends in a refinancing at higher leverage. The initiating condition is that lubricant costs, which management expects to stay elevated, rise faster than prices at the same time as an economic slowdown or higher energy prices lead customers to defer oil changes. The first operational break is gross margin: the fall of 1.00 percentage points already seen in the third quarter of fiscal 2026 is worth USD 19.64 million of Adjusted EBITDA over a year of revenue, and fewer vehicles served per day would add to it through operating leverage on store labour. The financial transmission is arithmetic on net debt of USD 1,517.8 million: a 10% fall in Adjusted EBITDA lifts net leverage from 2.79x to 3.10x and a 20% fall to 3.49x, expense-based cover falls from 5.32x to 4.79x and 4.26x, and free cash flow before any tax relief falls from USD 140.5 million to USD 86.15 million and USD 31.80 million, still positive before any cut in growth spending.
Basis: calculation F7; calculation F4; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 19

Liquidity holds under this path: USD 554.3 million of liquidity compares with USD 47.6 million of installments printed for fiscal 2026 and fiscal 2027 and an undrawn revolver. The consequence arrives at refinancing, where the fiscal 2028 bucket and the revolver would be refinanced at leverage nearer 3.5x than the ceiling of 2.5x, with 67.00% of principal floating, so a rise in base rates of 1.00 percentage points adds USD 10.86 million of annual interest. Management's levers are to defer the USD 129.0 million of nine-month growth spending, keep repurchases paused, slow acquisitions and refranchise stores. They appear sufficient to avoid a liquidity shortfall over the next 12 months to 24 months but not to return leverage to the range without a recovery in earnings. The observable triggers are gross margin below 39.46% in consecutive quarters, same-store sales growth turning negative and net leverage rising above 2.79x. The sensitivities are Analyst Judgement arithmetic, not forecasts, and the covenant tests cannot be applied because their definitions are not supplied.
Basis: calculation F5; calculation F7; calculation F8; calculation F10; 10-K_2025-09-30_vvv-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 97; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 29–30

### Overall credit view

Overall, Valvoline presents an adequate business profile supported by recurring maintenance demand, a franchised half of the network and deferrable growth capital expenditure, but credit quality is constrained by net leverage of 2.79x above management's ceiling of 2.5x, concentration in one service line, one country and one supplier, and a continuing control weakness. Cash-flow durability appears adequate due to an Adjusted EBITDA margin of 27.67% that has been stable across three periods and free cash flow of USD 140.5 million over the last twelve months, which is thin against net debt at 10.80x. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the maturity dates of the revolver and term loans, on the control remediation and on whether leverage reaches the ceiling before the fiscal 2028 bucket falls due. The primary downside path is a lubricant cost squeeze or a demand slowdown that raises leverage toward 3.5x with two-thirds of the debt floating.
Basis: calculation F1; calculation F4; calculation F6; calculation F7; calculation F8

No peer data are supplied, so the business profile cannot be compared with typical leveraged-credit issuers in the sector. Further analysis would require the fiscal 2026 annual report, pro forma earnings for Breeze Autocare, the credit agreement with its covenant definitions and maturity dates, cash interest paid after the Term Loan B and a ratings view. The top risk is leverage that stays above management's range while lubricant costs outrun pricing.
