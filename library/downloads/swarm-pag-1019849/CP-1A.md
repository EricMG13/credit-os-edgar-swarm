## Findings

### Bottom line

Governance and sponsor risk is Medium: Penske Corporation and Mitsui, holders of 72.6% of the shares, propose a take-private that could add up to USD 1.8 billion of company debt to USD 2,367.2 million today. At that ceiling, debt would be 2.72x last-twelve-month EBITDA, against 1.54x now.
Basis: calculation A5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 25, 89–91; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_q326exhibit99_1.htm.jsonl, page 17

The dealership and truck businesses are diversified, the filings show no priming, exchange or other creditor-adverse step, and the U.S. credit agreement lenders consented to the deal. The risk is that the controlling holders sit on both sides of the buyout, paid USD 519.4 million to related parties for Penske Motor Group in 2025, and that the debt left at the company is not yet established; the level moves to High if most of the committed debt lands there.
Basis: calculation B1; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 149–150; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25–26, 90–91

### Key findings

- Control rests with Penske Corporation and Mitsui and is fixed by contract to March 2030, so the board's makeup is set by the same two groups that are now the buyer. The two hold 52.3% and 20.3% of the shares; under the stockholders agreement Mitsui votes for up to fourteen directors that Penske Corporation votes for, and the Chair and Chief Executive Officer holds the same posts at Penske Corporation. A voting agreement caps the discretionary votes of Penske Corporation and the Chair at 43.57% of the shares, and a special committee of independent directors negotiated the deal, with a majority of disinterested holders also required to approve it; those steps protect minority shareholders but give creditors no vote.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10, 76–77; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 48, 97–98

- The take-private is a debt-assisted buyout with the controlling holders on the buy side, and existing creditors gain no exit right. The price is USD 212.00 per share, USD 2.00 or 0.95% above the proposal made 85 days earlier, and about USD 3.8 billion is payable to the 27.4% of shares the investors do not hold; the buyer plans up to USD 2.2 billion of equity and up to USD 1.8 billion of debt. The issuer states the deal is not a change of control under the mortgage facilities, or under the 3.75% notes if technical requirements are met, so no 101% repurchase right arises, and the lenders under the U.S. credit agreement gave a limited consent and a waiver.
  Basis: calculation A1; calculation A2; calculation A4; calculation A6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 25–26, 28, 90–91

- One related-party transaction is large, and the filings do not show how it was priced or approved. In 2025 the issuer bought Penske Motor Group for USD 519.4 million from affiliates of Penske Corporation and from an entity controlled by an affiliate of the Vice Chair, paying USD 363.6 million in cash and USD 155.8 million in a 4.50% subordinated note that the issuer guarantees, and USD 401.7 million was charged to retained earnings; the price was 10.75x the business's USD 48.3 million of 2025 net income, which carried no income tax because it was a pass-through partnership. Routine fees, rent and asset sales with Penske Corporation and its affiliates were small, about USD 14.7 million in 2025.
  Basis: calculation B1; calculation B2; calculation B4; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 119, 148–150

- Dividends are rising while repurchases have fallen away, and no dividend is described as funded by borrowing, which is neutral for creditors but fixes a growing claim. Dividends were USD 281.0 million in the first nine months of 2026, up from USD 252.7 million a year earlier; the quarterly rate rose every quarter from USD 1.22 in early 2025 to an announced USD 1.46, and the merger agreement lets it keep rising by at most USD 0.02 a quarter. Repurchases were USD 26.3 million against USD 119.0 million a year earlier and stopped in the third quarter, and the merger agreement restricts further repurchases. Penske Corporation and Mitsui receive about 72.6% of each dividend, USD 69.6 million of the December payment.
  Basis: calculation A8; calculation C1; calculation C2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 68, 75, 89; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 96–97

- Acquisition spending in 2026 was large and mostly paid for franchise rights, so book equity is a softer cushion than it looks. Two U.S. dealerships bought in the first quarter cost USD 669.7 million, of which USD 537.6 million (80.3%) was booked as indefinite-lived intangibles; their third-quarter pre-tax income of USD 7.7 million annualises to USD 30.8 million, a price of 21.7x. Goodwill and franchise rights are now 68.9% of book equity against 64.5% at year end 2025, and other interest expense rose 39.8% to USD 92.4 million over nine months.
  Basis: calculation C4; calculation C5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 22, 64; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_q326exhibit99_1.htm.jsonl, page 11

- Operating risk is moderated by mix, but in 2026 the equity investee and disposal gains are holding up profit that the dealerships alone are not. Service and parts earn 45.5% of retail automotive gross profit at a 59.3% margin, yet retail automotive segment income excluding disposal gains fell 17.5% to USD 531.6 million over nine months and the new vehicle gross margin slipped to 8.6% from 9.2%. Penske Transportation Solutions supplied 15.3% of 2025 pre-tax earnings, and the non-automotive segment it dominates earned USD 166.4 million in nine months, but it paid 43.7% of its nine-month equity earnings in cash before its November distribution, and its managed fleet is 14.1% smaller than the 2024 annual report stated.
  Basis: calculation C6; calculation C7; calculation C8; calculation C9; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_q326exhibit99_1.htm.jsonl, pages 11–13; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 60–61; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 32, 70

- Manufacturers hold as much practical control as the shareholders, and the filings do not say whether the take-private needs their consent. Franchise agreements can be terminated or not renewed, framework agreements give manufacturers rights on a merger, a change of control, an acquisition of 20% of the voting stock or a material change in capital structure, and the agreements with BMW, the largest U.K. brand, expire in December 2026. The three lenders under the U.S. credit agreement are the finance arms of Mercedes-Benz, Toyota and Daimler Truck, so the same manufacturers sit on both sides of the relationship.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 22, 33–35; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 11

- Reporting and audit are a strength, with gaps around the deal. The statements carry an unqualified audit opinion and effective internal control, no material legal proceedings are reported, and related-party fees are reviewed by the Audit Committee; against that, the internal control audit excluded the related-party acquisition, the issuer will hold no earnings call during the deal, and no covenant level is printed.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 98, 110, 112–113, 148; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26, 29; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_q326exhibit99_1.htm.jsonl, page 2

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a lower risk level is that nothing in the record shows the controlling holders taking value from creditors: no exchange, drop-down or non-pro-rata step appears, the lenders consented, the investors plan up to USD 2.2 billion of new equity beside up to USD 1.8 billion of debt, and at the ceiling the debt would equal about 25.6% of the USD 16.3 billion enterprise value the issuer states at the offer price. The strongest case for a higher risk level is that the equity money pays selling holders and not the company, so the extra debt would add leverage without adding cash to the business, and the rate, ranking and maturity of that debt are not stated.
Basis: calculation A5; calculation A6; calculation A7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25, 90–91; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_q326exhibit99_1.htm.jsonl, page 2

The view would move to Low if executed documents place most of the new debt outside the company's guarantor group or cut the committed amount. It would move to High if the full USD 1.8 billion lands on the company under a bridge of 364 days, if a manufacturer withholds consent, or if the arrangement under which Penske Corporation has agreed to pledge shares for a loan facility is triggered.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25, 28, 91; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 22, 49

### Limitations

- The merger agreement, the debt commitment letters and the credit agreement amendment are described by the issuer but not supplied, so the debt that becomes the company's, its ranking, rate and maturity cannot be fixed and the 2.72x figure is a ceiling, not a forecast, and the Medium level could move either way once they are seen.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 25, 90–91

- The proxy statement is not in the set, so board independence, committee membership, pay and the related-party approval policy are not established; if they show independent oversight of insider dealings, the Medium level could be Low.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 2

- How the price of Penske Motor Group was set and who approved it are not described, so whether USD 519.4 million was a fair price is not established and the related-party conclusion rests on the amount and the parties alone.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 149–150

- No restricted payment, investment or related-party covenant level is printed, so what the controlling holders may take out of the company cannot be separated from what they have done, and no conclusion on legal capacity is drawn.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 144

- The record covers filings from the 2024 annual report onward, so any creditor-adverse or related-party episode before 2022 is outside this assessment, and the finances and intentions of Penske Corporation and Mitsui are not in the sources, so their willingness to support or extract is not inferred from who they are.
  Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_pag-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 49

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

On 2026-10-15 the issuer agreed to be acquired by Motown Acquisition Corp., a vehicle owned by Penske Corporation and Mitsui, for USD 212.00 in cash per share, with the buyer merging into the issuer so that it survives as a private company owned about 64% by Penske Corporation and 36% by Mitsui. The issuer states an enterprise value of about USD 16.3 billion and expects to close in the first quarter of 2027, and either side may terminate if closing has not occurred by 2027-10-15. The issuer would owe the buyer USD 383.0 million in specified cases, including a superior proposal, and the buyer would owe the issuer USD 488.0 million in other specified cases; approval needs a majority of all shares and a majority of the votes cast by disinterested holders, and the holders of 72.6% have agreed to vote in favour.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10, 89; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_q326exhibit99_1.htm.jsonl, page 2

The buyer expects to fund the price and costs with up to USD 2.2 billion of equity from the two investors and up to USD 1.8 billion of debt committed by three banks, reduced by up to USD 1.8 billion of senior subordinated notes and by up to USD 1.1 billion of added borrowing under the U.S. credit agreement. The Thirteenth Amendment to that agreement, signed on 2026-10-15, lets the issuer raise its revolving commitment from USD 1.5 billion to as much as USD 2.4 billion, including up to USD 400.0 million that may be borrowed above the borrowing base, and permits up to USD 1.8 billion of new senior unsecured bridge borrowing of 364 days or senior subordinated notes. The increase takes effect only if the merger closes and at least USD 2.2 billion of equity is funded. At the ceiling the issuer's debt would be USD 4,167.2 million, 2.72x last-twelve-month EBITDA, and the rate, ranking and maturity of the new debt are not stated.
Basis: calculation A5; calculation A6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25, 90–91

The filings show no new notes issue in 2025 or 2026. The USD 550 million 3.50% notes were repaid at maturity on 2025-09-01, and the only note issue left is USD 500 million of 3.75% senior subordinated notes due 2029, guaranteed by the issuer's 100% owned U.S. subsidiaries. Debt added in 2026 sits in revolving and mortgage facilities: the U.S. revolver stood at USD 498.0 million on 2026-09-30 and USD 606.0 million on 2026-10-12, and the revolving mortgage facility was drawn to USD 598.0 million against a capacity of USD 598.4 million, after its maximum was raised to USD 750.0 million in July 2026.
Basis: calculation A10; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 96; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26–28
