## Findings

### Bottom line

Fundamental credit quality is adequate: service and parts, earning 37.8% of 2025 gross profit, support EBITDA near USD 1,533.6 million against debt of 1.54x, but the controlling holders' take-private could lift debt to 2.72x, so its financing is the main credit risk.
Basis: calculation G1; calculation G2; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

Free cash flow of USD 497.9 million over the last twelve months, down 41.6% from USD 852.8 million in 2024, still covered dividends of USD 372.1 million 1.34 times, but nine-month net acquisitions of USD 518.0 million exceeded the free cash flow left after dividends and repurchases, and retail automotive profit excluding disposal gains is falling. Liquidity of about USD 1,568.3 million fits the current structure; the downside is the deal debt arriving while earnings soften.
Basis: calculation G5; calculation H1; calculation H2; calculation H18; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Key findings

- Scale and mix put a floor under earnings. Dealerships and trucks produced 97.1% of revenue, service and parts earned 37.8% of 2025 gross profit on 10.6% of revenue, the U.S. supplied 62% of revenue and the U.K. 26%, and premium brands were 71% of automotive revenue; and EBITDA has stayed within USD 1,527.6 million to USD 1,539.0 million across 2024, 2025 and the last twelve months to September 2026, USD 1,533.6 million.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10, 60–61; Financial foundation (CP-1)

- Margins, not volumes, show the pressure. EBITDA margin fell from 5.54% in 2023 to 4.71% over the last twelve months, a loss of 83 bp; gross profit per new vehicle fell 6.0% to USD 4,695, which the issuer attributes to a tariff-related pull-forward in the prior period, vehicle mix and affordability, and selling, general and administrative expense rose to 73.2% of gross profit from 71.5% while gross profit grew 0.2%. Revenue grew 3.1% in nine months, 2.00% excluding currency, but operating income fell 7.6%, so each point of lost gross profit costs about 3.4% of EBITDA before any cost response.
  Basis: calculation H3; calculation H4; calculation H6; calculation H10; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_q326exhibit99_1.htm.jsonl, pages 10–12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 56–57, 64

Free cash flow has fallen from USD 852.8 million in 2024 to USD 650.5 million in 2025 and USD 497.9 million over the last twelve months; in nine months it was USD 507.5 million, against USD 281.0 million of dividends, USD 26.3 million of repurchases and USD 518.0 million of net acquisitions, a shortfall of USD 317.8 million, and Total Debt rose USD 201.7 million from year end 2025. Operating cash flow fell USD 174.8 million, partly because an inventory build outran floor plan funding, so cash conversion depends on vehicle inventory and its financing staying in step.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a better view is that even the ceiling case leaves debt at 2.72x EBITDA and about 25.6% of the USD 16.3 billion enterprise value the issuer states, the investors are committing new equity, lenders have consented, and EBITDA moved only 0.74% between 2024 and 2025 through a weak truck cycle and a falling U.K. premium market. The strongest case for a worse view is that a repeat of the 2023 to 2025 EBITDA decline of 10.74% would take current debt to 1.73x but ceiling debt to 3.04x, with interest cost, ranking and maturity of the new debt not stated and the three lenders to the main facility also the manufacturers whose consent is open.
Basis: calculation G2; calculation G7; calculation H11; calculation H14; Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.1_2026-10-28_q326exhibit99_1.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8

The view would improve if executed documents put most of the new debt outside the company or cut the committed amount, and would weaken if the full USD 1.8 billion is drawn under a short bridge, if gross profit per new vehicle keeps falling while selling, general and administrative expense stays near 73.2% of gross profit, or if the mortgage line stays fully drawn while acquisitions continue.
Basis: calculation H13; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25, 28

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strength one. Service and parts give the credit its steadiest earnings base. Retail automotive service and parts earned 37.8% of 2025 gross profit on 10.6% of revenue and retail truck service and parts a further 7.1%, so 44.9% of gross profit comes from work that follows vehicles already on the road rather than from new sales. That keeps gross profit and interest cover steadier when new vehicle margins fall, which supports debt service through a vehicle downturn.
Basis: calculation H8; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9

Strength two. Debt is low against earnings on the issuer's own measure. Total Debt is 1.54x last-twelve-month EBITDA and EBITDA covers cash interest 5.49x, or 6.05x like-for-like, so earnings can fall a good way before leverage becomes a constraint, although covenant levels are not printed.
Basis: calculation G1; calculation G3; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Strength three. Free cash flow still funds the dividend, and spending is flexible. Free cash flow of USD 497.9 million over the last twelve months, down from USD 852.8 million in 2024, covered dividends of USD 372.1 million 1.34 times, and capital expenditure, about 1.02% of revenue, was cut from USD 386.0 million in 2023 to USD 324.6 million in 2025, a lever that can be pulled in stress.
Basis: calculation H2; calculation H7; calculation H18; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 96; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Strength four. Earnings are spread across brands, countries and businesses, so a single market shock reaches only part of EBITDA. Premium brands were 71% of automotive revenue, the U.S. 62% of revenue and the U.K. 26%, truck retail 10.7% and Penske Australia 2.9%; in 2025 U.K. premium sales fell 6.8% and North American heavy truck sales fell 18.2%, yet EBITDA moved only 0.74%.
Basis: calculation H14; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 7–10; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Strength five. Reporting and lender support aid monitoring. The 2025 statements carry unqualified audit opinions with effective internal control, liquidity is about USD 1,568.3 million, and the lenders under the U.S. credit agreement consented to the take-private, so near-term bank funding looks available on the printed evidence.
Basis: calculation G6; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 110, 113; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25–26; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

Weakness one. The take-private could roughly double debt. At the ceiling, new debt of up to USD 1.8 billion would lift Total Debt to USD 4,167.2 million, 2.72x EBITDA against 1.54x, with the holders of 72.6% on both sides of the deal, equity that may pay selling holders rather than reach the company, and the rate, ranking and maturity of the debt not stated. That would remove most of the room that the low leverage gives and raise refinancing risk.
Basis: calculation G2; calculation G7; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Weakness two. Retail automotive profit and free cash flow are eroding. Segment income excluding disposal gains fell 17.5% to USD 531.6 million over nine months, free cash flow is 41.6% below 2024, gross profit per new vehicle fell 6.0%, selling, general and administrative expense rose to 73.2% of gross profit, and EBITDA margin has lost 83 bp since 2023, so each point of lost gross profit costs about 3.4% of EBITDA before any cost response.
Basis: calculation G10; calculation H3; calculation H6; calculation H10; calculation H18; Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.1_2026-10-28_q326exhibit99_1.htm.jsonl, pages 10–12

Weakness three. Acquisitions and related-party spending outran cash generation, and book equity is soft. Nine-month free cash flow fell USD 317.8 million short of dividends, repurchases and net acquisitions, Total Debt rose USD 201.7 million, goodwill and franchise rights are 68.9% of book equity, and the two dealerships bought in 2026 earn a pre-tax return that implies a price of 21.7x, on top of a USD 519.4 million related-party purchase in 2025.
Basis: calculation H1; calculation G13; calculation G14; Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1)

Weakness four. Claims outside the debt measure and one full facility narrow the margin. Including floor plan notes payable, debt is 3.92x EBITDA before floor plan interest, operating lease liabilities are USD 2,509.8 million, the revolving mortgage facility is 99.9% drawn, and the U.S. revolver rose USD 108.0 million after the quarter end, so the headline leverage understates the claims on cash flow.

Franchise and framework agreements give manufacturers rights on a merger or change of control and the filings do not say whether consent is needed, while Penske Transportation Solutions supplied 17.0% of nine-month pre-tax earnings, paid 43.7% of its equity earnings in cash and runs a fleet 14.1% smaller than the 2024 annual report stated.

### Company description and ownership

The issuer is a franchised dealer group that retails new and used vehicles, finance and insurance products, service and parts and commercial trucks, distributes trucks and power systems in Australia and New Zealand, and owns 28.9% of a truck leasing and logistics business. Retail automotive was 86.4% of 2025 revenue, retail trucks 10.7% and distribution 2.9%. Customers are mostly individual consumers and fleets with no stated concentration, but supply, floor plan assistance and warranty reimbursement all come from manufacturers, so cash generation is durable in service and parts and dependent on manufacturers for new vehicles.
Basis: calculation G15; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 35; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Penske Corporation and Mitsui hold 52.3% and 20.3% of the shares, contract to elect the board together until 2030, and are the proposed buyers; the notes are guaranteed only by the 100% owned U.S. subsidiaries, which produced 59.3% of 2025 revenue, so about 40.7% of revenue sits outside the guarantor group. Dividends are rising, repurchases have stopped, acquisitions are lumpy and large, and a related-party purchase in 2025 cost USD 519.4 million; the sponsor and governance risk level is Medium.
Basis: calculation G14; calculation G16; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is cyclical, discretionary and priced by manufacturers rather than contracted. New vehicles were 45.4% of retail automotive revenue in nine months and used vehicles 34.2%; new retail units fell 2.4% while revenue per new retail unit rose 2.8%, yet gross profit per unit fell, so price increases flowed to manufacturers and not to the issuer. Service and parts, 12.2% of revenue, is recurring in nature and grew 2.7%, retail truck revenue grew 2.2%, and Penske Australia grew 21.4%, or 11.1% excluding currency, on standby power demand from data systems customers. Reported revenue grew 3.1%, or 2.00% excluding currency.
Basis: calculation H4; calculation H6; calculation O1; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_q326exhibit99_1.htm.jsonl, pages 11–13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 63

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins can be defended against modest volume pressure but not against falling unit profit. The issuer states that most selling expenses are variable and a significant part of general and administrative expense is within its control, and floor plan interest fell 10.9% to USD 115.1 million on lower rates, while manufacturers' floor plan credits were USD 50.8 million. Against that, selling, general and administrative expense is 73.2% of gross profit, personnel expense alone 43.5%, and rent 8.7%, so operating margin slipped to 3.8% from 4.2% on gross margin of 16.1% against 16.5%.
Basis: calculation O2; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 61–62; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 64; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_q326exhibit99_1.htm.jsonl, pages 10–12

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital intensity is low: capital expenditure was about 1.02% of 2025 revenue and 0.88% in nine months of 2026, and free cash flow was USD 497.9 million over the last twelve months, 32.5% of EBITDA. Conversion is exposed to vehicle inventory and its financing, because operating cash flow fell USD 174.8 million in nine months, and after dividends the USD 226.5 million of free cash flow that remained in nine months was smaller than the USD 518.0 million of net acquisitions. Cash conversion therefore supports debt service but not debt-funded growth at the 2026 pace.
Basis: calculation H7; calculation H15; calculation G5; calculation G8; calculation G13; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Competitive forces, external factors and credit SWOT

Supplier power is the strongest of the five competitive forces: manufacturers provide the vehicles exclusively, decide franchise renewal and hold rights on a change of control, which can remove revenue for a dealer with no alternative source. Rivalry among franchised dealers and manufacturers that sell directly, including agency models where units rose 9.2% in nine months, caps new vehicle margins; buyers are mostly price-aware individuals; online used sellers and private sales are partial substitutes; new entrants are electric vehicle makers selling directly. Together they help explain thin new vehicle margins of 8.6% and a gross profit that is not expanding.
Basis: calculation O1; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 22–24, 35; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_q326exhibit99_1.htm.jsonl, page 12

Among external factors, tariffs, the U.K. zero emission mandate of 33% of new car sales in 2026, a U.K. vehicle finance redress scheme, currency and interest rates matter to credit: a 10% move in average exchange rates changes revenue by about USD 1.0 billion, the issuer's new U.S. electric vehicle sales fell 48.8%, and the issuer says the share of Chinese manufacturers in the U.K. grew from 8.7% to 17.5% in nine months. A credit-translated SWOT therefore reads: strengths in service and parts, low non-vehicle leverage and free cash flow; weaknesses in manufacturer dependence, thin new vehicle margins and soft book equity; opportunities in a truck replacement cycle with a backlog 21.1% above year end; threats in U.K. premium pressure and the take-private debt.
Basis: calculation H16; calculation H17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 38–39, 86; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 26–27

### Outlook

Over the next 12 to 24 months the issuer expects lower U.S. electric vehicle sales, a challenging U.K. market, strong service and parts demand from an ageing vehicle base, an improving truck market with orders placed ahead of the 2027 emissions rules, firm energy systems orders in Australia and a smaller rental fleet at its equity investee. U.S. industry new light vehicle sales fell 2.0% in nine months, U.K. registrations rose 10.2%, and the truck backlog is 21.1% above year end, so near-term revenue is supported but unit profit is not. Over three to five years the electric vehicle transition in the U.K., agency selling and freight cycles at the equity investee are the factors that decide whether margins recover; the closing of the take-private, expected in the first quarter of 2027, decides leverage.
Basis: calculation H17; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 7–9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 38–39; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_q326exhibit99_1.htm.jsonl, page 2

### Downside scenario

The most credible downside is a repeat of the margin squeeze of 2023 to 2025 while the take-private debt arrives. Initiating condition: gross profit per new vehicle keeps falling, as it did 15.9% from 2023 to 2025, and U.K. premium and U.S. affordability weaken unit sales. First break: selling, general and administrative expense at 73.2% of gross profit, only partly variable, turns each 1% of lost gross profit into 3.4% of lost EBITDA before any cost response. Financial transmission: a decline matching the 10.74% seen from 2023 to 2025 would cut last-twelve-month EBITDA to USD 1,368.9 million, lifting debt to 1.73x today and 3.04x at the ceiling, while a 100 bp rate rise adds about USD 55.9 million of interest. Levers: capital expenditure, repurchases already stopped, disposals that raised USD 151.7 million, and dividend increases that insiders, who receive 72.6%, control.
Basis: calculation H6; calculation H9; calculation H10; calculation H11; calculation G2; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 68; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 75, 85–86

Resilience looks sufficient to avoid liquidity distress in the current structure, since liquidity is about USD 1,568.3 million and debt would stay under 2x, but not clearly so at the ceiling, where leverage above 3x would depend on bridge refinancing terms that are not stated and on lenders that are also the manufacturers. Observable triggers are set out in the monitoring register.
Basis: calculation G6; calculation H11; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Overall credit view

Overall, Penske Automotive Group presents an adequate business profile supported by a service and parts earnings base, low non-vehicle leverage and free cash flow that still covers the dividend, but credit quality is constrained by eroding retail automotive profit, acquisitions that outran cash generation and controlling holders who are also the proposed buyers. Cash flow durability appears adequate but weakening, with USD 497.9 million of free cash flow over the last twelve months against USD 852.8 million in 2024. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the terms of the take-private debt, the extended U.S. credit agreement and the manufacturer finance arms that lend under it. The primary downside path is up to USD 1.8 billion of new debt arriving while unit profit falls. Further analysis would require the financing documents, covenant levels, peer data and the manufacturers' consent position. Fundamental credit analysis completed. Top risk: the financing of the take-private.
Basis: calculation G2; calculation G5; calculation H18; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)
