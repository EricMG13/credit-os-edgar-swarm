## Findings

### Bottom line

Net debt of USD 6,172 million at 30 September 2026 cannot be set against a comparable twelve-month EBITDA, so creditors must judge leverage from net debt, liquidity and the maturity wall rather than from a ratio. The January 2026 Civitas merger and the April 2026 South Texas sale mean no reported period before the third quarter of 2026 describes the company as it now stands.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13; calculation C10

At carrying value the company owed USD 6,614 million, all of it Senior Notes, with nothing drawn on the revolver and USD 2,940 million of cash and undrawn commitments; on the company's own principal definition net debt was USD 6,014 million. In FY2025, before the merger, net debt was 1.06x reported EBITDA and 1.04x Adjusted EBITDAX. No Senior Note matures before July 2028.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19; EX-99.1_2026-11-03_exhibit99111032026er.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 130–131; 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 135; calculation C5; calculation C18; calculation C11

### Key findings

Before the merger leverage was low and falling. Net debt fell from USD 2,777 million at the end of FY2024 to USD 2,347 million at the end of FY2025 while reported EBITDA rose from USD 1,916 million to USD 2,210 million, so net leverage eased from 1.45x to 1.06x. The merger then lifted net debt to USD 7,527 million at 31 March 2026, and South Texas sale proceeds of USD 889 million and cash generated above capital spending brought it down to USD 6,416 million at 30 June 2026 and USD 6,172 million at 30 September 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 134; 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 135; 10-Q_2026-03-31_sm-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-06-30_sm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8; calculation C6; calculation C7; calculation C8; calculation C9; calculation C10; calculation C20; calculation C21

The ratio cannot be carried to 30 September 2026. A twelve-month window would join the fourth quarter of 2025, when SM Energy stood alone, to nine months in which Civitas appears only from 30 January 2026 and the South Texas assets only until 30 April 2026. The pieces describe different companies, so leverage and interest cover are left as not calculable rather than estimated, and the arithmetic that mixes them is shown in the calculations and not used.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 130–131; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 60; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13; calculation C40; calculation C41

Interest cost has stepped up. Cash interest paid rose from USD 88 million in FY2024 to USD 166 million in FY2025, which cut reported EBITDA cover from 21.77x to 13.31x, and cash interest for the nine months of 2026 reached USD 331 million against USD 165 million a year earlier because most of the assumed Civitas notes carry higher coupons than the notes SM Energy had issued itself. The weighted-average interest rate was 7.6% in the third quarter of 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 137; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 53; calculation C20; calculation C21

Cash generation covered investment in the nine months to 30 September 2026. Operating cash flow of USD 2,865 million funded capital expenditure of USD 2,020 million and left free cash flow of USD 845 million, or 22.7% of Adjusted EBITDAX, after a working-capital outflow of USD 210 million and hedge settlement losses of USD 457 million. Capital spending fell from 45.8% of production revenue in FY2025 to 34.9% because revenue grew faster than spending, and the company places USD 227 million of one-time integration and divested-asset cash costs inside these figures, so underlying conversion is somewhat better than reported.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 60; EX-99.1_2026-11-03_exhibit99111032026er.htm.jsonl, page 14; calculation C35; calculation C53

Liquidity is adequate and no bond falls due soon. It was USD 2,940 million at 30 September 2026: USD 442 million of cash and USD 2,498 million of undrawn commitments after USD 2 million of letters of credit. The USD 5,000 million borrowing base is double the USD 2,500 million of commitments, so commitments set the limit, and the lenders reaffirmed both after the quarter end. Elected commitments mature in 2031; the maturity of the other commitments, and whether the October 2025 springing-maturity clause survives the January 2026 amendment, are not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 158; calculation C18; calculation C19

The maturity wall starts late and is spread out. No Senior Note falls due before July 2028, when USD 856 million matures, and the rest of the USD 6,456 million principal falls in 2029 to 2034, never more than USD 1,350 million in one year. The revolver is the only secured claim and is undrawn; all eight note series are unsecured, rank equally and carry the subsidiary guarantees that support the credit agreement, so any revolver drawing would rank ahead of the notes against the secured properties.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation C13

Claims outside debt are mostly operating. Asset retirement obligations were USD 426 million and derivative liabilities USD 565 million at 30 September 2026; delivery commitments carry undiscounted deficiency payments of up to USD 205 million if volumes are not delivered, and a drilling-footage agreement carries liquidated damages of up to USD 64 million. Operating lease liabilities were USD 138 million at the end of FY2025, the latest figure in the sources. The sources show no finance leases, factoring or supplier-finance programme.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 179–181; calculation C49

Accounts payable and accrued expenses rose by USD 1,815 million to USD 2,505 million between year-end 2025 and 30 September 2026. This is operating float rather than debt, but it unwinds as invoices are paid and so draws on liquidity; the quarterly report does not break the balance down, so its composition is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 181; calculation C48

Three definitions change the numbers. Reported EBITDA is built from statement components because the company states none, and Adjusted EBITDAX exceeded it by USD 661 million in the nine months of 2026 after derivative losses, merger costs and the divestiture gain; debt is carried at USD 6,614 million, USD 158 million above principal because of fair-value premiums on the Civitas notes; and the covenant definitions were amended on 30 January 2026 with the amended text not supplied, so covenant headroom cannot be measured.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 60–61; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–19; calculation C30; calculation C12; calculation C31

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The case for a safer reading rests on earnings power rather than on any ratio the sources print. Third-quarter Adjusted EBITDAX was USD 1,341 million on the post-sale asset base, and management states a target of low one-times leverage at mid-cycle prices by year-end 2026. The only arithmetic available, FY2025 plus nine months of 2026 less nine months of 2025, gives 1.42x on the company's net debt, but it omits Civitas earnings before 30 January 2026 and includes seven months of earnings from South Texas assets since sold, so it is shown for orientation and is not a measurement.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 60; EX-99.1_2026-11-03_exhibit99111032026er.htm.jsonl, page 2; EX-99.1_2026-11-03_exhibit99111032026er.htm.jsonl, page 14; calculation C46; calculation C40

A combined-company pro forma Adjusted EBITDAX for twelve months, or four reported quarters after the sale, would let leverage be stated on one basis. The amended credit agreement could change the reading in either direction: if its definition of EBITDAX or total funded debt is stricter than the company's, headroom under the annual report's maximum of 3.50x total funded debt to trailing adjusted EBITDAX could be thinner than the low stated leverage suggests.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 161; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17
