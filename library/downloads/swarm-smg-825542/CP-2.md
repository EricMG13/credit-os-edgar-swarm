## Findings

### Bottom line

Scotts Miracle-Gro presents an adequate but leveraged business profile: a branded franchise with improving margins supports cash generation, yet unadjusted leverage of 6.12x, thin free cash flow after dividends and retailer concentration make creditors dependent on revolver access for the December 2026 notes.

The USD 250.0 million of senior notes are due December 15, 2026, 27 days after the as-of date and near the seasonal low in liquidity, which was USD 988.1 million at December 27, 2025 against USD 1,409.5 million at June 27, 2026. Management intends to repay them from operating cash flow and the revolver; whether it has is not established. Free cash flow after dividends over the last twelve months of USD 108.9 million covers only 43.56% of the notes, and reported covenant leverage of 3.78x against a 5.00x maximum is the main reassurance, yet it rests on an earnings definition the filings do not reconcile.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against the cautious reading is the record of repayment and the room in the structure: the company prepaid USD 250.0 million and USD 75.0 million of term loan in FY2024 and FY2025, carrying debt fell USD 120.4 million between those year-ends, reported covenant leverage is 3.78x against a 5.00x maximum, revolver loans fell from USD 427.0 million at December 27, 2025 to USD 25.0 million at June 27, 2026 as receivables were collected and sold, availability was USD 979.8 million at its lowest fiscal 2026 quarter-end, and the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025 produced USD 131.2 million of free cash flow. The view would improve if the notes are repaid without lifting revolver use above its December 2025 level, if the receivables programme is renewed on similar terms and if the gross margin rate holds above the prior-year level. It would worsen if the notes are repaid mainly from revolver borrowings while the programme lapses, if third-quarter freight costs persist, or if dividends keep exceeding free cash flow before the autumn collection.

### Company description and operating model

Scotts Miracle-Gro is the leading marketer of branded consumer lawn and garden products in North America, selling growing media and mulch, lawn care, controls and gardening products under the Scotts, Miracle-Gro, Ortho and Tomcat brands through two operations: U.S. Consumer, 91.70% of nine-month net sales, and Other, mainly Canada, 8.30%. It is also the exclusive agent for Monsanto's consumer Roundup products in the United States, earning commissions, and holds an equity interest in the Bonnie Plants joint venture. Growing media and mulch was 42.27% and lawn care 27.85% of nine-month net sales, so demand follows spring planting and weather. The credit link is that cash flow is visible only from late winter, is concentrated in two quarters and is financed between peaks by the revolver and the sale of receivables, so the franchise supports debt service in a normal season but gives creditors little warning of a bad one.

### Ownership and group structure

Scotts Miracle-Gro is a listed company with no sponsor; a family partnership holds about 23% of the shares, down from 24% a year earlier, and the incoming chief executive, in office from June 26, 2026, is a general partner of that partnership. Substantially all domestic subsidiaries guarantee the credit facilities and the senior notes, but the note guarantees can be released, including when a guarantor is designated an unrestricted subsidiary, so structural support depends on indenture terms that are not supplied. Hawthorne North America was sold on April 8, 2026 for Vireo shares and a warrant, which the company transferred to a consolidated partner-owned vehicle for a promissory note, and an earlier sale of an investment holding company went to a second such vehicle; the company holds call options and the vehicles can put assets back. The credit consequence is that owner behaviour is more predictable than under a sponsor, with no extraction through fees or leveraged recapitalisation seen in the filings, but the dividend of USD 0.66 per share each quarter keeps priority over faster debt paydown.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is recurring in category but not contractual: consumers replenish lawn and garden products each season, yet retailers place orders from late winter and the company reports minimal carryover of open orders, so there is no backlog to underwrite the year. Net sales fell 3.93% in fiscal 2025 with Hawthorne included and rose 2.41% in nine months to June 27, 2026 without it, from volume and mix of 2.0% and pricing of 0.4%, with a favourable mix from branded soils, grass seed, controls and fertiliser offsetting lower mulch sales. Pricing power is limited: two retailers hold 52% of sales, the company states that market conditions may limit its ability to raise prices to offset raw material costs, and private label competes in its markets. The credit implication is that cost inflation is absorbed in margin before it is recovered in price, and that demand is discretionary and exposed to weather and consumer behaviour.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Materials were 50.98% of nine-month cost of sales, manufacturing labor and overhead 23.97% and distribution and warehousing 21.20%, so most cost is variable with volume or input prices rather than fixed. Inputs include urea and other fertiliser, resin, diesel, natural gas, peat, bark and grass seed; the company buys part of its needs ahead of the season and hedges diesel and urea, but imports from China and other countries expose it to tariffs. Selling cost carries operating leverage: advertising and other marketing were USD 196.8 million over nine months, 43.67% of selling, general and administrative cost, after planned increases. Margins can be defended against modest volume pressure through marketing and incentive pay, but not against a sustained freight or tariff shock, as the 0.9 percentage point fall in the third-quarter gross margin rate shows.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Scotts Miracle-Gro presents an adequate business profile supported by leading brands, a nine-month gross margin rate of 35.67%, up 1.26 percentage points on the prior-year nine months, and a USD 1,500.0 million revolver with availability of at least USD 979.8 million at each fiscal 2026 quarter-end reported, but credit quality is constrained by retailer concentration, a two-quarter earnings season, thin free cash flow after dividends in the first nine months and negative equity. Cash-flow durability appears adequate over a full year, with USD 108.9 million left after dividends over the last twelve months, and weak before the autumn collection. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on revolver availability, the receivables programme and the covenant earnings measure for the 2026 notes, and on market access for the 2029 notes. The primary downside path is a weak spring season with a lapsed receivables programme, which raises revolver use and leverage together. Comparison with sector peers is not established because no peer data is supplied; both rating agencies kept ratings in the single-B category in August 2025. Further analysis would require the fiscal 2026 annual report, the renewal status of the receivables programme, the credit agreement's earnings definition and the proxy statement. The top risk is dependence on revolver capacity and the receivables programme for the spring peak and the December 2026 maturity.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The strongest support is the franchise. Leading brands and the exclusive Roundup agency produced a gross margin rate of 35.67% over nine months, up 1.26 percentage points, which lifts EBITDA and interest cover and lets the company absorb moderate cost increases. The second support is liquidity: availability under the USD 1,500.0 million revolver was USD 979.8 million at the lowest fiscal 2026 quarter-end reported and USD 1,381.8 million at June 27, 2026, so liquidity covered the USD 250.0 million of notes 3.95x at the low and 5.64x in June. The third is a record of paying debt down, with carrying debt falling USD 120.4 million between FY2024 and FY2025 and a lower interest bill, which gives creditors evidence that cash is applied to debt in good years.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_smg.htm.jsonl, pages 78, 103; 10-Q_2025-12-27_smg.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-06-27_smg.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 40, 54; calculation K4; calculation K15; calculation K28; calculation K45

The largest weakness is the funding of the seasonal build. The programme that removed USD 654.1 million of receivables at March 28, 2026 is uncommitted, and without it liquidity at that date would have been USD 507.7 million rather than USD 1,161.8 million. The second is thin cash after dividends, USD 15.8 million for nine months and USD 108.9 million for twelve months, which leaves little internal funding for the notes. The third is retailer concentration, 52% of fiscal 2025 net sales in two customers, which limits pricing and concentrates risk in the two quarters that earn most of the year. Unadjusted leverage of 6.12x, negative equity, cost exposure to freight and tariffs, and free cash flow that benefits from cash taxes of only USD 5.6 million over nine months complete the weaknesses.
