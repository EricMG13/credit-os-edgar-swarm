## Findings

### Bottom line

Creditors rely on a concentrated, externally managed landlord whose manager's owner also controls its largest hotel operator, yet 2026 equity and debt actions favoured creditors and the documents record no debt-funded payout to shareholders or the manager. Two counterparties carry 74.1% of the investment base: TravelCenters of America (TA), which pays 68.2% of net lease minimum rent under leases to 2033 with rent coverage of 1.44x, and Sonesta, the hotel operator controlled by the manager's owner, which manages 62 of 87 hotels.

The hotel segment is shrinking and reported a loss in 2026 after impairments, so credit quality increasingly rests on TA's rent and on a manager, RMR, that cannot be removed cheaply: its agreements run to 2045 and extend each year, a termination fee applies, and the credit agreement treats RMR ceasing to be business manager as a change of control.

### Key findings

Cash flow rests on two counterparties, and one is tied to the manager's owner. TA pays 68.2% of net lease minimum rent and Sonesta manages 62 of 87 hotels; together they represent 74.1% of historical investment, against 74.8% at the year end, so concentration has barely moved despite disposals. SVC also owns 34% of Sonesta, and Mr. Portnoy, who controls the manager, controls Sonesta, so a problem at the hotel operator would hit hotel returns and the equity stake together.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 12–14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 28–29; 10-K_2025-12-31_svc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 210

The net lease book is the stable core and has strengthened, but its largest tenant has the thinnest coverage among the ten largest tenants. Net lease rent coverage rose to 2.17x from 1.98x and occupancy to 97.6% from 96.6%; TA coverage rose to 1.44x from 1.20x, and each of its five leases improved. That coverage uses unaudited tenant figures, 69.4% of net lease minimum rent expires in 2033, nearly all of it the TA leases, and the BP Corporation North America guaranty is capped at 10.32 times annual TA rent, down from 11.44 times at the year end with no reason given; the cap is 1.46 times the TA rent left in the initial term.
Basis: calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C15; calculation C16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 43; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 56–58; 10-K_2025-12-31_svc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 147–148; 10-K_2025-12-31_svc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 184–185

The hotel segment is the weaker business and is shrinking, so the credit rests increasingly on rent. The hotel count fell 57.77% since the end of 2024 to 87, and hotels now provide 74.52% of revenue, down from 78.80% in the first nine months of 2025; the segment reported a loss in 2026 after impairments while the net lease segment stayed profitable. The retained 77 hotels are improving, with RevPAR up 7.5% for the nine months, but the hotel margin before depreciation was 13.58% against 93.42% for net lease, and in FY2025 hotel capital spending exceeded the segment margin before depreciation.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C6; calculation C12; calculation C13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 30–32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 59–60; 10-K_2025-12-31_svc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 124; 10-K_2025-12-31_svc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 211–212

The manager is entrenched and is paid whatever the results, though its fees are falling and no incentive fee has been earned. The management agreements run to 2045 and extend each year; ending them for convenience costs the present value of 19 years to 20 years of fees, or ten years if ended for performance, and the credit agreement defines RMR ceasing to be business manager as a change of control. Fees to RMR fell 6.19% in FY2025 and 2.29% in the first nine months of 2026, and RMR's own lender holds a pledge over its rights under the SVC agreements.

Financial policy in 2026 favoured creditors, with two qualifications. The April offering issued shares equal to 73.82% of the count at 2026-09-30, RMR took 8.70% of it, and the proceeds with USD 745 million of new net lease mortgage notes and cash on hand redeemed USD 1,550 million of senior notes; distributions were cut in the fourth quarter of 2024 and fell 94.95% between 2023 and 2025. The qualifications are make-whole premiums of 2.97% of the principal redeemed, and secured note issuers whose assets are not available to other creditors.

Ownership leaves little room for a control event or a sponsor exit, which removes one class of risk but entrenches the structure. No sponsor owns SVC: RMR holds about 6.4% and Mr. Portnoy about 6.8% including through ABP Trust, up from 1.2% at the year end after the offering; the declaration of trust caps other holders at 9.8% and the bylaws at 5%; and the April offering brought SVC closer to a tax ownership-change threshold that would restrict use of its loss carryforwards.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against caution is the quality of the net lease income: it earns a margin of 93.42% before depreciation, is 97.6% occupied and carries 2.17x rent coverage; TA coverage has risen at all five leases since the year end; the BP guaranty covers 10.32 times annual TA rent; and the 2026 equity raise and note redemptions cut debt without any debt-funded payout. The manager has earned no incentive fee in the years covered and the distribution is small, so nothing in the supplied documents shows governance costing creditors so far.
Basis: calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C9; calculation C13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 43; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26–27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 56–57; 10-K_2025-12-31_svc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 203

The view would improve if TA coverage kept rising above 1.44x on audited or BP-level support, the guaranty cap stopped falling, retained hotel returns kept rising and hotel sales continued to repay debt. It would worsen if TA coverage fell back toward 1.20x, the guaranty cap fell again, a related party received new capital, fees or term extensions without a matching benefit to SVC, the distribution rose while leverage stayed high, or the proxy statement showed weak trustee independence.
Basis: calculation C4; calculation C5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 46; 10-K_2025-12-31_svc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 156–157

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The 2026 transactions reduced debt with equity and secured note proceeds rather than with a sponsor injection, and the documents describe no sponsor, buyout, recapitalisation or sale of SVC itself; the only change of control stated is the 2023 acquisition of its tenant TA. On 2026-03-06 three bankruptcy-remote issuers owned by SVC issued USD 745 million of net lease mortgage notes in three classes, weighted coupon 5.96%, due March 2031, backed by a pool of net lease properties SVC contributed; on 2026-04-02 SVC sold 95,833,333 shares at USD 6.00 each in an underwritten offering, and RMR bought 8,333,333 of them. The proceeds and cash on hand redeemed USD 1,550 million of senior unsecured notes between January and May 2026, which cost make-whole premiums of 2.97% of the principal redeemed.
Basis: calculation C10; calculation C17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21–26; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 35

The new secured notes share one collateral pool with the 2023 series and are non-recourse to SVC. In September 2026 SVC repaid and terminated a USD 45 million variable funding note secured on that pool, and it has said it is exploring options to extend or refinance its USD 650 million secured revolving credit facility, which matures on June 29, 2027; availability depends on collateral performance and covenants. SVC reported no new unsecured issue in the period.
Basis: calculation C18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 22–23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47
