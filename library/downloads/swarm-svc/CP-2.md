## Findings

### Bottom line

Credit quality is weak but stabilising: stable net lease income and 2026 deleveraging do not yet offset analyst-normalised leverage of 9.21x, cash interest cover of 1.46x and a revolver that matures on June 29, 2027, so creditors depend on asset sales and refinancing. Total debt is 22.72x unadjusted EBITDA, which impairments depress, and 34.13% of principal falls due by the end of 2028.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C7; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 48–49

The primary downside is that the revolver is not extended while hotel sales slow, leaving the 2027 and 2028 maturities to be met from asset sales and new secured debt, which already makes up 62.92% of principal against 40.51% at the year end.
Basis: calculation C5; calculation C7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20–23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 47–49; 10-K_2025-12-31_svc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 133–134

### Key findings

The strongest support is a net lease core that is stable, contractual and improving. It earns a margin of 93.42% before depreciation, is 97.6% occupied with 7.0 years of average remaining lease term and carries rent coverage of 2.17x, while its largest tenant, TravelCenters of America (TA), holds leases to 2033 with coverage of 1.44x and a BP guaranty equal to 10.32 times annual rent.
Basis: calculation C4; calculation C12; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 56–57

The weakest dimension is cash flow after interest and capital spending, with liquidity close behind, and together they leave no internal funding for deleveraging. Cash interest takes 68.50% of analyst-normalised EBITDA; operating cash flow covered capital improvements only 0.61 times over the last twelve months, against 1.20 times in the nine months of 2026; and the 2026 debt reduction came from asset sales, new secured notes of USD 745 million and new equity rather than from retained cash flow.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C14; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_svc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 173–174; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 35

Leverage is high on every measure and is falling only as fast as assets are sold. Total debt fell 15.17% in the nine months to 2026-09-30, but last-twelve-month analyst-normalised EBITDA fell 7.45% from FY2025 and revenue fell 12.81% as hotels were sold, so normalised leverage of 9.21x is lower than at the year end only because debt fell faster than earnings.
Basis: calculation C1; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; 10-K_2025-12-31_svc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 171–172

Refinancing capacity is adequate but expensive and increasingly secured. SVC raised new secured debt and equity in 2026 and only scheduled amortisation falls due in the rest of 2026, but 34.13% of principal falls due by the end of 2028, secured debt is 62.92% of principal against 40.51% at the year end, the unsecured notes are estimated at 93.30% of principal, and a rate one percentage point higher on refinancing would raise annual cash coupons by 15.48%.
Basis: calculation C5; calculation C7; calculation C8; calculation C9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 48–49; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 62–63

The hotel segment weakens the credit and consumes cash, but it is being reduced. Hotels earn a margin of 13.58% before depreciation against 93.42% for net lease, they reported impairments in 2026 that turned the segment into a loss, SVC funds their capital spending, and the eight hotels sold in October 2026 fetched 23.71% of the year-end depreciated book value per key of the whole hotel portfolio, though the exit hotels they belong to earned RevPAR of only 58.22% of the retained hotels, so asset sales cut debt but also cut earnings.
Basis: calculation C4; calculation C11; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 30–32; 10-K_2025-12-31_svc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 118–119; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 35

Covenant protection exists but is measured on assets at original cost. The issuer reports total debt to adjusted total assets of 53.9% against a maximum of 60%, secured debt of 33.9% against 40%, income cover of 1.83x against 1.50x and unencumbered asset cover of 273.2% against 150%, yet adjusted assets are 150.89% of balance-sheet assets and debt is 79.11% of balance-sheet assets, and the revolver's own covenant results are not printed.

Business & governance rates sponsor and governance risk as Medium because related parties control the manager and the largest hotel operator and the manager is costly to remove, while 2026 financial policy, a small distribution of 13.76% of Normalized FFO and no incentive fee point the other way.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a weak view is that SVC has repeatedly raised capital and met its maturities: it cleared the 2026 notes, issued USD 745 million of secured notes at a 5.96% weighted coupon, raised new equity, and reports indenture headroom of 6.1 percentage points on total debt and on secured debt and 0.33x on income cover. All of its debt is fixed rate, the revolver is undrawn, the distribution is small, hotel capital spending is expected to ease to between USD 25 million and USD 40 million in the last quarter of 2026, and the net lease segment carries contractual rent to 2033.
Basis: calculation C6; calculation C10; calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 22–23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 45; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 51; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 62–64

The view would improve if the revolver were extended or replaced on acceptable terms before June 29, 2027, if TA coverage kept rising and the BP guaranty stopped falling, if hotel sales kept repaying debt at prices near book, and if cash flow covered capital spending over twelve months. It would worsen if the revolver were not extended, if the first extension test of 1.50x fixed charge coverage were missed, if impairments continued and hotel sale prices fell further, or if distributions, fees or related-party support rose before leverage fell.
Basis: calculation C3; calculation C7; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_svc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 133; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47

### Company, ownership and revenue durability

SVC is a Maryland REIT with no employees, managed by RMR, that owns net lease properties let on triple-net terms and hotels run by third-party managers. In the nine months of 2026 hotels were 74.52% of revenue and rent 25.48%, yet net lease produced 70.16% of the combined segment margin before depreciation, so cash flow is steadier than revenue suggests. Ownership is dispersed with no sponsor; RMR holds about 6.4% and Mr. Portnoy about 6.8% including through ABP Trust, and the manager's owner also controls the hotel operator Sonesta.
Basis: calculation C4; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 31

Revenue is durable in the net lease book and cyclical in hotels. Rent is contractual with 7.0 years of average remaining term and a 2% yearly step on TA leases, with 69.4% of minimum rent expiring in 2033, nearly all of it TA; hotel revenue depends on occupancy and rate, which rose in 2026 for the retained hotels (RevPAR up 7.5% in the nine months), and on disposals, which cut revenue 12.81% over the last twelve months. Two counterparties carry 74.1% of investment, so revenue durability rests on TA's rent and on Sonesta's operation of hotels more than on the breadth of the portfolio.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C12; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 58–60

### Cost structure, margins and cash conversion

Net lease costs are mostly the tenants' own, so SVC keeps 93.42% of rent before depreciation; hotel costs sit with SVC, whose hotel margin before depreciation was 13.58% in the nine months, 13.77% a year earlier and 13.22% in FY2025. The filings do not split hotel costs into fixed and variable parts and report no seasonality figures beyond higher revenue in the second and third quarters, so operating leverage in hotels cannot be measured, though a thin margin means a small revenue fall removes a large share of hotel profit. Sonesta's fees are paid ahead of SVC's returns, and RMR's fees are paid whatever the results.
Basis: calculation C4; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_svc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 149; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 31–32

Capital intensity and cash conversion are the weak link. SVC must fund hotel capital spending under its management agreements, and over the last twelve months operating cash flow covered capital improvements only 0.61 times, so free cash flow before asset sales was negative; capital improvements were lower in the nine months of 2026 and are expected to be between USD 25 million and USD 40 million in the last quarter of 2026 against a larger spend in the same quarter of 2025. Interest absorbs 68.50% of analyst-normalised EBITDA, and the cash coupon run rate on the debt outstanding at 2026-09-30 is lower than the last twelve months paid, which would lift cover to 1.87x if earnings held.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C13; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 44–45; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 62

### Competitive forces, external factors and credit SWOT

Competition affects credit mainly through TA and the hotels. The net lease market is competitive and some competitors have lower cost of capital; TA's travel centers compete with Pilot Flying J and Love's, which with TA are believed to hold most of long-haul fuel sales, and the issuer weighs site and zoning barriers to entry when it buys properties; substitutes include home sharing for hotels and commercialised state rest areas for travel centers. Buyer power is concentrated in TA, which pays 68.2% of net lease minimum rent; supplier power sits with RMR and Sonesta, whose fees do not depend on SVC's results.
Basis: calculation C12; 10-K_2025-12-31_svc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-K_2025-12-31_svc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Macro, tax and regulatory factors matter in two ways. Higher rates and recession would hit hotel demand and refinancing cost, and the issuer cites inflation, tariffs and labour costs; REIT distribution rules, the tax limit on a change in ownership and licensing rules for properties with gambling operations constrain financial flexibility and takeovers. Social and technology factors are not primary credit drivers on the supplied materials.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 34–35; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 70; 10-K_2025-12-31_svc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 104; 10-K_2025-12-31_svc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 16

In credit terms the strengths are contractual net lease rent, rising tenant coverage, fixed-rate debt and reduced near-term maturities; the weaknesses are leverage, thin cash conversion, hotel exposure, tenant and operator concentration and a costly-to-remove manager. Opportunities that could lift credit quality are a revolver extension, continued hotel sales at fair prices and net lease growth; threats are a TA coverage reversal, a falling guaranty cap, a failed revolver test and tax limits on ownership change.
Basis: calculation C6; calculation C12; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 70

### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale and business mix are average: the portfolio spans many brands and states, but travel centers are 69.1% of net lease rent and two counterparties carry 74.1% of investment, so diversification is weak and the competitive position rests on lease terms rather than a moat. Cost and capex flexibility is average, because net lease costs sit with tenants while hotel capital spending is partly discretionary; margin stability is average, with net lease margins between 93.42% and 95.05% and hotel margins thin, between 13.22% and 14.87%; free cash flow stability is weak.
Basis: calculation C4; calculation C12; calculation C13; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 57

Liquidity is weak and refinancing capacity is average. Unrestricted cash is small, the only large source of liquidity is a revolver that matures within eight months and whose covenant results are not printed, and the first extension needs 1.50x fixed charge coverage against a 1.30x covenant; against that the issuer raised secured debt and equity in 2026, and the issuer's own fair value estimates put the unsecured notes at 93.30% of principal. Financial policy and governance are average: the 2026 actions favoured creditors and the distribution is 13.76% of Normalized FFO, offset by manager entrenchment and related-party structure. The trend is improving leverage and falling earnings, and the missing datapoints are the revolver covenant results, a lender's EBITDA definition and the proxy statement.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 months to 24 months the issuer's stated priorities are to reduce debt, shift the portfolio to net lease and improve the retained hotels, and it gives no earnings or leverage guidance. Tailwinds are a lodging market the issuer calls generally favourable in 2026, rising tenant coverage, falling hotel capital spending and lower cash interest as redemptions work through, which supports cover. Headwinds are the revolver maturity, the 2027 zero coupon notes and the 2028 maturities, a falling BP guaranty cap, loss of hotel earnings as hotels are sold, and inflation in labour and other costs.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 34–35; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 45; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47

Over three years to five years the 2028 to 2032 maturities must be refinanced at current market rates, which on the issuer's own sensitivity adds 15.48% to coupon cost for each percentage point, and the net lease share of earnings should rise as hotels are sold; beyond that horizon TA's initial lease term ends in 2033 with renewal at TA's option and the manager agreements run to 2045.

### Downside scenario

The scenario is an operating and refinancing squeeze, not a recession narrative. The initiating condition is that hotel demand or sale prices weaken during 2027 while the lenders do not extend the revolver; the first operational break is slower hotel sales and a retained-hotel margin that is already thin at 13.58%, so earnings fall faster than cash interest, which is fixed. Financial transmission follows: operating cash flow after interest, which covered capital improvements 1.20 times in the nine months of 2026, turns negative, SVC must fund hotel capital spending from sales or borrowings, and without the revolver liquidity is limited to unrestricted cash that is small against the 2027 zero coupon notes.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_svc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 47–49

The liquidity, leverage and refinancing consequence is that SVC would have to refinance the 2027 notes and the 2028 maturities, 34.13% of principal by the end of 2028, in a weaker market, and with secured debt already 62.92% of principal the new money would again be secured, leaving unsecured holders behind more collateral. Management levers are real: capital spending can be cut, the distribution is small, more hotels and net lease properties can be sold, and the unencumbered assets of the indenture test, covered 273.2% on cost, give room to raise secured debt, but hotels have sold for less than book value per key. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid distress over twelve months, because the 2026 maturities are cleared, and insufficient to be comfortable through the 2027 and 2028 maturities without a revolver extension. Observable triggers are the revolver outcome, TA coverage, hotel sale prices and operating cash flow against capital spending.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Service Properties Trust presents an adequate business profile in net lease and a weak one in hotels, supported by a contractual net lease book with rising coverage, fixed-rate debt and active deleveraging, but credit quality is constrained by leverage of 9.21x on analyst-normalised EBITDA, cash interest cover of 1.46x, concentration in two counterparties and a hotel segment that consumes cash. Cash-flow durability appears adequate in net lease and weak after interest and capital spending, because interest takes 68.50% of normalised EBITDA. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on extending the revolver, continuing hotel sales near book and the unencumbered assets that support new secured debt. The primary downside path is a failed revolver extension with slower hotel sales ahead of the 2027 and 2028 maturities. Further analysis would require the revolver covenant results, a lender's EBITDA definition, the executed debt documents and the proxy statement. Top risk: refinancing of the revolver and the 2027 and 2028 maturities.
