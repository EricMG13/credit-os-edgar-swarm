## Findings

### Bottom line

Magnite is a sound credit for a leveraged-finance issuer, with net cash, liquidity of 1.61x debt and no maturity before 2029, but its earnings rest on uncontracted advertising demand, so the main risk is a cash-flow shock, not leverage.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B06; 10-Q_2026-09-30_mgni-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19

Free cash flow before working-capital changes was 80.68% of reported EBITDA over the last twelve months, but reported EBITDA is only 70.94% of the issuer's adjusted figure and the excess of seller payables over receivables equals 65.56% of cash, so the cushion is thinner than headline net cash suggests.
Basis: calculation B01; calculation B05; calculation B08

### Key findings

Leverage and maturities leave creditors a wide cushion. Total debt is 1.76x reported EBITDA, down from 4.94x at the end of FY2024, cash exceeds debt, liquidity is 1.61x debt, and the nearest maturity is the undrawn revolver in 27 months, with the term loan 51 months away. Free cash flow before working-capital changes plus cash interest covers scheduled interest and amortization 6.38x, so debt service is a small claim on cash generation, and the only financial covenant is a springing leverage test that applies only when more than 35% of the revolver is drawn.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_mgni-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_mgni-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; calculation B06; calculation B14; calculation B15; calculation B12

Free cash flow is genuine but flattered by working capital. Free cash flow before working-capital changes was 80.68% of reported EBITDA over the last twelve months and 112.58% after them, because working-capital inflows were 21.36% of operating cash flow. The inflow comes from amounts owed to sellers, which rise with volume and reverse if volume falls, so the cash flow that can service debt in a downturn is better measured before working capital. Capital expenditure is 9.54% of revenue after a step-up in FY2025, and the annual report expects investing outflows to fall in 2026, although maintenance and growth spending are not split.

Earnings quality is the main gap between headline and conservative leverage. The issuer states only an adjusted EBITDA, and reported EBITDA, calculated from the statements, is 70.94% of it because stock-based compensation makes up most of the add-backs. Leverage is 1.76x on the reported basis and 1.25x on the adjusted basis, so low leverage holds on either basis, but the adjusted figure overstates the cushion.

The mechanism is the take rate: it is lower on connected television and in reserve auctions, connected television is 53% of revenue, and sellers and new entrants are building their own reserve-auction technology.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The credit strengths are these. Leverage is low and falling: total debt is 1.76x reported EBITDA, cash exceeds debt, and the term loan matures in 51 months, so refinancing pressure is distant and a shock would meet a balance sheet that does not need the market. Cash generation covers debt service 6.38x before working-capital changes, which leaves room for returns and investment without borrowing. Connected television is growing fastest, with Contribution ex-TAC up 39% in Q3 2026 against 5% for mobile and desktop combined, and the gross margin has widened from 61.26% in FY2024 to 66.62% over the last twelve months, so growth is converting into earnings. Financial policy has been creditor-neutral to supportive, with the convertible notes repaid from cash and no priming or debt-funded distribution. Disclosure is timely and audited with effective internal control.

### Company description

Magnite runs a sell-side advertising platform that connects publishers, mainly connected-television, mobile and desktop sellers, with advertising buyers through auctions and direct deals, and it earns a fee that is generally a percentage of the advertising spend it transacts, with most revenue reported net. Connected television is 53% of revenue in Q3 2026, and about three quarters of revenue follows United States sellers. The model supports debt service through a short cash cycle, since buyers are billed monthly on terms generally of seventy-five days or less, but it weakens revenue visibility because there are generally no long-term contracts, advertiser budgets weight the fourth quarter, and counterparties are concentrated. The history that matters to creditors is the 2021 acquisition of SpotX, financed in part with debt that has since been repaid or refinanced, and the 2024 refinancing that moved the term loan to February 2031.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is transactional and discretionary: fees depend on advertising spend, impression volume, price and mix, and on a take rate that is lower on connected television and in reserve auctions than on other channels. Connected television drives growth, with Contribution ex-TAC up 39% in Q3 2026, but connected-television inventory sits with a few large sellers that have negotiating leverage, buyers can press for fee concessions, and sellers and new entrants are building their own reserve-auction technology, so pricing power is limited. The issuer generally has no long-term contracts, so revenue is predictable only as far as the guided quarter, and the managed-service business that reports gross is down to 3% of revenue, which lowers reported revenue but not Contribution ex-TAC. Revenue is also exposed to substitution by seller-owned technology and direct buyer-seller connections, and to identity and privacy changes that could favour platforms with first-party data.

### Porter's five forces

Competitive rivalry is high: Google, Facebook, Comcast and Amazon are named as larger competitors with greater resources, which keeps fees under pressure and ties margin durability to share. The threat of new entrants is moderate and rising in reserve auctions, where new entrants and sellers building their own technology can lower fees. The threat of substitutes comes from sellers connecting directly with demand-side platforms or buyers and from walled gardens with first-party data, which would cut volume through the platform. Buyer power shows in requests for fee concessions and in two buyers owing 40% and 12% of receivables, so collection and pricing risk is concentrated. Seller power is high in connected television, where a few large sellers hold negotiating leverage, may demand guarantees or minimum spend commitments, and are consolidating, so the loss of one relationship could cut revenue and cash flow quickly.

### PEST analysis
