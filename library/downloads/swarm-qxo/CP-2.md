### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is that the structure is funded and not close to any test. Liquidity covers a replayed seasonal outflow 9.20x, no principal is due before 2030, the ABL covenant is not in force, the first two deals were paid largely with equity, preferred stock and cash, and tangible assets of USD 12,052 million cover gross debt 2.00x; TopBuild also adds an earnings base that is in no ratio, so if its earnings are large against the USD 3,000 million of added debt, leverage could fall below the pre-closing 4.51x on adjusted EBITDA. Most of the add-backs are tied to the acquisitions and should fall as integration ends.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_qxo-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-06-30_qxo-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 50; 10-Q_2026-06-30_qxo-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 62; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C-02; calculation C-14; calculation C-16; calculation C-20

Three developments would raise the risk assessment: a first combined report showing net leverage above the pre-closing level on adjusted EBITDA, organic sales still falling with adjusted gross margin below its second-quarter level, or ABL drawings to fund the first-quarter seasonal outflow. Three would lower it: combined results showing net leverage below the pre-closing level, a return to organic growth with stable gross margin, and cash dividends cut through share payment or early repayment of the incremental term loan.

### Limitations

- No TopBuild earnings, cash or debt are supplied, so leverage and cover after the closing are bounds rather than measurements; if TopBuild's contribution is large against the added debt, the conclusion that fixed charges exceed the earnings base would reverse.
- The margin on the incremental term loan is not stated, so the interest estimate assumes the existing term loan rate; a higher margin would deepen the shortfall in cover and a lower one would narrow it.
- The organic sales trend rests on the company's unaudited pro forma net sales for Beacon, which do not separate volume from price; if that base is overstated, the decline would be smaller and the trading conclusion weaker.
- No ratings, dated market quotes or peer set are supplied and the only market input is the fair value of the debt at 30 June 2026, so the comparison with other leveraged distributors is qualitative and the view of refinancing capacity rests on maturities, liquidity and that single pricing point.
- The downside figures repeat observed first-half 2026 trends on the pre-TopBuild base and pair them with post-closing charges; if the enlarged business behaves differently, the scenario outcome moves in either direction.

### Company description

QXO is a distributor of roofing, waterproofing and complementary building products in North America that grew from a small software business through three acquisitions in 14 months. In the second quarter of 2026 residential roofing was 39.0% of net sales, non-residential roofing 22.7% and complementary products 37.9%, and TopBuild adds insulation distribution and installation from 1 July 2026, so the mix is moving away from roofing and dilutes the demand evidence the business has built up.

Demand is mostly repair and re-roofing, which the company puts at about 80% of industry revenue with new construction the remaining 20%; that supports cash-flow visibility and debt service in a downturn, but results are seasonal and the earnings history is not clean, because Beacon is in the figures from 29 April 2025, Kodiak from 1 April 2026 and TopBuild not at all. Customers are many and small, so credit exposure to any one account is low, and about 97% of net sales are in the United States.

### Ownership and group structure

Control is concentrated: the founder held about 22.1% of voting power and his investment vehicle may designate 40% of the board while the 2024 investors keep at least 30% of fully diluted votes, figures stated as of the annual report and not updated for the 2026 issuances. The debt sits at the operating subsidiary, QXO Building Products, with Holdings pledging the borrower's equity and subsidiary guarantors behind each facility, while preferred and common equity are issued by QXO, Inc.; the filings do not say how cash reaches the parent to pay preferred dividends or whether the parent guarantees the debt. For creditors the points to monitor are the route of cash from the borrowing group to the parent and the restricted-payment limits, which the company calls not significant.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is meaningful but the industry is fragmented: the company describes thousands of small distributors alongside specialty distributors and big-box retailers competing on availability, technical advice, delivery, price and credit, and claims scale as the largest publicly traded roofing distributor. Scale helps purchasing and rebates, but the filings give no market-share, retention or entry-barrier data, so the strength of the moat is not established, and substitutes are not evidenced as a threat.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_qxo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-31_qxo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11

### Downside scenario

The downside that fits this issuer is a repeat of first-half 2026 trading on the enlarged capital structure. The initiating condition is a soft housing and re-roofing season in the first quarter of 2027, the seasonal low, with organic volume falling again and selling prices lagging cost increases from asphalt, steel or tariffs. The first operational break is gross profit: repeating the 5.05% volume decline and a 1.0 percentage point margin loss for twelve months would remove about USD 216 million of adjusted EBITDA, 29.79% of the pre-TopBuild base, with little cost relief because many expenses are fixed.
