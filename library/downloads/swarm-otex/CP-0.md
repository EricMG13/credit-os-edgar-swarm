## Findings

### Bottom line

The seven supplied documents are enough for a fundamentals-only credit snapshot of Open Text Corporation at 5 November 2026, but not for legal, ratings, market or peer work, so creditors should treat the post-October 2026 capital structure as management-described.

The documents rebuild five consecutive discrete quarters to 30 September 2026, four of which tie to the audited year, and they print debt, covenant, liquidity and outlook disclosure to that date. They end one day before the issuer replaced its USD 1.0 billion secured notes, so the latest balance sheet shows the old debt stack and the new one appears only in notes.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_otex-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_otex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 43–44; calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C3

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against a pass is that every document is written by the issuer, so debt terms, covenant ratios and the net leverage ratio of 2.55x are management statements that cannot be checked against executed agreements, rating agency views or market prices. A restricted verdict would also be reasonable for any work that needs the post-refinancing capital structure, because only the notes to the latest quarterly report describe it.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_otex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 43–44; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_q1-27pressreleaseexhibit991.htm.jsonl, page 3

The verdict stays at pass with limitations because the requested work is a fundamentals snapshot, and audited statements, quarterly statements, adjusted EBITDA reconciliations and the debt notes meet its evidence demands. It would move to conditional for legal or recovery work, which needs the executed documents. It would improve if a pro forma balance sheet for 2 October 2026 appeared, and the investor day scheduled for 10 February 2027 may change the strategy and the product-category basis.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-11-05_q1-27pressreleaseexhibit991.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_otex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 53–54
