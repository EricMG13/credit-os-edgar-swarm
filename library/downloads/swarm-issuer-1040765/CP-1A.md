## Findings

### Bottom line

Vornado Realty L.P. is a Manhattan-weighted office landlord with diversified tenants and rising occupancy, but acquisitions, joint ventures, development and buybacks under concentrated insider influence make its governance and capital-allocation risk medium for creditors.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_vno-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 53, 57, 58; 10-K_2025-12-31_vno-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 38, 153

New York earns 81.93% of revenue and its occupancy rose to 91.5% from 90.0% over nine months; no tenant exceeds 10% of revenue and the ten largest are 26.9% of rent.
Basis: calculation CALC-01; calculation CALC-07; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vno-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20, 58; 10-K_2025-12-31_vno-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 51

Capital is committed ahead of income: USD 230.00 million for a 49.0% stake in Park Avenue Plaza, USD 133.31 million of repurchases in nine months, and a USD 6,250.00 million development whose lender, partner and anchor tenant trace to one founder, with the partnership's cash contribution of about USD 290.00 million starting in 2028.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_vno-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 22, 27, 45, 53, 57, 73

### Key findings

Creditors lend to the operating company itself: the trust is its sole general partner and owns about 91.2% of common units, third-party unitholders hold the rest, and the partnership is the borrower under the bank facilities, so the trust's own claim sits behind the partnership's creditors.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_vno-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 18; 10-K_2025-12-31_vno-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 8

The portfolio is concentrated by market but not by tenant: New York produced 81.93% of revenues in the nine months to 2026-09-30, no tenant is above 10% of revenues and the largest is 4.5% of rent. Office occupancy rose to 93.0% and second-generation New York office space was signed 37.2% above prior straight-line rent in the third quarter, but street retail occupancy is 78.4% and THE MART fell to 79.5%, so the Other segment is the weaker part of the operating model.
Basis: calculation CALC-01; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vno-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20, 57–58; 10-K_2025-12-31_vno-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 51

Growth is being funded ahead of cash flow. The partnership paid about USD 230.00 million for a 49.0% stake in Park Avenue Plaza, USD 141.00 million for a redevelopment site, and has spent USD 254.27 million of USD 450.00 million on the Fifth Avenue office redevelopment due for delivery in 2027; the larger commitment is a USD 6,250.00 million joint-venture development in which its share is a USD 500.00 million net contribution of the site and about USD 290.00 million of cash from mid-2028, which competes with debt service and maturities in that year.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_vno-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 22, 25, 45, 53, 73

One founder's affiliates are lender, joint-venture partner and anchor tenant on the largest project: an affiliate lent USD 400.00 million at 4.00% in March 2026, owns 60% of the new venture and the founder's company is the anchor tenant, while the founder's company is also the third-largest tenant at 3.4% of rent, so the project concentrates financing, equity and rent in one relationship.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_vno-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 45, 73; 10-K_2025-12-31_vno-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 51

Control is concentrated but extraction is not documented. The chairman and chief executive of the trust also leads Alexander's, and Interstate Properties and its partners held about 7.1% of the trust's shares and 26.0% of Alexander's, which the annual report says gives them substantial influence; against that, related-party management fees are small (USD 0.20 million from Interstate and USD 5.72 million from Alexander's in FY2025), reporting is audited and no uptier, priming or non-pro-rata transaction is documented. Governance risk is therefore medium, driven by influence and capital allocation rather than by documented creditor-adverse conduct.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_vno-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 38, 123, 153–154

Shareholder distributions are modest against liquidity but real: the trust repurchased USD 133.31 million of shares in nine months to 2026-09-30 at an average of USD 29.41, authorised a further USD 300.00 million in April 2026, and pays one common dividend a year in the fourth quarter. The program can be paused, none was bought in the third quarter, and nothing in the sources links the buybacks to borrowing, so this is a mild negative for debt reduction rather than extraction.
Basis: calculation CALC-10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vno-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 53, 57; 10-K_2025-12-31_vno-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 60; 10-Q_2026-03-31_vno-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 41

Distress is confined to non-recourse assets so far. The USD 244.54 million loan on the Seventh Avenue office building matured unpaid in December 2025 and is under a forbearance agreement that waives default interest through March 2027, and a Broadway loan was extinguished through a sale in May 2026 with a USD 32.07 million gain; no recourse to the partnership is stated, but a joint-venture loan of USD 48.00 million also matured unpaid in October 2026.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_vno-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 23, 25–26; 10-K_2025-12-31_vno-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 127

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a medium risk rating is that the insider group holds only about 7.1% of the trust's shares, related-party management fees are small, reporting is audited and reviewed, repurchases are small against liquidity and paused in the third quarter, defaults were on non-recourse loans, and a partner funds early equity on the largest development. What would change the view is a debt-funded buyback or distribution, a related-party transaction on non-market terms, an amendment or exchange that weakens unsecured creditors, recourse guarantees in the Park Avenue development joint venture agreement, or a lower-than-expected share of joint-venture partners' funding; any of these would move the rating to high, while an end to repurchases and disclosure of the joint-venture terms would move it toward low.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_vno-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 38, 153; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vno-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25–26, 45, 57, 73
### Limitations

- The proxy statement is not supplied, so board independence, executive pay and the approval of related-party agreements are not assessed; this limits the medium governance rating, which rests on disclosed ownership, fees and reporting only.
- The credit agreements, indentures and forbearance agreement are not supplied, so legal capacity for restricted payments and asset transfers is not assessed and willingness is judged only from documented actions; this caps confidence in the capital-allocation view.
- Debt of unconsolidated joint ventures is not totalled in any source, so the view that development and joint-venture risk is moderate rests on consolidated figures and could be understated.
- The joint venture agreement for the Park Avenue development is not supplied, so the partnership's equity commitment beyond mid-2028 and any guarantees are not established; recourse terms would raise the capital-allocation risk.
- No report after the third-quarter filing signed on 2026-11-02 is supplied, so later transactions or defaults would not be seen and the events timeline ends there.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The 2026 financing steps changed the debt stack. In January the partnership refinanced one revolving credit facility to USD 1,130.00 million and upsized the other to USD 1,000.00 million, and upsized the term loan to USD 850.00 million; it sold USD 500.00 million of 5.75% senior unsecured notes due in 2033 and repaid USD 400.00 million of 2.15% notes at maturity in June; and in February it refinanced USD 525.00 million on One Park Avenue to 2031 at a wider spread. These moves push the bank facilities to 2029 and 2031, the term loan to 2031 and the new notes to 2033, at a higher coupon than the notes repaid.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_vno-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26–27, 56

The new debt that matters most is off the consolidated balance sheet: the USD 575.00 million fixed-rate loan on Park Avenue Plaza, the USD 3,300.00 million construction loan of the 350 Park Avenue joint venture and a USD 48.00 million joint-venture mortgage in default, none of which enters consolidated debt but each of which sits behind equity the partnership has committed or invested.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_vno-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 22–23, 45
