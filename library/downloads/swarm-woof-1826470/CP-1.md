## Findings

### Bottom line

Leverage has fallen sharply but is still elevated: net debt is 3.58x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA at August 1, 2026, against 6.58x at fiscal 2024, so creditors depend on the earnings recovery holding rather than on a thick cushion. On issuer-adjusted EBITDA the ratio is 2.78x, but that rests on about USD 94 million of add-backs, chiefly joint venture EBITDA and equity compensation, that cannot be tested against lender definitions.
Basis: calculation C21; calculation C22; calculation C23; calculation C20; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 95; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 22

Liquidity is adequate at USD 781.1 million, and the February 2, 2026 refinancing moved the term loan and the secured notes out to February 2031, but cash interest paid is running below interest expense because of timing, so interest cover of 3.25x on reported EBITDA flatters. The USD 75.0 million prepayment in September 2026 is a subsequent event; no pro forma balance sheet is supplied, so pro forma net debt and leverage are not established.
Basis: calculation C25; calculation C24; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 10–12; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 11

### Leverage trajectory and drivers

Reported leverage fell because earnings recovered and debt was repaid, not because add-backs grew. Net debt on balance-sheet carrying values went from USD 1,417.681 million at fiscal 2024 to USD 1,236.399 million at fiscal 2025 and USD 1,181.928 million at August 1, 2026, while reported EBITDA rose from USD 215.568 million to USD 317.143 million and USD 330.469 million on a last-twelve-months basis, taking net leverage from 6.58x to 3.90x to 3.58x. The fiscal 2025 gain came from a SG&A reduction of USD 132.7 million (2,184.639 against 2,317.351) on net sales that fell USD 155.0 million, and from a USD 95.25 million voluntary term loan repayment in the fourth quarter; the issuer links the SG&A decrease to lower payroll and consulting costs and improved actuarial results from employee benefits optimisation.
Basis: calculation C16; calculation C06; calculation C21; calculation C22; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 111; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 92–93; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 132

The adjusted measure tells a more favourable story than the reported one because the gap between them is large and moves with items the issuer defines. In fiscal 2025 the gap was USD 91.024 million: equity compensation of USD 32.65 million, USD 46.135 million for 50% of the Mexico joint venture's EBITDA, and USD 12.239 million of other costs; the joint venture is equity accounted, so its EBITDA is not cash the issuer controls.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 95; calculation C20

### Coverage and cash interest

Reported EBITDA covered cash interest paid 1.56x in fiscal 2024, 2.54x in fiscal 2025 and 3.25x over the last twelve months, but the last figure is flattered. Interest paid, net, in the 26 weeks to August 1, 2026 was USD 39.419 million against USD 62.441 million a year earlier, while interest expense was USD 65.34 million against USD 66.791 million, and the issuer attributes the stronger operating cash flow partly to the timing of interest and rent payments. The senior secured notes pay semiannually, so the timing of the coupon dates around the quarter end is not established.
Basis: calculation C24; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 11

### Cash-flow quality

Operating cash flow was USD 314.05 million in fiscal 2025 against USD 177.673 million in fiscal 2024, helped by a USD 63.119 million inventory reduction and partly offset by a USD 42.992 million fall in payables; capital expenditure was USD 127.097 million, leaving free cash flow of USD 186.953 million. In the 26 weeks to August 1, 2026, free cash flow was USD 60.796 million against USD 9.922 million, and the issuer cites timing of interest and rent payments, lower inventory purchases and net tariff refunds, with a USD 6.8 million net tariff benefit inside second quarter results. Operating lease cash costs run through operating cash flow, while finance lease principal of USD 5.614 million in fiscal 2025 sits in financing.
Basis: calculation C33; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 113; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 23–24; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 18

### Liquidity and refinancing

Liquidity at August 1, 2026 was USD 781.1 million, made up of USD 293.498 million of cash and USD 487.6 million of undrawn ABL availability after USD 58.4 million of letters of credit. The February 2, 2026 refinancing replaced the USD 1,500.0 million term loan, due March 2028, with a USD 900.0 million term loan and USD 600.0 million of 8.25% senior secured notes, both due in February 2031, at a loss on extinguishment and modification of USD 11.84 million and USD 28.442 million of cash refinancing costs and original issue discount. The undrawn ABL matures in March 2029, before the term debt, and the annual report says it must be repaid, refinanced or extended before that debt matures; no facility matures within twelve months of that date, and term loan amortisation is USD 9.0 million a year, so almost all principal falls due in 2031.
Basis: calculation C25; calculation C27; calculation C28; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 10–12; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 70

### Subsequent events and multi-figure items

Two events fall after the balance-sheet dates and are kept out of period figures. The February 2, 2026 refinancing falls one day after the fiscal 2025 year-end, so the January 31, 2026 balance sheet still shows the old term loan; the USD 75.0 million voluntary prepayment of the term loan in September 2026 falls after the August 1, 2026 balance sheet. Neither has a pro forma balance sheet in the sources, so pro forma net debt and leverage are not established.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 133; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 11; EX-99.1_2026-09-02_woof-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 2

The refinancing costs appear as three different figures for different purposes: a loss on extinguishment and modification of USD 11.84 million in the income statement, USD 28.442 million of refinancing costs and original issue discount paid in cash, and about USD 20.7 million of costs capitalised against the new debt (USD 1.4 million on the term loan, USD 9.0 million of original issue discount and USD 10.3 million on the notes). The cash figure serves net debt and liquidity, the expense figure serves earnings, and the capitalised figure explains the gap between principal and carrying value.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 8

### Definition and data-quality risks

The issuer's own net debt includes all finance leases, so it differs from the carrying-value measure used here by the non-current finance leases that sit in other long-term liabilities: USD 4.481 million at August 1, 2026. Adjusted EBITDA is described by the issuer as calculated differently under its debt agreements, and the lender definition and the covenant levels are not in the sources, so no covenant capacity is stated. Cash and equivalents at August 1, 2026 exclude restricted cash of USD 1.004 million, while the cash flow statement's cash includes it.
