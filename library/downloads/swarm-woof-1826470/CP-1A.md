## Findings

### Bottom line

Petco is a sponsor-controlled, lease-heavy pet retailer whose documented financial policy has been neutral to supportive for creditors, but the protections that matter to them sit in debt documents and a proxy statement that are not in the evidence. The sponsors hold about 65% of the votes through one holding vehicle, the company pays no dividends and has repurchased no shares, and it has prepaid USD 245.3 million of term loan voluntarily since fiscal 2023, so no extraction is documented.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 70; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 74; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 85; calculation D05

Operating risk matters more than control: comparable sales fell 1.6% in fiscal 2025 and stores keep closing, on a base where total lease cost is 9.1% of net sales. Fund life, sponsor economics and related-party fees are not disclosed, so exit pressure and fee leakage are not established.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 92; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 128; EX-99.1_2026-09-02_woof-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation D04

### Key findings

- Demand is mostly consumables and services, about 67% of net sales, with no customer concentration described, and vendor dependence is moderate. Consumables were 49.8% of fiscal 2025 net sales and services and other 17.2%, up from 16.3%, while the ten largest vendors supplied about 40% of sales and none exceeded 10%; sales to customers are substantially all on a cash basis, so receivable and customer-credit risk is small, but vendor terms and allowances drive working capital and cost of sales, and the auditor names vendor allowances as a critical audit matter.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 92; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 98; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 108; calculation D01; calculation D08

- Rent is the largest fixed claim and makes earnings sensitive to sales. Total lease cost was USD 542.55 million in fiscal 2025, 9.1% of net sales, across a pet care center base that is almost entirely leased and every distribution center, so a small sales fall compresses margins unless costs fall with it; the company has net closed stores, plans 15 to 20 more net closures in fiscal 2026, and cut selling, general and administrative expense to 36.6% of net sales in fiscal 2025.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 128; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 85; EX-99.1_2026-09-02_woof-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 92; calculation D04

- Sponsors control the company without an independent-majority check. The principal stockholder controls about 65% of the votes, sponsors can designate a majority of directors, four of ten directors are affiliated with them, the company uses every controlled-company exemption, and the corporate opportunity duty is waived; the creditor consequence is that sponsor-friendly actions are constrained by the debt documents and the dividend policy rather than by board independence, and those documents are not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 70–72; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 74

- Documented behaviour is creditor-neutral to supportive, and no extraction is evidenced. The company has paid no dividends, repurchased no shares and prepaid term loan principal of USD 75.0 million in fiscal 2023, USD 95.3 million in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025 and USD 75.0 million in September 2026, and the February 2026 refinancing pushed maturities out to 2031 at a higher cost, with the term loan's weighted average rate at 8.1% on 2026-08-01 against 7.3% on 2026-01-31.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 74; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 85; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 132; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 11; calculation D05

- The borrower and note issuer is a holding company, so creditors depend on subsidiary guarantees. The annual report says the company and certain subsidiaries are holding companies with nominal net worth that conduct business through an indirect operating subsidiary, Petco Animal Supplies Stores, Inc., and the guarantors are not named. This corrects the earlier source readiness description of the issuer as the operating company itself; the consolidated statements remain the right perimeter for debt, earnings and liquidity, but structural priority is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 74–75; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 150; Source readiness (CP-0)

- Trading has stabilised rather than recovered. Comparable sales turned positive at 0.6% in the 26 weeks of 2026 after a 1.3% fall a year earlier, but net sales rose only 0.1%, and the second quarter carried a USD 6.8 million net tariff refund benefit and a sales disruption from the membership relaunch, so the profit gain rests on cost and margin rather than growth.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 18–20; EX-99.1_2026-09-02_woof-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation D12

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a creditor-neutral reading is control without creditor-side visibility. The sponsors hold the voting majority, hold registration rights that allow large sales, are exempted from the anti-takeover provisions, and have waived the corporate opportunity duty, while the refinancing priced the term loan 100 bp wider and the adjusted earnings measure includes a 50% share of a joint venture's EBITDA, 11.3% of the fiscal 2025 total, that is equity accounted rather than consolidated. A debt-funded distribution, a transfer of assets to a non-guarantor subsidiary, a sponsor sell-down that changes control, or fees to sponsors disclosed in the proxy statement would move the view toward higher risk; restricted-payment limits that prove tight in the executed documents, a continued run of voluntary prepayments and sustained positive comparable sales would move it the other way.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 72–74; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 79; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 95, 132; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 10; calculation D09

### Limitations

- The executed credit agreements and indenture are not supplied, so the capacity of the company and sponsors to pay distributions, move assets or add debt is not established; this limits the behaviour conclusion to what was done, not what is permitted.
- The proxy statement is not supplied, so sponsor fees, related-party transactions, director independence by name and executive pay are not established; the governance view rests on the risk-factor description of control.
- Sponsor fund vintage, remaining fund life, entry price and economic ownership are not disclosed, so no exit-pressure or sell-down inference is drawn.
- The earlier source readiness description of the issuer as the operating company is corrected by the annual report's holding-company statement; guarantor identities and structural priority remain open.
- Nothing dated after September 4, 2026 is supplied, so sponsor actions or financings between that date and the cutoff are not established.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

On February 2, 2026, the day after fiscal 2025 closed, the company amended its First Lien Credit Agreement and issued USD 600.0 million of 8.25% senior secured notes due February 1, 2031. New term loans of USD 900.0 million, due February 2, 2031 and priced at Term SOFR plus 4.25%, together with the notes and cash on hand, repaid the USD 1,500.0 million term loan that was due in March 2028 and priced 100 bp lower. The refinancing removed the 2028 maturity. It cost USD 28.442 million in cash, of which about USD 20.7 million was capitalised, and it produced a USD 11.84 million loss on extinguishment and modification that includes a non-cash write-off. The new borrowings of USD 1,500.0 million equalled the principal repaid, so it raised no new money, and no sponsor equity is described.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 8; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 100, 133; calculation D10

The notes may be redeemed at a make-whole price before February 1, 2028 and at prices set in the indenture afterwards, and a change of control may trigger redemption; the indenture restricts additional debt, distributions, asset sales, liens and affiliate transactions subject to exceptions that are not supplied. The asset-based revolver was undrawn at 2026-08-01 with USD 546.0 million of commitments maturing on March 29, 2029, after a March 2024 amendment extended most of it, and its first USD 35.0 million tranche matured on March 4, 2026. The credit agreements name covenants on debt, liens, fundamental changes, investments and restricted payments and, for the revolver, a fixed charge coverage ratio, and the company reports compliance at 2026-08-01; the levels are not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 100; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 131–133; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11

In September 2026, after the second quarter closed, the company prepaid USD 75.0 million of the term loan from cash on hand, applied to scheduled payments in order of date; this subsequent event is not in any period figure of this module and no new debt issue is recorded after February 2, 2026.
