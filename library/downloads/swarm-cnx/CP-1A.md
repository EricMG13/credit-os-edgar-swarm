## Findings

### Bottom line

CNX is a scaled, largely hedged Appalachian gas producer with no controlling holder identified, so creditor risk comes from capital-return policy and a legacy coal tail rather than sponsor extraction, though ownership and board facts are not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 3

Share repurchases of USD 432.78 million in the nine months to 2026-09-30 exceeded issuer-defined free cash flow of USD 402.61 million, so leverage can rise without an earnings change, while the 2026 refinancing extended maturities and the convertible notes were settled in shares rather than cash.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 22–23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 35

### Key findings

Business risk is concentrated in one commodity and one basin, but most near-term price exposure is hedged. Natural gas is 92% of sales volumes and the Shale segment is 94% of them, so earnings track Appalachian gas prices; the issuer states that 82% of its expected 2026 gas volumes are hedged. The creditor consequence is that cash flow in 2026 is the best protected and later years carry progressively more price exposure.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 16; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_ex991supplementalq326.htm.jsonl, page 7

Capital return, not sponsor extraction, is the governance lever that moves creditor risk. The board raised the repurchase authority by USD 2,000 million to USD 4,900 million on 2026-01-29, and the company paid USD 432.78 million to buy back shares in the nine months, USD 30.17 million more than issuer-defined free cash flow; no dividend has been paid since 2016. Net revolver borrowings of USD 67.10 million in the same period covered the excess, so repurchases transmit directly into leverage if they continue at that pace.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11; 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 81

The 2026 refinancing was creditor-neutral to positive. The company issued USD 500 million of senior notes due March 2034 in February 2026 and repurchased all USD 500 million of senior notes due January 2029 at 101.6% of principal, extending maturity by five years at a lower coupon and paying a premium of USD 7.97 million, with a loss on extinguishment recorded. The new notes carry the same guarantor scope as the old, and no change in priority is stated.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 65

The legacy coal liabilities are indemnified by Core Natural Resources, not extinguished. The company settled a pension claim for USD 75 million payable over five years and Core has reimbursed every payment so far; a second suit under the Coal Act alleges that both companies are jointly liable, with a loss possible but not probable and no accrual. The credit consequence is counterparty risk on Core's indemnity rather than a balance-sheet liability today.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25–27

Governance evidence is thin because the proxy statement is not in the set. Board independence, holder percentages and related-party policy are not established, and the finance officers who signed the July 2026 quarterly report differ from those who signed the October 2026 report with no explanation in the set. Until the proxy statement is read, the absence of a controlling holder is a reading of silence, not a finding.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 178–180; 10-Q_2026-06-30_cnx-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 75; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 77

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against reading capital return as the main governance risk is balance-sheet room: USD 1,673.05 million of revolver capacity was undrawn at 2026-09-30, and the company used share settlement, not cash, to retire the convertible notes, so buybacks above free cash flow have so far been absorbed without a covenant concern, since compliance with every financial covenant is stated. The view would change toward higher governance risk if the proxy statement shows a holder with control or board rights, if the indentures cap restricted payments in a way that repurchases approach, if repurchases keep exceeding free cash flow while revolver borrowings rise, or if Core disputes its indemnity on the Coal Act suit. It would change toward lower risk if repurchases fall back below free cash flow and the proxy statement shows an independent board with no holder able to direct capital policy.
Basis: calculation C5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 22–23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25–27

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The company's 2026 debt activity was a refinancing, not new leverage. In February 2026 it issued USD 500 million of senior notes due March 2034 at par under an indenture dated 2026-02-26, guaranteed by the restricted subsidiaries that guarantee the CNX credit facility, and during the nine months it repurchased all USD 500 million of senior notes due January 2029 at 101.6% of principal for USD 507.97 million, recording a USD 12.01 million loss on extinguishment. The CNX credit facility borrowing base and elected commitments were unchanged at the 2026-05-13 redetermination.
Basis: calculation C7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 65

The equity-linked debt was removed without cash. On 2025-12-15 the company exchanged about USD 122.10 million of convertible notes for shares, and on 2026-05-01 the remaining USD 208.55 million of principal matured and was settled in newly issued shares, with the capped call transactions expiring and their shares retired. No convertible notes remain outstanding.
Basis: calculation C8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 23–24

Acquisitions and disposals were modest and are funded from operating cash flow and the revolver. The Apex Energy II acquisition closed on 2025-01-27 for about USD 517.60 million in cash, expanding central Pennsylvania Shale acreage, and the company is paying USD 16.50 million a year for three years for the right to acquire the Utica interests under that footprint; it sold about 7,500 acres of Marcellus rights in Ohio in 2025 for USD 57.09 million and monetised federal tax credits for about USD 30 million on 2026-07-01.
Basis: calculation C8; 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 134; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18
