## Findings

### Bottom line

Sponsor and governance risk for creditors is medium: the former sponsor has held no shares since January 2024, but the company has funded sponsor-exit repurchases, acquisitions and growing buybacks partly from borrowings, and the covenants that would cap this are not supplied.
Basis: calculation SE4; calculation CR4; 10-K_2025-02-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-08-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, page 8

The operating base is diversified, with no customer above 1% of net sales in fiscal 2024, so the governance structure drives this view. Creditors face a recurring cash claim from the tax receivable agreements: USD 685 million is owed to former owners and the management holding vehicle, with USD 41 million expected within 12 months. In the second quarter of fiscal 2026 debt principal rose USD 318 million while the company repurchased USD 169 million of shares.
Basis: calculation TR1; calculation RF5; 10-K_2025-02-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-08-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, page 20; EX99.1_2026-08-02_cnm_cnmq22026earningspressrele-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1

### Key findings

No sponsor controls Core & Main, so no sponsor-driven distribution or sponsor equity support is documented in either direction. CD&R, the former sponsor, sold down through secondary offerings, seven of them between April 2023 and January 2024, and held no Class A or Class B shares from January 25, 2024. The company bought 28.05% of the shares those sellers disposed of in fiscal 2023, for USD 1,344 million. What remains is a classified board, Class A shares that are in many cases held through brokers and institutions (three holders of record at February 2, 2025) and the management holding vehicle's 3.30% of the combined Class A and Class B shares, held as Class B; larger current holders are not established because the proxy statement is not supplied.
Basis: calculation SE1; calculation SE2; calculation SE3; calculation SE4; calculation OW5; 10-K_2025-02-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 68, 75, 110

The sponsor exit was financed partly with borrowings, and it is the clearest case of cash going to shareholders while debt rose. In fiscal 2023, the year of the USD 1,344 million repurchases, net borrowings on the revolver rose USD 430 million and cash fell USD 176 million. A new USD 750 million term loan on February 9, 2024 repaid that revolver balance and named share repurchases among its permitted uses, so the exit financing became term debt; the revolver stood at USD 93 million at February 2, 2025 and was undrawn at February 1, 2026 and August 2, 2026.
Basis: calculation SE4; calculation SE5; calculation SE6; 10-K_2024-01-28_cnm.htm.jsonl, pages 96, 113, 135; 10-K_2025-02-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, pages 79, 127; 10-Q_2026-08-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, page 18

Shareholder returns have grown and now exceed free cash flow, so leverage depends on earnings growth rather than debt paydown. In the first half of fiscal 2026 the company repurchased USD 257 million of shares and paid USD 42 million under the tax receivable agreements, USD 299 million in all against free cash flow of USD 112 million, while net new debt was USD 293 million. The authorization is USD 1 billion with USD 412 million unused at August 2, 2026, and a further USD 11 million was repurchased after the quarter end, so the pattern can continue. In fiscal 2024 acquisition spending was 4.21x repurchases of USD 176 million; in the first half of fiscal 2026 there were no acquisitions.
Basis: calculation BB2; calculation BB3; calculation CR1; calculation CR2; calculation CR3; calculation CR4; calculation AQ5; 10-K_2025-02-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, pages 70, 108; 10-Q_2026-05-03_cnm.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-08-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 10, 13; EX99.1_2026-08-02_cnm_cnmq22026earningspressrele-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1

The July 2026 refinancing lengthened maturities but raised debt principal. A USD 800 million term loan due 2033 and USD 750 million of 6.0% unsecured notes due 2034 repaid the term loan due 2028, so the first term maturity is now 2031. Debt principal rose USD 318 million in the second quarter and USD 320 million of the new borrowing exceeded the balance repaid; the offering notice named acquisitions and share repurchases among the uses and the quarterly report does not say how the excess was used. The notes rank equally in right of payment with other senior debt, while substantially all assets of the borrower secure the term loans and the revolver.
Basis: calculation RF1; calculation RF2; calculation RF3; calculation RF4; calculation RF5; calculation RF6; 10-Q_2026-05-03_cnm.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-08-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 16, 18, 19; EX99.1_2026-05-03_cnm_jun2026seniornoteslaunchpr-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1

Tax receivable agreements are the largest related-party claim and the main change-of-control exposure. USD 685 million was owed at August 2, 2026, USD 35 million less than the USD 720 million at February 1, 2026, with USD 41 million expected within 12 months; the payments do not depend on holders keeping their shares. Each agreement can accelerate on a change of control, a prolonged payment default or an early termination, and the CD&R investors' consent may be needed to refinance debt on terms more restrictive on those payments. The amount payable on acceleration is not established.
Basis: calculation TR1; calculation TR2; 10-K_2025-02-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, pages 56, 60, 61; 10-Q_2026-08-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, page 20

Acquisitions drove past growth and were funded with cash and term loan borrowings, but have paused. Ten acquisitions with USD 769 million of transaction value closed in fiscal 2024 and two with USD 76 million in fiscal 2025, and none closed in the first half of fiscal 2026. Sales grew 1.27% in that half and the quarterly report attributes the increase mainly to acquisitions, so organic growth, including seven greenfield locations opened in fiscal 2026, has not yet replaced purchased volume.
Basis: calculation AQ1; calculation OP1; calculation OP7; 10-K_2025-02-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, page 122; 10-Q_2026-08-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 33; EX99.1_2026-05-03_cnm_cnmq12026earningspressrele-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1; EX99.1_2026-08-02_cnm_cnmq22026earningspressrele-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1

The customer base is highly diversified, but sales are concentrated in one product family and in construction-linked end markets. No customer exceeds 1% of net sales and the top 50 are about 10%; pipes, valves and fittings were 67.28% of fiscal 2024 sales; non-municipal end markets were about 58% of sales on management's estimates; and the first half of fiscal 2026 absorbed USD 242 million of working capital. Gross profit was 27.0% of net sales in that half against 26.7%, so the margin cushion is steady.
Basis: calculation MX1; calculation EM1; calculation OP4; 10-K_2025-02-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 9, 14; 10-Q_2026-08-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 34

Disclosure supports monitoring of reported facts: the latest audited accounts carry opinions without qualification and effective internal control over financial reporting, and the quarterly report states covenant compliance at August 2, 2026. What lenders cannot see in the documents supplied is the covenant calculation itself or the restricted-payment capacity behind it.
Basis: 10-K_2025-02-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, pages 103, 144; 10-Q_2026-08-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, page 19

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for lower risk is that the structure has been handled prudently: the company has no approved dividend plan, the revolver was undrawn at February 1, 2026 and August 2, 2026, three term loan repricings since May 2024 have cut the margin, final maturities now start in 2031, and the quarterly report states compliance with all debt covenants. If fiscal 2026 buybacks and tax receivable payments are met from operating cash flow rather than new term debt and the next annual report shows principal falling, the view would move to low. It would move to high if a new controlling holder appears, if the credit agreements show wide restricted-payment capacity that the company uses for larger debt-funded buybacks, if tax receivable payments accelerate, or if any amendment subordinates or releases lenders.
Basis: 10-K_2025-02-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, pages 75, 79, 128; 10-Q_2026-08-02_cnm.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 19, 23
