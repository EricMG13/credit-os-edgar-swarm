## Findings

### Bottom line

Zebra's business and cash generation support its debt, but credit quality is constrained by a refinancing that must close before May 2027 and by buybacks that absorb nearly all free cash flow.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 26; calculation FC-4; calculation FC-6

Trailing unadjusted EBITDA of USD 1,141 million gives leverage of 2.25x total debt and 2.09x net debt with interest cover of 7.66x, so operating risk is moderate; the larger uncertainty is USD 2,077 million of current debt, against liquidity of USD 1,227 million that falls to cash of USD 182 million when the revolver matures on May 25, 2027.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 14, 26; calculation FC-1; calculation FC-2

### Key findings

Refinancing, not operations, is the central credit issue: USD 2,077 million of debt is current at October 3, 2026, 80.6% of principal matures by May 25, 2027, and liquidity of USD 1,227 million is mostly revolver availability of USD 1,045 million that ends with that maturity. Management intends to refinance in the fourth quarter of 2026, but no lender, amount or terms are stated, so creditors are exposed to the market window and to the terms Zebra accepts. Current debt is 2.10x trailing free cash flow of USD 988 million, which makes repayment from cash flow a fallback that works only if buybacks stop.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 13–14, 26; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation FC-2; calculation FC-3; calculation FC-4

Leverage is moderate on a reported basis but rests on an earnings base helped by one-offs: trailing EBITDA of USD 1,141 million includes a USD 73 million tariff refund benefit, and without it EBITDA is USD 1,068 million with leverage of 2.41x total debt and 2.24x net debt. Third-quarter gross margin of 45.7% was 230 bp below a year earlier on memory costs and mix, and at that margin across the trailing year EBITDA would be USD 197 million lower, lifting leverage to 2.72x total debt and 2.53x net debt with interest cover of 6.34x; this is an illustrative sensitivity, not a forecast.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 21–22; calculation FC-1; calculation FC-7

Cash generation is the main support but it is being returned to shareholders: trailing free cash flow of USD 988 million is 86.59% of EBITDA, yet repurchases of USD 643 million in the first nine months of 2026 equalled 97.28% of free cash flow of USD 661 million and 104.55% of it once USD 46 million of tariff refund cash is excluded. Debt carried on the balance sheet still rose USD 69 million in 2026 and cash interest rose 25.3% to USD 99 million, so free cash flow has not reduced debt.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 21, 26; calculation FC-5; calculation FC-6; calculation FC-10

Customer concentration and cost shocks are the operating risks that could break the cash flow: three distributors bought 59% of 2025 net sales and owed 58% of receivables, and the filings name neither the distributors nor their contract terms. Memory costs and tariffs reach gross margin before Zebra can reprice, and services and software, the only contracted revenue, were 18.1% of 2025 net sales.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 42–43, 109; calculation FC-11; calculation FC-14

The balance sheet offers little tangible protection: goodwill and other intangibles of USD 5,385 million are 61.1% of total assets and equity after deducting them is negative USD 1,817 million, and the Elo purchase of USD 1,303 million is 98.0% goodwill and intangibles at 3.2x implied annual sales with no disclosed profit. Creditors therefore rely on cash flow and on the receivables and inventory behind the facilities, and USD 868 million of receivables are pledged to the receivables facility against USD 124 million borrowed.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3, 15; calculation FC-12; calculation FC-13; calculation FC-15

Financial policy is the main discretionary risk and carries medium risk: no sponsor, dividend or related-party leakage is evidenced, but management has funded buybacks partly with revolver and receivables borrowing while the credit facility approached maturity, and the credit documents that would show payment limits are not supplied. Growth supports the earnings base meanwhile: organic growth was 8.3% in the first nine months of 2026 and operating income rose 37.8%.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-04-04_zbra-20260404.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-Q_2026-07-04_zbra-20260704.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation FC-14; calculation FC-17

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against the cautious view is that leverage and cover are moderate and cash generation is large: net leverage of 2.09x, interest cover of 7.66x and trailing free cash flow of USD 988 million, which would repay the current debt in about two years if buybacks stopped. Zebra also issued USD 500 million of senior notes in 2024, states compliance with all debt covenants at October 3, 2026, has revolver availability of USD 1,045 million until May 2027 and has declared no dividend since 1991, so near-term liquidity is not stretched before the revolver matures and the risk is concentrated in the refinancing.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2024-12-31_zbra-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 61; 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15; calculation FC-1; calculation FC-2; calculation FC-4

What would strengthen the view: a signed refinancing that extends the term loan and revolver well beyond 2027 with covenant levels disclosed and no added security, a stated buyback policy tied to leverage, and credit documents that limit payments to shareholders. What would weaken it: no signed refinancing by the end of the fourth quarter of 2026, buybacks above free cash flow, a debt-funded acquisition, gross margin below the third-quarter level of 45.7%, or a purchasing cut by one of the three distributors.

### Company description

Zebra designs and sells, through contract manufacturers, mobile computers, barcode and card printers, scanners, RFID and location systems, machine vision and self-service touchscreens with related software, supplies and services; FY2025 net sales were USD 5,396 million, split between Connected Frontline at USD 2,960 million and Asset Visibility and Automation at USD 2,436 million. The range and an installed base that pulls through supplies and services support cash-flow visibility, but 81.9% of sales are hardware and three distributors take 59% of sales, so visibility is partial and concentration limits it.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 7–11, 109; calculation FC-11; calculation FC-14

Recent growth was bought rather than built: Elo for USD 1,303 million in September 2025, Photoneo for USD 62 million in February 2025 and a sale of the robotics business in the first quarter of 2026, with segments realigned in the fourth quarter of 2025 so that segment history starts at FY2023. The consequence for creditors is higher intangibles and integration risk, and a debt-funded step up that has to be refinanced in 2027.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 88–89; calculation FC-13

### Ownership and group structure

Zebra is a Delaware corporation with one class of listed common stock and no sponsor or controlling holder named; holder percentages, board composition and related-party dealings sit in a proxy statement that is not supplied, and the guarantors of the senior notes are described only as certain subsidiaries. Without a sponsor there is no equity cure or extraction risk, leverage tolerance is revealed by the board's buyback authorizations, and the group structure includes a receivables financing subsidiary named in the exhibits, with lenders to the receivables facility holding first-priority security over U.S. receivables.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 56, 115; 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, page 15; calculation FC-15

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is a mix of cyclical hardware demand and contracted services and software; deferred revenue of USD 852 million and USD 1.14 billion of remaining performance obligations over about two years give visibility for services but not for hardware orders, whose backlog Zebra says is not a reliable indicator. Pricing power is partial: in the second quarter Zebra says it fully mitigated higher memory costs through price realization, yet in the third quarter gross margin fell 230 bp on memory costs and mix, so pricing has not kept pace with cost in every quarter. Organic growth was 8.3% in nine months 2026 with both segments growing, which supports the earnings base, but concentration in three distributors means a single buyer's decision can move revenue and collections together.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-07-04_zbra-20260704.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8, 21–22; 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 42; calculation FC-11; calculation FC-14

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Assembly is outsourced, so product costs flex with volume, but operating expenses, including research and development of USD 593 million or 11.0% of FY2025 net sales, are a largely fixed base that deleverages if sales fall. Margins can be defended against modest cost inflation through pricing, as the second quarter showed, but the third-quarter decline and the one-time USD 73 million tariff refund show that reported margins are not a clean run rate; the 2025 Productivity Plan is substantially complete, so little further cost relief is available. The consequence is that a margin squeeze would reach EBITDA quickly: the sensitivity at the third-quarter margin lowers trailing EBITDA by USD 197 million.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 16, 21–22; calculation FC-7

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital intensity is low: capex was 1.6% of 2025 net sales and USD 85 million to USD 95 million is expected for 2026, so free cash flow converts 86.59% of trailing EBITDA. Working capital absorbed cash in 2026 as receivables rose to USD 1,025 million from USD 801 million and inventories to USD 851 million from USD 729 million, and operating cash flow includes USD 46 million of tariff refund cash with USD 27 million still in receivables. Free cash flow is therefore strong but partly one-time, and it is available to repay debt only if buybacks give way to the refinancing.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 64–66; 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 16, 26; calculation FC-5

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense among large named competitors in each category, yet gross margin held at 46% to 48% from FY2023 to FY2025, which shows stable pricing over the cycle; the credit risk is the third-quarter margin decline, not a lasting loss of position. Entry barriers rest on channel relationships and installed-base switching costs, which management asserts and the stable margins support. Substitution by tablets and smartphones exists for some mobile computing uses and limits pricing in that segment. Buyer power is the sharpest force because three distributors take 59% of sales, and supplier power matters through sole-source components, memory costs and multi-year purchase commitments of about USD 101 million, so cost pass-through, not demand, is the main margin risk.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 14–15, 42, 66; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation FC-11

### PEST analysis

Trade policy is the one macro factor that materially alters earnings and cash: Zebra imports a significant share of its offerings into the U.S., a Supreme Court ruling in February 2026 invalidated certain tariffs, and a USD 73 million refund benefit was recognised, of which USD 27 million is uncollected. Interest rates matter because USD 2,078 million of principal floats, so a 100 bp rise adds USD 20.8 million a year and takes interest cover from 7.66x to 6.72x; currency is hedged for euro sales with a stated one percentage point sensitivity of about USD 2 million of pre-tax income. Technology transitions in Wi-Fi, 5G, Android and imaging create demand but also product cost, and no other political, social or regulatory factor is shown to alter credit quality.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 43, 70; 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 14–16; calculation FC-8

### SWOT analysis

Strengths that support creditors are cash generation, moderate leverage, breadth of products and regions and low capital intensity. Weaknesses that constrain them are the 2027 maturity wall, buybacks near free cash flow, customer concentration and an intangible-heavy balance sheet. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are a signed refinancing with a longer maturity, a buyback policy tied to leverage, and growth in services and machine vision that raises contracted revenue. Threats that could weaken it are a purchasing cut by a leading distributor, memory costs and tariffs, a slow or costly refinancing, and an impairment of the Elo intangibles.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation FC-2; calculation FC-6; calculation FC-11; calculation FC-12

### Financial profile and credit quality

The main credit supports are trailing free cash flow of USD 988 million, net leverage of 2.09x, interest cover of 7.66x and liquidity of USD 1,227 million; the constraints are the USD 2,077 million of current debt, buybacks near free cash flow and a margin base helped by a one-time refund. The trend is improving on earnings and flat on debt: trailing EBITDA is up 28.93% on FY2025 and trailing revenue up 13.81%, while carrying debt rose USD 69 million in 2026. Key missing datapoints are covenant levels, the refinancing terms, distributor contract terms and Elo's own earnings, which keeps the financial policy and refinancing conclusions provisional.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation FC-1; calculation FC-2; calculation FC-5; calculation FC-10

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months the outcome that matters is the refinancing: a signed facility removes the maturity wall, while a delay pushes the decision toward the 2027 dates when the revolver availability of USD 1,045 million is about to end. Company tailwinds are organic growth of 8.3%, broad-based growth across regions and verticals, completed cost actions and low capex of USD 85 million to USD 95 million; headwinds are memory costs, tariff exposure, distributor concentration and USD 616 million of remaining buyback authorization that can be used at any time. Over three to five years the technology transitions in mobile computing, imaging and RFID and growth in services and software are the supports, but no sales or earnings outlook is supplied, so the medium-term view is directional only.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 22, 26, 31; 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 66; calculation FC-2; calculation FC-14

### Qualitative downside scenario

The scenario starts with a purchasing cut or credit event at one of the three distributors while memory costs hold gross margin near the third-quarter level of 45.7% and the debt market is slow or costly in the first half of 2027. The first operating break is sales and collections falling together, because the same distributors owed 58% of receivables, so working capital absorbs cash as it did in 2026 and free cash flow drops below the pace of buybacks. At the third-quarter margin alone trailing EBITDA is USD 197 million lower, leverage is 2.72x total debt and 2.53x net debt with interest cover of 6.34x, and a refinancing priced 100 bp higher on the floating-rate debt adds USD 20.8 million of interest, so that combined interest cover would be 5.56x. The liquidity consequence is that revolver availability ends on May 25, 2027, leaving cash of USD 182 million against USD 2,078 million of principal due by then unless a refinancing is signed. Management can stop buybacks, which have USD 616 million of authorization left, hold capex near USD 85 million to USD 95 million and use factoring, so resilience appears sufficient to avoid a liquidity shortfall on these levers if access to the debt market stays open; the observable trigger is whether a refinancing is signed by the end of the fourth quarter of 2026.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 26, 31; calculation FC-2; calculation FC-3; calculation FC-7; calculation FC-8; calculation FC-11; calculation FC-18

The primary downside path is a distributor or margin shock arriving while the refinancing of the credit facility is still unsigned.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Zebra presents an adequate to strong business profile supported by cash generation, breadth of products and regions and low capital intensity, but credit quality is constrained by near-term maturities and by buybacks that absorb free cash flow. Cash-flow durability appears strong because free cash flow is 86.59% of EBITDA, although customer concentration and component costs can move it. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the debt market, the covenant and security terms Zebra accepts, and whether it pauses buybacks until the refinancing closes. The primary downside path is a distributor or margin shock before the refinancing is signed. Further analysis would require the credit agreement, indenture and receivables agreement, the refinancing terms, distributor contract terms and Elo's own earnings. Top risk: refinancing the credit facility before it matures on May 25, 2027.
