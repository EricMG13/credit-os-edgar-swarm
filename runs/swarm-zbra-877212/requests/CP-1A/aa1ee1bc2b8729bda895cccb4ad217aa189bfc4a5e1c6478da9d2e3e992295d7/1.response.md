## Findings

### Bottom line

Zebra is a widely held hardware group with no sponsor named, so creditor risk comes from management's cash use: buybacks were partly debt-funded while USD 2,077 million of debt falls due within a year, and the refinancing is intended, not signed.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 26; 10-Q_2026-07-04_zbra-20260704.htm.jsonl, page 27; calculation BG-8; calculation BG-11

Buybacks of USD 643 million in the first nine months of 2026 equalled 97.3% of free cash flow of USD 661 million, and exceeded all of it once USD 46 million of one-off tariff refund cash is removed. Three distributors bought 59% of 2025 net sales, so one customer's purchasing or credit matters more than the product mix suggests.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 21, 26; 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 109; calculation BG-9; calculation BG-13; calculation BG-1

### Key findings

Customer concentration is the largest operating risk: three distributors bought 59% of 2025 net sales, up from 44% in 2023, and the largest took 29%. The same three owed 58% of receivables at the end of 2025, so a purchasing cut or credit event at one of them would reduce sales and slow collections at the same time, and could erode the receivable base that backs the secured receivables facility. The filings name neither the distributors nor their contract terms, so contract length and termination rights are not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 39, 109; 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, page 15; calculation BG-1; calculation BG-14

Recurring revenue gives some visibility but little protection: services and software were 18.1% of 2025 net sales and fell to 16.1% in the first nine months of 2026 as hardware grew faster. Deferred revenue of USD 852 million and remaining contract value of USD 1.14 billion over about two years support service cash flow, while the 81.9% hardware majority depends on component costs and customer demand; the third-quarter gross margin fell to 45.7% from 48.0% on memory costs and mix.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 86; 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8, 22; calculation BG-2

Reported growth overstates underlying growth: nine-month 2026 net sales rose 19.0%, but organic growth was 8.3%, with acquisitions and disposals adding 8.5 percentage points and currency 2.2 percentage points. The acquired growth sits mostly in the Connected Frontline segment, whose organic growth was 8.9%, while Asset Visibility and Automation grew 7.7% organically; the profit on the acquired sales is not disclosed.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 22–25; calculation BG-5; calculation BG-4

Acquisitions have made the balance sheet intangible-heavy: goodwill and other intangibles of USD 5,385 million are 61.1% of total assets and equity after deducting them is negative USD 1,817 million, so creditor protection rests on cash flow and working capital, not tangible assets. Elo cost USD 1,303 million in cash, 98.0% of it goodwill and acquired intangibles, or 3.2x its implied annual sales of about USD 411 million, and its own profit is not disclosed; the auditor names the valuation of its technology and customer relationships as a critical matter.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 71–72, 88–89; 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3, 8; calculation BG-6; calculation BG-7

Buybacks are the main discretionary claim on cash and have been partly debt-funded: repurchases rose from USD 47 million in 2024 to USD 587 million in 2025 and USD 643 million in the first nine months of 2026, and Zebra states that it raised revolver and receivables borrowing to fund them in the first quarter and again in the first half of 2026. Debt principal rose from USD 2,183 million at the end of 2024 to USD 2,578 million at October 3, 2026, USD 616 million of authorization remains, the program can be changed or stopped at any time, and no dividend has been declared since 1991.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 56–57, 65–66; 10-Q_2026-04-04_zbra-20260404.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-Q_2026-07-04_zbra-20260704.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 31; calculation BG-8; calculation BG-10

Governance risk is medium on mixed evidence: Zebra names no sponsor, pays no dividend, shows no related-party items in its statements and carries unqualified audit opinions on its accounts and internal control, but the proxy statement and the credit documents that would show board control and payment limits are not supplied. The cover of the 2025 annual report ticks the box for correction of an error in prior statements without describing one, so whether any figure was revised is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 56, 71, 115–118; 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, page 30

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against caution is cash generation: free cash flow was USD 661 million in the first nine months of 2026, up USD 157 million, debt principal rose only USD 67 million since the end of 2025, cash rose to USD 182 million, the revolver had USD 1,045 million available, and Zebra states it complied with all debt covenants at October 3, 2026. Organic growth of 8.3%, a USD 73 million tariff refund and no disclosed dividend, sponsor fee or related-party leakage all point to a company that can fund both buybacks and a refinancing from operations.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 14–16, 26; calculation BG-9; calculation BG-10; calculation BG-13

What would strengthen the view: a signed refinancing that extends the term loan and revolver beyond 2027 with disclosed covenant levels, a stated buyback policy tied to leverage, and a proxy statement and credit agreements showing no controlling holder and tight payment limits. What would weaken it: buybacks above free cash flow before the refinancing closes, another debt-funded acquisition, a refinancing that is secured, shorter or more restrictive than the present facilities, or evidence that a leading distributor is cutting purchases.

### Limitations

- The proxy statement is not supplied, so board composition, holders above a reporting threshold and related-party dealings are not established; the finding that no controlling holder exists would change if a concentrated holder or a related-party arrangement appears.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 115
- The credit agreement, indenture and receivables financing agreement are not supplied, so the baskets that govern buybacks, acquisitions, liens and new debt are not established (the notes are said only to limit liens, subsidiary guarantees and mergers); the medium governance rating rests on observed behavior and would move to low or high if the documents show tight or loose payment limits.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 116–118
- Distributor identities and contract terms are not supplied, so the concentration finding cannot be tested for contract length, exclusivity or termination rights; long-term contracts would lower the concentration risk.
- No evidence is dated after November 3, 2026, so a refinancing, new buyback commitment or acquisition announced since then would change the view on maturity dependence and capital allocation.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, page 32
- Elo's own earnings, integration costs and synergies are not disclosed, so whether the purchase supports the debt it added is not established, and the 3.2x implied sales multiple cannot be set against profit; a profit disclosure could move the acquisition finding in either direction.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

No transaction is pending in the supplied filings. Elo Holdings was bought on September 30, 2025 for USD 1,303 million in cash, net of cash acquired, after a purchase agreement dated August 3, 2025; Photoneo was bought on February 28, 2025 for USD 62 million after an agreement signed on December 27, 2024 at about EUR 60 million; and the robotics automation business was sold to Skild AI in the first quarter of 2026 for USD 20 million of consideration, of which USD 9 million was cash, with a net pre-tax gain of USD 5 million.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 88–89, 116; 10-K_2024-12-31_zbra-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9

Elo and Photoneo were funded with cash and revolver borrowing: Zebra states that it increased revolver borrowing in the fourth quarter of 2025 to help fund Elo, and in 2025 it raised USD 347 million of debt and repaid USD 19 million while cash fell from USD 901 million to USD 125 million. The Elo purchase price allocation became final on October 3, 2026 after USD 4 million of adjustments to goodwill and deferred tax liabilities.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_zbra-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 65, 73, 75; 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, page 8; calculation BG-9; calculation BG-6

The filings describe no new debt instrument since the USD 500 million 6.5% senior notes due June 1, 2032, issued in the second quarter of 2024 for net proceeds of about USD 492 million; later borrowing was drawn under existing facilities, taking the revolver from nil at the end of 2024 to USD 445 million at October 3, 2026. Management intends to refinance the term loan and revolver, which mature on May 25, 2027, in the fourth quarter of 2026, but the filings state no lender, amount, structure or terms, and the separate receivables facility, with a USD 180 million limit and USD 124 million drawn, matures on March 19, 2027.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_zbra-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 61; 10-Q_2026-10-03_zbra-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 13–15, 26; calculation BG-10; calculation BG-12
