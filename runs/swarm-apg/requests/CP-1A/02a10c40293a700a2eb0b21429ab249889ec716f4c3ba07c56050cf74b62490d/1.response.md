## Findings

### Bottom line

APi Group is a diversified safety and specialty services business whose creditor risk comes from acquisition-led financial policy rather than sponsor control, because no controlling sponsor is named in the supplied filings and cash returned to shareholders is small beside acquisition spending.

Revenue is spread across two segments, many customers and several countries, with no customer above 5% of 2025 revenue, but the recurring share of revenue is not quantified. The Series A preferred holder, an entity controlled by a board co-chair, receives an annual dividend that cost USD 590 million of new shares for 2025; it is settled in shares at the board's election, so creditors face dilution of the equity base rather than a cash claim unless that election changes. Board composition, ownership percentages and the legal capacity to pay distributions are not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_apg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-K_2025-12-31_apg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 134; 10-K_2025-12-31_apg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 135–136; calculation CALC-4

### Key findings

The business is diversified by service line, geography and customer, and its largest dependency is Safety Services. Life Safety was 67.14% of nine-month 2026 net revenues, the United States invoiced 67.66% of them, and no single customer exceeded 5% of 2025 net revenues. About half of employees are covered by collective bargaining agreements, which limits how quickly labour cost can be cut in a downturn even though management describes its cost structure as highly variable. Mandated inspection work gives recurring demand in principle, but the share of revenue that recurs is not quantified, so cash-flow durability rests on management's own description.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_apg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13; 10-K_2025-12-31_apg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-K_2025-12-31_apg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 36; calculation CALC-1; calculation CALC-2

Acquisitions are the dominant driver of change and are paid for in cash at closing. In 2026 the three large acquisitions cost USD 271 million, USD 532 million and USD 394 million, and ten smaller ones cost USD 227 million, so the year's acquisition spending reached USD 1,424 million before any acquired earnings. The filings state no pro forma earnings or purchase multiples, so the leverage effect of these purchases cannot be sized from this module's evidence, and the seller of the largest was funds managed by Blackstone Tactical Opportunities. In May the company issued USD 500 million of 2034 senior notes and enlarged its revolver to help fund the later purchases.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_apg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9; 10-Q_2026-03-31_apg-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 30; 10-Q_2026-09-30_apg-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25; calculation CALC-3

Ownership is not disclosed in the supplied documents beyond one equity-linked instrument that matters to creditors. The Series A preferred, held through a vehicle controlled by a board co-chair, entitles its holder to an annual dividend equal to 20% of the rise in the share price above its previous high-water mark, applied to a fixed block of shares, which was USD 590 million for 2025 and was settled in new shares in January 2026. The dividend is payable in shares or cash at the board's election and the company states it intends to use shares, so the mechanism is dilution today; a cash election would create a large claim that ranks ahead of common equity, and the 2026 dividend and conversion into common shares fall at the end of 2026. A related entity of a co-chair also receives advisory fees of USD 4 million a year, which is small but is cash paid to a shareholder-linked party.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_apg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 134; 10-K_2025-12-31_apg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 135–136; 10-Q_2026-09-30_apg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 29–30; calculation CALC-4

Financial policy favours growth over deleveraging while keeping cash distributions limited. No cash dividend is paid on common shares and the credit agreement restricts distributions in some situations, but the company has USD 750 million of buyback authorisation remaining after repurchasing about USD 250 million in the nine months of 2026 and USD 75 million in 2025, and in 2024 it paid USD 600 million to repurchase half of the shares from the converted Series B preferred, partly funded by a USD 300 million incremental term loan. Buybacks alongside debt-funded acquisitions raise leverage, and the legal capacity for further distributions is not established because the credit agreement is not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_apg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 57; 10-K_2025-12-31_apg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 74; 10-K_2025-12-31_apg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 137; 10-Q_2026-09-30_apg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 56–57; calculation CALC-5

The finance function has changed recently and its systems are in transition. The chief executive has led the company since 2004, but the chief financial officer has held the role since March 2025 after serving as interim from December 2024, the principal accounting officer designation moved to the chief financial officer from August 2026, and a series of enterprise system implementations began in 2026 with the review of internal controls under way. This matters because reporting reliability underpins monitoring, and because systems and business enablement costs are being added back to adjusted earnings.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_apg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-06-30_apg-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 64; 10-Q_2026-09-30_apg-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 64

Disclosure supports monitoring of results but not of structure. Segment, geography and revenue-line data are given every quarter, but guarantor information, a list of subsidiary guarantors, covenant certificates and the proxy statement are not in the supplied set, and customer contract terms are not described beyond general statements. Creditors can follow operating performance but cannot confirm which entities stand behind the debt.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_apg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 2; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is that value going to shareholders has mostly been settled in shares or has been small: no cash dividend on common shares, a Series A dividend settled in shares, advisory fees of only USD 4 million a year, and USD 458 million of equity raised in 2024 to help fund the Elevated acquisition. The company has also used amendments to extend maturities and enlarge the revolver rather than to weaken creditor terms, as far as the supplied summaries show. A move to cash settlement of the Series A dividend, repurchases that exceed free cash flow for a sustained period, or a further large acquisition paid mainly in debt would move the view toward higher governance and financial-policy risk. Disclosure of ownership percentages, pro forma acquired earnings and the credit agreement's distribution limits would reduce the uncertainty in either direction.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_apg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 57; 10-K_2025-12-31_apg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 134–137; 10-Q_2026-09-30_apg-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 24; calculation CALC-4

### Limitations

- The proxy statement is not supplied, so board independence, director ownership and ownership percentages are not established, which limits the ownership and governance conclusion to the control features visible in the filings.
- The share of revenue from inspection, service and monitoring is not quantified, which leaves the business-quality conclusion resting on management's description of recurring revenue.
- No pro forma earnings are given for the 2026 acquisitions, which prevents sizing the conclusion that acquisitions drive the change in credit risk.
- No executed debt documents are supplied, which means the legal capacity to make distributions, buy back shares or add debt for acquisitions is not assessed in this module.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

In 2026 the company completed three large acquisitions in its Safety Services segment and ten smaller ones. CertaSite, a United States inspection-first fire and life safety provider, closed on 2026-02-02 for consideration of USD 271 million, of which USD 268 million was paid in cash at closing. Onyx-Fire Protection Services, a Canadian provider bought from funds managed by Blackstone Tactical Opportunities under an agreement signed on 2026-04-22 for about C$725 in cash, closed on 2026-06-08; the second-quarter report states consideration of USD 522 million and the third-quarter report USD 532 million, a difference of USD 10 million that is not explained. WTech Fire Group, a European fire sprinkler, suppression and detection provider agreed on 2026-04-17 for about EUR 324 in cash, closed on 2026-07-01 for about USD 394 million in cash. The ten smaller acquisitions cost USD 227 million, of which USD 36 million is accrued consideration.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_apg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9; 10-Q_2026-03-31_apg-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 30; 10-Q_2026-06-30_apg-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-06-30_apg-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 36

New debt in the period comprised USD 500 million of 5.750% senior notes due 2034, issued by the borrower subsidiary on 2026-05-14 with the net proceeds used for general corporate purposes including funding the announced acquisitions, and the Ninth Amendment to the credit agreement of the same date, which raised the revolver from USD 750 million to USD 1,000 million, lifted the letter of credit sublimit from USD 250 million to USD 300 million, and extended the revolver to 2031-05-14 and the term loan to 2033-05-14. The revolver was drawn USD 335 million at 2026-09-30 and a further USD 100 million after quarter end. These debt facts are stated here as transaction context only; their effect on leverage is for Financial foundation.
