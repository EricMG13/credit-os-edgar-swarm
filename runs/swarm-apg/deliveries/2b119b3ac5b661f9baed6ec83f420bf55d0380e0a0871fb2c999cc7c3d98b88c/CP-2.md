## Findings

### Bottom line

APi Group's credit profile is adequate but weakening: strong earnings growth in a diversified, inspection-led business has been matched by debt-funded acquisitions that lifted net leverage to 3.21x and cut liquidity, so creditors bear integration and cash-conversion risk until acquired earnings arrive.

The primary downside path is a squeeze on project margins and working capital while the revolver stays drawn and buybacks continue. Net debt of USD 3,201 million at 2026-09-30 excludes a USD 100 million revolver borrowing and a USD 143 million share repurchase after quarter end, and their effect on net debt is not established. Governance and financial-policy risk is at a medium level, and rating, market and legal-document evidence is absent.

### Key findings

The strongest support is earnings momentum in a diversified business. Nine-month adjusted EBITDA rose 20.11% to USD 896 million, trailing reported EBITDA reached USD 998 million, no customer exceeded 5% of revenue in 2025, and mandated inspection work gives recurring demand, although the recurring share is not quantified. Growth lowers leverage only if it persists, because the added debt is already on the balance sheet.

The weakest dimension is leverage after debt-funded acquisitions. ### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better view is that trailing figures include acquired businesses only from their closing dates, so leverage of 3.21x net may overstate the run-rate, and that earnings have grown 20.11% in nine months with no maturity pressure until 2029. If acquired earnings are disclosed and are material, or if the revolver is repaid from operating cash flow, leverage could fall toward the year-end level. The strongest evidence for a worse view is that interest cover on current expense is lower than on cash paid, that free cash flow conversion fell, and that the legal limits on further debt-funded buybacks and acquisitions are unknown. A further large debt-funded acquisition, a rise in revolver use, or a margin fall below the current 30.82% would move the view toward weaker credit quality.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation CALC-1; calculation CALC-2; calculation CALC-5; calculation CALC-9

### Limitations

- Ratings are stated only for 2025 in a lease note and no agency report or market price is supplied, so the refinancing conclusion rests on the issuer's own funding record rather than on evidence of current market access.
- The springing covenant is known only from its summary, so the liquidity conclusion cannot test the reported headroom independently and treats the 30% usage level as a signal, not a confirmed breach condition.
- Acquired earnings are not annualised in any supplied figure, so the leverage conclusion could be more cautious than run-rate leverage by an amount the filings do not state.
- Board and ownership detail is missing, so the medium governance risk level rests on documented behaviour alone and a higher or lower level cannot be excluded.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Credit strengths are the diversified, inspection-led service model, earnings momentum and a long-dated maturity profile. The service model spreads revenue across two segments, many customers and several countries, and inspection work is often required by law or insurance, which supports demand through a downturn; nine-month adjusted EBITDA growth of 20.11% shows that demand is converting into earnings. Debt matures late, with USD 614 million due in 2029, USD 335 million of revolver in 2031, USD 2,147 million of term loan in 2033 and USD 500 million of notes in 2034, so scheduled refinancing pressure is distant.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation CALC-5; calculation CALC-8

Credit weaknesses are rising leverage, thinner liquidity and weaker cash conversion. Net leverage of 3.21x against 2.10x at the year end reflects acquisitions and buybacks funded by debt and cash; liquidity fell by USD 625 million; and free cash flow conversion fell as working capital grew. A related weakness is the lack of evidence on legal capacity and ownership, which leaves the limits on further distributions and borrowing unknown.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation CALC-1; calculation CALC-3; calculation CALC-6

### Downside pathway and outlook

The realistic downside starts with slower contracting demand or higher tariff-driven material costs. Growth is concentrated in the lower-margin Specialty Services segment, which is 32.92% of revenue, grew 26.63% in nine months against 13.07% for Safety Services and earns a segment margin of 10.87% against 16.74%, so a slowdown there would be expected to hit earnings and working capital first. Lower volume and margin reduce earnings and leave working capital tied up, so free cash flow weakens while acquisition integration costs and buybacks continue; the revolver then funds the gap, undrawn liquidity falls below its current USD 1,032 million, and revolver use stays above the 30% level at which the first lien test applies. Management has levers: buybacks are discretionary with USD 607 million of authorisation remaining at 2026-10-29, acquisitions can be paused, and capital expenditures are typically below 1.5% of revenue, although about half of employees are covered by collective bargaining agreements, which limits discretion over labour cost. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid distress in a moderate downturn because no scheduled maturity of size falls due before 2029, but the reported covenant headroom is wide and has not been tested independently.

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months, tailwinds are regulation-driven inspection demand, infrastructure and technology end markets and the 20.11% earnings growth already shown; headwinds are tariffs, material cost and the integration of three large acquisitions. Management raised full-year guidance for net revenues and adjusted EBITDA in each of the three 2026 releases, to USD 1,235 million to USD 1,255 million of adjusted EBITDA in the latest, but lowered its adjusted free cash flow conversion guidance to 110% from 115% of adjusted net income. The guidance supports the near-term earnings view and is management outlook, not evidence, and the lower conversion guidance fits the weaker cash conversion shown above.
