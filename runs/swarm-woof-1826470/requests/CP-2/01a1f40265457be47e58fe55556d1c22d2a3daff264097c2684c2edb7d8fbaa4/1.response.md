## Findings

### Bottom line

Petco's credit is improving but still leveraged: cost cuts lifted reported earnings and cut net debt to 3.58x EBITDA, and liquidity of USD 781.1 million with maturities pushed to 2031 buys time, yet flat sales leave the recovery unproven.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation E01; calculation E03

Coverage is thinner than the headline suggests, with EBITDA covering interest expense 2.55x and EBITDA before rent covering rent plus cash interest 1.36x, and the documents that would show covenant room and ranking are not in the evidence. The primary downside is a sales relapse on a rent-heavy cost base, which the scenario below puts at 4.33x net leverage before any cost response.
Basis: calculation E07; calculation E12; calculation E22

### Key findings

- Leverage is falling for the right reasons but remains the main constraint. Net debt over reported EBITDA fell from 6.58x at fiscal 2024 to 3.90x at fiscal 2025 and 3.58x over the twelve months to August 1, 2026, because earnings recovered and free cash flow was kept rather than distributed, not because add-backs grew; on the issuer's adjusted EBITDA the ratio is 2.78x, but that rests on about USD 94 million of add-backs that cannot be tested against the lender's definition. Net debt and leverage after the February 2026 refinancing and the September 2026 prepayment are not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation E01; calculation E02

- Coverage is adequate on cash paid but only moderate on any steadier measure. EBITDA covered cash interest 3.25x over the last twelve months, yet cash interest ran USD 28.10 million below interest expense because of payment timing, so cover on interest expense is 2.55x and on the run-rate coupons of the new debt 2.70x; after rent, EBITDA plus rent covered rent plus cash interest only 1.36x in fiscal 2025.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation E05; calculation E06; calculation E07; calculation E08; calculation E10; calculation E11; calculation E12

- Cash generation is the strongest support, but part of it is timing. Free cash flow was USD 186.95 million in fiscal 2025 and USD 237.83 million over the last twelve months, equal to 16.12% of total debt; removing the interest timing gap leaves USD 209.73 million, still enough to take net debt to the issuer's 2x adjusted target in about 1.59 years at constant adjusted EBITDA.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation E04; calculation E09; calculation E21

- Liquidity and the maturity profile are the main protection. Liquidity of USD 781.1 million covers the USD 40.50 million of scheduled term loan payments to 2030 by 19.29x, and 97.30% of principal falls due in 2031, but the undrawn revolver matures in March 2029, ahead of the term debt, and the covenant terms that govern access to it are not supplied.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation E03; calculation E15; calculation E16

- The operating recovery rests on cost cuts, not growth. Selling, general and administrative expense fell USD 132.71 million in fiscal 2025 against a USD 155.0 million fall in net sales, and comparable sales turned from a 1.6% decline to a 0.6% gain in the first half of 2026, but net sales rose only 0.1% and the second quarter included a USD 6.8 million net tariff refund, without which the issuer puts second quarter adjusted EBITDA at USD 115.4 million.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation E18; calculation E27; calculation E28

- Rent makes the cost base rigid and earnings sensitive to sales. Operating lease cost was 1.30x reported EBITDA in fiscal 2025 and total lease cost was 9.1% of net sales, so a modest sales fall compresses margin unless costs fall with it; cost and capex flexibility is real but already used, since capital expenditure fell USD 98.50 million between fiscal 2023 and fiscal 2025 and is expected to rise to about USD 140 million in fiscal 2026.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation E14; calculation E19; calculation E30; EX-99.1_2026-09-02_woof-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 2

- Financial policy is creditor-aligned, but control and structure limit what can be assumed. The company pays no dividends and has prepaid USD 245.25 million of term loan since fiscal 2023, yet the sponsors control about 65% of the votes, the borrower is a holding company with unnamed guarantors, and the lender's definition of EBITDA, the covenant levels and the ranking of the debt are not supplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation E24; calculation E32

- Interest cost is rising even as debt falls. The term loan's weighted average rate was 8.1% on August 1, 2026 against 7.3% on January 31, 2026, about 59.94% of principal is floating, and 100 bp on the floating term loan would cost USD 8.98 million a year before any hedge, whose terms are not supplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation E17; calculation E31

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against the improving view is that trading has not yet recovered: net sales are flat, supplies and companion animals sales are still falling, the footprint is shrinking, and the profit gain came from cost cuts and a one-off refund while fixed rent absorbs any shortfall. If comparable sales turned negative again, the issuer's fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA outlook of USD 415 million to USD 430 million proved too high, or the revolver could not be extended before 2029, the view would move toward weaker; two further quarters of positive comparable sales with cost savings holding, continued voluntary prepayments and adjusted net leverage reaching the issuer's 2x target would move it toward stronger.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-09-02_woof-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation E20; calculation E21; calculation E30

### Limitations

- No peer, market-share or industry-growth data is supplied, so the judgements on scale, competitive advantage and diversification rest on the issuer's own account of its markets; this limits how firmly the Average ratings on those dimensions can be held, not the leverage or cover figures.
- The downside scenario applies one illustrative sales decline at the reported gross profit rate with no cost response; it shows the direction and rough size of the pressure on leverage and cover, not a forecast of either.
- The fixed charge style cover and lease-adjusted leverage are the analyst's own definitions built from reported lease cost and lease liabilities; they are not the lender's definitions and must not be read as covenant capacity.
- Run-rate interest uses a weighted average rate printed to one decimal and ignores hedges whose terms are not supplied, so the interest sensitivity could be overstated or understated.
- The view stops at the second quarter 2026 report and release; the September 2026 prepayment is reflected only as a stated event, and trading since then is not known.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The main strengths are recovering earnings and falling leverage, liquidity of USD 781.1 million with almost all principal due in 2031, repeat consumables and growing services at about two thirds of sales, a financial policy that has favoured creditors, and demonstrated cost and capital expenditure flexibility. The main weaknesses are leverage that is still elevated with thin cover after rent, flat sales in a shrinking footprint, a rent-heavy fixed cost base, earnings that lean on issuer add-backs and one-off items, and sponsor control with unnamed guarantors and unseen covenants.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation E03; calculation E12; calculation E15

### Company description

Petco is a United States pet specialty retailer that sold USD 5,961.467 million in fiscal 2025, with consumables at 49.8% of net sales, supplies and companion animals at 33.0% and services and other, mainly veterinary care, grooming and training, at 17.2%, up from 16.3%. It sells through leased pet care centers, an e-commerce site and an app, and sales are substantially all on a cash basis, so receivable risk is small. The credit linkage is that roughly two thirds of sales are consumables and services that recur through the year, which supports cash-flow visibility and debt service, while the shrinking store base, with 16 net closures in fiscal 2025 and 15 to 20 more planned, means the visibility is of a smaller business each year. The 2016 sponsor acquisition, the 2021 listing and the February 2026 refinancing are the history that shapes the capital structure.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 92; calculation E25; calculation E33; EX-99.1_2026-09-02_woof-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 2

### Ownership and group structure

Two sponsors, CVC funds and Canada Pension Plan Investment Board, control the company through Scooby Aggregator, LP, which holds about 65% of the votes, can designate a majority of directors and benefits from controlled-company exemptions, so there is no independent-majority check. The borrower and note issuer is itself a holding company with nominal net worth that operates through an indirect subsidiary, and the guarantors are not named, so structural priority cannot be established even though consolidated figures describe the group. The credit implication is that sponsor-friendly action is limited by the debt documents and not by the board, which the evidence has not let us read; conduct so far has been creditor-aligned, with no dividends, no repurchases and three voluntary prepayments totalling USD 245.25 million.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation E24

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Sales are partly recurring and partly discretionary, and the discretionary part is weakening. Consumables fell 2.5% in fiscal 2025 and were flat in the first half of 2026, supplies and companion animals fell 5.1% and then 2.3%, and services grew 2.6% and then 5.2%, so the mix is shifting toward services, which carry veterinary labour cost but differentiate the offer from online sellers. The issuer attributes the fiscal 2025 sales decline to lower transaction volume and a lower center count, and says competitors have cut prices and forced it to cut prices on some products, so pricing power is limited. No customer concentration is described and no vendor exceeds 10% of sales, so the credit risk is volume and price in a contested market, not concentration.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 92; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 19; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation E26

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins can be defended against modest volume pressure but not against a repeat of fiscal 2025's sales decline without further cost action. Total lease cost was 9.1% of net sales and operating lease cost alone was 1.30x reported EBITDA, so rent is the largest fixed claim after debt service; selling, general and administrative expense is also largely labour and is exposed to wage inflation and veterinary shortages. Management cut that expense by USD 132.71 million in fiscal 2025 and lifted the gross profit rate from 38.0% to 38.7%, so the cost lever has worked once; the reported EBITDA margin rose from 3.52% in fiscal 2024 to 5.32% in fiscal 2025 and 5.54% over the last twelve months, but part of the first half 2026 gross margin gain is the tariff refund and normalised second quarter gross margin was about flat.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 24, 41; EX-99.1_2026-09-02_woof-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation E14; calculation E18; calculation E29

### Capital intensity and free cash flow conversion

Free cash flow conversion is solid and capital expenditure is flexible, but the last twelve months flatter it. Capital expenditure was USD 127.097 million in fiscal 2025, 2.1% of net sales, split between about USD 54.06 million for pet care centers, USD 53.572 million for digital and technology and USD 19.465 million for supply chain and other, and down from USD 225.598 million in fiscal 2023 on fewer new centers and hospitals; free cash flow of USD 186.953 million in fiscal 2025 was helped by a USD 63.119 million inventory reduction and the last twelve months by interest paid USD 28.10 million below expense. Operating leases run through operating cash flow, so EBITDA is already after rent, and the fixed charge style cover of 1.36x shows how little room rent and interest leave.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 98–99; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation E04; calculation E06; calculation E08; calculation E12; calculation E19

### Porter's five forces

Rivalry is the force that matters most. Specialty chains, independent stores, online retailers, supermarkets, warehouse clubs and mass merchants all sell the same consumables, some with more capital, and price cuts by competitors have forced Petco to reduce prices, which caps gross margin and makes comparable sales the key swing factor for leverage. New entrants are a moderate threat, mainly independent stores with distinctive offers and online sellers; substitutes are strong for products, because the same goods can be bought online or at a grocer, and weaker for services, where veterinary care, grooming and training anchor the visit. Buyer power is high because consumers can switch freely and discretionary spending has softened, while supplier power is moderate: the ten largest vendors supplied about 40% of sales, none above 10%, vendors may leave on 90 days' notice, but owned brands carry higher margins and vendor allowances reduce cost of sales.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 15, 22–23, 108; calculation E33

### PEST

Two factors are material to credit and the others are supportive background. Tariffs are the political factor: the issuer sources from Asia, tariffs and fuel have raised costs before, and a USD 6.8 million net refund flattered the second quarter, so cost pass-through is uncertain and the refund is not a run-rate benefit. Economic conditions matter through softening discretionary spending, which hits supplies and companion animals, and through floating rates on about 59.94% of principal; pet humanization and premiumization and a steady pet population are social tailwinds that support consumables and services, and e-commerce is the technological force that raises price transparency.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 29, 36; 10-Q_2026-08-01_woof-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 20; calculation E17; calculation E27

### SWOT

- Strengths: repeat consumables and growing services; liquidity of USD 781.1 million; creditor-aligned financial policy; cost and capital expenditure flexibility; free cash flow of USD 237.83 million over the last twelve months.
- Weaknesses: leverage of 3.58x on reported EBITDA with thin cover after rent; a rent-heavy, labour-heavy fixed cost base; flat sales and a shrinking footprint; earnings that lean on issuer add-backs; sponsor control and unnamed guarantors.
- Opportunities that could improve credit: services growth, a membership relaunch that lifts repeat visits, owned brands with higher margins, and prepayments that reach the 2x adjusted leverage target.
- Threats that could weaken credit: a renewed fall in comparable sales, competitor price cuts, tariff and wage costs, a failure to extend the revolver before 2029, and sponsor action funded by debt.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation E03; calculation E04

### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale and market position are Average: the business is large, but market share is not supplied and the footprint is shrinking. Competitive advantage is Average because services, owned brands and an omni-channel offer differentiate it from pure retailers while rivals cut prices. Diversification is Average: three product groups, services and several channels, but one segment and one country, and no customer concentration is described. Cost and capex flexibility is Average because savings were delivered in fiscal 2025 and capital expenditure was cut by USD 98.50 million over two years, yet rent is fixed. Margin stability is Weak, though improving, because reported EBITDA margin moved from 3.52% to 5.32% in a year while sales fell. Free cash flow stability is Weak on history, being negative in fiscal 2023 and USD 49.68 million in fiscal 2024 before USD 186.95 million in fiscal 2025, with timing items in the latest year. Ability to refinance is Average: the February 2026 refinancing extended maturities to 2031 at a higher cost, no ratings are supplied and the revolver must be extended by 2029. Liquidity is Strong at USD 781.1 million with minimal amortisation, subject to covenant terms not seen. Financial policy and governance is Average: conduct is creditor-aligned but control is concentrated and disclosure of covenants and guarantors is not available. The main supports are liquidity, cash generation and policy, the main constraints are leverage, rent and flat sales, the trend is improving, and the missing data are covenant levels, ranking, peer data and pro forma debt.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation E01; calculation E19; calculation E29

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months the issuer guides fiscal 2026 net sales flat to up 1.5%, adjusted EBITDA of USD 415 million to USD 430 million, net interest expense of about USD 122 million and capital expenditure of about USD 140 million; those figures imply second-half adjusted EBITDA of USD 195.45 million to USD 210.45 million against USD 204.86 million a year earlier, so the guide assumes flat earnings, not recovery, and any miss reaches leverage quickly. The coupon arithmetic of USD 122.22 million agrees with the interest guide, and the September prepayment saves about USD 6.1 million a year at the term loan rate. Reaching the 2x adjusted target needs USD 333.11 million less net debt at constant adjusted EBITDA, about 1.40 years of last twelve months free cash flow. Over three to five years the revolver must be extended before 2029 and the 2031 balloon refinanced; sector tailwinds are pet humanization and a steady pet population, headwinds are competition, softer discretionary spending, tariffs and wage costs, and company tailwinds are services growth and the membership relaunch against higher interest cost and the loss of the tariff refund.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-09-02_woof-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 11; calculation E10; calculation E20; calculation E21; calculation E23

### Downside scenario

The realistic downside is a relapse in sales while the membership relaunch and store closures are still working through, on a base where rent is fixed. If net sales fell 2.5% from the last twelve months level, the same fall as in fiscal 2025, the gross profit lost at the fiscal 2025 rate of 38.7% would be about USD 57.72 million; with no cost response, reported EBITDA would fall to USD 272.75 million, net leverage would rise from 3.58x to 4.33x, EBITDA cover of interest expense would fall from 2.55x to 2.10x and cover of run-rate coupons from 2.70x to 2.23x. That is a deliberate upper bound for volume loss, as management cut USD 132.71 million of expense in fiscal 2025 and can cut capital expenditure, and resilience looks sufficient to avoid liquidity distress, because liquidity of USD 781.1 million covers scheduled amortisation many times over and nothing falls due before the revolver in 2029; the financial transmission is to leverage, cover and covenant headroom, not to liquidity, and the observable triggers are comparable sales, the gross profit rate and the revolver extension.
Basis: calculation E22; calculation E18; calculation E16; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2026-01-31_woof-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 92

### Overall credit view

Overall, Petco presents an adequate business profile supported by repeat consumables, growing services and a creditor-aligned financial policy, but credit quality is constrained by leverage of 3.58x on reported EBITDA, thin cover after rent and flat sales. Cash-flow durability appears adequate, with free cash flow of USD 237.83 million over the last twelve months, of which USD 28.10 million is interest timing. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on comparable sales holding, the revolver being extended before 2029 and the 2031 balloon being addressed from a lower leverage base. The primary downside path is a sales relapse on the rent-heavy cost base. Further analysis would require the executed debt documents, the lender's EBITDA definition, peer data and a pro forma balance sheet. A comparison with typical leveraged retail issuers is not established because no peer data is supplied. Top risk: a sales relapse on a rent-heavy cost base.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation E01; calculation E04; calculation E22
