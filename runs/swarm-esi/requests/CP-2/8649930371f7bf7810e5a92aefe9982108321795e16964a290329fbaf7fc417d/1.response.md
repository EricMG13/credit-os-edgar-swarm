## Findings

### Bottom line
Element Solutions has an adequate fundamental credit profile: consumable-driven, diversified earnings and 6.59x interest cover support debt service, but debt-funded 2026 acquisitions lifted net leverage to 3.29x, so creditors rely on acquired earnings arriving before the USD 800.0 million notes mature in 2028.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C05

Net leverage was 2.13x at the 2025 year-end and is 3.96x pro forma for the two acquisitions closed on 2026-01-02 and 2026-02-02, before any acquired earnings; on issuer-adjusted EBITDA it is 2.76x at 2026-09-30. Cash plus undrawn revolver of USD 702.9 million covers 0.88x of the 2028 notes, and covenant headroom cannot be measured.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C05; calculation C08

### Key findings
Revenue is durable but tied to one cycle. Customers reorder products consumed in their production lines and no customer reaches 10% of net sales, but Electronics is 77.5% of nine-month 2026 net sales and 76.2% of segment Adjusted EBITDA, order backlog is minimal and organic growth of 13% leans on AI infrastructure demand, so a demand slowdown would reach earnings within quarters.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_esi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 16; EX-99.1_2026-10-21_aearningsreleaseex99120269.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 8

Margin resilience is better than reported margins suggest. Gross margin fell to 35.51% in nine months 2026 from 42.34%, and Adjusted EBITDA over total net sales fell by 2.86 percentage points to 19.08%, but pass-through metals of 31.7% of net sales add sales at little margin, and the issuer's margin excluding them rose by 110 bp to 27.9%. Creditors should therefore track EBITDA dollars and the ex-metals margin, not margin on total sales.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C03; EX-99.1_2026-10-21_aearningsreleaseex99120269.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9; 10-K_2025-12-31_esi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 62

Cash conversion weakened in 2026 and is the main watch point. Working capital absorbed USD 174.1 million in nine months 2026 against USD 78.4 million a year earlier as metal prices rose, so operating cash flow less capital expenditure was USD 108.8 million against USD 153.5 million, and dividends and buybacks of USD 97.0 million took most of it. The last-twelve-months figure of USD 182.9 million shows the drag is partly timing.
Basis: calculation C04; calculation C09; 10-Q_2026-09-30_esi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_esi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 45

Leverage is the weakest dimension. Net debt of USD 1,857.2 million is 3.29x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA, or 2.76x issuer-adjusted EBITDA, because debt was drawn in full for acquisitions whose earnings enter from closing; the reported ratio therefore overstates run-rate leverage, while the adjusted ratio depends on add-backs that include USD 52.6 million of integration and transaction costs in nine months 2026. A USD 100.0 million shortfall in last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA would lift net leverage on that measure from 2.76x to 3.24x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C07

Liquidity is adequate and the maturity wall is distant but not covered. Cash of USD 224.9 million and USD 478.0 million of undrawn revolver total USD 702.9 million against USD 800.0 million of notes due 2028-09-01, a USD 97.1 million shortfall that cash generation or a refinancing must close. The springing first lien test applies only if revolver borrowings exceed USD 150.0 million, USD 135.0 million above today's draw, and the reported covenant ratio is not printed.
Basis: calculation C05; 10-Q_2026-09-30_esi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–18; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Interest rate risk returns before the 2030 maturities. About 94% of debt is fixed through swaps, which hold the effective term loan rate near 4.4%, but swaps covering USD 690 million expire in December 2028 and USD 483 million in December 2029, and refinancing the 2028 notes at a higher coupon adds USD 8.0 million of annual interest for each 100 bp, 9.3% of last-twelve-months cash interest.
Basis: calculation C06; 10-Q_2026-09-30_esi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; EX-99.1_2026-10-21_aearningsreleaseex99120269.htm.jsonl, page 9

Financial policy risk is medium. No controlling sponsor is identified and the dividend is modest, but acquisitions and buybacks proceeded while leverage rose, and the merger with Solstice was agreed and terminated within weeks, which shows appetite for transformative deals.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_esi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 12

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest argument for a stronger profile is that leverage is lower than it looks. Issuer-adjusted net leverage is 2.76x, interest cover is 6.59x, 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance was raised to about USD 720 million including full-year contributions from both acquisitions, and last-twelve-months operating cash flow less capital expenditure of USD 182.9 million compares with USD 227.6 million in FY2025, so the 2026 drag is partly timing. If fourth-quarter results confirm the guidance and working capital reverses as metal prices stabilise, net leverage on a full-year earnings base would fall without any debt repayment.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-21_aearningsreleaseex99120269.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation C04; calculation C07; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The view would weaken if Electronics organic growth slows from 18% toward zero, if operating cash flow stays below dividends plus buybacks, if revolver borrowings rise above the springing threshold, or if a further large transaction is announced before the 2026 acquisitions are absorbed. It would strengthen if the notes due 2028 are refinanced early at a modest coupon increase, or if executed loan documents show tight restricted-payment capacity and a covenant ratio with wide headroom.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-21_aearningsreleaseex99120269.htm.jsonl, page 8; calculation C05; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Company description
Element Solutions formulates specialty chemistries and materials that customers consume in manufacturing, in two segments: Electronics, which supplies assembly materials, circuit-board and semiconductor chemistries and, since 2026-02-02, advanced inks and pastes, and Specialties, which supplies industrial surface finishing and offshore energy fluids and, since 2026-01-02, high-purity specialty gases. Because products are consumed in production and replaced as customers produce, revenue recurs without contracted backlog, and the electronics share of net sales has risen to 77.5% with the acquisitions; this supports cash-flow visibility in normal demand but ties debt service to electronics hardware output and to the integration of two acquired businesses.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_esi-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 6–8, 16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_esi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26

### Ownership and group structure
The company is a listed Delaware corporation with no controlling sponsor identified, so extraction by an owner is not a documented risk; the group borrows through the parent and its co-borrower MacDermid, Incorporated, guaranteed by certain domestic subsidiaries on a first-lien secured basis, while the 2028 notes carry senior unsecured guarantees from generally the same subsidiaries. The related-party advisory fee of USD 2.0 million a year is small, and the sponsor and governance risk level from Business & governance is medium because capital returns and acquisitions continued as leverage rose and the proxy statement and executed loan documents were not supplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_esi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 18, 26

### Revenue drivers and pricing power
Volume growth is led by AI infrastructure and other high-performance electronics, with Electronics organic growth of 18% against 2% for Specialties in nine months 2026, while reported growth of 50% is lifted by acquisitions and by pass-through metals pricing of USD 891.6 million of net sales. Pricing power is shown by the issuer's margin excluding metals rising rather than falling, switching costs from customer qualification, and no customer at 10% of net sales; the exposure is that metal price moves change reported sales and working capital without changing underlying demand, and that minimal backlog gives no contractual cushion.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-21_aearningsreleaseex99120269.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9; 10-K_2025-12-31_esi-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 16; calculation C03

### Cost structure and margin resilience
Selling, technical, general and administrative expense fell to 21.24% of net sales in nine months 2026 from 25.13%, partly because metals inflate the denominator, and capital expenditure of USD 62.2 million in FY2025 shows a modest fixed-asset base because the company blends purchased raw materials rather than manufacturing basic chemicals. Acquisition, integration and transaction costs of USD 52.6 million were added back to Adjusted EBITDA in nine months 2026, so margin defence depends on integration costs ending; specialties margin fell in the third quarter on higher corporate cost allocation, which is the segment to watch.
Basis: calculation C02; 10-Q_2026-09-30_esi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 44; 10-K_2025-12-31_esi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 14

### Capital intensity and cash conversion
Capital expenditure was 2.44% of FY2025 net sales and is expected at about USD 110 million for 2026 for Kuprion capacity and plant consolidation, so free cash flow of USD 227.6 million in FY2025 was 41.56% of Adjusted EBITDA and covered the dividend of USD 77.8 million 2.93 times. In 2026 working capital, higher cash interest and integration costs reduced conversion, and capital expenditure can be deferred as the main flexible lever, with dividends and buybacks the next.
Basis: calculation C04; 10-K_2025-12-31_esi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 84; 10-Q_2026-09-30_esi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 44

### Porter's Five Forces
Rivalry is moderate: named competitors include larger groups, but competition is on qualification, technology and service, which favours incumbents with embedded products. New entrants face qualification and technical-service barriers, which protects margin; substitutes arise through technology change such as chromium-free plating, which the company addresses with research and development. Buyer power is limited by diversification, as no customer reaches 10% of net sales, but large electronics manufacturers can resist price outside qualified products; supplier power is low for most raw materials, which are obtainable from multiple sources, with metals priced through to customers.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_esi-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 16, 19–20

### PEST analysis
Regulatory and trade factors are material: tariffs and trade policy can change cost and demand, and about 79% of FY2025 net sales came from outside the United States, so currency translation and geopolitical disruption affect reported results; the economic driver that matters most is metal prices, which move sales and working capital. Interest-rate exposure is largely hedged until the swaps expire in December 2028 and December 2029, and the technological driver is AI-related electronics demand, which sets the growth rate of the largest segment.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_esi-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 26, 39; 10-Q_2026-09-30_esi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21

### SWOT
Strengths are consumable recurring demand, no customer at 10% of net sales, interest cover of 6.59x and USD 702.9 million of liquidity; weaknesses are electronics concentration, higher leverage after the acquisitions, working-capital absorption and covenant opacity. Opportunities that would improve credit quality are acquired earnings arriving ahead of guidance and early refinancing of the 2028 notes; threats are an electronics demand slowdown, a metal price reversal that strains working capital, and a further large transaction before integration is complete.
Basis: calculation C05; EX-99.1_2026-10-21_aearningsreleaseex99120269.htm.jsonl, page 4; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds
Over the next twelve to 24 months management guides 2026 Adjusted EBITDA to about USD 720 million, raised from USD 690 million to USD 710 million in July, including a full year of both acquisitions; tailwinds are AI-driven electronics demand and acquired earnings, and headwinds are metal price volatility, tariffs, currency and integration costs. Over three to five years the debt maturities of 2028, 2030 and 2031 fall inside the horizon, so deleveraging and refinancing access matter more than growth; the guidance is a management figure, not a forecast, and no lender projection was supplied.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-21_aearningsreleaseex99120269.htm.jsonl, page 4; EX-99.1_2026-07-27_aearningsreleaseex99120266.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_esi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–18

### Downside scenario
A slowdown in AI-linked electronics demand would reach revenue within quarters because backlog is minimal, and Electronics produces 76.2% of segment Adjusted EBITDA; operating leverage on a cost base that includes research, technical service and integration cost would compress margin, while a fall in metal prices would release working capital and cushion cash flow. Free cash flow would fall toward the distributions it funds, and a USD 100.0 million fall in last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA would lift net leverage on that measure to 3.24x, while headroom to the springing first lien test of 5.0 to 1.0 cannot be measured because its definition is not printed. Management levers are deferring capital expenditure of about USD 110 million, suspending buybacks and using the revolver, so resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress through 2028, but the notes then fall due against liquidity of USD 702.9 million.
Basis: calculation C05; calculation C07; 10-Q_2026-09-30_esi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 44; 10-K_2025-12-31_esi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 16

### Overall credit view
Overall, Element Solutions presents an adequate business profile supported by consumable recurring demand, customer diversification and strong interest cover, but credit quality is constrained by higher leverage after debt-funded acquisitions and concentration in Electronics. Cash-flow durability appears adequate due to modest capital intensity and a fixed-rate cost of debt, with 2026 working-capital drag expected to be partly timing. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on delivery of acquired earnings, early action on the 2028 notes and the rate environment when swaps expire. The primary downside path is an electronics demand slowdown while leverage is elevated and capital returns continue. Further analysis would require covenant definitions, ratings and market pricing, and acquired earnings. Fundamental credit assessment completed. Top risk: leverage after debt-funded acquisitions.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C05; calculation C08
