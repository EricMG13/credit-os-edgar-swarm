## Findings

### Bottom line

SAIC's reported leverage is 3.29x gross and 3.12x net on last-twelve-months EBITDA at July 31, 2026, and fiscal 2026 cash interest cover was 6.69x; these are accounting ratios, not the covenant test, whose headroom cannot be established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 14; 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 93, 131; calculation C10

Gross leverage rose from 2.36x in fiscal 2024 mainly because that year's reported EBITDA included a USD 240 million divestiture gain; on issuer-adjusted EBITDA it was 3.14x then and is 3.18x now. Net debt rose by USD 54 million in the first half of fiscal 2027 because shareholder returns of USD 319 million exceeded free cash flow of USD 249 million.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 60, 93; 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 31; calculation C09; calculation C20; calculation C21

### Key findings

Leverage has moved less than the reported series suggests. Gross debt over reported EBITDA was 2.36x at fiscal 2024, 3.14x at fiscal 2025, 3.68x at fiscal 2026 and 3.29x on the last twelve months to July 31, 2026, while gross debt over issuer-adjusted EBITDA was 3.14x, 3.13x, 3.51x and 3.18x. The fiscal 2024 reported figure is flattered by the divestiture gain, and the fiscal 2026 peak reflects both lower EBITDA, down from USD 708 million to USD 676 million, and higher debt after a year in which the company borrowed USD 271 million net, paid USD 203 million for the SilverEdge acquisition and returned USD 515 million to shareholders.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 60, 93, 131; 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 14; calculation C10; calculation C19; calculation C20

Cash generation is strong but leans on items that can reverse. Operating cash flow was USD 609 million in fiscal 2026 and free cash flow USD 577 million, or 85.36% of reported EBITDA, yet cash taxes paid were USD 22 million against USD 66 million a year earlier, and collections of receivables added USD 161 million. Sales of receivables sit inside operating cash flow, so a smaller facility balance lowers it, as when the facility cut operating cash flow by USD 41 million in fiscal 2025.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 93, 129, 136; calculation C09; calculation C27

Shareholder returns, not operations, are what lift net debt. In fiscal 2026 buybacks and dividends of USD 515 million and the USD 203 million acquisition exceeded free cash flow, and net debt rose by USD 141 million; in the first half of fiscal 2027 returns of USD 319 million exceeded free cash flow of USD 249 million, taking net debt to USD 2,359 million.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, page 93; 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 4; calculation C02; calculation C20; calculation C21

Near-term liquidity and maturities are not pressing. Unrestricted cash is USD 126 million and the USD 1,000 million revolver due September 2030 is undrawn, so committed resources are USD 1,126 million before USD 8 million of letters of credit whose facility the filings do not name; scheduled maturities are USD 48 million through fiscal 2028 and USD 489 million in fiscal 2029, mostly the notes due April 2028.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 14, 15, 20; calculation C18; calculation C25

Definitions flatter leverage and cover. EBITDA adds back interest and the loss on sold receivables of USD 140 million in fiscal 2026 against interest expense of USD 128 million, so USD 12 million of receivables financing cost is left out of EBITDA, and USD 120 million of sold receivables at July 31, 2026 stay outside debt. Cash interest of USD 101 million is below the USD 128 million expense, which lifts cash cover.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 60, 91, 93, 136; 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, page 15; calculation C28; calculation C30

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against concern is that last-twelve-months gross leverage of 3.29x is below the 3.68x of fiscal 2026, that the USD 1,000 million revolver is undrawn and that maturities are small until fiscal 2029. It would win if first-half reported EBITDA growth of 24.02% persists and free cash flow again covers shareholder returns.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 15, 31; calculation C10; calculation C26

The evidence for concern is that net debt has risen in every period since fiscal 2024, that revenue fell 2.90% in fiscal 2026 and that 98% of revenue comes from one customer, the U.S. government. A fall in federal funding, or an adverse result in the Department of Justice investigation, which the company says it cannot estimate, would change the view.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 91, 140; 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, page 19; calculation C02; calculation C26

### Limitations

Cash interest is not printed for interim periods, so cash interest cover cannot be updated beyond fiscal 2026 and last-twelve-months cover is not calculable.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 36

The covenant ratio is not reported and the debt documents are not supplied, so none of these ratios is a covenant measure and headroom is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 82, 131

The senior notes due April 2028 carry no stated security or rank, so they are left out of the debt stack and its cumulative figures are partial.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, page 134
