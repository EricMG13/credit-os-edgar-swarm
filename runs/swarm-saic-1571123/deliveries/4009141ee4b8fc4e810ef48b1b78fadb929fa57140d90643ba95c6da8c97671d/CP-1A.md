## Findings

### Bottom line

SAIC is a listed, mostly cost-reimbursed U.S. government contractor with no sponsor identified, so creditors depend on contract cash flows and board restraint rather than outside support, and shareholder returns took 89.25% of fiscal 2026 free cash flow.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 46, 79, 82, 93; calculation A01; calculation A02; calculation A03

Revenue is almost wholly federal, 98% in fiscal 2026 and 52.02% from the Department of War, and USD 22,136 million of backlog at July 31, 2026 gives visibility, but only USD 3,818 million of it is funded. A criminal antitrust investigation is open with no estimate of penalties, the chief executive changed in October 2025, and a funding lapse or contract review would hit volume directly.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 77, 140; 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 34; calculation A05

### Key findings

No sponsor stands behind the debt, and none is drawing value out of it. The filings name no parent, sponsor or controlling holder; the shares are listed on Nasdaq and held by about seventeen thousand holders of record, so there is no sponsor support to count on and no sponsor-led recapitalisation to fear. Holdings of large stockholders sit in a proxy statement that is not supplied, so concentration among them is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 46, 79, 82; Source readiness (CP-0)

Customer concentration is very high but cushioned by contract type. Federal customers supply 98% of revenue and cost-reimbursement contracts supply 61.76% against 15.75% for firm-fixed-price work, so most cost increases are passed through to the customer while volume depends on appropriations: the Department of Homeland Security was shut down from February to April 2026, and open audits by the government's cost auditor can still disallow billed costs. The mechanism that matters for creditors is lost or delayed volume, not margin.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 142; 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 23; calculation A06

Backlog gives visibility, but little of it is funded and it is not growing. Total backlog was USD 22,136 million at July 31, 2026 against USD 22,622 million at January 30, 2026, only USD 3,818 million of it, or 17.25%, is funded, and net bookings of about USD 3.3 billion in the half were 0.87 times revenue. Remaining performance obligations are USD 6.4 billion, of which 75% is expected within twelve months, so most of the backlog still needs later funding or the exercise of options before it becomes revenue.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 34; calculation A09; calculation A10; calculation A13

Shareholder returns have priority over deleveraging. Buybacks and dividends of USD 515 million took 89.25% of fiscal 2026 free cash flow of USD 577 million and, with the USD 203 million SilverEdge acquisition, exceeded it by USD 141 million; in the first half of fiscal 2027 returns of USD 319 million were 128.11% of free cash flow of USD 249 million. The dividend of USD 0.37 per share each quarter is small beside the buyback, which the board authorised up to USD 1,200 million in December 2024, so returns can be cut quickly, but the filings do not commit the board to do so and say only that dividends and buybacks may be limited at certain leverage ratios.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 46, 47, 64, 93; 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation A01; calculation A02; calculation A03; calculation A04

Leadership and structure changed together, which raises execution risk in a period when revenue fell 2.90% in fiscal 2026 and grew 3.84% in the first half of fiscal 2027. The chief executive and other executives left in the third quarter of fiscal 2026, an interim chief executive served from October 2025 until February 2026, executive transition costs of USD 16 million net were added back in adjusted EBITDA, and five business groups were consolidated into three from 2026-01-31 with both reportable segments unchanged.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 58, 60, 77; 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation A14

An open criminal antitrust investigation is the main unquantified claim. The company received federal grand jury subpoenas in April 2022 and October 2023, is producing documents and says it cannot estimate fines or penalties; separately it warns that adverse findings in government investigations and audits can lead to suspension or debarment, which for a business with 98% federal revenue would threaten most of its revenue if it occurred.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 140, 142; 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, page 19

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a more benign view is that several customer relationships have lasted 20 years or more, that 61.76% of revenue is cost-reimbursed, that backlog is 2.99 times last-twelve-months revenue, and that returns to shareholders are discretionary, so the board could retain cash quickly. The view would move toward benign if returns fell back below free cash flow for several quarters and the investigation closed without a penalty.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 115; 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, page 34; calculation A06; calculation A12

It would move toward cautious if revenue fell again after the 2.90% decline of fiscal 2026, if returns again exceeded free cash flow while net debt rose, or if a penalty, a debarment proceeding or a funding lapse longer than the 2026 shutdown appeared. The first-half revenue growth of 3.84% is partly acquired, since SilverEdge added USD 39 million, and the company puts growth excluding acquisitions at about 2.9%, so it does not by itself show recovery.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 27; 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, page 115; calculation A14

### Limitations

  Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 79, 82; Source readiness (CP-0)

  Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, page 64; Source readiness (CP-0)

  Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, page 19

  Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 16

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The filings state no sponsor, buyout, recapitalisation or pending acquisition financing. The transactions that matter to creditors are the September 2025 refinancing and the SilverEdge acquisition that followed it. On 2025-09-25 the company issued USD 500 million of unsecured senior notes due November 2033 and used the proceeds to repay the revolver due June 2027; on 2025-09-30 it replaced its 2027 term loan A and revolver with a USD 1,100 million term loan A and a USD 1,000 million revolver, both due September 2030; and on 2025-10-15 it acquired SilverEdge for a preliminary USD 203 million net of USD 6 million cash acquired, funded from increased borrowings and cash on hand.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 116, 132, 134

After the quarter end, on 2026-08-14 the receivables purchase facility limit was raised from USD 300 million to USD 400 million, and on 2026-08-27 a quarterly dividend of USD 0.37 per share was declared, payable 2026-10-23. No later debt issue is stated, and the executed debt documents are not supplied.
