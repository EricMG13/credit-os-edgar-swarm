SAIC is an adequate credit: cost-reimbursed federal contracts and light capital needs give free cash flow, but shareholder returns that absorb most of it, a mostly unfunded backlog and one dominant customer cap it, leaving gross leverage at 3.29x and net debt rising.

### Company description

SAIC sells technical, engineering and IT services almost entirely to the U.S. government: 98% of fiscal 2026 revenue and 97% in the first half of fiscal 2027, with the Department of War alone at 52.02%. Customer relationships include several of 20 years or more, and cost-reimbursement contracts carry 61.76% of revenue, so cash flow is visible and cost-protected, but it depends on annual appropriations and on contracts that customers may end for convenience, recovering only the costs incurred and profit on work done.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 9, 10; 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, page 23; calculation F05; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The credit link is direct: stable, cost-protected cash flow supports debt service and refinancing confidence, while concentration in one buyer means a funding lapse reaches revenue at once, as the shutdown of the Department of Homeland Security from February to April 2026 showed. A government shutdown also cut fiscal 2026 revenue by about USD 26 million.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, page 55; 10-Q_2026-07-31_saic.htm.jsonl, page 23; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Ownership and group structure

SAIC is listed on Nasdaq with no sponsor, parent or controlling holder named, so creditors have no sponsor to inject equity and no sponsor to extract it. The credit facility is secured on substantially all assets and guaranteed by each wholly owned domestic subsidiary, and the notes due November 2033 are unsecured and guaranteed by the same subsidiaries. Financial policy is set by the board: dividends and buybacks may be limited at leverage ratios that are not printed, and governance risk is Medium because returns absorb most free cash flow and an antitrust investigation is open.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-30_saic.htm.jsonl, pages 46, 64, 132, 134; calculation F05; Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is driven by volume on existing contracts and new awards, with little price lever: contracts generally provide labour on a reimbursable basis and cost-type contracts pass costs through. Revenue fell 2.90% in fiscal 2026 on contract completions and ramp-downs, including the shutdown effect, and was about 3.3% lower excluding SilverEdge; it grew 3.84% in the first half of fiscal 2027, of which about 2.9% was organic and 5.3% in the second quarter alone. Revenue is concentrated, regulated and budget-cyclical rather than economically cyclical, and renewal of task orders each year refreshes bookings.

The threat of new entrants is moderate. Security clearances held by most of the workforce and past performance protect incumbents, but other-transaction agreements lower barriers to entry and small-business set-asides reduce the addressable market, so share loss is a gradual, not sudden, risk.

Buyer power is very high. One buyer provides 97% to 98% of revenue, it may terminate contracts for convenience and it funds work by annual appropriation, so any adverse change in budgets or contract priorities reaches volume directly; cost recovery on terminated work softens the loss.

Supplier power is moderate and sits in labour. Cleared talent is scarce and competitors recruit aggressively, and subcontractors can fail to perform, but cost-type contracts pass wage and subcontract costs through, so the exposure is mainly on fixed-price work.
