## Findings

### Bottom line

Leverage remains high and has improved less than reported EBITDA implies: net debt was 8.84x trailing reported EBITDA at 2026-09-30 against 12.59x at 2025-12-31, but 8.20x against 8.63x on the issuer's adjusted measure, with debt flat, so creditors still depend on earnings growth.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3, 13–16; 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 182–185; EX99.1_2026-09-30_dhc_tm2629915d1-ex99-1-htm.htm.jsonl, page 20; calculation K7; calculation K9

Total debt at carrying value was USD 2,404.05 million, net debt fell by only USD 2.02 million since 2025-12-31 and trailing free cash flow was negative at USD 64.65 million. Liquidity of USD 260.66 million covers the USD 2.55 million of scheduled principal before 2028 many times but only 0.41x of the USD 640.61 million due in 2028. Trailing cash interest cover was 1.34x, or 2.04x excluding accreted interest paid in 2025. Net leverage of 8.84x is 8.85x pro forma for the distribution declared on 2026-10-08 and 8.83x pro forma for the reimbursement received from the LSMD joint venture in October 2026. Covenant levels are not supplied, so headroom is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 5, 13–16, 48–50; 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, page 185; calculation K6; calculation K8; calculation K11; calculation K13; calculation K17; calculation K19

### Credit analysis by dimension

#### Leverage trajectory and drivers

Net debt to reported EBITDA fell from 18.39x at 2024-12-31 to 12.59x at 2025-12-31 and 8.84x at 2026-09-30, but the two steps have different causes. In 2025 total debt at carrying value fell by USD 510.08 million, as USD 589.23 million of property sale proceeds, USD 343.16 million of new mortgage financings and USD 369.38 million of new senior secured notes were applied to redeem USD 750.00 million of senior secured notes and USD 380.00 million of senior unsecured notes, so the first step was real deleveraging, funded mainly by property sales. Since 2025-12-31 total debt has risen by USD 3.23 million and net debt has fallen by only USD 2.02 million, so the second step came entirely from trailing reported EBITDA rising by USD 77.21 million to USD 259.49 million.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, page 177; 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 182, 184; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 2–5; calculation K1; calculation K2; calculation K6; calculation K19

That rise overstates the operating improvement, because reported EBITDA is struck after impairments and includes gains on sale. The 2025 figure carries an impairment of USD 165.70 million and a gain on sale of USD 117.73 million, while the trailing twelve months carry impairments of USD 17.54 million and a net gain on sale of USD 11.03 million. On the issuer's adjusted measure, which removes those items and deducts incentive management fees, net debt to trailing earnings moved only from 8.63x to 8.20x, because Adjusted EBITDAre rose by USD 13.53 million, from USD 266.08 million to USD 279.61 million. The creditor consequence is that leverage of about 8x to 9x is the fair description of the capital structure today, and that further deleveraging needs higher earnings or asset sale proceeds applied to debt, since free cash flow is negative.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, page 183; EX99.1_2026-09-30_dhc_tm2629915d1-ex99-1-htm.htm.jsonl, page 20; calculation K5; calculation K6; calculation K9; calculation K15; calculation K19

The driver of the earnings gain is the senior housing operating portfolio. Its quarterly net operating income rose from USD 29.62 million in Q3 2025 to USD 49.66 million in Q3 2026, a rise of 67.66%, with occupancy across all properties of 83.8% against 81.5% and an average monthly rate of USD 5,670 per occupied unit against USD 5,472. The segment is now 61.49% of net operating income, so debt service increasingly depends on operator performance in one segment, while medical office and life science net operating income fell from USD 26.68 million to USD 23.87 million over the same year.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21, 31, 46; EX99.1_2026-09-30_dhc_tm2629915d1-ex99-1-htm.htm.jsonl, page 19; calculation K19

#### Coverage trends

Cash interest cover on reported EBITDA was 1.14x for 2024, 0.67x for 2025 and 1.34x for the twelve months to 2026-09-30. The 2025 and trailing figures are depressed by accreted interest paid in cash when the zero-coupon senior secured notes due 2026 were redeemed: USD 152.87 million in 2025, of which USD 65.88 million fell in the fourth quarter and so sits inside the trailing window. Excluding it, cover was 1.53x for 2025 and 2.04x trailing, and the issuer's own 2.0x is reproduced as Adjusted EBITDAre over interest expense less non-cash amortization at 2.01x. Creditors should read run-rate cover as about 2x, with the lower trailing figure reflecting a one-off cash cost that leaves the window with the fourth quarter of 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 183, 185; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 5, 47; EX99.1_2026-09-30_dhc_tm2629915d1-ex99-1-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 22; calculation K8; calculation K10; calculation K11

The interest burden is largely fixed: 94.27% of principal is at fixed rates, in line with the 94.3% the issuer states, and the only floating exposure is the USD 140.00 million mortgage loan, whose 4.50% SOFR cap runs to March 2027, a year before the loan's March 2028 maturity. Rate moves therefore matter for refinancing in 2028 more than for current interest cost.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 13–16; calculation K13

#### Cash-flow quality

Operating cash flow was USD 54.01 million for the twelve months to 2026-09-30 against real estate improvements of USD 118.66 million, so free cash flow was negative at USD 64.65 million. The nine months to 2026-09-30 were less negative at USD 12.11 million against USD 113.90 million a year earlier, mainly because interest paid fell once accreted interest of USD 86.99 million stopped. The 2026 shortfall was more than covered by a USD 27.20 million dividend from AlerisLife and USD 21.69 million of sale proceeds, not by borrowing, and cash rose by USD 5.25 million to USD 110.66 million. The consequence is that no free cash flow is available to repay debt, so the maturity wall in 2028 must be refinanced or met from asset sales.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 184–185; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dhc.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5, 47; calculation K6; calculation K19

Liquidity was USD 260.66 million: cash of USD 110.66 million plus the undrawn USD 150.00 million revolver, with restricted cash of USD 20.29 million excluded. Scheduled principal before 2028 is only USD 2.55 million, so near-term refinancing risk is low, but USD 640.61 million falls due in 2028, made up of USD 500.00 million of senior unsecured notes due February 2028 and the USD 140.00 million floating rate mortgage loan with two one-year extension options. An incentive management fee for 2026, if earned, is payable to the manager in January 2027; USD 22.00 million had been recognised by 2026-09-30.

#### Strongest contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious reading is the pace of the senior housing recovery and the direction of the ratings. Quarterly SHOP net operating income rose in each quarter from Q3 2025 to Q2 2026 and eased by USD 3.83 million in Q3 2026; Moody's upgraded the issuer to B3 with a positive outlook in April 2026, and S&P raised its senior unsecured rating to B and put the B- issuer rating on a positive outlook in October 2026.
