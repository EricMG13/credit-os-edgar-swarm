## Findings

### Bottom line

AdaptHealth's credit profile is adequate but weakening: liquidity covers scheduled debt service and no bond matures before 2029, yet growth is consuming cash and margins are falling, so creditors should expect little deleveraging until the Diabetes Health proceeds arrive.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C4; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 40–43; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 75

Net debt was USD 1,803.17 million at September 30, 2026, or USD 1,568.17 million pro forma for the Diabetes Health sale expected in the first quarter of 2027 (closing day not established); trailing leverage and pro forma net leverage are not established, and the main downside path runs from lower capitated-contract margins through weaker free cash flow to a concentrated 2029 refinancing.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C7; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27

### Key findings

Liquidity and the maturity profile are the main support: cash of USD 80.69 million and USD 268.7 million of revolver availability give USD 349.39 million, and scheduled principal before 2029 is small, so a near-term liquidity squeeze needs a severe shock.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C6; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Cash generation is the weakest dimension: nine-month capital spending was almost equal to operating cash flow and free cash flow was close to zero, so net debt rose by USD 173.01 million in nine months even though revenue grew.
Basis: calculation C3; calculation C5; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8

Margin pressure follows the capitated growth: revenue rose 9.72% in nine months while Adjusted EBITDA fell 4.23%, and the third-quarter margin was 19.0% against 21.7%, a fall of 2.7 percentage points that management ties to labour cost and a manufacturer price increase.
Basis: calculation C3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 75; EX-99.1_2026-08-04_ahco-20260804x8k_ex991.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3

Leverage cannot be shown to have improved: FY2025 net debt was 3.45x reported and 2.64x adjusted EBITDA on a perimeter that included Diabetes Health, and net debt over the midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance is 3.48x, an indication rather than a trailing measure.
Basis: calculation C4; Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-11-03_ahco-20261103x8k_ex991.htm.jsonl, page 2

Refinancing risk is concentrated in 2029 to 2031, with nearly all principal due then, and a springing maturity could pull the bank debt forward to May 2029 if more than USD 150.0 million of the 2029 notes remain outstanding.
Basis: calculation C6; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 40–43

Governance adds no extraction risk but limited visibility: there is no sponsor and no distribution, yet the debt documents, board terms and capitated contract terms are not supplied, and open government investigations are unquantified.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 52–56

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a more favourable view is that adjusted net leverage of 2.64x at year-end 2025 was moderate, that liquidity of USD 349.39 million is larger than a year of scheduled amortisation, that the company sold businesses and repaid debt in 2025, and that USD 235.0 million of Diabetes Health proceeds are agreed. The view would improve if restated FY2025 continuing earnings and fourth-quarter results show trailing adjusted leverage falling as the capitated contracts mature and the sale closes with proceeds applied to debt; it would worsen if fourth-quarter Adjusted EBITDA falls below the USD 131.54 million implied by the low end of guidance, if free cash flow misses the guided range, or if more than USD 150.0 million of the 2029 notes remain as the springing date approaches.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C4; calculation C8; Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-11-03_ahco-20261103x8k_ex991.htm.jsonl, page 2

### Limitations

- Trailing earnings on one perimeter do not exist in the sources, so the leverage conclusion rests on year-end 2025 figures that include Diabetes Health and on guidance, and cannot say whether leverage is now higher or lower than a year ago.
- The legal documents behind the debt are not supplied, so the assessment of refinancing capacity and of structural rank rests on labels in the filings and cannot show how much unsecured debt sits behind the secured term loans.
- The capitated contracts are described only in general terms, so the durability of the fastest-growing revenue line and the size of any cost-of-care loss are not established, and the downside path is built from disclosed cost lines rather than contract economics.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Liquidity and a back-ended maturity profile support the credit. Cash of USD 80.69 million and revolver availability of USD 268.7 million total USD 349.39 million, and no bond falls due until 2029, so scheduled debt service in the next year is covered several times and creditors are not exposed to a near-term refinancing.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C6; Financial foundation (CP-1)

A recurring, payor-diversified revenue base supports cash flow. Fixed monthly equipment rentals were 37.7% of third-quarter revenue, insurers 64.2% and government programmes 23.8%, and no single referral source is material, so no single payor or referrer can remove a large part of revenue, although government reimbursement stays a rate risk.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 64; 10-K_2025-12-31_ahco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10

The absence of distributions and the 2025 debt paydown support creditor alignment. No dividends or buybacks were paid and USD 250.0 million of debt was repaid in FY2025, so equity holders have not drawn cash out while leverage was above its 2024 level, although the debt documents' capacity to do so is not supplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1)

The pending Diabetes Health sale gives a discrete deleveraging source. USD 235.0 million of cash proceeds, if applied to debt, would cut net debt to USD 1,568.17 million, but closing depends on conditions and the use of proceeds is not stated, so it supports the credit only once completed.
Basis: calculation C2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27

Scale and market position are meaningful. The company serves about 4.8 million patients from about 660 locations, so it can absorb local disruption and negotiate with payors and suppliers better than a small provider, although supplier concentration of two to three suppliers per category limits that advantage.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-11-03_ahco-20261103x8k_ex991.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-K_2025-12-31_ahco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11

Cash generation is the main weakness. Nine-month capital spending of USD 392.98 million nearly equalled operating cash flow of USD 395.61 million, so free cash flow was close to zero and net debt rose, which leaves creditors reliant on asset sales rather than internal cash for deleveraging.
Basis: calculation C3; calculation C5; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Margins are falling as capitated revenue grows. The Adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 19.0% from 21.7% and management cut its outlook, so profit is not yet scaling with revenue and a further cost overrun on an at-risk contract would reduce cash available for debt service.
Basis: calculation C3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 75; EX-99.1_2026-08-04_ahco-20260804x8k_ex991.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3

Debt is concentrated in 2029 to 2031 with a springing maturity. Almost all principal falls due in those three years and the bank debt can come due in May 2029 if the 2029 notes are not reduced, so refinancing risk is bunched and sensitive to market access in that window.
Basis: calculation C6; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 40–43

The equity cushion is thin against goodwill. Two goodwill impairments in the last two years reduced goodwill to USD 2,158.31 million, which with intangibles exceeds total equity of USD 1,233.23 million, so book equity gives little loss-absorbing capacity and impairment risk remains in the segments tested in 2026.
Basis: calculation C9; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 68

Legal and regulatory exposure is open ended. Two federal civil investigative demands and a pending billing class settlement remain, so a large adverse outcome would be a cash claim ahead of equity, and the company gives no estimate.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 52–56

### Company description

AdaptHealth delivers home medical equipment, supplies and related services to patients' homes, earning revenue from resupply sales, fixed monthly equipment rentals and, increasingly, at-risk capitated arrangements, which were 14.8% of third-quarter revenue against 4.7% a year earlier. Rental and resupply revenue gives reasonable visibility, but the capitated share ties revenue to fixed per-member fees while the company carries the cost of members' care, so revenue visibility is rising while cash-flow predictability is not.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 64

### Ownership and group structure

The company is publicly listed with no financial sponsor; holders of about 10% and 8% and a tax receivable agreement with a liability of USD 238.9 million are the only stated claims beside creditors, so leverage tolerance is set by management rather than a sponsor and the governance risk level is medium because legal capacity under the debt documents cannot be tested.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Growth in 2026 came from the capitated contracts and acquisitions: nine-month revenue rose 9.72% while net sales of supplies fell as a share of revenue. Pricing is set by Medicare, Medicaid and commercial payors rather than by the company, government programmes were about 26% of FY2025 revenue and competitive bidding favours payor relationships, so pricing power is limited and a reimbursement cut would pass straight to margin.
Basis: calculation C3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 64; 10-K_2025-12-31_ahco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-12-31_ahco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 146

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Cost of net revenue was 84.6% of third-quarter revenue against 79.4%, driven by labour, product cost and patient equipment depreciation, and two to three suppliers serve each product category, so input cost increases from a manufacturer cannot easily be avoided; restructuring savings stated at about USD 19 million a year are a partial offset, so margins can be defended only if capitated contract profitability keeps improving.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 66–68; 10-K_2025-12-31_ahco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11; EX-99.1_2026-08-04_ahco-20260804x8k_ex991.htm.jsonl, pages 1–3; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Capital intensity and FCF conversion

Capital intensity is high because rental equipment is bought and depreciated: patient equipment depreciation was 14.4% of third-quarter revenue and nine-month purchases of equipment were 99.33% of operating cash flow, so EBITDA converts poorly into free cash flow when growth accelerates; the split between maintenance and growth capital is not disclosed, so how much spending can be cut is not established.
Basis: calculation C5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 68; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Porter's Five Forces

Rivalry is high in a fragmented market with large national providers and many regional and local providers, buyer power sits with Medicare and large payors that set rates and choose exclusive suppliers, and supplier power matters because two to three suppliers serve each category and one raised prices in 2026; the threats of new entrants and substitutes are not assessed in the materials read, so the forces shown to matter to margins are payor and supplier power.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ahco-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 11–13; EX-99.1_2026-08-04_ahco-20260804x8k_ex991.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3

### PEST analysis

Regulatory factors are material: reimbursement rates, competitive bidding and False Claims Act investigations can change revenue and cash outflows, whereas macroeconomic, social and technological factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver in the materials read.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ahco-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 12–14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 56

### SWOT analysis

Strengths are liquidity, scale, recurring rental revenue and absence of distributions; weaknesses are near-zero free cash flow, falling margins, a thin equity cushion against goodwill and 2029 to 2031 debt concentration; opportunities that could improve credit quality are closing the Diabetes Health sale and applying proceeds to debt and capitated margin recovery; threats are reimbursement cuts, at-risk cost overruns, an unresolved investigation and loss of market access before the 2029 notes mature.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 40–43

### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale and market position are strong because of national reach; competitive advantage is average because payors and suppliers set the terms; business diversification is average with three segments but one sleep segment above half of revenue; cost and capex flexibility is not assessable because maintenance and growth capital are not split; margin stability is weak after a fall of 2.7 percentage points; free cash flow stability is weak; the ability to refinance is average because the 2029 notes and a springing maturity concentrate risk and a new USD 1.1 billion credit facility was put in place in April 2026; liquidity is average to strong at USD 349.39 million; and financial policy and governance is average with no distributions but missing legal documents.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C3; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 40–43

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over 12 to 24 months the company guides to FY2026 net revenue of USD 2.88 billion to USD 2.90 billion, Adjusted EBITDA of USD 510 million to USD 525 million and free cash flow of USD 50 million to USD 75 million, which imply fourth-quarter Adjusted EBITDA of USD 131.54 million to USD 146.54 million and free cash flow of USD 47.37 million to USD 72.37 million; tailwinds are improving capitated contract profit, savings and the sale proceeds, headwinds are labour and supplier costs and reimbursement. Over three to five years the 2029 notes and springing maturity dominate the refinancing path.
Basis: calculation C7; calculation C8; EX-99.1_2026-11-03_ahco-20261103x8k_ex991.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 40–43

### Qualitative downside scenario

A plausible downside starts with at-risk capitated cost running above the fixed per-member fees, which lifts labour and equipment cost faster than revenue and pushes Adjusted EBITDA below the guided range; free cash flow then turns negative, the revolver is drawn above the USD 147.0 million outstanding and availability falls below USD 268.7 million, net debt rises further, and the 2029 notes cannot be reduced before the springing date. Management levers are cutting growth capital, slowing acquisitions, applying the Diabetes Health proceeds to debt and extracting the stated restructuring savings; the observable triggers are fourth-quarter Adjusted EBITDA below USD 131.54 million, free cash flow below the guided range and revolver drawings rising.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 40–43; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Overall credit view

Overall, AdaptHealth presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, recurring rental revenue and covered liquidity, but credit quality is constrained by near-zero free cash flow, falling margins and a thin equity cushion. Cash-flow durability appears weak to adequate because growth capital and at-risk contracts absorb cash. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on reducing the 2029 notes ahead of the springing maturity, on the Diabetes Health proceeds and on capitated margin recovery. The primary downside path is at-risk cost overruns reducing free cash flow into a concentrated 2029 refinancing. Further analysis would require restated FY2025 continuing earnings, the debt documents and the capitated contract terms. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: cash absorption by growth ahead of the 2029 refinancing.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C4; calculation C6
