## Findings

### Bottom line

AdaptHealth is a scaled home-equipment provider with no financial sponsor and no documented creditor-adverse behaviour, but growing at-risk capitated revenue and heavy equipment spending are straining cash generation, and governance risk is only partly assessable because key terms are not supplied.
Basis: calculation A1; calculation A5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 64; 10-K_2025-12-31_ahco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 70; 10-K_2025-12-31_ahco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 201

Control sits with two minority holders of roughly 10% and 8% rather than a sponsor, a tax receivable agreement still carries USD 238.9 million of liability, a securities class action has settled with insurers funding most of it, and open government investigations are unquantified; the USD 235.0 million sale of Diabetes Health is the main pending change.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ahco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 70; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 50–53; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 56

### Key findings

Ownership gives creditors minority-holder influence rather than sponsor leverage appetite: two investors held about 10% and 8% at the end of 2025, one also holds convertible preferred stock, and no controlling holder is named, but board seats, consent rights and preferred-stock terms are not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ahco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 70; 10-K_2025-12-31_ahco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 201

Documented behaviour on distributions has been creditor-neutral to supportive, with no dividends, no buybacks, USD 250.0 million of debt repaid in FY2025 and open-market repurchases of the 2028 notes, yet 2026 reversed the direction as capital spending and acquisitions of USD 127.3 million absorbed cash and the refinancing moved unsecured debt into secured, amortising term loans.
Basis: calculation A6; 10-K_2025-12-31_ahco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 71; 10-K_2025-12-31_ahco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 129; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 40–43; Financial foundation (CP-1)

At-risk capitated contracts rose to 14.8% of third-quarter revenue from 4.7% a year earlier, driven by a large contract entered into in the third quarter of 2025 and later agreements, which lifts revenue but gives the company the cost of members' care and explains the rise in labour cost and patient equipment depreciation.
Basis: calculation A1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 64; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 66–68; EX-99.1_2026-05-05_ahco-20260505x8k_ex991.htm.jsonl, page 1; EX-99.1_2026-08-04_ahco-20260804x8k_ex991.htm.jsonl, page 1

The operating model is trading margin for scale: Adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 19.0% from 21.7% in the third quarter, free cash flow was close to zero for nine months, and management cut its 2026 outlook in August after a price increase from a manufacturer and the labour cost of the capitated transition.
Basis: calculation A5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 75; EX-99.1_2026-08-04_ahco-20260804x8k_ex991.htm.jsonl, pages 1–3

The legal and regulatory overhang is material but mostly funded or accrued: insurers paid USD 34.0 million of the securities class action settlement, a North Carolina billing class action is set to settle for USD 14.5 million, and two federal civil investigative demands remain open with no estimate of exposure.
Basis: calculation A4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 52–56

Portfolio change adds execution risk: the sale of Diabetes Health to RGH Enterprises, LLC is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027, the eCommerce business moved into a minority-held joint venture in July 2026, and the finance chief changed in 2026 while a cyber incident in June 2026 remains unquantified.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 45; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 60–62; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 96

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is that the company paid no dividends, bought back no shares, repaid USD 250.0 million of debt in FY2025, had its securities settlement funded mainly by insurers, and reported effective internal controls in each 2026 quarter after remediating a 2024 weakness. The view would soften if the debt documents show tight limits on distributions, investments and asset transfers, if the proxy statement shows no related-party fees, and if capitated contract margins keep recovering as management says; it would harden if the open investigations produce a material claim or the sale of Diabetes Health fails.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ahco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 68; 10-K_2025-12-31_ahco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 71; 10-K_2025-12-31_ahco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 129; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 52–56; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ahco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 93

### Limitations

- Board composition, committee structure, executive pay and director independence sit in a proxy statement that is not supplied, so the governance conclusion rests on what the annual and quarterly reports say and cannot weigh conflicts between holders and creditors in detail.
- The terms of the Series B-1 preferred stock and of the capitated contracts, including counterparty, term, pricing and termination rights, are not supplied, so neither the preferred holder's rights nor the durability of capitated revenue is established.
- No source shows what the debt documents permit for distributions, investments, asset transfers or priming, so no capacity assessment is made and the risk level rests on documented behaviour alone.
