## Findings

### Bottom line

Creditors rely on a large, long-dated government contract book whose main risks are concentration and collection timing rather than ownership, since no sponsor appears in the supplied documents.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 50

Backlog of USD 15,327 million at 2025-09-30 had a weighted remaining life of 5.0 years, yet the ten largest contracts produced about 60% of fiscal 2025 revenue and almost all contracts can be cancelled for convenience, so one contract change moves earnings: a temporary modification on a major federal program cuts diluted earnings per share by about USD 0.35 per quarter. Management has kept returning cash to shareholders while borrowing, which makes capital allocation the governance point to monitor.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 49; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 50; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 31

### Key findings

Revenue is government-funded, concentrated and mostly performance-based, so transaction volumes, not price, decide earnings. The ten largest contracts gave about 60% of fiscal 2025 revenue, one federal agency about one-fifth of it, and performance-based contracts 54.4% of it; the effect is already visible in fiscal 2026, when U.S. Federal Services revenue fell 2.5% over nine months on lower volumes on large transaction-based contracts and less disaster-recovery work, while its operating margin rose to 17.5%.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 80; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 26

Contract life gives visibility but little protection. Backlog was USD 15,327 million with a weighted remaining life of 5.0 years, but almost all government contracts allow the customer to terminate or modify them at any time and pay only for work already done, so backlog is an estimate of potential revenue, not committed cash.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 49; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 50

A single customer event reached earnings and cash in the same quarter. A major federal customer imposed a temporary modification from 2026-07-01 to 2026-12-31 that management expects to cut diluted earnings per share by about USD 0.35 per quarter, and payments on one large federal contract were delayed so that days sales outstanding reached 98 days; management reports USD 245 million collected after the quarter end and expects the days figure to finish the year below 70 days. For creditors the question is how quickly the receivable converts, because buybacks and dividends are paid from the same cash.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 31; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_mms-2026x06x30x8kxex991.htm.jsonl, page 4

Demand drivers are supportive but depend on policy timing. Federal legislation signed in 2025 requires more frequent Medicaid redeterminations and work requirements for the expansion population from 2026-12-31, which states can ask to delay by up to two years, and management reports year-to-date signed awards of USD 1.25 billion against a sales pipeline of USD 50.4 billion; the U.S. Services segment, where revenue fell 6.4% over nine months, has to return to growth for that demand to reach earnings.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 28; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_mms-2026x06x30x8kxex991.htm.jsonl, page 3

Capital allocation is the governance issue creditors should watch. Share repurchases were USD 447.5 million in fiscal 2025 and USD 204.9 million in the nine months to 2026-06-30, the Board refreshed a USD 400 million authorisation in May 2026, the quarterly dividend was raised to USD 0.33 per share from USD 0.30, and the Term Loan B was increased by USD 325 million on 2026-05-27 while operating cash flow was negative; this is a mixed signal, not extraction, because the buybacks were funded in part by borrowing but leverage stayed within the covenant limits the company reports.
Basis: calculation B4; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 51; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 31; EX-99.1_2026-05-07_mms-2026x03x31x8kxex991.htm.jsonl, page 2; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_mms-2026x06x30x8kxex991.htm.jsonl, page 4

Ownership and governance evidence is thin. The annual report incorporates director, compensation, ownership and related-party information by reference to a proxy statement that was not supplied, so shareholder concentration, board independence and fees are not established; what is visible is a separate Chair, eight directors including the chief executive, effective internal control over financial reporting, an unqualified audit opinion, and a bar on shareholder action by written consent. None of a sponsor, a controlling holder or a related-party payment is disclosed in the supplied documents.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 100–101; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 102–103; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 105

Legal and operating-risk claims are accrued but unquantified. The class actions that followed the 2023 file-transfer cybersecurity incident continue, about half of the claims against the company remain, and the amount accrued is not stated; performance bond commitments were USD 33.2 million at 2025-09-30.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 21–22; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 98

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against concern is that Maximus has no sponsor, a record backlog, margins that rose in fiscal 2026 (adjusted EBITDA margin of 14.0% over nine months against 13.2%), a cash-generative fiscal 2025 and an explanation for the weak cash flow that management says is administrative and already reversing. The same facts also support the opposite reading: scale and margin gains sit on a few cancellable contracts, and the cash shortfall coincided with continued buybacks.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 49; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 50; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 36

The view improves if collections normalise, with days sales outstanding below 70 days by the year end and free cash flow inside the guidance range of USD 425 million to USD 475 million, and if repurchases slow until they do. It worsens if a top-ten contract is lost or cut, if the customer modification is extended beyond 2026-12-31, if federal payment delays recur, or if buybacks continue to be funded by borrowing while operating cash flow is negative.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 27; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_mms-2026x06x30x8kxex991.htm.jsonl, page 4

No sponsor transaction, leveraged buyout or acquisition financing is described in the supplied documents; the transactions that matter are amendments to the bank credit agreement, divestitures and capital returns. The Term Loan B was increased by USD 325 million on 2026-05-27, following a first amendment dated 2025-03-20 and a USD 250 million expansion of the credit facility in fiscal 2025 that, with operating cash flow, funded USD 447.5 million of share repurchases; the filings do not state the use of the 2026 proceeds beyond saying the term loans were expanded to provide capital for operations and share repurchases.
