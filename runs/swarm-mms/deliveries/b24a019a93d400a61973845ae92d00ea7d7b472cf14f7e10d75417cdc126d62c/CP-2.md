## Findings

### Bottom line

Maximus is an adequate credit: scale, a long contract life and strong interest cover support it, while rising leverage and weak cash conversion from one slow-paying federal customer constrain it, so collections and buybacks, not solvency, are the creditor issues.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation Q1; calculation Q3

Net debt was 2.21x last-twelve-month EBITDA at 2026-06-30 against 1.68x at 2025-09-30, or 1.87x pro forma for the USD 245 million collected after the quarter end (date not stated) and 2.24x pro forma for the dividend payable on 2026-08-31, while cash interest cover was 8.28x. The balance sheet can absorb a delayed receipt, but it depends on that receipt arriving: unrestricted cash was thin and the revolver was the main buffer.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation Q1; calculation Q2; calculation Q3; calculation N5

### Key findings

Debt has grown faster than earnings, and that is the main reason the credit has weakened. Carrying-value debt rose 22.68% from 2025-09-30 to 2026-06-30 while last-twelve-month reported EBITDA rose 7.86%, as the Term Loan B was increased by USD 325 million and buybacks continued; the company's own covenant ratio was 2.00x, on a different definition.
Basis: calculation N1; calculation Q1; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 32–33; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Cash conversion, not profitability, is the weak point. Nine-month operating cash flow was negative because a federal customer delayed payments, days sales outstanding reached 98 days against 62 days at 2025-09-30, and unrestricted cash fell 74.39%; free cash flow guidance of USD 425 million to USD 475 million for the year depends on a large fourth-quarter recovery, of which the USD 245 million already collected is roughly 35.84% to 38.67%.
Basis: calculation N2; calculation N7; calculation N8; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 31; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_mms-2026x06x30x8kxex991.htm.jsonl, page 4; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Scale and contract life are the strongest support. Backlog was USD 15,327 million with a weighted remaining life of 5.0 years, credit losses on government receivables have not historically been significant, and margins rose in the nine months; but the ten largest contracts gave about 60% of fiscal 2025 revenue and almost all contracts can be cancelled for convenience, so durability is a function of a few customer decisions.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 49; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 50; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 71; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Liquidity is adequate only because of committed lines. Cash plus the stated revolver access covered the debt principal due within about fifteen months 8.30x, but a full draw of the revolver would lift carrying-value debt to 3.31x last-twelve-month EBITDA, and the receivables purchase facility is a funding dependency outside reported debt.
Basis: calculation N5; calculation N6; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 32; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Financial policy is mixed: buybacks of USD 447.5 million in fiscal 2025 exceeded free cash flow and USD 204.9 million more was repurchased in the nine months to 2026-06-30 while debt rose, which would turn adverse for creditors if collections do not recover; there is no sponsor and no related-party leakage is disclosed, but the governance evidence is incomplete.

The primary downside path is a prolonged federal payment delay or the loss or curtailment of a large contract while buybacks and dividends continue, which would push the business onto the revolver; the large maturities do not begin until 2029, so refinancing risk is distant but the facility terms and any lender response are not supplied.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a more positive view is that cash interest cover of 8.28x is high, the debt matures in 2029 and 2031, federal segment margins rose, receivables from government customers have not historically carried significant credit risk, and management says the delayed receivable is already converting. If the fourth-quarter recovery arrives and the USD 245 million collected is followed by the rest of the delayed balance, the nine-month cash shortfall will prove timing, and leverage will return towards the fiscal 2025 level of 1.68x.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 71; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_mms-2026x06x30x8kxex991.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 31; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation Q2

The view weakens if days sales outstanding stay above 70 days at the year end, if the temporary customer modification is extended beyond 2026-12-31, if a top-ten contract is lost, or if repurchases continue to be funded by borrowing while operating cash flow is negative. It strengthens if collections normalise and the Board slows repurchases until they do.

### Company and ownership

Maximus runs government health, eligibility, clinical-assessment and technology programs, earning 56% of fiscal 2025 revenue from U.S. Federal Services, 32% from U.S. Services and 11% from Outside the U.S., with 55.2% of revenue from federal agencies and 21.2% from state agencies other than New York. Because the customers are governments that fund programs by statute, cash-flow visibility is high and receivables carry little credit risk, but the same customers can modify or cancel contracts, so visibility does not equal protection.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 7–10; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 71; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 80; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Maximus is a listed public company with no sponsor, no disclosed controlling holder and a wholly-owned, guarantor subsidiary structure. Creditors therefore rely on the bank facility and operating cash flow rather than on sponsor support; the Board has authorised USD 400 million of repurchases and raised the dividend, and ownership percentages, director independence and related-party dealings sit in a proxy statement that was not supplied, so the governance risk level of medium rests on the annual report alone.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 84; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 102–103

### Revenue, cost and cash conversion

Revenue durability is average. Performance-based contracts were 54.4% of fiscal 2025 revenue, so volumes on a few large federal programs drive results; U.S. Federal Services revenue fell 2.5% over nine months to 2026-06-30 and U.S. Services fell 6.4%, partly from disposals, while backlog fell 5.2% in fiscal 2025. Management expects U.S. Services to return to organic growth in the fourth quarter and points to Medicaid and SNAP rule changes, but those are expectations, not contracted revenue.
Basis: calculation N10; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 80; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 49; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 26–28; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 17; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Margin resilience is average to strong. Labor is about two-thirds of cost of revenue and a large share of expenses is fixed, so a lost contract deleverages quickly, yet segment margins improved: the Federal segment operating margin rose to 17.5% over nine months against 15.3% and the group adjusted EBITDA margin to 14.0% against 13.2%, which management attributes to automation and AI-enabled tools; the modification is guided to bring the Federal margin for the year to 16.5% to 17.0%.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 26–27; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 36; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_mms-2026x06x30x8kxex991.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3

Cash conversion is average in a normal year and weak in the latest nine months. Capital spending is light at 1.16% of fiscal 2025 revenue, down from the technology refresh peak, so EBITDA converts to free cash flow when collections are normal: free cash flow was 0.55x reported EBITDA in fiscal 2025. In the nine months to 2026-06-30 receivables absorbed cash, income taxes paid were USD 81.479 million and the free cash flow was negative.
Basis: calculation N11; calculation Q5; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 65–66; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 51; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 19; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Competition and policy

Competition is credit-neutral to mildly negative. Competitors named in the annual report are large federal contractors and consultancies, buyer power is high because government customers rebid and can cancel for convenience, supplier power sits in labor, and the government may perform work itself, so pricing power is limited and margin gains come from efficiency, not price. Entry barriers are not quantified in the filings.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 26

Policy is the largest outside influence. The 2025 federal statute adds Medicaid redeterminations every six months and work requirements from 2026-12-31 that states may delay, and ties SNAP funding to error rates from fiscal 2028; interest rates matter because part of the debt floats. These factors can raise demand for eligibility work or delay it, and federal budget or contract changes can cut revenue quickly, so they are credit-material and are carried into the monitoring triggers.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-09-30_mms-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 36

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over twelve to twenty-four months the guided fiscal 2026 outcome is revenue of USD 5.2 billion to USD 5.35 billion toward the lower end, adjusted EBITDA margin of about 13.7% and free cash flow of USD 425 million to USD 475 million, all lowered or held after the customer modification; the modification runs to 2026-12-31 and reduces diluted earnings per share by about USD 0.35 per quarter, roughly USD 18.57 million of net income a quarter on about 53.057 million diluted shares. Tailwinds are the Medicaid and SNAP rule changes, pipeline of USD 50.4 billion and AI-enabled efficiency; headwinds are federal payment timing, lower volumes on large federal contracts, a loss-making international segment guided to break even, and floating-rate debt.

Over three to five years the credit depends on whether backlog converts, whether state demand arrives on the legislated schedule, and whether management uses cash for debt reduction or shareholder returns. The Term Loan A matures on 2029-05-30 and the Term Loan B on 2031-05-30, so refinancing capacity will depend on leverage and cash conversion in those years.

### Qualitative downside scenario

The realistic downside is a federal payment delay that lasts longer than management expects, combined with a curtailed large contract. The first operational break is a receivable build that turns operating cash flow negative again, as it did in the nine months to 2026-06-30; cash, which was USD 56.953 million, is then replenished from the revolver (stated access about USD 730.0 million), from receivable sales (limit USD 350.0 million) or from new debt, and if the revolver were fully drawn carrying-value debt would reach 3.31x last-twelve-month EBITDA, an arithmetic bound that is not a covenant measure. Management's levers are slowing buybacks, trimming the dividend and capital spending, and the revolver; the resilience looks sufficient to avoid liquidity distress through 2027 on the stated facilities, subject to terms of the credit agreement that were not supplied, and the observable triggers are days sales outstanding above 98 days, revolver drawings, a lower guidance range and further repurchases.
Basis: calculation N6; 10-Q_2026-06-30_mms-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 31–32; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_mms-2026x06x30x8kxex991.htm.jsonl, page 4; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Overall credit view

Overall, Maximus presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, contract life and a government customer base, but credit quality is constrained by rising debt, weak recent cash conversion and shareholder returns that continued while cash was thin. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because fiscal 2025 free cash flow was strong and the delayed receipts are being collected, but the recovery is not yet shown in a balance sheet. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on leverage and collections in 2029 and 2031. The primary downside path is a prolonged federal payment delay or a lost large contract while shareholder returns continue. Further analysis would require the results after 2026-06-30, the credit agreement and the proxy statement. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: federal collections.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)
