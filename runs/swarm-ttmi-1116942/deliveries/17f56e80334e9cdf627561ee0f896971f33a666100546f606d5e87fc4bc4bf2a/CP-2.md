## Findings

### Bottom line

TTM's fundamental credit is adequate and improving on earnings but now acquisition-dependent: net leverage of 1.31x at 2026-09-28 becomes 3.20x pro forma for the Epiq closing on 2026-09-30, and thin free cash flow leaves little cushion if AI-driven demand slows.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-28_ttmi-20260928.htm.jsonl, page 24; calculation G3; calculation G4

Earnings are the support: last-twelve-months EBITDA of USD 584.3 million is 2.88 times the USD 203.0 million of FY2023, and liquidity was USD 1,610.2 million before the Epiq payment. The 3.20x uses stated transaction amounts and excludes Epiq's own EBITDA, which is not supplied, so leverage after the acquisition is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation G1; calculation G5; calculation G4

It was 2.81x on a last-twelve-months basis at 2026-09-28, after the 2026 borrowings for the Swiss purchase and for Epiq.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation G1; calculation G2

The Ophir Optics purchase of USD 650.0 million, if debt-funded, would add to it.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against caution is that the earnings base is large and still rising: EBITDA margin reached 15.3% on a last-twelve-months basis, reported net leverage was only 1.31x before Epiq, management states covenant compliance, and long-term defense contracts carried USD 463.5 million of remaining obligations at 2026-09-28. If Epiq earns material EBITDA, the 3.20x overstates leverage, and the Incremental Facilities equal the Epiq price, so the revolver headroom was largely preserved. Epiq is described as adding technology for defense customers, which could rebalance a mix that is now led by data center sales.

The view would improve if the fourth-quarter report shows Epiq EBITDA near or above the illustration, free cash flow turns clearly positive after capital spending, and Ophir Optics is funded without new debt. It would weaken if data center and networking sales fall from their third-quarter level of USD 498.0 million, if capital spending runs at the implied fourth-quarter pace without a matching rise in operating cash flow, or if repurchases continue while leverage is elevated after the acquisitions.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The main strengths are these. First, earnings have more than doubled since FY2023 and drove total leverage from 4.52x to 2.22x at FY2025, which gives the debt a much larger earnings base and lowers refinancing risk. Second, liquidity of USD 1,610.2 million at 2026-09-28 and a maturity ladder with only USD 181.0 million due before 2029 give time to digest the acquisitions. Third, the defense franchise is anchored in long-term contracts with USD 463.5 million of remaining performance obligations and a US Navy radar position, which supports a steadier base beside the data center cycle. Fourth, margin gains in both segments show operating leverage working in the issuer's favour while volumes are high. Fifth, there is no sponsor and no dividend, so no external owner draws cash out.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-28_ttmi-20260928.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-28_ttmi-20260928.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-Q_2026-09-28_ttmi-20260928.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-29_ttmi-20251229.htm.jsonl, page 9; calculation G1; calculation G2; calculation G5; calculation G11

The main weaknesses are these. First, the Epiq purchase and the pending Ophir Optics purchase raise net leverage to 3.20x pro forma while the target's earnings are unseen, which makes the next two quarters decisive for creditors. Second, free cash flow is thin and capital spending is stepping up, so deleveraging depends on EBITDA growth rather than cash generation. Third, earnings are concentrated in the Commercial segment and in AI data center demand, which makes cash flow more cyclical than the defense history suggests. Fourth, customer concentration and the reliance on supplier payables expose operating cash flow to a single payment or terms change. Fifth, the new term loans are floating rate and the reported interest cover of 12.72x does not include them.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-28_ttmi-20260928.htm.jsonl, page 43; calculation G4; calculation G6; calculation G8; calculation G9; calculation G10

### Company description

TTM Technologies makes printed circuit boards, RF and microwave components and assemblies, and engineered mission systems, and sells to equipment makers, contract manufacturers, distributors and governments. About 59% of nine-month net sales came from operations outside the United States, revenue is mostly recognized over time as customer-specific products are built, and the business is not seasonal. The credit link is that long-term defense contracts and a diversified customer list support cash-flow visibility, while the AI-driven commercial boom, high fixed costs and acquisition debt make debt service more cyclical than the defense history alone would suggest.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-29_ttmi-20251229.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-12-29_ttmi-20251229.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-09-28_ttmi-20260928.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-28_ttmi-20260928.htm.jsonl, page 39; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Ownership and group structure

TTM is a widely held public company with no sponsor, parent or controlling holder named in the documents read, so sponsor extraction is not the risk; holder percentages sit in a proxy statement not supplied. The borrower is the parent, the notes and credit facilities are guaranteed by domestic subsidiaries only, and 63.1% of long-lived assets and USD 241.1 million of cash sit outside the United States, so the guarantee package covers domestic subsidiaries only and claims on foreign operating assets rank behind those subsidiaries' own creditors, which is structural subordination; foreign subsidiary borrowers may draw up to USD 300.0 million of the revolver. Epiq joined as a guarantor on 2026-09-30. Ranking and collateral are for the Structural priority analysis; this module records that the secured term loan and revolver pledge only 65% of certain foreign subsidiaries' stock.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue rests on two engines: defense electronics sold through government budgets and long-term contracts, and commercial circuit boards for AI data centers, automotive and medical customers. Data center and networking was 39% of nine-month net sales against 30%, aerospace and defense 37% against 45%, and long-term defense contracts carried USD 463.5 million of remaining obligations with about 55% to be recognized within 12 months. Pricing power is only partly evidenced: gross margin rose 2.0 percentage points to 22.4% on volume and mix, quick-turn work earns premium pricing, but no contract pass-through of raw material costs is described. Revenue is therefore predictable in defense, cyclical and concentrated in data center and networking, and moderately recurring overall; the implication is that cash flow durability is adequate rather than strong.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Management states that operating costs are relatively fixed and that small revenue shortfalls reduce operating margin, so operating leverage works both ways: gross margin rose to 22.4% and operating margin to 11.3% on higher volume, and the same fixed base would amplify a slowdown. Unallocated corporate costs are also growing, with stock-based compensation of USD 61.7 million against USD 29.6 million and other corporate costs of USD 103.5 million against USD 66.4 million in nine months, which reduces the margin that segment profit converts into group profit. Raw materials such as copper and gold are exposed to price swings and some components have sole sources. Margins can be defended against moderate volume pressure, but not against a sharp fall in data center orders.

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital spending is heavy and rising: gross purchases of USD 292.6 million in FY2025 were 10.1% of net sales, nine-month net purchases of USD 251.6 million in 2026 were 8.2%, and equipment of USD 167.3 million was unpaid in payables. The filings do not split maintenance from growth spending, so flexibility cannot be measured, although the new Eau Claire, Penang and Syracuse capacity is clearly growth-driven and could be slowed. Operating cash flow converts 58.5% of nine-month EBITDA, and free cash flow after capital spending was USD 21.2 million, so EBITDA does not yet translate into durable free cash flow for deleveraging.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense: printed circuit boards are fragmented with larger, lower-cost Chinese and Taiwanese competitors, while defense systems compete with BAE Systems, Curtiss-Wright, Leonardo DRS, Mercury Systems and Sanmina; the implication is price pressure in commercial boards and a premium for qualified defense work. New entrants face facility security clearances and customer qualification in defense, while the annual report notes that new competitors may enter commercial boards, which limits pricing power there. Buyer power is high because a few customers and contract manufacturers account for a large share of sales and receivables. Supplier power is moderate: laminates and chemicals are broadly available, but some components for assemblies have limited or sole sources. Substitution risk is not discussed in the documents read.

### Macro and policy factors

Policy and currency factors are material to this credit. Both the US and Chinese governments have put circuit boards under tariffs, about 59% of nine-month sales come from non-US operations, and costs and cash are exposed to the Chinese yuan, Malaysian ringgit and Swiss franc, with a net investment hedge on the Swiss purchase. Defense sales depend on US budget appropriations and contract terminations, and export rules shape competition. The credit effect is margin and cash volatility that is partly outside management's control.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months the tailwinds are AI data center capacity demand, defense budgets and program bookings, and new capacity at Eau Claire, Penang and Syracuse, which together support growth and margin. The headwinds are integration of STG, Epiq and Ophir Optics, capital spending that exceeds operating cash flow, higher interest on floating-rate loans, tariffs and currency swings. Over three to five years the main questions are whether AI demand proves durable rather than cyclical, whether Epiq earnings cover its price, and whether the 2029 notes and 2030 term loan balance are refinanced at acceptable cost; refinancing confidence rests on earnings, which is why a data center slowdown is the key risk to it.

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a pause in AI data center orders or the loss of volume at one of the largest customers; one customer was about 20% of third-quarter sales and the five largest were 44% of nine-month sales. The first operational break is lower volume in the Commercial segment, whose fixed cost base would cut margin quickly, and that segment supplied more profit than the whole group. Financially, EBITDA falls while the pro forma net debt of USD 1,867.5 million stays, so net leverage rises above 3.20x, free cash flow turns negative because committed capital spending continues, and the cash cushion funds the gap. Management levers are slowing growth capital spending, suspending repurchases and not closing Ophir Optics; whether they suffice depends on the maintenance share of spending, which is not stated, and on covenant definitions, which are not supplied. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress in a moderate downturn because liquidity was large and no large maturity falls before 2029, but leverage-based tests and refinancing access become the binding constraints. The observable trigger is a fall in data center and networking sales below the third-quarter level. Primary downside path: a data center slowdown that cuts Commercial segment profit while acquisition debt and capital spending stay fixed.

### Overall credit view

Overall, TTM presents an adequate business profile supported by a recovered earnings base, ample pre-acquisition liquidity and a defense contract anchor, but credit quality is constrained by acquisition-driven leverage, thin free cash flow and concentration in one segment and a few customers. Cash-flow durability appears adequate rather than strong because operating cash flow depends on supplier payables and capital spending is rising. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on Epiq's earnings, data center demand and market access for the 2029 notes and 2030 term loan. The primary downside path is a data center slowdown against fixed acquisition debt and capital commitments. Further analysis would require Epiq's financials, covenant definitions and compliance certificates, post-closing liquidity and rating or peer data. Fundamental credit review completed with limitations. Top risk: acquisition leverage built on an unseen target while free cash flow is thin.
