## Findings

### Bottom line

CoreCivic's net debt fell from USD 1,122.8 million at the end of 2025 to USD 75.9 million at 30 September 2026 after four facility sales, so leverage is now low, but the fall rests on one-off proceeds and earnings are still rebuilding.
Basis: calculation K26; calculation K27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 14

On trailing adjusted earnings of USD 429.8 million, built from the 2025 annual statements and the 2026 releases, gross debt is 1.45x and cash interest is covered 5.10x. Reported EBITDA includes the USD 1,770.7 million sale gain, so ratios on it are not meaningful. Operating cash flow was an outflow of USD 266.6 million for the nine months because USD 518.7 million of tax on the gains was paid in cash.
Basis: calculation K28; calculation K29; calculation K30; calculation K31; calculation K32; calculation K33; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxw_d165428dex991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4

### Leverage and coverage

Year-end leverage was high and stable before the sales: gross debt was 3.55x, 3.29x and 3.37x reported EBITDA at the end of 2023, 2024 and 2025, and net debt was 3.10x at the end of 2025. In the third quarter of 2026 the sale proceeds repaid the revolver, both term loans and the 4.75% notes due 2027, leaving the 8.25% notes due 2029 and the Kansas mortgage note, which is why gross debt is now 1.45x adjusted earnings. The mechanism that matters to creditors is that the claim is smaller and longer-dated but backed by fewer owned facilities: property and equipment fell from USD 2,132.2 million to USD 1,822.8 million while CoreCivic continues to run the sold facilities under existing federal contracts. The consequence is better leverage and cover with weaker asset backing and a concentration on one federal customer, and the supplied documents do not commit the remaining proceeds to debt reduction rather than buybacks.
Basis: calculation K05; calculation K07; calculation K29; 10-K_2025-12-31_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 162; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 2

### Cash flow quality

Cash conversion was weak even before the sales. Fiscal 2025 operating cash flow was USD 194.6 million against reported EBITDA of USD 362.1 million because receivables and other assets absorbed USD 145.0 million. For the nine months to September 2026 operating cash flow was an outflow of USD 266.6 million because USD 518.7 million of income tax was paid, primarily on the gain on the sale of the four facilities, while the proceeds arrived in investing cash flow as USD 2,113.4 million of net proceeds from asset sales. Financing outflows of USD 1,166.3 million included USD 561.1 million of share repurchases, so the cash balance rose to USD 547.2 million from USD 97.9 million, and the next move in net debt depends on further buybacks against future operating cash flow.
Basis: calculation K21; 10-K_2025-12-31_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 163; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxw.htm.jsonl, page 67

### Liquidity and maturities

Liquidity at 30 September 2026 was USD 547.2 million of cash plus USD 551.5 million undrawn on the revolver after USD 23.5 million of letters of credit. Scheduled principal is small until 2029: USD 1.6 million for the rest of 2026, USD 6.9 million in 2027 and USD 7.4 million in 2028, then USD 508.0 million in 2029 when the 8.25% notes fall due. The credit agreement allows unlimited restricted payments only while its secured leverage ratio is at or below 1.50x and the notes indenture only while its total leverage ratio is at or below 2.00x. Adjusted gross debt of 1.45x earnings is below both levels but is not either covenant measure, and covenant headroom cannot be calculated because the covenant definitions were not supplied.

### Data quality and definition risks

Five definition points limit how the figures can be used. The issuer reports net leverage of negative 0.1x at 30 September 2026 without a definition, while carrying-value debt exceeds cash on the balance sheet; the figure is consistent with leaving out the Kansas notes, which sit in an unrestricted subsidiary, but the issuer does not say so. Reported EBITDA is the primary denominator under the method, but the sale gain makes the trailing figure meaningless, so adjusted ratios are shown beside it and are labelled as built from a house-adjusted fiscal 2025 figure. The earlier source readiness note called USD 629.6 million total debt; that is principal, and the carrying value used here is USD 623.1 million. Gross interest is printed only to one decimal in millions for the full years, so fiscal EBITDA is exact to about USD 0.05 million. Segments changed in the second quarter of 2026 and fiscal 2025 has not been recast.

Events after the balance sheet date are few. On 3 November 2026 the board raised the repurchase authorization by USD 500.0 million, the accelerated repurchase agreement signed on 10 August 2026 settles before the end of the second quarter of 2027 and may deliver or require shares or cash in amounts not stated, and discussions with the federal customer about buying further facilities are preliminary. None is in the period figures. The sale of the four facilities, the repayments and the repurchase payment all fall inside the third quarter and are in the September balance sheet. Deferred revenue is small at USD 6.0 million, so there is no material non-debt funding float; accounts payable and accrued expenses rose to USD 497.5 million from USD 353.2 million, an increase the balance sheet does not explain and that is not tied here to any funding source.
