## Findings

### Bottom line

Net debt has risen at every balance-sheet date shown, to USD 8,457 million at September 30, 2026, and liquidity has fallen to its lowest of the five dates, so creditors depend on the Bay Minette start-up and insurance recoveries to turn cash flow positive.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_nvl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3–5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nvl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nvl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 61; calculation C06; calculation C07; calculation C08; calculation C09; calculation C10; calculation C26

On reported EBITDA the last-twelve-months net leverage is 7.99x; on the issuer's adjusted EBITDA, which adds back USD 1,140 million of Oswego fire losses net of recoveries and deducts USD 574 million of metal price lag gains, it is 4.62x, close to the 4.7x the issuer states on its own definition. Cash interest paid is printed for fiscal years only, so interim cover cannot be built.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-11-05_novelisq2fy27results.htm.jsonl, pages 8–10; calculation C15; calculation C18; calculation C10

### Leverage and coverage

Net debt rose from USD 5,117 million at March 31, 2025 to USD 8,457 million at September 30, 2026, while last-twelve-months reported EBITDA fell from USD 1,669 million to USD 1,058 million. Reported net leverage therefore moved from 3.07x to 7.99x, with the peak of 8.10x at June 30, 2026. The mechanism is a working-capital and capital-spending build that was financed with short-term borrowings, at a time when fire losses and start-up costs cut reported earnings. Fiscal 2026 net debt was held down by USD 950 million of equity contributions from the shareholder, so the underlying build is larger than the net debt series shows.
Basis: calculation C06; calculation C07; calculation C08; calculation C09; calculation C10; 10-K_2026-03-31_nvl-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 111

On the issuer's adjusted EBITDA the same net debt is 2.84x at March 31, 2025 and 4.62x at September 30, 2026, and each of the five dates is higher than the one before. The issuer targets about 3.5x during its investment cycle and says tariffs and the Oswego fires have pushed leverage above it. The adjusted measure also includes the share of joint-venture EBITDA the issuer manages on a proportional basis, so it is not a consolidated figure.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_nvl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 65; 10-K_2026-03-31_nvl-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 99; calculation C21

Interest cover is available for fiscal years only: reported EBITDA covered cash interest paid 6.76x in FY2025 and 3.62x in FY2026, and adjusted EBITDA 7.30x and 6.80x. The fall in reported cover is the fire-loss effect, not a rise in interest paid, which was USD 247 million and USD 242 million.
Basis: calculation C22; 10-K_2026-03-31_nvl-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 112

### Cash flow and liquidity

The six months to September 30, 2026 consumed USD 1,961 million before financing: operating cash flow was an outflow of USD 418 million and capital expenditure was USD 1,543 million, against USD 138 million of insurance proceeds. Inventories absorbed USD 733 million, and the issuer attributes the outflow to unfavorable working capital changes and the Oswego fires. Net revolving borrowing of USD 1,330 million, almost all on the ABL Revolver, funded the gap, and the USD 500 million 2026 term loan arrived in July. Short-term borrowings of USD 2,631 million now exceed cash of USD 1,277 million, and principal due within twelve months is USD 2,704 million, most of it short-term borrowings, of which the ABL Revolver draw is USD 2,266 million on a facility that matures in 2031.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_nvl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nvl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–19; calculation C27

Liquidity of USD 2,038 million at September 30, 2026 compares with USD 2,756 million at March 31, 2026, and committed availability fell from USD 1,502 million to USD 761 million. Confirmed supplier invoices under supply chain finance were USD 1.5 billion at September 30, 2026 and USD 1,262 million at March 31, 2026; they sit in accounts payable, not debt, and are float that would have to be funded if the programmes shrank.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_nvl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 61; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nvl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-K_2026-03-31_nvl-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 132; calculation C23

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against concern is that reported EBITDA is depressed by items the issuer treats as one-off: adjusted EBITDA of USD 508 million in the second quarter was 20% above the prior-year quarter, the Oswego hot mill restarted in June, and insurance recoveries of USD 519 million have been received cumulatively. If the Bay Minette plant moves to commercial shipments and capital spending falls as the issuer expects, adjusted net leverage near 4.62x would start to fall. The view would turn if working capital keeps absorbing cash or if start-up costs, which the issuer adds back, continue after commercial production begins.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-11-05_novelisq2fy27results.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nvl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 47–48; 10-Q_2026-09-30_nvl-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 67–68

### Data quality and definitions

Three definitions drive the numbers. EBITDA (Reported) is the issuer's unadjusted EBITDA, while Adjusted EBITDA adds back fire, restructuring, derivative and start-up items and deducts metal price lag gains. Total debt here is carrying value of USD 9,734 million, against contractual principal of USD 9,797 million, and the issuer's Adjusted Net Debt of USD 8,520 million is built on principal and cash, so it is USD 63 million higher than net debt on carrying value. The 10-K labels the Series 2026A Bonds as due June 2033 in its debt table while its own footnote and the later quarterly reports give a March 2033 tender date, and this answer uses the later reports' wording.
