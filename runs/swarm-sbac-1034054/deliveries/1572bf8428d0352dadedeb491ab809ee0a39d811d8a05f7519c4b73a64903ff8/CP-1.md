## Findings

### Bottom line

Leverage is easing slowly but stays high: net debt was 6.45x adjusted EBITDA at 2026-09-30 against 7.07x at end-2024, while adjusted cash interest cover fell to 4.07x from 4.79x, so creditors depend on refinancing access.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 102; calculation C02; calculation C06

On reported EBITDA, which is after impairment, acquisition and non-cash compensation charges, net leverage was 7.39x. The July 2026 refinancing put USD 3,500 million of unsecured notes in place of bank debt and left the USD 2,500 million revolver undrawn, but the USD 1,165 million 2021-1C Tower Securities tranche has an anticipated repayment date of 2026-11-09 that the filings do not show as settled, so liquidity and market access are the live tests.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 55; calculation C06; calculation C07; calculation C10; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Financial interpretation

Leverage has eased less than the headline suggests. Net debt on unrestricted cash fell from USD 13,401.897 million at the end of 2024 to USD 12,478.409 million at 2026-09-30, and net debt over adjusted EBITDA fell from 7.07x to 6.45x. But USD 1,165 million of the restricted cash held at the end of 2024 was escrow set aside to repay a Tower Securities tranche in January 2025, so counting it end-2024 net leverage on adjusted EBITDA was 6.46x, then 6.61x at the end of 2025 and 6.45x at 2026-09-30. Creditors should read adjusted leverage as roughly flat near 6.5x, with the fall in the headline ratio largely a cash-classification effect.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 102; 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 122; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 11; calculation C02; calculation C06; calculation C12

Reported EBITDA sits below the issuer's adjusted measure because the issuer adds back impairment and decommissioning costs, acquisition costs and non-cash compensation. In FY2025 impairment and decommissioning costs rose to USD 184.165 million from USD 107.925 million, which pulled reported EBITDA down to USD 1,635.071 million from USD 1,705.28 million while adjusted EBITDA rose to USD 1,912.104 million from USD 1,894.345 million. FY2025 net income of USD 1,054.456 million was lifted by a USD 208.399 million gain on sale of assets and a USD 119.925 million currency gain on intercompany loans, both outside EBITDA, so earnings quality is better judged through EBITDA and cash flow than through net income. The contrary view is that most impairment is non-cash (USD 174.085 million in FY2025), so adjusted EBITDA may describe cash earnings better; recurring impairment at this level would favour the reported measure.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 69; 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 103; 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 104; 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 105; calculation C03; calculation C09

The interest burden is rising faster than earnings. Cash paid for interest increased from USD 395.112 million in FY2024 to USD 467.404 million in FY2025 and USD 475.532 million in the last twelve months, so cover on adjusted EBITDA slipped from 4.79x to 4.07x and on reported EBITDA stands at 3.55x. The 2026 senior notes carry a blended coupon of 5.113%, against 1.631% on the 2021-1C Tower Securities the issuer expects to refinance, so that refinancing would add to interest cost. Operating cash flow of USD 1,298.132 million in the last twelve months funded capital expenditure of USD 246.325 million and left free cash flow of USD 1,051.807 million, which dividends of USD 517.343 million and buybacks of USD 518.552 million absorbed almost entirely; acquisitions and debt repayment therefore depended on asset sale proceeds and new borrowing.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 105–106; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 17; calculation C04; calculation C05; calculation C06; calculation C16

Liquidity at 2026-09-30 was USD 2,917.532 million, made of cash of USD 417.532 million and the undrawn USD 2,500 million revolver, which also backs the commercial paper program. That is less than the USD 3,557.467 million of debt classified as current: the 2021-1C Tower Securities at their anticipated date of 2026-11-09, the 2020 Senior Notes due 2027-02-15 and the 2021-2C Tower Securities at 2027-04-09. The issuer says cash, facility availability and operating cash flow will cover debt service and final maturities over the next twelve months and anticipates refinancing the 2021-1C tranche. The strongest support is the market access shown by USD 3,500 million of unsecured senior notes issued in July 2026 and described as investment grade; the view would worsen if the 2021-1C tranche is not refinanced on or soon after its date, which the filings do not resolve, or if distributions continue at the current pace while 2027 maturities are drawn on the revolver.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 54–55; calculation C07; calculation C08

Deferred revenue, a customer-funded and non-interest-bearing liability, was USD 115.222 million at 2026-09-30 against USD 127.308 million at the end of 2024, a fall of 9.49%.
