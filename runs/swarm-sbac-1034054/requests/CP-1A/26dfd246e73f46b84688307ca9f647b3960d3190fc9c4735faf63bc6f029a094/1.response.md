## Findings

### Bottom line

Governance is neutral to modestly supportive: no sponsor or controlling holder is identified and the articles generally cap any holder at 9.8%, but shareholder payouts absorb most free cash flow, so creditors rely on refinancing access and asset sales rather than retained cash for deleveraging.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 48; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 6; calculation C8

Customer concentration and domestic churn are the larger risks. Three U.S. carriers supplied 66.5% of FY2025 revenue, domestic site leasing revenue fell 3.14% in nine months 2026, and dividends and buybacks took 86.54% of operating cash flow after capital expenditure. The July 2026 sale of USD 3,500 million of unsecured notes eased near-term refinancing risk, but the outcome for the 2021-1C Tower Securities due on 2026-11-09 and the proxy statement are not seen.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 39; calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C8

### Key findings

Control risk is low because the filings identify no sponsor or controlling holder, the board alone decides dividends and repurchases, and the articles generally bar any holder above 9.8%. That removes the sponsor-extraction and leveraged-buyout event risks that usually dominate high-yield governance, though holder-level ownership is untested because the proxy statement is not supplied. We rate sponsor and governance risk Medium rather than Low, because the payout policy and the unseen debt documents offset the clean control picture.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 48; 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 54; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 1

The business model is a durable landlord franchise, but its domestic leg is shrinking. Site leasing produced 98.3% of segment operating profit in nine months 2026, on contracts with annual escalators; yet domestic site leasing revenue fell 3.14% as Sprint, EchoStar and other leases were not renewed, while international site leasing revenue rose 18.5% at constant currency, mainly from acquired and newly built towers. Management expects 2026 churn of USD 133.0 million to USD 136.0 million in the U.S. and USD 37.0 million to USD 39.0 million abroad, so net growth now leans on international acquisitions and tenancy rather than on U.S. rent.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 30; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 39; calculation C3; calculation C5; calculation C13

Customer concentration is the largest operating risk and one counterparty has already failed to pay. T-Mobile, AT&T and Verizon together supplied 66.5% of FY2025 revenue and 87.8% of domestic site leasing revenue, and T-Mobile and Verizon supplied 96.1% of site development revenue. EchoStar defaulted on its payment obligations in December 2025 and the default continued into 2026, with expected churn of USD 56.0 million in 2026; the company says the default has not had and is not expected to have a material adverse effect.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19; 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 23; calculation C4

Shareholder payouts, not sponsor extraction, are the governance leakage to watch. Dividends and buybacks took 86.54% of the cash that operating cash flow left after capital expenditure in nine months 2026, and 91.59% in FY2025, while debt principal ended the nine months USD 15.25 million higher. Management ranks acquisitions and new towers first, buybacks second and dividend growth third, and says debt repayment can be an accretive use of cash when rates are high; the nine-month record shows the ranking in practice put payouts ahead of debt reduction.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 54; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 46; calculation C8; calculation C12

The July 2026 refinancing improved maturity and security profile but changed the covenant yardstick. The USD 3,500 million of notes and the new USD 2,500 million revolver are unsecured, and they replaced a secured term loan and a drawn revolver, so the debt the filings state as secured fell to 49.90% of principal from 76.85% at the 2025 year end, all of it now in the Tower Securities. The leverage test moved to 7.50x on a consolidated basis from 6.5x on the old borrower group, so the earlier headroom cannot be carried over, and whether any subsidiary guarantees the new notes is not stated.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 128–129; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 52; calculation C11; calculation C12

Portfolio pruning narrows the footprint while Central America and Brazil gain weight. The company sold its Philippines, Colombia and substantially all Canada operations in 2025, sold its Chile and Ecuador towers in the third quarter of 2026 for USD 134.2 million, and agreed to sell Peru, leaving about 10 of 12 international markets by our count. Brazil produced 14.1% of site leasing revenue and Guatemala holds about 10% of towers after the Millicom purchase, so single-country exposure rises as the tail of small markets is cut.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 125; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 25; calculation C14; calculation C15; calculation C19

Disclosure supports monitoring but not governance diligence. The auditor's FY2025 opinion says the statements present fairly in all material respects, its opinion on internal control is unqualified and officers found disclosure controls effective at 2026-09-30, yet board independence, related-party dealings and beneficial ownership sit in a proxy statement that is not supplied, and a director's trading arrangement adopted on 2026-06-18 was omitted from the second-quarter report and disclosed in the third.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 93; 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 99; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 60

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against a merely neutral-to-modest governance view is that the payouts are funded from operating cash flow rather than borrowing, the board refinanced into unsecured notes that the company describes as investment grade, with an undrawn USD 2,500 million revolver, and the 9.8% ownership cap leaves no single holder positioned to direct a leveraged distribution. The company also describes debt repayment as potentially accretive, and no related-party fee, sponsor payment or dividend recapitalisation is disclosed in the supplied filings.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 48; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 46

The view would strengthen if the next filings show debt principal falling while payouts stay at the current rate, if the 2021-1C Tower Securities due on 2026-11-09 are refinanced on time, and if the proxy statement shows no holder near the cap. It would weaken if buybacks are funded by revolver or commercial paper borrowings, if payouts exceed operating cash flow after capital expenditure, if a further customer fails after EchoStar, or if the executed notes and credit agreement show wide restricted-payment capacity that the filings only describe as customary.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 51; calculation C8

### Limitations

- The ownership and control conclusion rests on the articles' 9.8% cap and on the absence of any named holder in the supplied text; holder-level ownership, board independence and related-party items are only in the 2026 proxy statement, so a holder near the cap or an interested director could exist unseen and would weaken the low control-risk view.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 48; 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 93
- The ranking and structure conclusion uses the filings' summaries of the notes, the revolver and the mortgage loan; the executed indentures and credit agreement are absent, so guarantors of the 2026 notes, restricted-payment capacity and the security of the 2020 and 2021 notes are not established and the 49.90% secured share could be understated or overstated.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 51; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 52
- The capital allocation conclusion compares payouts with operating cash flow after capital expenditure, but the filings do not tie the third-quarter buybacks to a funding source, so whether they were paid from the July borrowing proceeds or from cash flow is not established and the payouts-first reading could be too harsh or too mild.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 20; calculation C17
- The customer concentration conclusion uses revenue shares only; lease expiry dates, termination rights and the EchoStar receivable are not stated, so the pace at which concentration could turn into further churn is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 23
- The disposal and acquisition conclusions stop at 2026-11-05: the consideration for Peru, the price for the joint venture partner's put and the use of disposal proceeds are not stated, so the direction of the portfolio change is clear but its cash effect is not.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The defining transaction of 2026 is the 2026-07-23 refinancing. The company issued USD 3,500 million of unsecured senior notes under its automatic shelf registration in three tranches (USD 1,350 million due January 2030, USD 1,350 million due July 2031 and USD 800 million due July 2033), terminated its prior senior credit agreement and entered a new unsecured agreement with the parent as borrower that provides a USD 2,500 million revolving facility maturing 2031-07-23. Net proceeds repaid the revolver (USD 1.0 billion) and the 2024 Term Loan (USD 2.2 billion) and funded general corporate purposes; USD 16.1 million of deferred financing fees and USD 4.0 million of discount were expensed. The new agreement carries a total net leverage ceiling of 7.50x (up to 8.00x for four quarters after qualified acquisitions) and a senior secured leverage ceiling of 3.50x, and on 2026-09-25 the company added a USD 2,500 million commercial paper program backed by the revolver, with nothing drawn at the filing date.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 16–19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 49–52; calculation C11

The secured debt that remains is the Tower Securities, a non-recourse mortgage loan to a group of subsidiary borrowers backed by 8,603 towers at 2026-09-30 (9,498 at 2025-12-31). The 2020-1C Tower Securities of USD 750 million were repaid on 2026-01-09 with revolver borrowings, and the USD 1,165 million 2021-1C Tower Securities have an anticipated repayment date of 2026-11-09, which the company says it expects to refinance; the 2021-2C Tower Securities follow on 2027-04-09. If an anticipated repayment date is missed, cash flow from the pledged towers is trapped and the interest rate steps up, so the date is a hard test of market access rather than a legal maturity.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 131–133; calculation C7

Acquisitions have shifted from a large Central America purchase to small and opportunistic buying. The 2024 purchase agreement with Millicom for over 7,000 sites at about USD 975.0 million in cash closed during 2025 and brought 7,110 towers, a seven-year right to build up to 2,500 sites and 15-year lease-back agreements; total 2025 tower acquisitions cost USD 1,009.935 million. In nine months 2026 the company bought 23 towers and the rights to the land under about 3,900 Guatemala sites (USD 109.0 million), spent USD 204.677 million in all on acquisitions, and has 69 further sites for USD 17.9 million expected to close in the fourth quarter. Contingent consideration of up to USD 62.3 million is not recorded.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 70; 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 5 and 124; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 47

Disposals and shareholder actions complete the picture. The company completed the Philippines sale on 2025-01-10, sold the Philippines and Colombia towers and substantially all of its Canada operations in 2025 for USD 330.4 million in total with a USD 208.4 million gain, sold all Chile and Ecuador towers in the third quarter of 2026 for USD 134.2 million, and agreed on 2026-09-10 to sell its Peru towers, with USD 77.6 million of assets held for sale. The board authorised a USD 1,500 million repurchase plan on 2025-04-27, of which USD 822.884 million remained at 2026-09-30, and a joint venture partner has given notice of its put to sell its interest, expected to close in the fourth quarter subject to regulatory approval.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 106; 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 54 and 125; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 13–14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 26 and 60
