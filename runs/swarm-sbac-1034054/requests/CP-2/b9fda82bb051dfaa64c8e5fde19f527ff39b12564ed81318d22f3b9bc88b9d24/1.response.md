## Findings

### Bottom line

Fundamental credit is adequate: a high-margin, contract-backed tower portfolio and demonstrated bond-market access support creditors, but high leverage and payouts that absorb 98.49% of free cash flow leave refinancing of near-term maturities as the decisive risk.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 17; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C4

Liquidity covers 81.95% of the USD 3,560 million of principal due within twelve months at the Tower Securities' anticipated dates, and 96.97% with a year of free cash flow after dividends, so continued market access is needed even if buybacks stop.

### Key findings

Business quality is the main support: revenue is contracted, recurring and high-margin, but its domestic leg is shrinking. Site leasing produced 98.3% of segment operating profit in nine months 2026, tenant leases run 5 to 15 years with annual escalators, the domestic segment margin was 84.24% and the international 70.42%, and about 71% of towers sit on owned or long-dated land. Against that, domestic site leasing revenue fell 3.14% as Sprint, EchoStar and other leases were not renewed, and three U.S. carriers supply 66.5% of revenue, so a further carrier event would reach cash flow quickly.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an adequate-but-refinancing-dependent view is that the contractual picture is comfortable and the payouts are discretionary. Only the USD 1,500 million 2020 Senior Notes have a final maturity inside twelve months, and liquidity covers that 194.50%; the company states that cash, facility availability and operating cash flow will cover debt service and final maturities over the next twelve months; it sold USD 3,500 million of unsecured notes in July 2026 and repaid earlier Tower Securities on or within days of their anticipated dates; and ending buybacks alone would free cash flow equal to the dividend.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 54; 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 80; calculation C3; calculation C5

The view would strengthen if the 2021-1C Tower Securities are repaid or refinanced on 2026-11-09 at a coupon near the July 2026 notes, if buybacks are cut so that payouts fall well below free cash flow, and if adjusted EBITDA growth at constant currency turns positive. It would weaken if the date passes unrefinanced, if buybacks are funded by revolver or commercial paper borrowings, if domestic churn runs above the guided range, or if the unseen debt documents show covenant ceilings or restricted-payment capacity tighter than the filings imply.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The strengths, in rank order, are these. The contracted, high-margin site leasing base is first: it produced 98.3% of segment operating profit, with margins of 84.24% in the U.S. and 70.42% abroad, and its recurring rent covers fixed charges and keeps cash interest at 24.60% of adjusted EBITDA. Demonstrated capital-markets access is second: USD 3,500 million of unsecured notes in July 2026, an undrawn USD 2,500 million revolver and a commercial paper program give refinancing a path that does not depend on the securitisation market alone. Third is a fixed-rate, laddered debt stack with a low weighted average coupon of 3.94%, which holds near-term interest cost down until the older tranches reset. Fourth is control of the land: about 71% of towers sit on owned, perpetual or long-dated land interests with a 35-year average remaining life. Fifth is discretion: no sponsor is identified and the board can cut buybacks, which are about the size of the dividend, at its sole discretion.

### Company description

SBA owns and operates 45,252 wireless towers at 2026-09-30, in the United States, Brazil, Central America and Africa, and leases antenna space to carriers under long contracts; it has built towers for carriers since 1989 and owned them since 1997. Site leasing is 98.3% of segment operating profit and site development, a U.S. project business, is 6.90% of nine-month 2026 revenue. Tenant leases run 5 to 10 years in the U.S. and 5 to 15 years abroad with renewal options held by the tenant, so revenue is highly visible, while the business has grown through acquisitions, notably the Millicom purchase in Central America, and has exited five countries since the start of 2025. The model supports debt service because rent is contracted and escalating, but it is concentrated in three U.S. carriers and exposed to non-renewal when carriers consolidate, which is the source of the current churn.

### Ownership and group structure

The registrant is a Florida holding company whose only significant asset is 100% of SBA Telecommunications, LLC; it is a REIT, has one class of common stock outstanding, and no sponsor or controlling holder is identified, with the articles generally capping any holder at 9.8%. Cash to the parent comes only from subsidiary distributions, and the Tower Securities borrowers sit in a separate non-recourse pool that may not borrow elsewhere, so in our reading cash in that pool is structurally ahead of parent-level creditors until released. The July 2026 notes and revolver are obligations of the registrant and are unsecured, but their guarantors and ranking are not stated. The credit implication is that parent notes rely on distributions from subsidiaries and on the release of pool cash, which a missed anticipated date would interrupt.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 46 and 52; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is recurring, escalating and concentrated rather than cyclical. Domestic revenue grows only through escalators, new leases and amendments, and is offset by non-renewals; domestic site leasing revenue fell 3.14% in nine months 2026 while total revenue rose 1.77% because international site leasing grew 24.81% on reported figures and 18.5% at constant currency. Pricing power is limited: the annual report says competition has pushed it into master lease agreements with discounts and term concessions, and the three U.S. carriers hold the buyer power. Management guides 2026 churn at USD 133.0 million to USD 136.0 million in the U.S. and USD 37.0 million to USD 39.0 million abroad, which is 6.61% to 6.81% of FY2025 site leasing revenue; the credit implication is that new leases and escalators must outrun churn of that size for revenue to grow.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 39; calculation C2; calculation C11

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Costs are mostly fixed per tower: cost of revenue is primarily ground lease rent, and management says operating costs for owned towers do not generally rise when tenants are added, so lost tenants remove high-margin revenue while costs stay. The domestic segment margin eased to 84.24% from 85.16% in nine months 2025 and the international margin rose to 70.42% from 69.53%, helped by the ground lease purchase program, and the company-wide adjusted EBITDA margin on revenue fell to 67.78% from 70.69% in FY2024, partly because lower-margin site development grew in FY2025 and domestic rent fell. Inflation is partly passed through, since the South American and African leases are index-linked, but only 12.6% of international leases have fixed escalators, so the real risk is in markets where inflation exceeds fixed rates. Margins can be defended against modest churn but operating leverage works against the issuer if domestic revenue keeps falling.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, pages 24–25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 54; calculation C2; calculation C12

### Capital intensity and free cash flow conversion

Capital intensity is low for the business and largely discretionary. Maintenance and general corporate spending is guided at USD 61.0 million to USD 65.0 million for 2026, against discretionary spending of USD 480.0 million to USD 490.0 million that covers acquisitions, new builds, augmentations and ground lease purchases; capital expenditure was 12.74% of last-twelve-months adjusted EBITDA. Cash interest takes 24.60% and cash taxes USD 99.490 million over the same period, leaving operating cash flow after capital expenditure at 54.40% of adjusted EBITDA. That conversion is durable because the largest uses, buybacks and growth spending, can be cut, but it already funds a dividend of 49.19% of free cash flow, so little is left for debt reduction unless buybacks stop.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sbac.htm.jsonl, page 48; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C3; calculation C4

### Porter's five forces

Competitive rivalry is moderate: the company competes with large independent tower companies, regional owners and carriers' own towers, and says pricing pressure has produced master lease agreements with discounts and term concessions; the credit effect is slower rent growth and some non-renewals rather than a threat to existing contracts. The threat of new entrants is moderate to low because towers need zoning approval, land control and capital, although the annual report warns that competitors building near existing towers can draw tenants away. The threat of substitutes is a longer-dated risk, since small cells, distributed antenna systems and low-earth-orbit satellite could reduce macro tower demand, though the annual report gives no sign that it has yet. Buyer power is high because three national carriers account for 66.5% of revenue and can consolidate or share networks, as the Sprint and EchoStar churn shows, and this is the force that most affects cash flow. Supplier power is moderate: landowners can refuse renewal or sell to aggregators, but about 71% of towers sit on owned or long-dated land with an average remaining life of 35 years.

### PEST analysis

Economic and regulatory factors change the credit conclusion and are covered; social and political factors are secondary. Interest rates matter twice, because higher rates raise the cost of refinancing the low-coupon maturities and reduce the capital carriers can spend on networks, and a 1% rise in variable rates would lift nine-month interest expense by about 1.9%. Currency matters in Brazil, where a 10% fall in the real cuts revenue by 1.2%, and in a USD 863.7 million book of intercompany loans that is remeasured through income. Regulation matters through FCC and FAA tower rules and local zoning, which slow new builds, and through a Brazilian tax dispute with a reasonably possible loss range of up to USD 115.5 million before USD 201.0 million of penalties and interest, which the company has not accrued. The credit consequence is a modest, mostly manageable drag, with refinancing cost the largest macro channel.

### SWOT analysis

Strengths are contracted high-margin rent, demonstrated bond-market access, a fixed-rate laddered debt stack and long-dated land control. Weaknesses are leverage near 6.5x adjusted EBITDA, payouts that absorb nearly all free cash flow, customer concentration and a near-term maturity cluster. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are a decision to redirect buybacks to debt reduction, which would cut payouts to the dividend alone, spectrum deployment that adds tenants to existing towers, and tower-based data centre or edge services the company is exploring. Threats are further carrier consolidation or failure, an unrefinanced anticipated repayment date that traps cash flow, interest cost resets on the older tranches and currency weakness in Brazil.

### Financial profile and credit quality

The main supports are scale and market position in contracted tower rent, high and stable segment margins, long land tenure and open bond-market access. The main constraints are leverage near 6.5x adjusted EBITDA, 98.49% of free cash flow paid out, a maturity cluster at anticipated dates and customer concentration. The trend is flat to slightly weaker: adjusted leverage fell from 7.07x at the end of 2024 mainly because USD 1,165 million of escrow cash was classified as restricted, cover on adjusted EBITDA slipped from 4.79x to 4.07x, and EBITDA growth stalled at constant currency. Key missing datapoints are the outcome of the 2026-11-09 date, covenant ratios, the pool's coverage ratio, ratings and market pricing. The financial profile is therefore adequate with a negative bias on interest cover and a positive offset in market access.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over twelve to twenty-four months the outlook is stable operating results with rising interest cost. Management expects core leasing revenue to grow on a currency-neutral basis despite elevated churn, with discretionary spending of USD 480.0 million to USD 490.0 million in 2026, while USD 3,560 million of principal reaches anticipated dates by April 2027 and the older tranches reset toward the July 2026 coupons, which would take cover on adjusted EBITDA to about 3.22x. Over three to five years the maturities at principal are USD 1,450 million in 2028, USD 2,950 million in 2029 and USD 1,350 million in 2030, with USD 3,045 million after that, so 2027 is the largest date and 2029 the next. Tailwinds are carrier spectrum deployment and growing mobile data traffic, which the annual report cites, and international tenancy growth; headwinds are carrier consolidation, the EchoStar default and spectrum sale, higher rates for carriers and for the issuer, and substitute architectures. The net credit effect is neutral on revenue and negative on interest cost, so deleveraging has to come from payout restraint.

### Downside scenario

The most realistic downside combines a closed secured-debt window with another year of domestic churn. The initiating condition is that the 2021-1C Tower Securities are not refinanced on 2026-11-09 and the market stays shut into the first half of 2027; the first operational break is the passed date, which traps excess cash flow from the pool's towers and adds at least 5 percentage points to the tranche's rate, or at least USD 58.25 million a year. The company's remedy, used in January 2026 for the 2020-1C Tower Securities, is a revolver draw, which at the revolver's rate of 4.913% costs USD 57.24 million a year against USD 19.00 million at the tranche's coupon and uses USD 1,165 million of the USD 2,500 million facility. Then the 2020 Senior Notes fall due on 2027-02-15 and the 2021-2C Tower Securities on 2027-04-09; with buybacks stopped, resources to 2027-04-09 are USD 2,917.532 million plus about USD 279.678 million of free cash flow after dividends, leaving a shortfall of about USD 362.790 million against the USD 3,560 million due. If churn repeats at the top of the guided range, adjusted EBITDA falls by about USD 142.030 million to about USD 1,791.276 million, net leverage on adjusted EBITDA rises to 6.97x (pro forma adjustments for the post-balance-sheet events are not established on that basis), cover on run-rate coupons with the reset falls to 2.99x, and free cash flow after dividends falls to about USD 267.960 million. ### Overall credit view

Overall, Sba Communications Corp presents a strong business profile supported by contracted, high-margin tower rent, long land tenure and demonstrated bond-market access, but credit quality is constrained by leverage near 6.5x adjusted EBITDA, payouts that absorb nearly all free cash flow and a maturity cluster at anticipated dates. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because rent is recurring and buybacks are discretionary, although domestic churn and flat adjusted EBITDA at constant currency limit growth. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the outcome of the 2021-1C date, the willingness of the secured market and the bond market to take the 2027 tranches at coupons well above the legacy ones, and on whether the board redirects buybacks to debt reduction. The primary downside path is a missed anticipated date during a closed market combined with another year of churn, which would compress cover below 3.0x. Further analysis would require the executed debt documents, covenant ratios, the pool's coverage ratio, ratings, market pricing and filings after 2026-11-05. Top risk: refinancing of the 2026 and 2027 maturities at much higher coupons while payouts continue.
