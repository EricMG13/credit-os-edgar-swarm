## Findings

### Bottom line

Legence is a Blackstone-backed building-services group growing quickly through acquisitions and data-centre work, so creditors gain scale and a larger order book but rely on a sponsor-influenced board, unremediated reporting-control weaknesses and the execution of new acquisitions.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 126; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 248; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 79; calculation C13

Blackstone-associated holders owned about 61% of the voting stock in March 2026 and have since sold shares, so their present control is not established, and they have pledged their holdings under margin loans. The IPO repaid USD 780.3 million of term loan after a debt-funded distribution to the sponsor in 2024, but a new USD 150.0 million buyback authorisation and the pace of acquisitions show capital allocation moving from deleveraging to growth and returns.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 248; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 250; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 172; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33

### Key findings

Revenue is concentrating in one end market and in project work: data centers and technology supplied 42.7% of 2025 revenue and 62.6% of the nine months of 2026, and new building work rose to 61.1% of nine-month revenue from about 40% in 2025. The Maintenance & Service line, the closest to recurring revenue, fell from 12.95% to 10.03% of revenue, so earnings now depend more on the timing of large projects in one sector.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47; calculation C1; calculation C2

The order book supports near-term revenue but is partly bought and its momentum is easing: backlog rose 77.24% year on year, the filing attributes the rise in backlog and awarded contracts to the Bowers acquisition as well as new awards, and the quarterly book-to-bill ratio slipped to 1.2x from 1.5x while staying above 1.0x. A pipeline that depends on acquired work and on one end market's capital programmes leaves cash flow less visible than the headline backlog suggests.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 74; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 75; calculation C13

Sponsor control is loosening, but the sponsor's remaining stake is pledged and its present percentage is not stated. The sponsor vehicles held about 61% of voting shares on 2026-03-24, and share counts imply 46.67% on 2026-11-09 after the April 2026 secondary offering, assuming no other transfers, which is below the 50% level at which the charter lets Blackstone nominate a majority of directors. Lower ownership weakens the sponsor's incentive to support the company and raises the chance of further sell-downs, and both vehicles have pledged all their shares and units under margin loans, so a sponsor-level default could force a sale of the controlling block.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 211; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 248; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 250; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; calculation C14; calculation C15; calculation C16; calculation C17; calculation C18; calculation C19

Cash leaves the group toward the sponsor through the tax receivable agreement and through sponsor-linked debt: the liability rose 65.21% since the year end to reflect the April 2026 exchanges, it requires payment of 85% of net cash tax savings, and sponsor-associated lenders hold 8.81% of long-term debt. These flows compete with debt service for cash and mean that amendments and voting under the credit agreement involve parties aligned with the sponsor.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 110; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 39; calculation C23; calculation C24

The sponsor's behaviour is mixed rather than extractive: it took a USD 301.6 million distribution in 2024 funded by a USD 315.0 million incremental term loan, which absorbed 95.75% of that loan, but the 2025 IPO then repaid USD 780.3 million of term loan, and the sponsor's sell-downs are secondary sales that take no cash out of the company. We assign a Medium sponsor and governance risk level, driven by the 2024 distribution and the pledge of shares, with the IPO deleveraging and a board of five independent directors out of six as counterweights.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 107; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 172; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 211; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; 424B4_2024-12-31_lgn-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 158; calculation C27

Reporting controls remain a live governance weakness: management and the auditor identified material weaknesses in information-technology access controls and journal-entry review, still unremediated at 2026-09-30, and an auditor attestation on internal control is first required for the 2026 annual report. Until remediated, covenant certificates and the reported earnings that feed them carry more risk of error than a normally controlled issuer.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 204; 10-Q_2026-03-31_lgn-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 66; 10-Q_2026-06-30_lgn-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 75; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 79; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 80

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against the cautious view is deleveraging and diversification of ownership: the IPO repaid USD 780.3 million of term loan, five of six directors are independent, the sponsor is selling rather than adding to its stake, and backlog and awarded contracts rose 92.49% year on year. The view would improve if the material weaknesses are remediated and attested for 2026, if book-to-bill returns to 1.5x, and if the new buyback is funded from cash flow rather than borrowing. It would worsen if the sponsor sells down further and the credit agreement treats a change of control as a default, or if acquisitions continue to be paid for with the term loan.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 211; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 172; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 74; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 80; calculation C28

### Limitations

- The sponsor's voting power after the April 2026 secondary offering is not stated, so the conclusion that its control is loosening rests on share-count arithmetic and would change if a later filing states a different percentage.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 248; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation C19

- No credit agreement, stockholders agreement or limited liability company agreement was supplied, so the effect of a lower sponsor stake on change-of-control and restricted-payment terms is not established, and the distribution-risk view would change on reading them.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 260; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 261

- Earnings and cash flow of the 2026 acquisitions are not stated apart from consolidated results, so growth cannot be split between organic and acquired, and the conclusion on the quality of backlog growth would change with that split.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The initial public offering completed on 2025-09-15 and converted the sponsor-owned borrower into a listed holding company with an Up-C structure; net proceeds of USD 780.2 million and cash on hand prepaid USD 780.3 million of term loan, and the term loan margin fell by 0.25 percentage points. On 2025-10-30 the credit agreement was amended to extend the term loan maturity to 2031-12-16 and to replace the USD 90.0 million revolver with a USD 200.0 million revolver maturing 2030-09-22.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 109; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 126; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 173; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 174

In 2026 the group used new debt and stock to buy Bowers, Metrix and then CTI: Bowers closed on 2026-01-02 with total consideration of USD 426.68 million and was partly financed by a USD 200.0 million incremental term loan, Metrix closed on 2026-03-01, and CTI closed on 2026-11-01 for estimated consideration of USD 90.0 million cash and 256,411 Class A shares. The term loan was repriced on 2026-05-27 to a margin 0.25 percentage points lower, with a further 0.25 percentage points after ratings upgrades.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26

The sponsor vehicles sold stock in secondary offerings on 2025-12-16 and 2026-04-09 without proceeds to the company, and on 2026-11-10 the board authorised a USD 150.0 million share repurchase programme with no termination date.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33
