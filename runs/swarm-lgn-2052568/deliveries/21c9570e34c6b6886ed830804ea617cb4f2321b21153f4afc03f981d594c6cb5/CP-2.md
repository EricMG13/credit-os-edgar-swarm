## Findings

### Bottom line

Legence's fundamental credit profile is adequate and improving, with moderate leverage and covered near-term cash needs, but it rests on one cyclical end market and on acquired growth, so creditors bear the risk of a data-centre construction slowdown.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C3

Net leverage is 2.27x on reported earnings, or 2.56x pro forma for the acquisition completed on 2026-11-01, and cash plus the undrawn revolver covers identified near-term calls 3.02x. Data centers supplied 86.26% of the nine-month revenue increase, while the new buyback authorisation and the pace of acquisitions show financial policy moving from repayment toward growth and shareholder returns.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47; calculation C3; calculation C30

### Key findings

Revenue durability is the weakest dimension because growth is concentrated in data-centre construction. Data centers and technology supplied 62.6% of nine-month 2026 revenue against 39.2% a year earlier and 86.26% of the revenue increase, while all other end markets together grew 22.39%. New building is now 61.1% of revenue, the vast majority of contracts are fixed-price, and the bulk of performance comes late in a project, so a client deferral cuts revenue and raises working-capital needs at once. Maintenance & Service, the most recurring line, fell to 10.03% of revenue from 13.28% a year earlier, so little of the base repeats under contract.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against the cautious view is that deleveraging and cash generation are reported, not forecast: net leverage fell to 2.27x (2.56x pro forma for the acquisition completed on 2026-11-01), last-twelve-month free cash flow was 98.18% of reported EBITDA, two agencies upgraded the company in June 2026, and the average relationship with the ten largest clients is 23 years with no client above 10% of revenue. Those facts lower single-name risk and make a quick liquidity squeeze unlikely. The view would improve if book-to-bill stays above 1.0x while the data-centre share stops rising, if working-capital float persists without acquired help, if the control weaknesses are remediated, and if the buyback is paid from free cash flow. It would worsen if revolver usage passed USD 70.0 million, if book-to-bill fell below 1.0x, if the sponsor's margin lenders forced a sale of its controlling block, or if reported net leverage rose back toward 3.83x.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The four main supports are liquidity and maturities, cash generation, falling funding cost and contracted visibility; the five main constraints are data-centre concentration, acquisition-led financial policy, the quality of reported earnings, dependence on working-capital float and sponsor-linked claims on cash.

Liquidity and maturities are the main support. The undrawn USD 200.0 million revolver (USD 169.3 million available after letters of credit) and cash cover identified near-term calls 3.02x, no debt falls due before the revolver matures on 2030-09-22 apart from USD 10.0 million of yearly term loan amortisation, small acquisition notes and finance leases, and the only maintenance covenant is untested, so a liquidity-driven default over the next 12 to 24 months is unlikely.

### Company description and credit linkage

Legence designs, installs and maintains heating, cooling, electrical and plumbing systems for mission-critical buildings such as data centers, laboratories, hospitals and universities. It reports two segments, Engineering & Consulting at 15.86% of nine-month 2026 revenue and Installation & Maintenance at 84.14%, and most jobs other than maintenance finish within nine months. Acquisitions have changed the business in 2026: the Bowers acquisition alone explains about one third of the rise in Installation & Maintenance gross profit.

### Ownership and group structure

Legence Corp. is a listed holding company that consolidates Legence Holdings LLC, the borrower under the credit agreement, in an Up-C structure; the term loan and revolver are secured by a lien on substantially all assets of the borrower and its wholly-owned subsidiaries, and guarantors are not named. Blackstone-linked vehicles held about 61% of the votes in March 2026 and an indicative 46.67% in November 2026, which is below the 50% level at which Blackstone may nominate a majority of the board, and they have pledged all their shares and units under margin loans.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27

For creditors this means the sponsor's influence is declining but not gone, a forced sale by its lenders could change control, and the tax receivable agreement and related-party lenders give the sponsor claims on cash beside the credit agreement. The 2024 distribution of USD 301.6 million, funded by a USD 315.0 million term loan, shows the sponsor has extracted value before, while the IPO repayment of USD 780.3 million and sell-downs without company proceeds show support since; Business & governance rates the sponsor and governance risk as Medium.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is transactional, cyclical and concentrated, not recurring. Volume follows client capital programmes, led by data centers and technology (62.6% of nine-month revenue), with new building at 61.1%, and the issuer says downturns bring greater price competition and lower revenue and profit. Pricing power is limited on fixed-price work, where scope changes are billed but base prices are fixed, although tariffs on internationally procured materials are passed to clients and the issuer says it typically takes no exposure to equipment price increases.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins can be defended under moderate volume pressure but not under a sharp project slowdown. Wages are the largest cost and the workforce is partly unionised under 44 collective agreements, so labour is semi-fixed. Subcontractor and equipment spending, 40.32% of FY2025 revenue, may be passed to clients as a line item or priced into the job, and the issuer says it typically takes no exposure to equipment price increases, which protects margin against input inflation. Because contracts are fixed-price, errors in labour estimates fall on the company, and the issuer says engineering, project management and maintenance earn higher margins than installation, the line that grew fastest at 162.76% over nine months.

Operating leverage works both ways: nine-month gross margin of 18.89% against 21.43% a year earlier fell with mix, while the Installation & Maintenance margin rose to 16.58%, so resilience rests on installation project margins holding. The reported EBITDA margin of 6.99% is the conservative base, while the adjusted margin of 12.34% adds back non-cash compensation, impairments and deal costs, so conclusions on resilience should use the reported margin and treat the adjusted one as an upper bound.

Free cash flow of the last twelve months equals 98.18% of reported EBITDA, but FY2025 free cash flow before the working-capital lines was lower than reported, while the nine months of 2026 absorbed a working-capital outflow and cash taxes paid rose 147.62%.

### Qualitative downside scenario

The realistic downside starts with data-centre clients deferring projects, for example because utility services such as water are unavailable at a site or because a client's own capital budget is cut. The first operational break is slower backlog conversion on fixed-price new-building work, where most performance comes late in a project, with the labour force and fabrication capacity already in place; book-to-bill falling below 1.0x would confirm it. Revenue and installation margins fall together, and because wages are the largest cost and many employees are unionised, costs adjust slowly.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Legence presents an adequate business profile supported by backlog, long client relationships and bonding access, but credit quality is constrained by data-centre concentration, acquisition-led growth, thin reported earnings and sponsor-linked claims on cash. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow was 98.18% of reported EBITDA, though it depends on working-capital float. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the data-centre cycle, the ratings that underpin the margin step-down and the covenant staying untested; no large debt maturity falls due before 2030. The primary downside path is a pause in data-centre construction that reverses working-capital float while acquisition and sponsor claims keep cash leaving. Against typical leveraged building-services issuers the profile cannot be ranked because no peer data is supplied. Further analysis would require the executed credit agreement, the rating levels, revenue by client, and the earnings and net debt of acquired businesses. Top risk: concentration in one end market funded in part by acquisitions.
