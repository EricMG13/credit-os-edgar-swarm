## Findings

### Bottom line

MGM is an asset-light casino operator in which the filings identify no controlling sponsor, so creditor risk comes mainly from shareholder returns, leased real estate and joint-venture commitments, and governance risk is Medium.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_mgm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-12-31_mgm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 67; calculation C5

Buybacks took 84.10% of operating cash flow after capital expenditure in FY2025 but 25.21% in the nine months of 2026, and management says the amended voting agreement with IAC Inc. and Barry Diller, whose terms are not supplied, supports share repurchases. Rent of USD 1.8 billion a year, about USD 2.1 billion still to fund for MGM Osaka and landlord guarantees draw on the same cash, while the Macau subsidiary holds 40.89% of principal debt outside the parent's guarantee group.

### Key findings

Cash returned to shareholders is the main policy risk, and it has slowed. Repurchases took 112.05% of operating cash flow after capital expenditure in FY2024 and 84.10% in FY2025, against 25.21% in the nine months of 2026, and USD 1.4 billion of authorisation remains. The common dividend has been suspended since 2023, so buybacks are the discretionary lever that could stop leverage falling.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a lower risk level is conduct. No exchange offer, priming or drop-down is described, the only covenant relief found is a 2023 waiver at MGM China that expired after 2024, maturities were repaid or refinanced on schedule, covenant compliance is stated for all three credit facilities, the parent revolver is undrawn, debt at carrying value fell over the nine months and buybacks slowed to 25.21% of free cash flow. The strongest evidence for a higher level is what cannot be seen: the voting agreement, the credit agreements and a possible second landlord guarantee of USD 3.0 billion.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_mgm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-K_2024-12-31_mgm-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 123; 10-Q_2026-09-30_mgm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; calculation C5; calculation C15

The view would move to High if buybacks returned above free cash flow, if the amended voting agreement raised repurchase capacity or entrenched a holder, if the second guarantee were confirmed or MGM Osaka costs rose, or if the Springfield proceeds were returned to shareholders rather than used to reduce debt. It would move to Low if the agreement capped a holder's stake without enabling larger buybacks, the guarantees were confirmed at USD 3.01 billion, and the buyback pace stayed near the 2026 level.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-28_mgmex991q32026earningrelea.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-K_2025-12-31_mgm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_mgm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_mgm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13

### Limitations

- The voting agreement, its amendment, the holders' percentages and the proxy statement are not supplied, so the Medium level could rise to High if they entrench a holder or raise buyback capacity, or fall to Low if they cap a stake without enabling larger buybacks.
- No credit agreement, indenture or lease agreement is supplied, so restricted-payment capacity, guarantor release and landlord collateral triggers are not established, and the conclusion that buybacks are a policy choice rather than a documented constraint cannot be tested.
- The annual reports describe two landlord shortfall guarantees while the commitments notes describe one, so contingent claims outside debt may be about double what the notes show and the governance level could rise to High.
- Springfield's closing date and proceeds, and the use of the Northfield Park proceeds, are not stated, so the finding that disposals lower rent and earnings together cannot be turned into a net effect on debt.
- MGM Osaka's total cost, the other investors' funding and the guarantee share after the ownership changes are not supplied, so the contingent claim above the 40% interest is not sized.
- The latest document is dated 2026-10-28, so any buyback, financing or disposal after that date could change the level.

### Overall governance view

Overall, MGM presents Medium sponsor and governance risk. The key supported behaviour is a buyback programme that took most of free cash flow in FY2025 and slowed in 2026, which matters because it competes with debt reduction, MGM Osaka funding and rent, and implies that leverage depends on a policy choice rather than a documented limit. Countervailing evidence is the record of on-schedule repayments and refinancings, an undrawn parent revolver, a suspended dividend and clean audit opinions. Further analysis requires the voting agreement, the credit agreements, the proxy statement and a reconciliation of the landlord guarantees.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Two disposals of leased casinos are under way. MGM Northfield Park was sold in April 2026 for USD 546 million in cash, USD 540 million net of adjustments and costs, and the master lease was amended to remove it and cut annual cash rent by USD 53 million; MGM Springfield is agreed for USD 443 million, subject to approvals and termination rights, with no closing date or net proceeds stated. Management links the Northfield Park proceeds to a strong balance sheet and to share repurchases.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_mgm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; EX-99.1_2026-04-29_mgmex991q12026earningrelea.htm.jsonl, page 1

No new parent notes were issued in 2026. The only new bond is MGM China's USD 750 million of 6.25% notes due 2033, issued in May 2026 as USD 750 million of 5.875% notes matured, and the parent repaid its USD 400 million 4.625% notes due 2026 in September from cash. The newest parent borrowing is the senior secured yen facility, entered in October 2025 as a JPY45.2 billion term loan and raised to JPY54.2 billion in November 2025; it is secured by a pledge of equity in certain domestic operating properties and is expected to fund part of the MGM Osaka commitment.
