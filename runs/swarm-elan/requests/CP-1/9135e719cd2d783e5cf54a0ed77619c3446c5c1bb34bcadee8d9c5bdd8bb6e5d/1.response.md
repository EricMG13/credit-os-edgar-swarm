## Findings

### Bottom line

Elanco is deleveraging mainly through earnings growth, not debt repayment, but creditors carry net debt of USD 3,388 million, 4.38x reported and 3.36x adjusted EBITDA, above the 3.1x the issuer reports.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_elan-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 6–7; 10-Q_2026-06-30_elan-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 12; EX-99.1_2026-08-05_elanco-20260630exhibit991.htm.jsonl, page 14; calculation C02; calculation C10; calculation C11; calculation C17; calculation C18

The cushion for creditors is thinner than the headline because the issuer's figure leaves out a USD 255 million finance lease. Liquidity of USD 1,280 million, cash plus the undrawn revolver, only just covers the USD 1,236 million falling due in 2028. Last-twelve-months interest cover cannot be calculated because six-month cash interest is not printed, and covenant headroom is not established because the credit agreement is not supplied.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_elan-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-06-30_elan-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-K_2025-12-31_elan-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 111; 10-K_2025-12-31_elan-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 112; Source readiness (CP-0); calculation C14; calculation C29; calculation C12

### Leverage trajectory and drivers

Net leverage on carrying-value debt and reported EBITDA fell from 5.14x at the 2025 year-end to 4.38x at 2026-06-30, and on issuer-adjusted EBITDA from 3.85x to 3.36x. Pro forma for the AHV acquisition cash of USD 76 million paid on 2026-04-30, year-end net debt would have been USD 3,548 million and reported net leverage 5.25x; the June balance sheet already contains that payment, so the later figures need no adjustment.

### Evidence against the leverage view and what would change it

The strongest evidence against reading Elanco as slow to deleverage is operating momentum: reported revenue growth of 12.53% in the half-year, an issuer adjusted EBITDA margin of 22.9% against 21.2% a year earlier, and free cash flow of USD 206 million against USD 111 million. If that pace holds, EBITDA growth alone moves adjusted net leverage toward the issuer's year-end target of about 3.0x without debt repayment, although part of the revenue growth is AHV and currency rather than organic.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-08-05_elanco-20260630exhibit991.htm.jsonl, pages 1–4; EX-99.1_2026-08-05_elanco-20260630exhibit991.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-06-30_elan-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 6; calculation C13; calculation C27

The view improves if last-twelve-months cash interest shows cover above the FY2025 level, if the 2028 notes are refinanced or liquidity grows well beyond the 2028 maturities, or if the covenant calculation shows wide headroom. It worsens if restructuring cash and the deferred AHV payments pull free cash flow below the 2026 pace, if the scheduled August 2026 swap roll lifts interest cost materially, or if the third-quarter 2026 report shows adjusted EBITDA below the second-quarter run rate.
