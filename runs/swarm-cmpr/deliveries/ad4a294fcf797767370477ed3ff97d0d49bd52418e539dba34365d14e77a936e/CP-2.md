## Findings

### Bottom line

Fundamental credit quality is adequate but weakening: a scaled, cash-generative print group with no large maturity before 2031 is carrying rising leverage, thinner liquidity and falling cash conversion into its seasonal peak, so creditors hold less cushion than a year ago.

Net debt stood at 3.93x trailing reported EBITDA at 30 September 2026, or 3.96x pro forma for a minority put payment due in the December 2026 quarter, against 3.38x at 30 June 2026, which precedes the July 2026 Saxoprint purchase and has no pro forma figure established. Adjusted free cash flow was 26.70% of adjusted EBITDA in FY2026 against 55.70% in FY2024, and cash plus undrawn revolver capacity fell to 0.19x of debt. The main downside path runs through VistaPrint, which earns 61.14% of segment EBITDA.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation D03; calculation D04; calculation D02; calculation D12; calculation D10; calculation D21

### Key findings

- The refinancing runway is the main support, and the credit case rests on refinancing the 2032 notes before mid-2032. The notes and the term loan are 97.11% of principal; the term loan is due in June 2033 but moves to 16 June 2032 if the notes are not repaid, refinanced or extended, and the revolver matures in 2031 with 14.0% drawn. Nothing material falls due before 2031 apart from scheduled amortisation and small other debt, so near-term repayment is not the pressure point.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cmpr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21–22; calculation D19; calculation D11
- Leverage has stepped up and the cushion is thinner. Net debt rose 11.80% in the September 2026 quarter because Saxoprint was paid for from cash and the revolver while trailing EBITDA slipped, taking net leverage on reported EBITDA from 3.38x at June to 3.93x, or 3.96x pro forma for the minority put payment due in the December 2026 quarter. On the issuer's adjusted EBITDA the ratio is 3.39x, and 3.50x without the tariff refunds that sit in the adjusted figure. No pro forma for the July 2026 purchase is established against the June figure.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D01; calculation D02; calculation D03; calculation D04; calculation D05; calculation D06; calculation D07
- Liquidity is at its seasonal low and has thinned sharply. Cash fell 51.73% in the quarter and undrawn revolver capacity fell from USD 232.7 million to USD 197.7 million, so cash plus undrawn capacity is 0.19x of total debt against 0.30x at June. The leverage covenant applies only when drawings exceed 20% of commitments, which is USD 50.0 million against USD 35.0 million drawn, so USD 15.0 million more would put the test into play; the lender-defined ratio is not printed.

Less internally generated cash is left for debt reduction at a time when buybacks, acquisitions and minority puts compete for it.

Start-up costs for the North American network, tariffs, duties and energy explain most of the latest quarter's decline, so margin recovery depends on that investment paying back.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a weakening view is the structure of the debt and the seasonal rebuild. No large maturity falls due before 2031, interest cover has held between 3.34x and 3.67x, and cash rose 28.69% between September and December 2025 as the December quarter delivered adjusted EBITDA of 1.41x the September quarter, so the September 2026 trough may reverse. Management also has levers: repurchases have been paused since February 2026, capital spending is 4.48% of revenue, and a completed sale-leaseback of the Saxoprint facility, valued at 32.92% of the purchase consideration, would cut net debt. The view would improve if net leverage returned towards 3.38x after the December 2026 quarter and cash conversion stopped falling. It would worsen if drawings passed USD 50.0 million, if trailing EBITDA kept falling, or if repurchases resumed while the revolver stayed drawn.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation D02; calculation D11; calculation D13; calculation D17; calculation D25

### Limitations

- No ratings, market prices, peer figures or company guidance were supplied, so refinancing capacity rests on maturity timing and reported results alone; if market access proved impaired, the finding that the 2032 maturity is manageable would weaken.
- The December cash rebuild is observed once, in the 2025 quarter, because earlier balance sheets are absent; if the December 2026 quarter is weaker, the contrary case for liquidity would fail.
- The springing leverage test cannot be measured against any covenant ratio, so the downside scenario treats drawings above USD 50.0 million as a warning signal rather than a breach point.
- No sector or peer data were supplied, so the judgement that the business profile is adequate rests on the issuer's own record and not on a comparison with other leveraged issuers.

### Company description

Cimpress is a web-to-print group that sells customised marketing print, signage, packaging, promotional products, apparel and design services, mainly to small businesses and graphic professionals, through five autonomous segments. Revenue was USD 3.7 billion in FY2026, split 51.55% VistaPrint, 21.65% PrintBrothers, 10.48% The Print Group, 10.74% National Pen and 5.58% All Other Businesses, with 50.11% earned in Europe and 45.91% in North America. The model gives revenue visibility only at the order level: small orders, paid up front at VistaPrint, repeat purchase and a December peak, so cash generation is supported by prepayment and low inventory but not by contracts.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-06-30_cmpr-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15; 10-K_2026-06-30_cmpr-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 135

### Ownership and group structure

No sponsor or controlling holder is disclosed; about 70% of the shares sit with the top ten holders and the board and nominee detail is not supplied. The borrowers are Cimpress plc and five named subsidiaries, certain subsidiaries guarantee both the secured facility and the notes, and the guarantor names are not supplied. Financial policy is therefore set by a public board that defines value per share as its objective, and creditor protection rests on documents that were not provided.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is discretionary and small-business driven, with limited pricing power: the annual report says customers are price-sensitive and past pricing changes moved order volumes, and free social media channels are a substitute when small firms cut spending. Organic constant-currency growth was 4% in FY2026 and 2% in the September 2026 quarter, with currency and acquisitions supplying the rest of the reported 9.80%, and growth is led by elevated products while certain legacy products fell 2%. Revenue is therefore moderately durable but cyclical, and the credit consequence is that earnings can fall quickly in a small-business downturn while debt service is fixed.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_cmpr-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-K_2026-06-30_cmpr-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 43; 10-K_2026-06-30_cmpr-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 58–59; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cmpr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cmpr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40; calculation D22

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins can be defended only partly. Cost of revenue rose from 51.5% to 53.7% of revenue over FY2024 to FY2026 on higher fulfilment, manufacturing and shipping costs, while marketing and selling spend fell from 24.0% to 23.3% of revenue, so gross profit pressure outweighed operating leverage. The cost base is largely variable through third-party fulfilment and advertising, but production assets, platform costs and cloud commitments are fixed; the credit consequence is that volume shortfalls hit margins before costs adjust.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_cmpr-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 60; 10-K_2026-06-30_cmpr-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 141; calculation D16

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital intensity is moderate but rising and is the main drag on conversion. Capital spending including capitalised software was 4.48% of revenue in FY2026, mostly manufacturing and automation equipment and the North American network, and adjusted free cash flow was only 26.70% of adjusted EBITDA. Working capital is seasonal, with inventory built in the September quarter and released into the December peak, and a supplier finance programme sits inside payables. Cash conversion is therefore adequate in a good year and weak in a year of heavy investment, and the credit consequence is slower deleveraging.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_cmpr-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 69; 10-K_2026-06-30_cmpr-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 140; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D12; calculation D13

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense in a fragmented market, which limits pricing, while scale and central purchasing give Cimpress a cost edge over smaller printers. The threat of substitutes is rising through free social media channels and artificial intelligence products that compete in design services and content creation. Buyer power is high because small customers are price-sensitive and switch easily, and supplier power is moderate because paper, ink and equipment are bought centrally and hundreds of external fulfillers supply capacity. The credit consequence is that margins have little pricing buffer, so cost shocks pass into earnings.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_cmpr-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-K_2026-06-30_cmpr-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19; 10-K_2026-06-30_cmpr-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 35–36; 10-K_2026-06-30_cmpr-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 43

### Macro, policy and technology factors

Trade policy is the policy factor that matters to credit. The annual report describes volatile United States tariffs, with broad tariffs of 10% to 12.5% from 24 July 2026 and a 50% tariff on certain Canadian goods from August 2026, mainly affecting promotional products and goods fulfilled from Canada for United States customers, and the refund of invalidated duties has so far added USD 14.4 million to adjusted EBITDA. Currency lifted FY2026 revenue growth by 4%, so a weaker euro or sterling would reduce reported earnings, and new European forestry and sustainability rules add compliance cost from December 2026. Credit consequence: tariff and currency swings move EBITDA by tens of millions of dollars, against interest cover of 3.54x.

### Credit-translated SWOT

Strengths are the VistaPrint franchise, the 2031 to 2033 maturity profile and prepayment-funded cash generation; weaknesses are falling cash conversion, margin compression and high leverage. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are elevated products, the Canva partnership and a Saxoprint sale-leaseback; threats are tariffs and duties, artificial intelligence substitution, a small-business downturn and a financial policy that favours distributions or deals over debt reduction.

### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale and market position is strong on evidence: the group says it is the largest in its space, with USD 3.7 billion of revenue and VistaPrint at a 20.29% segment margin. Competitive advantage is average, resting on scale purchasing and the platform but with price-sensitive buyers. Business diversification is average, because VistaPrint produces 61.14% of segment EBITDA even though five segments and two continents are present. Cost and capex flexibility is average, margin stability is weak, and free cash flow stability is weak, given conversion of 26.70% and a margin down 2.43 percentage points since FY2024. The ability to refinance is average, on a long runway but no market evidence. Liquidity is weak at the seasonal low, at 0.19x of debt, and financial policy and governance is average, with transparent reporting but a value-per-share objective that competes with debt reduction. The trend is weakening.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 months to 24 months the December 2026 quarter decides whether cash and leverage recover: it carries the peak earnings, and the Saxoprint integration, the planned sale-leaseback and a further claim of about USD 10 million for tariff refunds are tailwinds, against headwinds from start-up costs for the North American network, tariffs, energy and a minority put payment. Over three years to five years the question is whether elevated products and the platform lift margins before the 2032 refinancing, and whether financial policy favours debt reduction when the notes approach their window. There is no company guidance in the sources, so the outlook is an analytical reading.

### Downside scenario

The most credible downside begins with weaker small-business demand or higher cross-border tariffs while North American start-up costs persist. VistaPrint, which earns 61.14% of segment EBITDA, loses volume and margin first; group EBITDA falls, and adjusted free cash flow, already 26.70% of adjusted EBITDA, turns negative after capital spending. A 10% fall in trailing reported EBITDA, with net debt unchanged, would lift net leverage to 4.37x, or 4.40x pro forma for the minority put payment due in the December 2026 quarter. Cash and USD 197.7 million of undrawn capacity cover the seasonal trough, but drawings above USD 50.0 million bring the leverage test into play, and the next levers are to stop repurchases, slow capital spending and complete the sale-leaseback. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress through the December quarter, but the margin for a second weak year is thin.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Cimpress presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, a prepaid VistaPrint franchise and a runway to 2032, but credit quality is constrained by rising leverage, falling cash conversion and margin compression. Cash-flow durability appears adequate but weakening because capital spending and cost of revenue are rising faster than earnings. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on EBITDA recovering before the notes approach their June 2032 window, and on the financial policy staying restrained. The primary downside path is a VistaPrint earnings shortfall that turns free cash flow negative while leverage is already high. Further analysis would require the executed credit agreement, ratings and market evidence, and management guidance. Top risk: VistaPrint earnings concentration combined with thin liquidity at the seasonal low.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D03; calculation D12; calculation D21
