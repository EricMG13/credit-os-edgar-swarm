## Findings

### Bottom line

Belden's credit has weakened materially because the debt-funded Ruckus acquisition lifted net debt to USD 2,669.70 million, or 5.83x trailing reported EBITDA at 2026-09-27, so creditors now depend on earnings growth and free cash flow to deleverage.
The business underneath is steady: FY2025 revenue grew 10.33% to USD 2,715.19 million, the Adjusted EBITDA margin was 16.90% and free cash flow was USD 218.88 million. The 5.83x overstates run-rate leverage because only one quarter of Ruckus earnings is in the trailing figures and the company states no pro forma figure, so the true level is not established; on the issuer's Adjusted EBITDA the trailing ratio is 4.79x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-27_bdc-20260927.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C01

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a negative reading is demand and earnings momentum: orders were a record USD 1,017 million, up 16% organically, the Adjusted EBITDA margin was 17.7%, up 70 bp, and fourth-quarter revenue guidance is USD 960 million to USD 980 million. Because the trailing ratios carry one quarter of Ruckus against its full term loan, true leverage is lower than 4.79x if Ruckus earns at its first-quarter rate before the non-cash charges, and the euro notes are fixed-rate with the nearest maturity in 2028. A company-stated pro forma leverage clearly below the 4.79x trailing figure, voluntary Term Loan B prepayments from free cash flow, or an explicit leverage target would move the view toward improvement; a book-to-bill below 1.00x, an Adjusted EBITDA margin back below 17.0%, or a resumed buyback before leverage falls would deepen it.

### Limitations

- Pro forma earnings for a full year of Ruckus are not stated, so the leverage conclusion is a range from 4.79x to 5.83x that this analysis cannot narrow, and a lower run-rate level is possible.
- The Term Loan B and Revolver covenant packages are not supplied, so the liquidity and refinancing conclusions assume no restriction beyond the Revolver's fixed charge test and carry no covenant headroom estimate.
- No rating agency view, market pricing or interim cash interest is supplied, so refinancing capacity is not tested against market access and interest cover is measured on coupons and accruals, not cash paid.
- The sources give no end-market or customer revenue split and no Ruckus customer data, so the diversification judgement rests on channel and geography alone.

### Credit view

Overall, Belden presents an adequate business profile supported by 11% organic growth, record orders and a 16.90% Adjusted EBITDA margin, but credit quality is constrained by net leverage of 5.83x trailing reported EBITDA after the debt-funded Ruckus acquisition. Cash-flow durability appears adequate before the deal and less certain after it, because free cash flow conversion fell to 24.69% of Adjusted EBITDA in the nine months of 2026 and coupon interest is about USD 158.69 million a year. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on leverage falling before the 2028 notes and the 2033 maturity wall. The primary downside path is distributor destocking or a reversal of tariff relief that cuts earnings while floating-rate interest stays high. Further analysis would require company pro forma EBITDA, interim cash interest and the credit agreements. Top risk: leverage that does not fall because Ruckus earns too little.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-27_bdc-20260927.htm.jsonl, page 35; calculation C01; calculation C03; calculation C04

### Company description and credit linkage

Belden supplies network infrastructure (copper and fiber cable, connectivity, racks and enclosures) and industrial networking and automation products, and since 2026-07-01 wireless and cloud-managed networking through Ruckus. Revenue is mostly short-cycle product sales through distributors and OEMs, 42% of 2025 sales were outside the United States, and the 2025 backlog of USD 567.7 million equals 20.91% of revenue, so cash flow is visible for months rather than years. That supports debt service in a normal year but gives little contractual cover in a downturn, which is why leverage matters more than for a contracted-revenue issuer.

### Ownership and group structure

Belden is a listed company with no sponsor or controlling holder named in the supplied filings, so no outside owner is documented to inject equity or extract cash, and its creditors rely on management's own policy. The euro notes, USD 1,253.78 million in total, are issued by Belden Inc., guaranteed on a senior subordinated basis by domestic subsidiaries and subordinated to the Revolver and all other senior debt. The rank and security of the USD 1,850.00 million Term Loan B are not stated, so how much debt ranks ahead of the notes is not established and priority of claim is left for the legal analysis.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is driven by volume and by copper pass-through pricing, and it is concentrated in a distributor channel with no contractual minimums: the ten largest customers were 47% of 2025 revenue, eight of them distributors, and the largest was 14%. Pricing power is limited because markets are described as highly competitive with a 5% to 15% market share, so margin is defended by mix and product breadth rather than price. The implication is that a destocking quarter at a few distributors would cut revenue before the cost base can adjust, which is the first break in the downside path.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bdc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-12-31_bdc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-K_2025-12-31_bdc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-K_2025-12-31_bdc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 106

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Gross margin was 37.98% in FY2025 and, because copper costs reach cost of sales about two months after price moves and OEM contracts pass cost through with a lag of up to three months, margin moves with copper in short windows. The 2026 margin figures are distorted by Ruckus: the Q3 gross margin of 35.07% includes USD 56.3 million of inventory step-up, and the Adjusted EBITDA margin of 17.7% includes tariff refunds the quarterly report names as a driver of margin expansion. Pass-through defends margin against input-cost moves but not against lost volume, and the 2026 improvement is partly one-off, so the run-rate level is lower than the reported rise.

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital expenditure was USD 136.17 million in FY2025, 5.02% of revenue, and rose to 5.51% of revenue in the nine months of 2026; the filings do not split maintenance from growth spending, so flexibility to cut it in a downturn is not established. ### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense and fragmented, which caps pricing and means share gains come from product breadth and acquisitions rather than price. Buyer power is high because the ten largest customers are 47% of revenue and customers are not obliged to buy minimum amounts, so a few distributors can reshape volumes. Supplier power is moderate: copper is the principal input, its 2025 spot price ranged from USD 3.99 to USD 5.80 a pound, and the company does not generally hedge it with derivatives. Entry barriers and substitution are not quantified in the filings, so they carry no weight in the conclusion.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months demand supports the credit: orders are at record levels entering the fourth quarter, guidance is USD 960 million to USD 980 million of revenue, and the data center agreement adds USD 100 million in 2027. Headwinds are the integration of Ruckus, the unwinding of tariff refunds, floating-rate interest and the sharp rise in non-cash charges that depress reported earnings. Over three to five years the 2028, 2031 and 2033 maturities and the Term Loan B balance of USD 1,734.38 million due in 2033 require leverage to fall, which management's goal of mid-single-digit growth and 25% to 30% incremental margins would support if met.

### Downside scenario

The scenario starts with distributors cutting inventory as demand slows while tariff refunds stop. Revenue falls first because the backlog of USD 567.7 million is short-cycle; gross margin follows because copper costs lag price by about two months; Adjusted EBITDA falls faster than revenue because operating costs and Ruckus integration spending do not adjust quickly. On the trailing base of USD 557.17 million, a 10% fall in Adjusted EBITDA lifts leverage to 5.32x and a 20% fall to 5.99x with no change in net debt, while coupon cover drops to 2.81x. Free cash flow shrinks toward the interest bill, so the Revolver is likely to be drawn first, but USD 754.21 million of liquidity and a nearest maturity in 2028 mean liquidity distress is not the likely outcome inside 24 months; management levers are capex cuts, a continued buyback pause and working capital release. The observable triggers are book-to-bill below 1.00x, margin below 17.0% and Revolver availability moving toward USD 27.0 million.
