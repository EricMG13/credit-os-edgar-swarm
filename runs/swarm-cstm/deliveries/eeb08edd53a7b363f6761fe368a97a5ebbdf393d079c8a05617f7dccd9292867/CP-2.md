### Limitations

- The size of the temporary part of the 2026 earnings lift is not stated by the company, so the downside sensitivity assumes the packaging and automotive sheet margin returns to its FY2025 level and is an illustration, not a forecast; a smaller or larger reversal would move the stress leverage of 1.89x accordingly.
- The legal terms of the notes and the asset-based facility are not supplied, so the financial-policy view cannot say how much of the USD 1,003.5 million of leverage headroom the documents let the board use.
- Availability and borrowing-base terms of the asset-based facility after the October 22, 2026 amendment are not established, so the liquidity of USD 992 million and its 93.06% coverage of the 2028 and 2029 notes use pre-amendment availability and may be understated.
- No peer set, rating or traded yield is supplied, so the business profile cannot be compared with typical leveraged-credit issuers in the sector and the refinancing view rests on quoted prices at a single date.

### Company description

Constellium converts aluminium into specialty rolled and extruded products for aerospace, packaging, automotive and industrial customers, so its credit is that of a conversion business whose margin, not the metal price, drives cash flow. FY2025 revenue was USD 8,449 million across three segments, the group operated 23 manufacturing facilities at September 30, 2026, and the only portfolio changes in the period are a USD 3 million purchase of a controlling interest in 2024 and the 2026 disposal of a 54% interest for USD 2 million, so cash flow comes from operations, not from deals. Cash-flow visibility is strongest in aerospace, where the largest contracts are multi-year and the segment earned 17.23% in FY2025, and weakest in general industrial work, where contracts run a year or less.

### Ownership and group structure

No sponsor or controlling shareholder appears in the supplied filings, so the board, not an owner, sets financial policy, and the parent is a French company listed on the NYSE whose US assets sit in a wholly owned US holding company. The senior notes are issued by the parent and guaranteed by unnamed subsidiaries, the asset-based facility is borrowed by three US operating companies and the inventory facility by two French ones, which layers the debt structure, and no dividend is paid while buybacks of USD 78 million were made in 9M 2026. A chief executive officer from within the company took over on January 1, 2026, and the board extended the buyback authority in March 2026, so the policy continued through the change.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue growth in 2026 came from price, not volume, so revenue durability depends on conversion margins rather than on the metal price. Revenue rose 25.61% in 9M 2026 and revenue per tonne 26.73% while shipments fell 0.88%; aerospace and transportation shipments rose 19.48%, packaging and automotive sheet shipments fell 3.29% and automotive structures fell 2.58%. Prices are set at metal cost plus a conversion margin and customers face high switching costs from long qualification, which supports margins. Demand follows aircraft build rates and light vehicle production, which are cyclical, and beverage can demand, which the annual report calls relatively resilient in downturns and which is 44.63% of revenue through packaging rolled products; the United States takes 39.15% of revenue by destination and trade policy is a stated risk.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins are defended against metal prices by pass-through but remain exposed to volume, because the company says its capital-intensive operations generally have high fixed costs that it may not be able to cut quickly, and conversion costs are large against conversion revenue, although the filings give no fixed and variable split. Cost of metal is 63.11% of FY2025 revenue and passes through, production costs are 24.12% and other segment expenses 4.25%, so conversion costs take 76.90% of conversion revenue and, if every one were fixed, a 1% fall in conversion revenue would cut Segment Adjusted EBITDA by up to 4.33%. The same leverage worked upward in 9M 2026: conversion revenue rose 20.75% against production costs of 6.38%, lifting Segment Adjusted EBITDA 68.05%. A large part of energy needs is fixed-price, a majority of slabs are cast in-house, the ten largest metal suppliers supply about 49% of volumes, and the largest US labour agreements run to 2029 and 2030, which holds the cost base steady but limits flexibility if volumes fall.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry and buyer power are the likely reasons margins are thinner in packaging and automotive sheet than in aerospace, where qualification barriers protect them. The industry is competitive on quality, price, delivery and innovation, the packaging and automotive sheet segment earned 6.95% on revenue in FY2025 against 17.23% in aerospace-led products, and the two largest customers are 21.25% of revenue, which gives them negotiating weight, although the filings do not say which segment they buy from. New entrants face heavy capital needs and long customer qualification, substitutes such as steel, glass, plastics and composites compete in some uses, and supplier power is limited because most slabs are cast in-house and the ten largest metal suppliers supply about 49% of volumes.

### Downside scenario

The primary downside is a normalisation of the packaging and automotive sheet margin that reaches creditors through leverage and refinancing, not through liquidity. The initiating condition is the end of the North American automotive sheet shortage and favourable scrap, which management says its 2028 targets exclude; the first operating break is a return of that segment's margin from 10.01% over the last twelve months to its FY2025 level of 6.95%, which removes about USD 188 million of Segment Adjusted EBITDA and takes it to USD 877 million. ### Overall credit view

Overall, Constellium presents an adequate business profile supported by qualified aerospace and automotive franchises, metal pass-through and no sponsor, but credit quality is constrained by thin margins in packaging and automotive sheet, customer and segment concentration, and a policy that permits leverage up to 2.5x.
