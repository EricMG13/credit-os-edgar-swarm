## Findings

### Bottom line
ACCO is an adequate but constrained credit: a mature brand business with falling volume has the liquidity and cost savings to reach its 2029 maturities, but its earnings absorb volume losses badly and its leverage leaves little room for a costly refinancing. Derived net debt is USD 703.4 million, or 4.49x last-twelve-months EBITDA, and USD 765.4 million, or 4.88x, pro forma for the Trust acquisition of 2026-10-01, while the October 2026 tariff refunds alone would lower it to USD 698.4 million, or 4.46x; total liquidity is USD 428.4 million.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_acco_acco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34; calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C03

The main downside path is volume loss on a fixed cost base, which cut derived EBITDA by 17.98% on a net sales fall of 8.49% in FY2025, and it leaves the 2028 refinancing of the USD 575.0 million 4.25% notes as the first hard test of that weaker earnings base.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest evidence for a more favourable reading is the issuer's own covenant ratio of 3.60x against a 4.25x maximum, liquidity of USD 428.4 million, free cash flow of USD 114.0 million over the last twelve months and USD 90.0 million of realized savings; on that evidence the credit is a stable, slowly deleveraging company. That evidence is weaker than it looks, because the free cash flow owes much to lower cash tax and other payments and the covenant ratio rests on a definition the filings do not give. The view would improve if the covenant reconciliation showed add-backs that bridge the 2.00x gap to the derived ratio, if fourth-quarter cash flow cut revolver borrowings from USD 206.3 million and the Trust purchase was funded from cash on hand, or if comparable sales stabilised. It would weaken if comparable sales fell by more than the 9.3% of FY2025, if the net tariff refunds proved to be the only reason operating income rose, or if the notes could not be refinanced near the 5.49% paid on the secured facilities.

### Company description and credit linkage
ACCO sells branded consumer, technology and business products to schools, homes and workplaces through retailers, e-commerce and dealers, and about 75% of FY2025 net sales came from brands the issuer ranks first or second in their categories; that supports shelf space, but it is the issuer's own ranking and demand follows school, office and consumer spending, which has fallen in each period since the start of 2024. The Americas supplied 58.7% and International 41.3% of FY2025 net sales, and 57.5% of sales were outside the United States, so the business carries euro, Australian dollar and tariff exposure that a domestic peer would not.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_acco_acco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-12-31_acco_acco-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 103–104; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C05

Cash generation is seasonal, which matters for debt service and liquidity preservation: operating cash flow arrives in the second half, so revolver availability fell to USD 204.8 million at 2026-06-30 before recovering to USD 253.7 million at 2026-09-30, and net debt moved from USD 824.0 million to USD 703.4 million in the same quarter. Cash-flow visibility is therefore good within a year and weak across years, because there are no long-term contracts that fix volume.

### Ownership and group structure
The issuer is a publicly listed parent with no sponsor, parent or controlling holder identified in the supplied filings, so there is neither sponsor extraction risk nor sponsor support, and the equity cushion is thin: shares held by non-affiliates were worth about USD 316.1 million at 2025-06-30 against debt of USD 878.1 million at 2026-09-30. Financial policy is set by a board that kept the quarterly dividend unchanged through a covenant amendment year and paid out 82.9% of free cash flow in FY2025, which is moderate but not conservative; the 2026 payout cap is the binding constraint for creditors, and what applies after 2026 is not stated.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power
Revenue is discretionary, concentrated and partly in structural decline. Comparable net sales fell 8.0% in FY2024, 9.3% in FY2025 and 3.7% in the nine months to 2026-09-30, and the 3.9% rise in reported nine-month sales came only from USD 56.4 million of EPOS sales and USD 27.6 million of currency. The issuer states that demand for paper-based products has declined as hybrid work and education spread and as the German government replaces paper-based processes, and that it expects the decline to continue; part of the volume loss is therefore not cyclical and will not return in an upturn. The stated growth strategy rests on technology peripherals, yet the issuer cites global declines in that category in 2026, so the offset to the paper-based decline is not yet visible in sales.

Pricing power is limited. Price increases net of programs were USD 9.5 million in the nine months to 2026-09-30 against volume losses of USD 50.3 million, an offset of 18.89%, and the issuer says it has historically been unable to raise prices fast enough to cover cost increases and that customers trade down to lower priced products. The five largest customers are 31.79% of net sales and some import their own private-label goods, so a pricing dispute or a lost listing would reach volume and receivables together; revenue visibility is low.

### Cost structure and margin resilience
Margins have been defended by cost actions but not held by them. Gross margin was 33.3% in FY2024, 32.8% in FY2025 and 33.1% in the nine months to 2026-09-30, but the last figure includes USD 20.1 million of tariff refunds; without them the nine-month margin is 31.31% against 32.48% a year earlier and the third-quarter margin is 30.42% against 32.99%, even though the temporary tariffs ended in February 2026. The derived EBITDA margin has moved from 12.08% in FY2024 to 10.83% in FY2025 and 9.99% over the last twelve months.

The cost base is largely fixed: selling, general and administrative expenses of USD 346.7 million are 22.74% of net sales, and with depreciation, amortization and rent they turn lower volume into larger profit declines, which is why derived EBITDA fell 17.98% on a net sales fall of 8.49% in FY2025. Operating leverage cuts both ways but, with comparable sales still falling, it is the main margin risk. The cost programme has delivered about USD 90.0 million of its USD 100.0 million target, so only about USD 10.0 million of savings remains to offset further declines.

### Capital intensity and free cash flow conversion
Capital intensity is low and free cash flow is positive in every year supplied but unstable. Capital expenditure was USD 17.9 million, 1.17% of FY2025 net sales, so there is little spending to cut in a downturn, and free cash flow has been 54.64% of derived EBITDA in FY2023, 65.72% in FY2024, 30.77% in FY2025 and 72.75% over the last twelve months. The FY2025 low reflects a USD 65.2 million net outflow through other assets and liabilities, including restructuring, taxes, interest, pensions and incentives, and the recovery to USD 114.0 million over the last twelve months comes from lower payments of that kind rather than higher earnings, so free cash flow is not a steady source for debt reduction.

### Competitive forces
Rivalry is intense and pricing is exposed: the issuer describes a highly competitive market with low barriers to entry for certain products, private-label suppliers and customers importing their own goods, so price competition rises in weak periods, and with price offsetting only 18.89% of volume loss the issuer has not been able to hold margin by pricing. Substitutes are the structural threat: digital tools replace paper-based products and demand for them is expected to keep declining, which caps the earnings that mature categories can contribute to debt service.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_acco_acco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-K_2025-12-31_acco_acco-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_acco_acco-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 33–34; calculation C09

Buyer power is high: the five largest customers are 31.79% of net sales, large customers demand lower prices and shorter lead times, and management says concentration increases customer credit risk. Supplier power is moderate but tariff-linked: a majority of products are bought from lower-cost countries, mainly in Asia, in U.S. dollars, and gaming accessories depend on licences from three major console makers, so a tariff change or a licence loss reaches gross margin directly and cannot be passed on quickly.

### Macro, currency and policy factors
Tariffs and currency are material to credit quality. The temporary tariffs were overturned in February 2026, USD 21.3 million of refunds was received in the third quarter and about USD 5.0 million more in October 2026, but new tariffs took effect on 2026-07-24, so the benefit is a one-off recovery on top of a renewed cost. Currency helped nine-month sales by USD 27.6 million in 2026, yet a stronger U.S. dollar raises the cost of dollar-sourced goods sold in local currency and the issuer says it cannot always raise prices fast enough; floating-rate debt is also repricing, with the secured facilities at a weighted 5.49% against 4.66% at 2025-12-31.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds
Over the next year or two the supports are the remaining USD 10.0 million of cost savings, USD 5.0 million of further tariff refunds received in October 2026 and the seasonal second-half cash inflow, which together should keep liquidity near the current level. The pressures are comparable sales still falling, new tariffs from 2026-07-24, restructuring cash of USD 14.8 million due within twelve months, and a covenant maximum that steps down to 4.00x in the second half of 2027; the reported ratio of 3.60x leaves a 0.40x gap to that level if nothing changes. ### Downside scenario
The realistic downside starts with another year of broad volume decline of the FY2025 kind, with tariff refunds ending and the new tariffs applying to a base in which a majority of products are sourced from Asia. The first operational break is gross margin: without refunds the nine-month 2026 margin was already 31.31% against 32.48%, and fixed costs of selling, general and administrative expenses and amortization do not fall with volume. Financial transmission follows the FY2025 pattern, where EBITDA fell 17.98% on a sales fall of 8.49%; if EBITDA fell by the same proportion from USD 156.7 million to USD 128.52 million, derived net debt on the unchanged USD 703.4 million would be 5.47x EBITDA (the pro forma Trust position would be higher). This is an illustration of scale and not a forecast.

### Overall credit view
Overall, ACCO presents an adequate business profile supported by established brand positions, low capital needs and a largely realized cost programme, but credit quality is constrained by volume loss on a fixed cost base, customer concentration and derived net leverage of 4.49x, or 4.88x pro forma for the Trust acquisition of 2026-10-01. Cash-flow durability appears adequate rather than steady: free cash flow has been positive every year but ranged between 30.77% and 72.75% of EBITDA. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on stabilised comparable sales, the covenant ratio and market pricing for a USD 575.0 million refinancing due by September 2028. The primary downside path is volume loss on a fixed cost base ahead of that refinancing. Further analysis would require covenant EBITDA and definitions, the funding and earnings of Trust, and ratings and market pricing. Fundamental credit review completed. Top risk: earnings sensitivity to volume ahead of the 2028 refinancing.
