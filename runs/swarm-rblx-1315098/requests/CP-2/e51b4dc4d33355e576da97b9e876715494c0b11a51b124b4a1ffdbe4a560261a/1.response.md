## Findings

### Bottom line

Roblox's credit profile rests on liquidity, not earnings: USD 6.2 billion of cash and investments covers total debt of USD 1,009 million 6.18 times, but reported EBITDA is negative and cash flow depends on advance payments that slowed sharply in the third quarter.

Repayment risk before the 2030 maturity is therefore low. The primary downside is a slow erosion: average daily paying users fell 21.74% year on year in the third quarter, bookings fell 11.71%, and costs and legal settlements keep rising while revenue still benefits from earlier advance sales.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is that distress is implausible for years on the numbers reported: revenue rose 28.97% in nine months 2026, the operating loss narrowed to 17.98% of revenue from 25.12%, adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 8.68% from 3.51%, free cash flow of USD 1,254 million exceeded repurchases and settlements together, capital expenditure was only 1.85% of revenue, and nine-month bookings were still 9.15% higher. The company also describes the age-check effect as unexpected and tied to a platform change, which could prove a one-off step rather than a trend. The view would improve if bookings returned to growth and paying users recovered, and it would worsen if bookings fell again in the fourth quarter, the strongest seasonal quarter, with deferred revenue growth below USD 136 million, if repurchases exceeded free cash flow, if settlements rose well above USD 140 million, or if any distribution were debt-funded.

### Company description

Roblox runs a single-segment immersive gaming and creation platform made of a client application, a free creation toolset and cloud infrastructure. Creators build experiences and users buy the virtual currency Robux to enhance them, either one-time or through the Premium subscription. Users on the platform averaged 138 million a day in the third quarter of 2026, of whom about 1.8 million paid, and 54% of nine-month revenue came from the United States, with 57% from the United States and Canada, 21% from Europe, 12% from Asia-Pacific and 10% from the rest of the world. Creators earned USD 1,503.1 million of Developer Exchange payouts in FY2025 against USD 922.8 million in FY2024, and more than 45,000 creators were registered in the program at 2026-09-30 against more than 35,500 at 2025-12-31.

The credit linkage runs through cash timing. Payment is taken upfront for substantially all bookings, recorded in deferred revenue and recognized over the estimated lifetime of a paying user, which gives visibility of revenue and a cash float of USD 7,040 million, and it also means the business can show growing revenue after demand has slowed. The user base includes many children, which the company names as a reputational risk and which exposes it to online-safety law, and 44% of FY2025 revenue passed through two app stores. Operating history is one of losses: net losses attributable to common stockholders were USD 1,065.1 million in FY2025, USD 935.4 million in FY2024 and USD 1,151.9 million in FY2023, with an accumulated deficit of USD 5,060.7 million at 2025-12-31. The only structural changes in 2026 are small: one acquisition of a business in the second quarter, two more headquarters buildings leased, and a USD 3.0 billion repurchase authorization.

### Ownership and group structure

Roblox is a listed, founder-led and founder-controlled company with no sponsor named: the founder and his affiliates own all Class B stock, which carries 20 votes a share and was 56.94% of all votes at 2026-09-30 on share counts, down from 58.74% at 2025-12-31. The group has one consolidated subsidiary with a minority, Roblox China Holding Corp., in which Roblox holds 51% of the voting shares and an affiliate of Tencent Holdings holds 49%; it has issued USD 30 million of notes, half held by that affiliate, due 2028-05-10, which are the only debt that sits below the parent. The filings define no restricted or unrestricted group and name no guarantor of the 2030 notes, so structural seniority of subsidiary debt is limited to USD 15 million.

For financial policy, Business & governance finds no dividend ever paid, USD 580 million of repurchases in nine months 2026 funded from cash, a USD 3.0 billion authorization, founder control that leaves distribution policy to one holder, and an indenture whose distribution limits cannot be read. The credit implication is policy risk, not structural subordination risk: creditors depend on the founder's choices, and the one documented distribution, buybacks, has been covered by operating cash flow.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is driven by volume of users, the number who pay and spend per payer. Average daily users were 127 million in FY2025 against 82.9 million in FY2024, 132 million, 123 million and 138 million in the three 2026 quarters, and about 1.8 million paid in the third quarter. Bookings rose 55.37% in FY2025 and 9.15% in nine months 2026, but quarterly growth slowed from 43.41% in the first quarter to 8.28% in the second and a fall of 11.71% in the third, and the third-quarter fall was driven by average daily paying users dropping to about 1.8 million from about 2.3 million. Average daily bookings per paying user were USD 9.98 in the third quarter against USD 10.42 in the second, and bookings per daily user USD 0.13.

Revenue is transactional and discretionary, with a subscription element, concentrated in channel and title, and sensitive to regulation. The company sets the creator payout rate in its sole discretion and has used that power to give value back: it raised the payout per earned Robux by 8.5% in September 2025 and the Developer Exchange rate by 42% for eligible adult US spend in June 2026, and it offers more Robux through lower-fee payment channels, so pricing power over creators and payers is limited and used defensively. The credit mechanism is that bookings, not revenue, drive cash; revenue is smoothed by deferral, durable items were 90% of virtual item revenue in nine months 2026, and the deferred balance gives revenue visibility of about twelve months, with USD 4,781 million of it classified as current and a tail to roughly 27 months.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Cost of revenue was 20% of nine-month revenue, developer exchange fees 27%, infrastructure and trust & safety 24%, research and development 28%, general and administrative 14% and sales and marketing 5%, so total costs were 118% of revenue against 125% a year earlier. Developer exchange fees follow bookings and fell in the third quarter; cost of revenue follows revenue because payment processing fees are expensed over the same period as the related revenue; infrastructure, safety, research and general costs are driven by headcount, hosting and legal activity, and the company says it expects to raise research, general and marketing spending and to increase infrastructure investment over the long term. Total costs rose 21.61% in nine months 2026 against revenue growth of 28.97%, and 12.02% in the third quarter against revenue growth of 15.59% and a fall in bookings.

Margins can be defended against a modest fall in volume because developer payouts flex, but not against a sustained one, because the headcount and hosting base, including USD 845 million of hosting commitments over three annual periods and about USD 403 million of new headquarters rent, does not flex. The reported operating loss was 17.98% of revenue in nine months 2026 against 25.12%, an improvement driven by revenue recognized from earlier bookings rather than by lower costs, and adjusted EBITDA margin was 8.68% against 3.51%. The credit implication is that margin gains are tied to deferred revenue release, and a bookings decline would first show in cash flow and then in revenue and margin over the following twelve to 27 months of the deferral period.

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital intensity is low: capital expenditure was USD 83 million in nine months 2026 against USD 143 million, 1.85% of revenue, although USD 125 million of property additions sat in payables and accruals against USD 48 million a year earlier, so cash capital spending is lagging accruals. Leases are operating leases with USD 901 million of liabilities on the balance sheet at 2026-09-30, and the two new headquarters buildings add about USD 403 million of rent over about 13 years from a start that had not yet occurred. Interest paid is small at USD 38.75 million a year, income taxes are immaterial at USD 4 million of provision in nine months 2026 with a full valuation allowance on deferred tax assets, and no restructuring or integration cash cost is described in the filings read.

Free cash flow was 3.22 times adjusted EBITDA in nine months 2026, which is a sign that cash conversion is flattered by working capital rather than a measure of operating strength: without the USD 903 million inflow from operating assets and liabilities, free cash flow would have been USD 351 million. ### Porter's five forces

Competitive rivalry is high. The company names global technology, entertainment, gaming and online content companies, including Apple, Google, Microsoft, Tencent, Netflix, YouTube and Epic Games, as competitors for user attention and creators, and the evidence of effect is the fall in paying users in the third quarter and the dip in average daily users in the second. The mechanism is that engagement and payer retention, not price, set bookings, and the implication is that bookings can move faster than costs.

Buyer power is high at the user and creator level and low at the payer level. Users play for free, only about 1.30% of daily users pay, and the company has raised creator payouts twice, in September 2025 and June 2026, which indicates that creator retention has a cost; the mechanism is cost creep in developer exchange fees and a bookings base that depends on a small payer group. ### Downside scenario

The most credible issuer-specific downside begins with a regulatory or enforcement event that removes more users or payers than the age-check change did, such as a finding under the Digital Services Act, an action by the Federal Trade Commission, or a further wave of state measures. The first operational break is a second consecutive fall in bookings: third-quarter bookings of USD 1,697 million were USD 136 million above the USD 1,561 million at which deferred revenue would stop growing at the third-quarter revenue level, so a further fall of 8.01% would end the float. Financial transmission follows in order: operating cash flow loses the deferred revenue inflow, which was USD 533 million in nine months 2026; revenue keeps rising for several quarters from the USD 7,040 million deferred balance, so reported margins stay flattered; costs of USD 1,855 million in the third quarter, hosting commitments and legal settlements do not fall; and free cash flow shrinks from USD 364 million in the quarter toward zero.

The liquidity and refinancing consequence is limited in the near term: on an indicative basis bookings of USD 1,441 million, 15.09% below the third quarter, would leave cash operating costs just covered before working capital, and even at zero operating cash flow cash and investments of USD 6,238 million would cover the identified fixed calls of USD 1,981 million more than three times. Management levers are suspending the repurchase program, which may be suspended at any time and has USD 2.4 billion of authority left, slowing hiring and infrastructure, and stopping further buybacks before any borrowing. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress over twelve to twenty-four months; the observable triggers are a second quarter of falling bookings, a quarterly deferred revenue change below USD 136 million, free cash flow below USD 364 million, settlement expense above USD 140 million, and repurchases above free cash flow. No default is assumed.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Roblox presents a business profile that is large and broad by geography but concentrated by channel, title and paying users, supported by liquidity of USD 6,238 million and advance payments, but credit quality is constrained by negative reported EBITDA, a falling bookings and payer base, open-ended legal and regulatory cost, and policy set by one holder. Cash-flow durability appears adequate while bookings hold and weak if they keep falling, because free cash flow leans on working capital. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on liquidity, which is several times the debt, not on market access, which the filings do not evidence. The primary downside path is regulation-driven loss of paying users that cuts bookings, ends the deferred revenue float and leaves a rising cost base and legal cost to be met from the cash cushion over several years.
