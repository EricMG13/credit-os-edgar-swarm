## Findings

### Bottom line

Crocs has a strong fundamental credit profile because leverage is low and cash generation is high, but dependence on one brand, concentrated sourcing and a repurchase policy that absorbs nearly all free cash flow mean creditors rely on management not levering up.

Net debt was 1.12x trailing twelve-month reported EBITDA at 2026-09-30 and EBITDA covered cash interest 14.28x, with USD 886.4 million of undrawn revolver and no maturity before November 2027. The primary downside path is weaker demand at HEYDUDE or in the Crocs Brand together with tariff costs while repurchases continue.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C1; calculation C2

### Key findings

Leverage and cover are the main support. Net debt was 1.12x and EBITDA covered cash interest 14.28x on a trailing basis; the FY2025 reading of 4.81x is an artefact of non-cash impairments, since before impairments it was 1.14x, and without the tariff refund the trailing figure is 1.19x, so the low leverage does not depend on one-off items.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C4

Cash generation is high but fully distributed. Repurchases took 99.7% of nine-month free cash flow, and the revolver rose while net debt did not fall, so debt service is secure today but deleveraging is a policy choice that management is not making.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 15

Business risk is concentrated. The Crocs Brand supplies 84.0% of revenue and 92.5% of segment operating profit, Vietnam makes 45% of its output, and tariffs of 10% to 12.5% apply to the main sourcing countries, so one brand, one sourcing region and one trade policy drive cash flow.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C6; 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, page 22

HEYDUDE is the weakest asset. Its trademark has a fair-value cushion of under 10% after USD 737.0 million of impairments in 2025, so a further charge is plausible if its recovery stalls; the charge would be non-cash but would cut equity and reported earnings.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, page 94; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, page 10

Liquidity is adequate and refinancing hinges on the revolver. Undrawn revolver of USD 886.4 million and cash of USD 163.5 million cover all needs until the revolver matures in November 2027, after which 64.7% of face value falls due in 2029, so the first test of market access is the revolver renewal.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16, 31

The margin outlook is flattered. The third-quarter gross margin rose 770 bp, of which 630 bp came from tariff refunds the issuer calls non-recurring, so the run rate is below the headline.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 24, 26

Governance and control are not established. The proxy statement was not supplied, so ownership, board independence and related-party items remain open, and the financial-policy reading rests on what management has done.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 106–107

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a strong reading is that policy, not capacity, limits leverage. About USD 1.7 billion of repurchase authorization remains, 2.25x trailing free cash flow and 1.92x the undrawn revolver, and long-term income taxes payable equal 57.0% of net debt with no payment schedule. A buyback programme that outruns free cash flow, or a tax cash call, could take leverage well above the current 1.12x without any weakness in operations.
The view would weaken if repurchases were funded from the revolver beyond free cash flow, if HEYDUDE revenue turned down again or its trademark were impaired further, or if tariffs rose on Vietnamese output. It would strengthen if management stated a leverage ceiling, if the revolver were extended well before November 2027, and if the credit agreements showed repurchase limits that bind at moderate leverage.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 17; calculation C7; calculation C8

### Strengths and weaknesses

A strength is low leverage and wide cover. Net debt of USD 1,125.587 million against trailing reported EBITDA of USD 1,006.666 million gives 1.12x, and cash interest of USD 70.509 million is covered 14.28x, so earnings can fall substantially before debt service is strained. The credit implication is high resilience to an operating shock.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C2

A strength is cash generation with light capital needs. Trailing free cash flow of USD 753.993 million is 74.9% of trailing EBITDA, capital expenditure is 1.46% of revenue and purchase commitments are largely cancellable, so cash preservation levers exist in a downturn.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C9; 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 90–91

A strength is liquidity and a clear maturity path. Cash of USD 163.457 million and undrawn revolver of USD 886.4 million face no maturity until November 2027, and both note issues are callable at declining premiums, so refinancing is optional until the revolver renewal.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16; 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, page 88

A strength is a diversified route to market. Direct-to-consumer is above half of revenue, no customer reaches 10% of revenue and sales reach more than 85 countries, so wholesale partner loss is less damaging than for a wholesale-only footwear company.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12, 155

A strength is reduced debt since 2023. Face value of borrowings fell from USD 1,710.0 million at year-end 2023 to USD 1,313.0 million, and the term loan was refinanced to 2029 at lower margins, so refinancing risk was pushed out.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, page 86; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, page 15

A weakness is that distributions absorb free cash flow. Repurchases of USD 506.194 million equal 99.7% of nine-month free cash flow of USD 507.574 million, so net debt rose to USD 1,125.587 million from USD 1,100.531 million at year-end 2025, and the revolver rose by USD 51.0 million. The credit implication is that deleveraging depends on a policy change.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C5

A weakness is that one brand carries the credit. The Crocs Brand supplies 84.0% of revenue and 92.5% of segment operating profit, and its North America revenue fell 2.4% in nine months while international grew 8.8%, so regional momentum in one brand sets cash flow.

A weakness is HEYDUDE valuation risk. The trademark of USD 1.14 billion has a cushion under 10% after USD 737.0 million of 2025 impairments; HEYDUDE grew 6.1% in the third quarter but is below its FY2023 revenue, so an impairment would cut equity of USD 1,345.007 million further.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, page 94; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 24

A weakness is concentrated sourcing and tariffs. Vietnam makes 45% of Crocs Brand output and one manufacturer 45%, tariffs of 10% to 12.5% remain, and USD 64.071 million of refunds reduced third-quarter cost of sales, so margins carry both a cost and a one-off flattering effect.

### Company description and operating model

Crocs designs and sells casual footwear and accessories through two brands, the Crocs Brand and HEYDUDE, in more than 85 countries. Direct-to-consumer was 52.4% of nine-month 2026 revenue and wholesale 47.6%, production is outsourced to third-party manufacturers, and capital expenditure is light. The business model supports cash-flow visibility through brand recognition, a proprietary material and a growing direct channel, but it is discretionary and fashion-led, so revenue visibility is limited to the season and cash flow depends on consumer demand and wholesale partners' orders. Debt service capacity is high because earnings are many times interest; refinancing confidence depends on brand durability.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 6–14

### Ownership and financial policy

Crocs is a listed company with one class of common stock, no disclosed sponsor and a small record holder count. Financial policy is to buy back shares, not to pay dividends: it has never paid a cash dividend and repurchased shares in each of the last three years and in 2026. The mechanism is that repurchases compete with debt repayment for free cash flow, so the implication is that leverage stays near current levels instead of falling. The legal capacity to distribute is set by credit agreements that were not supplied, so willingness is evidenced but capacity is not tested.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 73, 141; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17

### Revenue durability

Revenue growth was 2.0% in the nine months to 2026-09-30 on a reported basis, led by international Crocs Brand sales and direct-to-consumer, while North America wholesale for the Crocs Brand fell 11.1%. Volume and price both moved: unit volume fell in the nine months while average selling price rose. The mechanism is that price and mix support revenue while volume is soft, which makes revenue less durable than the headline; the implication is moderate revenue visibility with no contract or recurring element, and a fashion-cycle exposure at HEYDUDE, whose revenue fell from USD 949.393 million in FY2023 to USD 714.840 million in FY2025.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 25; 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, page 154; EX99.1_2026-09-30_crox_croxq32026-pressrelease-htm.htm.jsonl, page 12

### Margin resilience

Gross margin was 60.9% in the nine months against 59.5%, helped by tariff refunds, channel mix and lower product costs and offset by product mix and incremental duties. Selling, general and administrative expense rose 3.6%, driven by direct-to-consumer cost, so operating leverage is positive only while revenue grows. The mechanism is that rising rent and marketplace costs raise the fixed-cost share, and the implication is that margins can be defended against modest volume pressure but a demand shock would squeeze them; adjusted operating margin guidance of about 20.5% for the fourth quarter already points below the nine-month level.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 25–26; EX99.1_2026-09-30_crox_croxq32026-pressrelease-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 10

### Cash conversion and capital intensity

Trailing free cash flow of USD 753.993 million is 74.9% of trailing reported EBITDA, with capital expenditure at 1.46% of revenue and cash interest of USD 70.509 million. Working capital absorbed cash in the nine months, as inventories rose to USD 413.030 million and receivables to USD 355.022 million, and the tax cash timing improved operating cash flow. The mechanism is that low capital intensity leaves most earnings as cash; the implication is that capital expenditure can be cut in a downturn but the cash is currently directed to repurchases, not to deleveraging.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 32

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense: competitors include larger footwear groups with greater resources and some wholesale partners compete with Crocs' own stores, so price pressure and wholesale terms could weaken margins. Buyer power sits with wholesale customers, but none reaches 10% of revenue and direct-to-consumer exceeds half of revenue, which limits it. Supplier power is moderate because manufacturing capability is broadly available, though Vietnam and one manufacturer are concentrated. The threat of substitutes and new entrants is relevant to a brand-driven category with low barriers in casual footwear. The credit implication is that competition shows up in margins and wholesale volume, not in sudden loss of access to supply.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 17, 155

### Macro and policy factors

Political and regulatory factors are material: tariffs of 10% to 12.5% apply to imports from countries that supply Crocs, the Supreme Court ruling on emergency tariffs led to refunds, and further trade actions remain uncertain. Economic factors are material because the issuer says consumers face elevated rates and inflation and wholesale partners act cautiously, and the Middle East conflict has cut revenue in some distributor markets. Social and technological categories do not change the credit view and are skipped.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 22

### Credit-translated SWOT

Strengths are low leverage, wide interest cover, light capital needs, a direct channel above half of revenue and no maturity before November 2027. Weaknesses are one-brand dependence, HEYDUDE's valuation cushion, repurchases that absorb free cash flow and unscheduled tax and litigation claims. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are a stated leverage ceiling, a refinanced revolver and continued HEYDUDE growth. Threats that could weaken it are tariffs on Vietnamese output, a consumer slowdown and debt-funded buybacks.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Financial profile

- Scale and market position: Strong. Revenue of USD 4,101.148 million on a trailing basis, 500 Crocs Brand and 78 HEYDUDE stores and sales in more than 85 countries absorb shocks, although market share is not stated.
- Competitive advantage: Average. The clog and a proprietary material are distinctive, but fashion cycles and HEYDUDE's decline limit durability.
- Business diversification: Average. Two brands and two channels, but one brand carries most profit and sourcing is concentrated.
- Cost and capex flexibility: Strong. Capital expenditure is a small share of revenue and purchase commitments are largely cancellable.
- Margin stability: Average. Gross margin has ranged from 55.8% in FY2023 to 60.9% in the nine months to 2026-09-30 and now includes refunds and tariffs, while direct-to-consumer costs rise.
- Free cash flow stability: Strong. Free cash flow was positive and large in every period shown, with working capital swings.
- Ability to refinance and access capital markets: Average. The term loan was refinanced in 2024 and both notes are callable, but there is no rating and the revolver renews in November 2027.
- Liquidity position: Strong. Cash of USD 163.457 million and undrawn revolver of USD 886.4 million cover all near-term needs.
- Financial policy and governance: Average. No dividend and debt reduced since 2023, but repurchases absorb free cash flow and ownership and board evidence is incomplete.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 15–16, 30

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over twelve to twenty-four months, management guides 2026 revenue growth of about 3%, with the Crocs Brand up about 4% and HEYDUDE about flat, and fourth-quarter revenue up 7% to 9%; these support margin and cash flow if delivered, but they are management's expectations. Tailwinds are international Crocs Brand growth, HEYDUDE's return to growth and tariff refunds, of which about USD 67 million was received against an estimated USD 75 million to USD 80 million in total. Headwinds are tariffs, cautious wholesale partners, direct-to-consumer costs and the end of one-off refunds. Over three to five years the key factor is whether HEYDUDE stabilises and whether policy keeps leverage low; the filings give no medium-term targets.
Basis: EX99.1_2026-09-30_crox_croxq32026-pressrelease-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, page 22

### Downside scenario

The downside begins with a consumer slowdown or a tariff increase on Vietnamese output that cuts Crocs Brand demand and wholesale orders while direct-to-consumer costs stay fixed. Revenue falls, gross margin compresses as tariffs and discounting rise, and operating deleverage cuts EBITDA further. Free cash flow falls but stays positive because capital expenditure is small and purchase commitments are largely cancellable. If repurchases continue, the revolver is drawn and net debt rises from 1.12x EBITDA; a HEYDUDE impairment would cut equity without cash. Management levers are to suspend repurchases and to cut capital expenditure, either of which restores liquidity; resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress, because undrawn revolver of USD 886.4 million exceeds a year of cash interest by a wide margin. A first observable break would be negative Crocs Brand growth in two consecutive quarters or a rising revolver balance beside falling free cash flow.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16, 22

### Overall credit view

Overall, Crocs presents an adequate business profile supported by global brand scale, a growing direct channel and low capital needs, but credit quality is constrained by dependence on one brand, concentrated sourcing and a repurchase policy that absorbs nearly all free cash flow. Cash-flow durability appears strong because trailing free cash flow of USD 753.993 million is 74.9% of EBITDA. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on renewal of the revolver due November 2027 and on market access for the 64.7% of face value due in 2029. The primary downside path is a demand or tariff shock at the Crocs Brand while buybacks continue. Further analysis would require the credit agreements, the proxy statement, a covenant EBITDA definition and a rating or market quotes. Top Risk: a debt-funded repurchase programme meeting a tariff or HEYDUDE setback.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C5; calculation C7
