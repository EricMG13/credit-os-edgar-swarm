## Findings

### Bottom line

Crocs is a two-brand footwear company with no sponsor disclosed, so creditors depend on brand durability and on a financial policy that currently directs nearly all free cash flow to share repurchases while debt stays low.

The Crocs Brand supplies 84.0% of nine-month 2026 revenue and 92.5% of segment operating profit. HEYDUDE, 16.0% of revenue, is recovering but carries a USD 1.14 billion trademark whose fair value exceeded its carrying value by less than 10% at the last annual test. Governance evidence is incomplete because the proxy statement was not supplied.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 20; 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 94, 106–107; calculation C1; calculation C2

### Key findings

The business rests on one brand. The Crocs Brand earned 92.5% of segment operating profit and 84.0% of revenue in the nine months to 2026-09-30, and its international revenue grew 8.8% while North America fell 2.4%, so creditor cash flow depends on one brand's demand across regions.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 20; EX99.1_2026-09-30_crox_croxq32026-pressrelease-htm.htm.jsonl, page 12; calculation C1; calculation C2

HEYDUDE is a recovering asset with impairment risk. Its revenue fell 24.7% from FY2023 to FY2025, it returned to growth of 6.1% in the third quarter of 2026, and the trademark carried at USD 1.14 billion has a fair-value cushion of under 10%, after USD 737.0 million of impairments in 2025. A further charge would be non-cash but would cut earnings and equity.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 11, 24; 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 94, 154; calculation C3; calculation C4

Sourcing and tariff exposure is concentrated and flatters recent margins. Vietnam produced 45% of Crocs Brand output in 2025, and the largest manufacturer alone produced 45%. Third-quarter gross margin rose 770 bp to 66.2%, of which 630 bp came from tariff refunds that the issuer calls non-recurring, and tariffs of 10% to 12.5% still apply to the main sourcing countries.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 22, 26

Channel mix is moving to direct-to-consumer, which lifts the margin mix but adds fixed cost. Direct-to-consumer was 52.4% of nine-month 2026 revenue against 52.1% in FY2025 and 48.0% in FY2023, and higher direct-to-consumer costs of USD 29.4 million drove most of the 3.6% rise in selling, general and administrative expense.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 26; calculation C5

Ownership and control cannot be assessed from the supplied documents. The company has never paid a cash dividend, had 36 holders of record, and has spent USD 250.0 million on repurchases in the latest quarter with about USD 1.7 billion of authorization left; who holds the shares and who sits on the board are in a proxy statement that was not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 73, 106–107; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17

Litigation is real but unquantified. A securities class action and four derivative actions allege that statements about wholesaler inventory misled investors; the company has accrued USD 0.6 million and states no estimate of the possible loss.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21

No transaction or new debt issue is stated for 2026. The last financing event was the February 2024 refinancing of the term loan, and the last acquisition was HEYDUDE in 2022.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 86–87; 10-K_2024-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, page 128

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against caution is the balance sheet and the cash flow: the company holds USD 163.5 million of cash and USD 901.4 million of available borrowings, has paid no dividend and funds repurchases mainly from operating cash flow, and HEYDUDE has just returned to growth. If HEYDUDE keeps growing and the Crocs Brand holds its international momentum, the brand and impairment concerns would fade.
The view would turn more adverse if repurchases were funded from the revolving facility beyond free cash flow, if a further impairment of the HEYDUDE trademark were recorded, if the tariff refunds proved a one-off boost that margins cannot repeat, or if the proxy statement showed a controlling holder with different interests from creditors. It would turn more favourable if the issuer disclosed a leverage target and the credit agreements showed repurchase limits that bind well below current leverage.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 16–17, 22, 31; EX99.1_2026-09-30_crox_croxq32026-pressrelease-htm.htm.jsonl, page 3

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

No transaction or new debt issue is stated in the supplied documents for 2026. The most recent financing event was the February 2024 refinancing of the term loan: a new USD 820.0 million tranche refinanced the then-outstanding balance, repriced the margin and set the maturity at 2029-02-17, and USD 500.0 million remained outstanding at 2026-09-30. The senior notes date from 2021, when USD 350.0 million of 4.250% notes due 2029 and USD 350.0 million of 4.125% notes due 2031 were sold. The acquisition of HEYDUDE closed on 2022-02-17 for an aggregate preliminary purchase price of USD 2.3 billion, paid as USD 2.05 billion in cash and shares of Crocs common stock, financed by a USD 2.0 billion term loan and USD 50.0 million of revolver borrowings.
Since then the company has repaid debt: face value of borrowings fell from USD 1,710.0 million at year-end 2023 to USD 1,313.0 million at 2026-09-30, while the revolving facility rose from USD 62.0 million at year-end 2025 to USD 113.0 million. The creditor reading is that leverage has been reduced while shareholders were paid through repurchases, not through new debt-funded transactions.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 86–87; 10-K_2024-12-31_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 128; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crox.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16
