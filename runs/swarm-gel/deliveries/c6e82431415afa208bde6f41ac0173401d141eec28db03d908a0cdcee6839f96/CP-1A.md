## Findings

### Bottom line

Genesis Energy is a Gulf of America pipeline franchise with contracted but producer-dependent volumes, and in 2026 the partnership is directing improved cash flow to retiring preferred units and raising distributions while gross debt rises, which keeps noteholders reliant on continued offshore throughput.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-K_2025-12-31_gel-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 166; calculation B5; calculation B7; calculation B8

Offshore pipelines produced 67.74% of nine-month 2026 Segment Margin, and in the third quarter Shenandoah volumes fell below the minimum commitment, so only the minimum was recognised. Voting control rests with Class B units held mainly by one family, and the preferred investors keep a board observer right while they hold enough of the class. The executed debt documents that would bind these choices were not supplied, so the limits on distributions and further preferred purchases are not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_a90ex9918kearnings_q32026.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-12-31_gel-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 128–129; calculation B1; calculation B8; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

- The offshore pipeline segment is the credit, and it is concentrated in a few producer-operated fields. It supplied 66.75% of FY2025 Segment Margin and 67.74% of the nine months of 2026, on contracts with minimum volume commitments and tiered fees, but its Segment Margin fell 3.50% in the third quarter because Shenandoah and legacy fields underperformed. The segment's results therefore track producer drilling, not the partnership's own spending, which is now complete.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gel-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 90; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_a90ex9918kearnings_q32026.htm.jsonl, page 6; calculation B1; calculation B4; calculation B10
- Marine and onshore services diversify earnings but are small and cyclical. They supplied 17.38% and 14.87% of nine-month Segment Margin; marine utilization was high and onshore volumes rose on Gulf-produced crude and market dislocations that management itself calls possibly non-recurring.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_a90ex9918kearnings_q32026.htm.jsonl, page 8; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_a90ex9918kearnings_q22026.htm.jsonl, page 6; calculation B2; calculation B3
- Control is concentrated and creditors have no governance voice. Class B units elect the whole board, one family holds 76.9% of them and can appoint up to three directors, and the partnership has chosen not to follow listing rules on independent nominating and compensation committees. Creditors rely on contract terms, which are not supplied, rather than on governance.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gel-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 128–130; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19
- Preferred equity is shrinking quickly and its remaining holders keep influence. The partnership bought back 41.15% of the preferred units outstanding at the end of 2025 for USD 221,594 thousand in three purchases, at prices from the USD 33.71 issue price to USD 34.38. Two funds held 91.2% of the class at the end of 2025, and the filings do not say who sold. The remaining units carry an 11.24% coupon, and the initial purchasers keep a board observer right while they hold USD 200 million of the class.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gel-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 129; 10-K_2025-12-31_gel-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 152; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21–22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 48; calculation B5; calculation B6; calculation B14
- Capital allocation has turned from growth to returns. Growth capital fell 80.93% in the nine months, the common distribution is 21.21% above the level of a year ago, and a small common unit repurchase was made, while gross debt rose by USD 159.34 million. The pattern favours equity holders over debt reduction in 2026 and depends on the offshore volumes holding.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 51–52; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2025-12-31_gel-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 166; calculation B7; calculation B9; calculation B11
- The 2026 financing was creditor-neutral to positive on tenor, with the nearest note maturity pushed to January 2029 and a larger revolver, but it also widened flexibility for investments and added a secured receivables facility whose receivables are ring-fenced from other creditors.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 47–49

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is contractual and liquidity support: management says nine of sixteen active deepwater rigs work leases dedicated to flow through its facilities, four new high-impact wells are due to restore volumes, the revolver has USD 894.4 million available and no debt matures before 2029. The view would soften if offshore volumes returned above the minimum commitments for several quarters and the executed debt documents showed tight limits on distributions and preferred purchases. It would harden if volumes stayed weak into 2027, guidance for the year were missed, or distributions and preferred purchases continued to be funded with new debt.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-11-05_a90ex9918kearnings_q32026.htm.jsonl, page 3; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_a90ex9918kearnings_q32026.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; calculation B12; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

On 2026-03-04 the partnership issued USD 750.0 million of senior unsecured notes due 2034 and signed an Eighth Amended and Restated Credit Agreement that lifted revolver capacity from USD 800 million to USD 900 million and moved its maturity to 2031. Net proceeds of about USD 735.9 million bought USD 416.1 million of the 2028 notes in a tender that ended on 2026-03-20 and redeemed the remaining USD 263.3 million on 2026-03-22, with the rest helping to fund a preferred unit purchase on 2026-03-06. The partnership recorded a loss of USD 3.4 million on the tender premium, fees and written-off costs.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 47–48

On 2026-06-26 it signed a three-year USD 99.5 million receivables securitization facility priced at Term SOFR plus 1.375%, borrowed through a bankruptcy-remote subsidiary whose receivables are not available to other creditors, and the facility is fully drawn. On 2026-06-03 it sold non-core natural gas pipeline and platform assets in the offshore segment for USD 95.0 million, recognising a gain of USD 17,436 thousand, and used the proceeds to buy preferred units on 2026-06-08.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 48; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_a90ex9918kearnings_q22026.htm.jsonl, page 2

Across 2026 the partnership bought preferred units on three dates for USD 221,594 thousand, which cut the class by 41.15%, and paid common distributions of USD 0.20 per unit for the second and third quarters. Its earlier 2025 transactions were the sale of the Alkali business for USD 1,425 million gross, the redemption of the 2027 notes, and a purchase of preferred units for USD 262.5 million. No new debt issue after 2026-09-30 is stated up to the cutoff, although whether a later event exists is not established because the quarterly report has no subsequent-events note.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21–22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-K_2025-12-31_gel-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; calculation B5; calculation B6; calculation B7
