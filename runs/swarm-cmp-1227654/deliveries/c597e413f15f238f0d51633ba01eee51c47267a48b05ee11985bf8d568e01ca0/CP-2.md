## Findings

### Bottom line

Compass Minerals' fundamental credit profile is adequate but fragile: adjusted leverage of 3.07x and no maturity before 2028 support creditors, but weather-driven earnings, one dominant mine and a new management team mean one weak winter could reverse the gains.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F1; calculation F2

Cash interest cover of 5.21x overstates strength, because the last twelve months hold one coupon on the 2030 notes; cover on interest expense is 3.19x, and USD 650.0 million of USD 728.0 million of principal falls due in 2030. Liquidity of USD 328.1 million is struck at the seasonal low for working-capital needs. The primary downside is a mild winter with unit-cost inflation, which would be absorbed by liquidity before it threatens a covenant or a maturity.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-06-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 45; 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 176; calculation F1; calculation F3; calculation F9

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against calling the profile fragile is that adjusted EBITDA rose to USD 233.2 million over the last twelve months, that the company reports first lien net leverage of about 0.01x at September 30, 2025 against a covenant maximum that was 2.75x then and is 2.50x now, that S&P is reported to have raised the rating to B+ with adjusted leverage of 3.1x, that management describes the coming highway bid season as constructive, and that guidance of USD 218 million to USD 242 million implies earnings hold near the current level. These points make a stable view plausible. The view would improve if net leverage on reported EBITDA stays at or below 3.05x at the December 31, 2026 balance sheet, when working capital peaks, if remediation of the control weaknesses is reported complete, and if the revolver is extended before May 2027. It would worsen if the fiscal 2026 fourth quarter falls below the guidance midpoint implied for it, if revolver borrowings rise above USD 35.0 million at a seasonal low, or if Goderich output is interrupted.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. The first strength is a low-cost, high-scale salt franchise. The Goderich mine is described as the largest underground rock salt mine in the world, with a deep-water port, and the company has logistics depots close to its customers, so delivered cost is competitive in a product where freight is a large part of price; this lowers the risk of losing tenders and supports volume through weak winters. The second strength is near-term refinancing safety: no principal falls due before May 2028 and liquidity is USD 328.1 million, so a poor quarter can be absorbed without a financing event. The third is financial policy: dividends are suspended and disposal proceeds and cash retired the 2027 notes, so cash accrues to debt reduction. The fourth is a Plant Nutrition segment that has turned from a USD 86.4 million operating loss in fiscal 2024 to USD 20.8 million of operating income in the nine months to June 2026, which diversifies earnings away from weather. The fifth is an improving earnings base: adjusted EBITDA rose to USD 233.2 million over the last twelve months.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, pages 9–13; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F10

Weaknesses. The first weakness is weather dependence with an annual price cycle, so volume shocks hit earnings at once and price offsets arrive late. The second is concentration: Goderich produced 50.29% of fiscal 2025 salt tonnage, so a single-site disruption would remove about half of salt output. The third is interest burden: the 8.00% notes cost about USD 52.0 million a year, and cover on interest expense is 3.19x, so cost or volume misses have a fast effect on cash flow after interest. The fourth is a single large maturity, with 89.29% of principal due in 2030, which makes refinancing access in 2027 and 2028 the key test. The fifth is governance and reporting quality, with unremediated control weaknesses and a new management team, which raises the risk that figures or execution disappoint.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 12; Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F3; calculation F6; calculation F9

### Company description

Compass Minerals produces rock salt, evaporated salt and magnesium chloride for road deicing, consumer and industrial uses, and sulfate of potash fertilizer, operating mines in Ontario, Louisiana and Cheshire and a solar evaporation plant in Utah. Salt is about 82% of sales, and highway deicing alone is about 63% of Salt segment sales, sold mostly through annual government tenders. The credit link is that demand for salt has no known cost-effective substitute for most high-volume uses, which gives cash-flow visibility on volumes in an average winter, but the same sales are weather-driven and seasonal, with salt earnings concentrated in the December and March quarters and cash flow in the first half of the calendar year, so liquidity preservation and the revolver matter more than annual averages. The company exited a fire retardant acquisition in 2025, ended a lithium project in January 2024 and sold its Wynyard sulfate of potash plant in March 2026, so the business that remains is narrower and more focused than it was three years ago.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, pages 9–13; 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 130

### Ownership and group structure

Compass Minerals International, Inc. is a Delaware holding company whose subsidiaries conduct all operations, so creditors of the parent notes depend on subsidiary cash flows and on guarantees from certain unnamed domestic subsidiaries; the revolver is secured on substantially all U.S. assets, the Goderich mine and stock of certain subsidiaries, and the receivables facility sits in a separate special purpose subsidiary whose assets are not available to other creditors. There is no sponsor or controlling shareholder in the filings; Koch held about 17% after its 2022 placement, with the current holding not supplied. The credit implication is that distribution and exit pressure from an owner is absent, but so is a committed source of equity support, and structural priority between the secured revolver, the receivables facility and the unsecured notes cannot be fully read without the executed documents.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-06-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, pages 13–16; 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 190

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue follows volume times price. Highway volumes depend on snowfall and are sold through annual tenders and some multi-year contracts, with about 75% of UK highway customers on multi-year contracts, so price is set once a year while volume is known only after the winter; consumer and industrial salt, 37% of Salt segment sales, is steadier and higher priced. No customer or affiliated group exceeded 10% of sales in the last three fiscal years. Plant Nutrition sells sulfate of potash, which commands a premium to muriate of potash, mostly to U.S. customers (88%), and its average price rose 10.16% in the nine months to June 2026. The revenue is therefore predictable in direction but cyclical in size, exposed to weather and, for sulfate of potash, to imports and commodity potash; the creditor consequence is that one winter's revenue is a poor guide to coverage and the prudent reading uses a multi-year average.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, pages 12–16; 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 158; calculation F4

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Costs are dominated by product cost (57.07% of fiscal 2025 sales) and shipping and handling (27.60%), with selling, general and administrative cost at 9.11% after a reduction of USD 24.5 million in fiscal 2025. Mining and processing carry a large fixed element and are energy-intensive (electricity, diesel and natural gas), freight depends on distance and fuel, and about half of the workforce is unionised, so operating leverage is high: lower volume raises cost per ton, and inflation in freight or labour is hard to offset until the next tender. Management expects production costs to stay elevated near term and says mine-level costs have not yet improved at the pace expected. Margins can be defended against modest cost inflation through pricing gains, as the 7.80% highway price rise shows, but not against a combined volume and cost shock, so creditors should treat the 18.05% adjusted EBITDA margin as a good-winter margin.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-06-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 32; calculation F4

### Capital intensity and FCF conversion

Capital expenditure was USD 114.2 million in fiscal 2024, USD 69.7 million in fiscal 2025 and USD 62.1 million in the nine months to June 2026, with fiscal 2026 guidance of USD 90 million to USD 110 million; no split between maintenance and growth is stated, so flexibility cannot be measured. Cash interest paid was USD 59.3 million in fiscal 2025 and USD 41.6 million over the last twelve months, cash taxes were USD 38.5 million over the last twelve months, and working capital swings with inventory, which released USD 87.3 million in fiscal 2025. The result is that free cash flow of USD 77.9 million over the last twelve months is about 0.33x adjusted EBITDA, enough to repay debt gradually but not to retire the 2030 notes, which means refinancing will be needed. Capital spending can be cut as it was in fiscal 2025 (down 39.0%), which gives some protection in a downside, but cuts that defer mine maintenance would raise cost later.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F5; calculation F11

### Porter's five forces

Competitive rivalry is strong: salt is a commodity, competitors in North America include large nationally recognised companies and regional producers, and two competitors serve the UK highway market, so price competes within delivered cost and the company's depots, Great Lakes position and deep-water port are its edge; creditors should expect price gains to be contested. Entry barriers differ: the filing says they are low in consumer and industrial salt, and, as an analyst reading rather than a company statement, they are high for rock salt mining because few mines combine scale and port access. Substitutes are weak for high-volume uses by management's account, and for sulfate of potash the substitute is muriate of potash, which is cheaper but contains chloride. Buyer power is high in highway deicing because government buyers tender annually, and low in sulfate of potash where the company is the only North American producer. Supplier power matters through energy, freight and, for some products, purchased potassium chloride and calcium chloride, with natural gas partly hedged. The net effect on credit is that pricing power is moderate and cost pass-through slow, which supports the view of volatile but recoverable margins.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, pages 11–16; 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, pages 131–132

### PEST analysis

Only three factors change credit outcomes. Political and regulatory: U.S. tariffs of up to 50% on certain Canadian imports were announced for August 19, 2026, and Canadian mines supply U.S. highway customers, but the company reports its products are compliant with the United States-Mexico-Canada trade agreement and its Canadian exports were previously exempt, so the effect is not quantified and could affect Goderich margins; Utah rule-making on the Great Salt Lake may affect extraction at Ogden, which underpins sulfate of potash and solar salt output. Economic: freight, fuel and labour inflation and interest rates bear on cost and on the revolver rate of about 5.8%. Social and technological factors are not credit drivers on the filings read. The consequence is that the Canadian tariff outcome and Ogden's lake access are monitoring items, not conclusions.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, pages 44 and 71–72; 10-Q_2026-06-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 14

### SWOT analysis

Strengths: scale and port-linked logistics at Goderich, near-term maturity clearance, aligned financial policy, Plant Nutrition recovery and a rating upgrade, each supporting debt service. Weaknesses: weather dependence, mine concentration, interest burden, single 2030 maturity and weak reporting controls, each constraining cash flow or visibility. Opportunities that could improve credit quality: pricing momentum in highway bids, further debt reduction from free cash flow, refinancing the 2030 notes after the first call date at a lower coupon, and mine efficiency gains that management has not yet delivered. Threats that could weaken credit quality: a mild winter, freight and labour inflation, tariffs on Canadian exports, loss of Goderich output, a change in dividend policy and a failure to remediate control weaknesses.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 176

### Financial profile and credit quality

Business scale and market position are average: the company leads in North American sulfate of potash and operates the largest underground salt mine in the world, but sells commodities and has USD 1,292.1 million of last-twelve-month revenue. Competitive advantage is average: logistics and asset quality help, but there is little differentiation. Diversification is weak: two product families, one mine for about half of salt tons and weather exposure dominate. Cost and capex flexibility is average: spending has been cut but costs are largely fixed and logistics-driven. Margin stability is weak to average: the adjusted EBITDA margin moved from 18.46% in fiscal 2024 to 15.98% in fiscal 2025 and 18.05% over the last twelve months. Free cash flow stability is weak: negative in fiscal 2024, positive since, with seasonal and inventory effects. Ability to refinance is average: a single 2030 bond at a high coupon, no maturity before 2028, and a recent rating upgrade reported by the company. Liquidity is average to strong against near-term obligations but flattered by the seasonal low. Financial policy and governance is average: aligned in behaviour but with weak controls. The credit supports are leverage near 3x, clear maturities and aligned policy; the constraints are volatility, concentration and interest burden; the trend is improving; the missing data are covenant definitions, peers and the fiscal 2026 year end.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F1; calculation F5

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

In the next 12 to 24 months management guides fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA to USD 218 million to USD 242 million with a USD 230 million midpoint, capital expenditure to USD 90 million to USD 110 million and net interest expense to USD 62 million to USD 67 million, and says fiscal 2027 demand should be lower than fiscal 2026 on normal weather after a stronger-than-average winter. Guidance and nine-month results imply a fiscal fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA of USD 38.4 million at the midpoint against USD 41.6 million a year earlier. Tailwinds are the constructive bid season, Plant Nutrition momentum and lower debt. Headwinds are volume normalisation, cost inflation, tariffs and the 2028 revolver. Over three to five years the issue is refinancing the 2030 notes, callable from July 2027 at 104.0% and at par from July 2029, and keeping leverage low enough to do so; sector demand for salt grows slowly (flat to about 1% a year by management's estimate) while prices have risen about 3% to 4% a year, which supports modest growth only.

### Qualitative downside scenario

The scenario replays fiscal 2024, the issuer's own weak-winter year. The initiating condition is a mild 2026-27 winter in which highway volumes fall by about the 16.95% by which fiscal 2024 volumes trailed fiscal 2025, on top of the normalisation management already expects. The first operational break is unabsorbed fixed cost at the mines and unsold salt that raises inventory, as in fiscal 2024 when payables fell and inventory built. The financial transmission is lower Salt earnings and a working-capital build that turned operating cash flow to USD 14.4 million in fiscal 2024 and free cash flow to negative USD 99.8 million; the same result today would use about 30.42% of liquidity of USD 328.1 million, mainly through revolver borrowings. Leverage would rise because debt would not fall and adjusted EBITDA would decline, and the covenant tests and the revolver maturity in May 2028 would become more relevant. Management's levers are capital expenditure, inventory release, suspension of dividends already in place and asset sales. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress in one such year because liquidity and the maturity profile are stronger than in fiscal 2024, and the observable triggers are revolver borrowings above USD 35.0 million at a seasonal low, a December quarter working-capital build and adjusted EBITDA below the guidance range.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, pages 125 and 141–142; 10-K_2024-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 128; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F7

### Overall credit view

Overall, Compass Minerals presents an adequate business profile supported by the scale and port-linked logistics of the Goderich mine, a recovering Plant Nutrition segment and aligned financial policy, but credit quality is constrained by weather dependence, mine concentration and an 8.00% bond that makes interest the heaviest fixed claim. Cash-flow durability appears adequate in an average winter and weak in a mild one, because free cash flow is seasonal and was negative in fiscal 2024. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on leverage staying near 3x and on market access in 2027 and 2028, before the revolver falls due in May 2028. The primary downside path is a mild winter with cost inflation that builds inventory and draws the revolver. Further analysis would require the covenant definitions and compliance certificates, the executed documents, the fiscal 2026 annual report and a peer set. The review is complete with limitations, and the top risk is weather-driven earnings volatility.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F1; calculation F7
