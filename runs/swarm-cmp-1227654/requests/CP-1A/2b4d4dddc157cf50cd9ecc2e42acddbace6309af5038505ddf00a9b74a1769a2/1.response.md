## Findings

### Bottom line

Compass Minerals is a weather-exposed producer that depends on one large mine and a largely new management team, with no controlling sponsor behind it, so creditors rely on operating delivery and cash discipline, not owner support.
Basis: calculation B5; 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 63; 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, pages 101–103; 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 190

Salt supplies about 82% of sales, and the Goderich mine alone produced about half of the salt tonnage in fiscal 2025. Financial policy has been aligned with creditors since April 2024: no dividends, and disposal proceeds used with cash to retire debt. Governance remains the weak point, with unremediated control weaknesses and a new operations chief in August 2026, so sponsor and governance risk is Medium.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 63; 10-Q_2026-06-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 48; EX99.1_2026-06-30_cmp_cmp-q32026earningsreleasee-htm.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-06-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, pages 53–55; EX99.1_2026-06-30_cmp_cmp-risnerex991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation B5; calculation B7

### Key findings

- Earnings follow winter weather, so volumes swing before prices can respond. Highway deicing volumes rose 20.41% in fiscal 2025 on a more normal winter and fell 2.54% in the nine months to June 2026, while the average highway price rose 7.80%. Highway sales are won mostly in annual government tenders, so a mild winter cuts volume at once and price can only recover at the next bid, which is why one winter's results should not be read as the run rate.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 110; 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 125; EX99.1_2026-06-30_cmp_cmp-q32026earningsreleasee-htm.htm.jsonl, page 11; calculation B10
- One mine carries the portfolio, which concentrates operating and labour risk. Goderich produced 50.29% of fiscal 2025 salt tonnage, nearly half of the workforce is under collective agreements, and a new three-year Goderich agreement was announced in May 2026, which removes the nearest labour deadline at that site.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-09-30_cmp.htm.jsonl, page 63; EX99.1_2026-03-31_cmp_cmp-q22026earningsreleasee-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation B5

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against caution is that, in management's words, salt has no known cost-effective alternative for most high-volume uses, that no customer reached 10% of sales in any of the last three fiscal years, that highway prices rose 7.80% in the nine months to June 2026 and management describes the coming highway bid season as constructive, and that Plant Nutrition earnings have recovered. Management also reports a stronger-than-average winter in fiscal 2026. These support a view that the business is steadier than its weather exposure suggests. The view would improve if the next winter is average and the fiscal 2027 volume decline management expects is mild, if remediation of the control weaknesses is reported complete, and if Goderich production costs fall. It would worsen if a mine or labour disruption hits Goderich, if the tariffs announced for August 2026 reach Canadian salt exports, or if the board reverses the dividend suspension before debt is lower.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

No new debt issue or acquisition is reported after June 30, 2026 in the documents read. The transactions that shape the creditor position are all earlier. On June 16, 2025 the company issued USD 650.0 million of 8.00% senior unsecured notes due 2030 and used the proceeds to repay the revolver and term loan in full and to redeem USD 350.0 million of its 6.75% notes due 2027; the same day a fifth amendment fixed revolver commitments at USD 325.0 million and reset the financial covenants to a first lien leverage test and an interest coverage test. On March 30, 2026 it redeemed the remaining USD 150.0 million of the 2027 notes at par from cash, and on March 19, 2026 it extended its receivables financing facility to March 2029. On March 1, 2026 it sold the Wynyard sulfate of potash business for total consideration of about USD 30.8 million, receiving USD 23.3 million of net cash and booking a USD 14.5 million loss, of which USD 13.1 million is non-cash. In 2025 it exited the Fortress fire retardant business it had bought in 2023, selling the assets for USD 20.0 million, and in January 2024 it ended its lithium project after an impairment of USD 74.8 million. For creditors, the sequence moved the company from secured bank debt and subordinated notes to a structure dominated by one unsecured bond due 2030, with a small revolver and receivables facility, removed near-term maturities and stopped capital going to projects outside salt and sulfate of potash.

### Overall governance view

Overall, Compass Minerals presents Medium sponsor and governance risk. The key supported behaviour is the suspension of dividends in April 2024 and the use of asset-sale proceeds and cash to retire notes, which matters because it keeps cash in the business and lowers refinancing exposure, and implies a financial policy aligned with creditors. Countervailing evidence is the unremediated control weaknesses, a restatement, a notice of default in September 2024 for a late quarterly report that was cured, and a management team in which every listed officer took the current post since January 2024. Further analysis requires the proxy statement, the executed credit agreement and indenture, and the fiscal 2026 annual report. The review is complete with limitations at a Medium risk level.
