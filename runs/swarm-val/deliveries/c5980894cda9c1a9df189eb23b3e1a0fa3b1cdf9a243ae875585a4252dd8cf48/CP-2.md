## Findings

### Bottom line

Valaris presents an adequate fundamental credit profile: a contracted drillship recovery and USD 900.1 million of liquidity outweigh rising trailing leverage, but negative free cash flow and a pending change of control mean creditors should expect liquidity to erode before earnings fully recover.
Basis: calculation F8; calculation F6; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Last-twelve-month reported leverage was 2.97x and net debt was USD 563.4 million, or USD 555.9 million pro forma for the October 2026 rig sale; net leverage was 1.54x, or 1.51x pro forma. Operating cash flow less capital additions was negative USD 172.7 million in the first nine months of 2026, which is why a quarter of Adjusted EBITDA of USD 150.6 million, more than twice the first quarter's USD 66.7 million, matters more than the trailing ratio.
Basis: calculation F1; calculation F2; calculation F4; calculation F5; calculation F9; calculation F7; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Key findings

- Revenue visibility is good but concentrated. Contract backlog of USD 4,750.1 million is 2.39x last-twelve-month revenues, yet floaters hold 61.1% of it and four customers supplied 49% of Q3 revenue, so a few customers' programmes decide whether the recovery holds.
Basis: calculation K1; calculation F15; calculation F16; Business & governance (CP-1A)
- Margins are recovering through operating leverage, and day rates are rising where the fleet is working. Contract drilling expenses fell from 79.1% of revenues in Q1 to 68.4% in Q3 while average daily revenue for floaters rose 19.4% in nine months, so each restarted drillship adds earnings faster than cost; the risk is that jackup day rates fell 2.2% and war-risk insurance cost USD 23.0 million more over nine months, although it eased in the third quarter.
Basis: calculation F17; calculation K2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_val-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-Q_2026-09-30_val-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40; 10-Q_2026-09-30_val-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 42; Business & governance (CP-1A)
- Cash conversion is the weak point. Operating cash flow less capital additions was negative USD 172.7 million in nine months against positive USD 236.8 million a year earlier, and the 2026 capital additions range of USD 445.0 million to USD 470.0 million implies USD 117.0 million to USD 142.0 million more in the fourth quarter, so no debt has been repaid and cash has fallen by USD 74.3 million since year-end to fund maintenance, upgrade and contract-specific spending.
Basis: calculation F9; calculation F11; calculation K12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_val-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 52; Financial foundation (CP-1)
- Liquidity is adequate and refinancing is a 2030 question. Liquidity of USD 900.1 million (cash of USD 525.1 million at 2026-09-30, for which a pro forma figure after the October 2026 rig sale is not established, plus the undrawn USD 375.0 million revolver) sits against no principal maturity before 2030, one secured bond of USD 1,100.0 million and a revolver due in 2028 that is undrawn; the revolver becomes a current obligation from April 2027. Its three financial covenants (equity to total assets, interest cover and minimum liquidity) have no levels printed, and the issuer reports compliance in all material respects at 2026-09-30.
Basis: calculation F8; calculation F13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_val-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-K_2025-12-31_val-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 129; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Reported leverage rose from 1.74x at the end of FY2025 to 2.97x as strong 2025 quarters rolled out of the twelve months, and cover on net interest expense is 3.74x, against cash interest cover of 6.89x in FY2025; trailing leverage falls at the next quarter end if fourth-quarter 2026 reported EBITDA exceeds the USD 80.0 million of the quarter it replaces, and the third quarter was USD 48.6 million above that.

Adjusted EBITDA of USD 266.8 million, the coupon and last-twelve-month capital additions give a cash outflow of USD 259.6 million, leaving liquidity of USD 640.5 million; the scenario is illustrative and ignores cash taxes, working capital, disposals, interest income and any ARO call.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an adequate profile is cash conversion: free cash flow has been negative for the last twelve months while capital additions run well above operating cash flow, trailing leverage has climbed to 2.97x, and the Middle East conflicts, shipyard delays and war-risk insurance still cut operating income by about USD 24.0 million in the third quarter. The view would weaken if fourth-quarter Adjusted EBITDA fell back toward the first-quarter level of USD 66.7 million, if capital additions came in above USD 142.0 million for the quarter, if ARO called capital, or if the change of control left the notes in a weaker position. It would strengthen if earnings held near the third-quarter level and the combined company kept the 2026 pause on repurchases.
Basis: calculation F9; calculation F11; calculation F7; calculation F1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_val-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Business and ownership in credit terms

Valaris contracts ultra-deepwater drillships and premium jackups on day rate contracts, so earnings follow rig utilisation and day rates, and a stacked or idle rig earns nothing while its holding costs continue. The group is widely held with no sponsor, which means no committed equity support and no sponsor-driven leakage; the creditors' main exposure is the pending sale to Transocean and the 50% ARO joint venture, which Valaris does not consolidate, so ARO's own debt is outside group net debt and its earnings reach Valaris only as an equity share that Adjusted EBITDA excludes.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Revenue durability

Revenue is contracted for periods of about 1 month to 4 years, which gives visibility, but some contracts allow termination for the customer's convenience or if a final investment decision is not taken, so backlog is not guaranteed cash. Pricing is improving for floaters (average daily revenue up 19.4% over nine months) and softer for jackups (down 2.2%). For creditors, the recovery in revenue is a contracted-restart story whose realisation depends on customers' programmes.
Basis: calculation K2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_val-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_val-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 42; 10-K_2025-12-31_val-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10

### Margin resilience

Costs look largely fixed once a rig is working, so margins widen as utilisation rises and would narrow just as fast if it fell; contract drilling expenses were USD 379.9 million in Q3 against USD 380.4 million in Q2 while revenues rose from USD 502.3 million to USD 555.6 million. Over nine months expense was flat because USD 67.3 million of higher repair and maintenance cost and USD 23.0 million of higher insurance, mainly war-risk cover, were offset by USD 103.8 million of lower costs for rigs sold. Margins can be defended on volume only while utilisation stays high, and the creditor consequence is higher earnings sensitivity in both directions.
Basis: calculation F17; EX-99.1_2026-11-02_a09302026ex991pressrelease.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_val-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-Q_2026-09-30_val-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40

### Cash conversion and capital intensity

Capital intensity is high while idle drillships are returned to work and upgraded: nine-month capital additions of USD 328.0 million were more than twice operating cash flow of USD 155.3 million, and part of that spending is contract-specific and recoverable from customers. Operating cash flow also included about USD 43.0 million of U.S. tax refunds and asset sales brought in USD 102.6 million, while the interest on the notes is fixed. EBITDA is therefore not converting to free cash flow now, and spending is met from cash and asset sales rather than from retained earnings.
Basis: calculation F10; calculation F9; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_val-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 52

### Market forces and external factors

Rivalry is strong because contracts are awarded by competitive bid and price is often the deciding factor, and ten drillships across the industry were idle at the end of the third quarter with nine due back to work by early 2027, which could cap day rates; the supply of benign environment floaters has fallen by more than 45% since 2014, which supports them. New entry is limited by high newbuild cost and shipyard capacity, substitutes are other oil supply such as onshore shale and, over time, the energy transition, buyers are a few large oil companies with termination rights, and suppliers (shipyards, insurers, crews) have pushed costs up. Politically, the Middle East conflicts cost about USD 24.0 million of operating income in Q3, and tax disputes and the 15% Bermuda tax add earnings uncertainty.

### Financial profile

On the method's nine dimensions the assessment is: scale and market position average, with a fleet the issuer describes as among the largest in the world (13 drillships and 28 jackups owned at 2026-11-03) but a concentrated customer base; competitive advantage average, resting on modern seventh-generation drillships (12 of 13) rather than on contract terms; business diversification weak to average, with floaters 61.1% of backlog; cost and capex flexibility average, because contract-specific capital spending is recoverable but a fixed cost base is not; margin stability average, improving; free cash flow stability weak; ability to refinance average, with one bond due in 2030 and market quotations not supplied beyond the quarter-end fair value; liquidity position strong relative to maturities; and financial policy and governance average, with no sponsor but event risk from the combination. Supports are the contracted base and liquidity; the main constraint is free cash flow.
Basis: calculation F15; calculation F8; calculation F9; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_val-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-09-30_val-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36

### Outlook

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months the debt-service case depends on drillship restarts continuing: management says day rates are moving higher and deepwater opportunities are converting to contracts. Reinvestment stays high, with USD 117.0 million to USD 142.0 million of capital additions still implied for the fourth quarter, and ARO has ordered four newbuild jackups that Valaris may be asked to help fund. Sector tailwinds are customer emphasis on energy security and deepwater sanctioning (about 65% of expected deepwater activity has breakeven prices below USD 50 a barrel), and headwinds are the Middle East conflicts, cost inflation and war-risk insurance. Over three to five years the 2028 revolver and the 2030 notes fall due, so the outlook for earnings through 2027 decides refinancing terms.
Basis: calculation F11; calculation F19; EX-99.1_2026-11-02_a09302026ex991pressrelease.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_val-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_val-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-Q_2026-09-30_val-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Downside scenario

An issuer-specific downside starts with a customer programme delay or a Middle East shipyard stoppage that returns Adjusted EBITDA to the first-quarter level of USD 66.7 million a quarter; at that level revenue and utilisation fall first, the largely fixed cost base turns the margin gain back, and free cash flow turns more negative because capital additions are committed. Holding that level for four quarters gives Adjusted EBITDA of USD 266.8 million, a coupon cover of 2.90x and a cash outflow of USD 259.6 million after the coupon and capital additions, so cash falls from USD 525.1 million at 2026-09-30 to USD 265.5 million, liquidity to USD 640.5 million and net leverage rises to 3.08x. Management can slow discretionary capital additions and sell stacked rigs, as it did in 2026, so resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress within twelve months, but the margin for the 2028 revolver renewal narrows and the revolver's covenants, whose levels are not printed, could become the binding constraint.
Basis: calculation K3; calculation K4; calculation K5; calculation K6; calculation K7; calculation K8; calculation K9; calculation F7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_val-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22

### Overall credit view

Overall, Valaris presents an adequate business profile supported by contracted drillship backlog, modern assets and liquidity, but credit quality is constrained by negative free cash flow, trailing leverage that has risen and concentration in customers and floaters. Cash-flow durability appears adequate only if the third-quarter earnings level holds. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on earnings through 2027 and on how the change of control treats the notes. The primary downside path is a fall back to first-quarter earnings while capital spending stays committed. Further analysis would require the indenture, the credit agreement, the acquirer's disclosure and ARO's accounts. Fundamental credit review completed with limitations. Top risk: negative free cash flow combined with event risk from the change of control.
Basis: calculation F1; calculation F9; calculation F8; calculation F7; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

## Gaps

- The levels of the revolver's three financial covenants (equity to total assets, interest cover and minimum liquidity) are not printed in the annual report or the quarterly report, which say only that the issuer complied in all material respects, so headroom is not calculated and liquidity is stated before any covenant restriction (High for the liquidity finding).
- Cash interest paid is stated only in the annual report (USD 90.5 million for FY2025), not for quarters, so latest-period cover uses net interest expense as a proxy (Medium).
- The scenario inputs are an analyst choice: the first-quarter Adjusted EBITDA level held for four quarters, the coupon and last-twelve-month capital additions; it excludes cash taxes, working capital, disposals, interest income and any ARO call (Medium).
- Ratings, peer data and market prices other than the quarter-end fair value of the notes were not supplied, so the profile is not compared with peers (Medium).
- The proxy statement, the indenture and the credit agreement were not supplied, so governance and capacity conclusions rest on observed behaviour (Medium).
- Pro forma cash for the October 2026 rig sale is not established, so cash and liquidity are stated as at 2026-09-30 (Low).
- Page numbers cited in this analysis are chunk numbers of the supplied text files, not printed page numbers (Low).
