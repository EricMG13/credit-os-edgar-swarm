## Findings

### Bottom line

Goodyear's credit profile has weakened sharply in 2026: net leverage on reported earnings reached 6.62x from 3.98x at year-end 2025 and cash flow is negative, so creditors rely on large undrawn lines rather than earnings to bridge the 2027 maturities.
The strongest support is liquidity: USD 3,639 million of unused credit arrangements and USD 812 million of cash against USD 1,339 million due within a year. The weakest point is earnings: segment operating income fell to USD 305 million from USD 641 million in nine months, so net debt rose by USD 1,160 million, reversing 73.7% of the 2025 reduction. Whether the nine-month cash outflow is timing or structural will show in the fourth quarter.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against a weakening view is timing: the fourth quarter of 2025 produced USD 1,335 million of free cash flow after nine months of USD -1,365 million, management expects neutral working capital for 2026, further transformation savings of about USD 110 million are expected in the fourth quarter, tariff refunds were received in 2026, and Asia Pacific segment income rose to USD 186 million from USD 139 million. Against that, management also expects raw materials to be USD 170 million worse and unabsorbed overhead USD 45 million higher in the fourth quarter, and the 2025 comparison included deferred cash from the three sales that will not recur. A fourth-quarter inflow that brings year-end net debt back toward the USD 5,397 million of year-end 2025, repayment of the 2027 notes from cash and stabilising replacement volume would move the view toward average; a further revolver draw to meet the 2027 maturity, a wider borrowing-base shortfall or a larger fourth-quarter outflow would move it toward weak.

### Business and revenue durability

Goodyear makes and sells tires in three regions; tire units were 84% of 2025 net sales and replacement tires were 71.3% of 2025 units, so revenue follows replacement demand, which is discretionary, price-competitive and weak in 2026. Replacement volume fell 10.7% worldwide in nine months, partly by design as lower-tier lines are rationalised, while original-equipment volume rose 4.5%; price and mix added USD 267 million to net sales, which shows pricing support but not enough to hold margin. Net sales fell 5.2% in nine months, helped by price and mix and hurt by the Dunlop and chemical sales and lower volume.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gt-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 44–45; calculation C5; calculation C6

No customer or channel concentration is disclosed and the business is not seasonal to a significant degree, so revenue visibility rests on the volume trend rather than on contracts. The credit linkage is moderate cash-flow visibility with no contracted base, so debt service depends on volume stabilising and on cost actions rather than on recurring revenue.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 6–8; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Ownership and group structure

Goodyear is a widely held listed company with no sponsor or controlling holder identified, so the financial-policy risk that sponsor ownership usually brings is not documented, while owners, directors and legal capacity are not established and the governance level is medium. The parent issues most dollar notes, a European subsidiary issues the euro notes, and wholly-owned United States and Canadian subsidiaries guarantee the first lien revolver and most notes.

### Competitive forces and external factors

Rivalry is high: Bridgestone and Michelin are the two major competitors, others make tires in low-cost countries and lower-tier imports have raised competition in the United States, which limits price recovery and makes volume and absorption the swing factors. New entrants matter mainly as lower-tier imports from Asia. Substitutes are not a near-term credit factor, although electric and autonomous vehicles may require new products. Buyer power is not established because no customer share is stated, and supplier power rose after the chemical sale because synthetic rubber is now bought under a 15-year take-or-pay contract.

### Outlook

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months the tailwinds are about USD 110 million of further transformation savings in the fourth quarter and about USD 300 million expected for 2026, lower capital spending, one-off tariff refunds, original-equipment share gains and Asia Pacific income growth; the headwinds are weak replacement volume, USD 170 million of adverse raw materials and USD 110 million of other inflation in the fourth quarter, tariffs, the loss of about USD 185 million of 2026 segment income from the Dunlop and chemical sales, restructuring cash of about USD 210 million in 2026 and the 2027 and 2028 maturities. ### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is that replacement volume keeps falling and raw materials turn against Goodyear as management expects in the fourth quarter. The first operational break is a fourth-quarter segment operating income below the USD 416 million of the fourth quarter of 2025 because stated cost headwinds exceed planned savings by USD 215 million, so inventory of USD 3,748 million and receivables of USD 3,081 million are not converted into the cash that management assumes for a neutral working-capital year.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 55; calculation C18

Financial transmission follows: operating cash flow stays negative, net borrowing continues, the revolvers move above USD 676 million, and the first lien borrowing base, which depends on receivables and inventory, would not give back its USD 270 million shortfall. If nine-month cash burn persisted with no seasonal recovery, an upper-bound stress rather than a forecast, free cash flow of USD -1,660 million over twelve months plus USD 1,339 million due would use USD 2,999 million of the USD 4,451 million of cash and unused lines, and a second such year with USD 1,224 million due in 2028 would exceed what is left by USD 1,432 million. The observable triggers are quarterly operating cash flow, the revolver balance, the borrowing-base shortfall, how the 2027 notes are repaid and the coupon on any new issue.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gt-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 22–24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 54; calculation C11; calculation C13; calculation C22

Management levers are further capital-spending cuts, more plant closures, a larger working-capital release and asset sales, although the three large sales are done and no further sale candidates are named. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress through the 2027 maturity even if the nine-month outflow repeated, but it is not sufficient to fund a second year of such outflow and the 2028 maturities without recovery or refinancing.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 55; 10-K_2025-12-31_gt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 42–44; calculation C22

### Overall credit view

Overall, Goodyear presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, recognised brands, a simpler perimeter and cash-preserving financial policy, but credit quality is constrained by negative cash flow, compressed margin and net leverage of 6.62x on reported earnings. Cash-flow durability appears weak due to three years of negative free cash flow and a 2.4% segment margin. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on revolver availability and the borrowing base, on the repayment of the 2027 notes and on market access at a higher coupon. The primary downside path is continued replacement weakness with adverse raw materials that prevents the fourth-quarter cash recovery, forcing more revolver use ahead of the 2028 maturities. Further analysis would require ratings, prices, the executed agreements, management projections and the proxy statement.
