## Findings

### Bottom line

AMC's fundamental credit profile is weak but improving: a sharp 2026 earnings recovery has cut gross leverage from 18.72x to 5.17x, yet thin interest cover, cash-only liquidity and a larger post-refinancing stack leave creditors dependent on sustained attendance.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01

Net debt was USD 2,995.5 million at 30 September 2026, or 4.13x reported EBITDA, and net debt and cash after the 5 October 2026 refinancing are not established; principal rose to USD 4,086.1 million from USD 3,782.6 million. Reported EBITDA covered cash interest 1.85x, cash was USD 751.9 million with no undrawn facility described, and nine-month free cash flow was USD 124.7 million. The refinancing pushed maturities to 2031 and 2033, but a weak release slate would meet largely fixed rent and little liquidity backstop.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 33–38; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 79–82; calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C04; calculation C06

### Key findings

- Scale gives AMC market leadership in its core territories, but revenue is hit-driven and concentrated in few suppliers, so scale does not protect cash flow by itself. The seven largest studio distributors supplied about 83% of U.S. admissions revenue in 2025, North American box office was still about 22% below 2019, and the company itself says film supply, release windows and streaming decide attendance; a weaker slate cuts admissions and food and beverage sales together.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, pages 39–40; 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 97; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C09

- Margin resilience rests on fixed rent: operating leverage lifted earnings in 2026 and would reverse in a weak year. In the nine months revenue rose by USD 792.6 million while rent rose by only USD 4.3 million, so adjusted EBITDA rose by USD 452.9 million, an incremental margin of 57.14%; 86.90% of theatre rent is fixed lease cost, so the same arithmetic works against creditors when attendance falls.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, page 75; 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 139; calculation C03; calculation C05

- Cash generation has turned positive only recently and conversion is modest. Free cash flow was USD 124.7 million in the nine months and USD 168.0 million over twelve months against USD -365.9 million in 2025, which is about 19.99% of twelve-month adjusted EBITDA; the improvement depends on a strong summer box office rather than on a lower cost base.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C04

- Liquidity is cash only and covers interest for under two years. Cash of USD 751.9 million at 30 September 2026 (cash after the 5 October 2026 refinancing is not established) is 1.92x the latest twelve months of cash interest, no undrawn committed facility is described, and the working capital deficit excluding restricted cash was USD 792.8 million, so a fall in sales that reverses the deferred revenue float would draw on cash quickly.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 79–82; calculation C02; calculation C07

- Refinancing capacity improved sharply near term but at the cost of a larger and layered stack. The October refinancing moved maturities to 2031 and 2033 and left only USD 150.1 million of principal due before 2031, yet principal rose by USD 303.5 million, 68.92% of principal falls due in 2031 and USD 1,120.0 million is second lien debt at a fixed rate, so refinancing risk is deferred rather than removed.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 34–38; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, page 85; calculation C06; calculation C10

- Governance and financial policy are the weakest dimension, because documented transfers of theatres to unrestricted subsidiaries and a non-pro-rata exchange moved priority between creditor classes; equity-funded deleveraging and no cash dividend are the offsets. Holders in different entities and collateral pools face different loss given default, and the equity capacity that has funded repayment is limited by authorized shares.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C08

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a weak assessment is that the 2026 recovery is broad: attendance rose 18.85% in the nine months, revenue rose 22.26% and adjusted EBITDA rose 178.10%, free cash flow turned positive, the 2029 maturities were replaced, and the company reports records in the second and third quarters. If the cash margin holds and equity or conversion keeps lowering principal, adjusted net leverage of 3.56x, which does not reflect the 5 October 2026 refinancing because its effect on net debt is not established, would continue to fall and the view would move toward adequate. The view would turn more negative if attendance returned to 2025 levels, if cash interest after the refinancing proved well above the USD 392.3 million paid in the latest twelve months, or if the equity market closed before the exchangeable notes and the 2031 maturities were addressed.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-11-04_amc-ex99d1.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C01; calculation C03; calculation C08

### Company description

AMC operates 855 theatres and 9,640 screens in 11 countries, 533 theatres in the United States and 322 in Europe, and earned 54.71% of its USD 4,848.9 million of 2025 revenue from admissions, 34.47% from food and beverage and 10.82% from other theatre sources. The business is licensed film by film from studios on a share of box office, so supply and release timing sit outside management's control, and the theatre count fell from 871 to 855 during 2025 and to 840 by 30 September 2026 as closures continued. For credit, scale and urban locations support attendance, but the model has no contracted revenue: every period's cash flow depends on the film slate, which makes debt service less predictable than the leverage ratio suggests.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 113; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 49–50; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C09

### Ownership and group structure

AMC is publicly held with no sponsor or controlling holder identified; holder percentages and board composition are in a proxy statement that was not supplied. Holdings sits above Muvico, LLC and Odeon Finco PLC, and since July 2024 the Muvico Group has been unrestricted under the indenture for the 7.5% notes (discharged in October 2026) while co-borrowing the term loans that were repaid in October 2026, so structurally the first lien and second lien debt now sits at Holdings level with guarantees from wholly-owned subsidiaries. In 2025 the Muvico Group produced 39.15% of adjusted EBITDA. Financial policy has been to fund debt reduction with equity, which supports creditors but dilutes holders, and the share count rose 74.02% in the nine months.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, pages 100–102; calculation C08

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Admissions and food and beverage revenue move with attendance, ticket price and spend per patron: attendance rose 18.85% in the nine months, average ticket price rose to USD 12.36 from USD 11.96 and food and beverage revenue per patron rose to USD 7.80 from USD 7.57. Revenue is transactional, discretionary, seasonal and exposed to substitution from streaming and premium video on demand, and about 51% of U.S. attendance comes from loyalty members, which adds some repeat visits but not contracted revenue. Pricing power exists in premium formats but is capped by competition for leisure spending; the creditor implication is that growth in 2026 came mainly from volume on a strong slate, which is the least controllable driver.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, pages 39–40; EX-99.1_2026-11-04_amc-ex99d1.htm.jsonl, page 9; calculation C03

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Film rental is a share of box office and moves with admissions, but rent of USD 887.3 million in 2025 is 18.30% of revenue and 86.90% of theatre rent is fixed lease cost, with operating expense of USD 1,786.0 million also largely unavoidable while theatres are open. In the nine months revenue rose by USD 792.6 million and rent by USD 4.3 million, which is why adjusted EBITDA more than doubled. Margins can be defended only if attendance holds; under volume pressure fixed rent and staffing absorb the shortfall, and closures are the main cost lever, as the 150 theatre closures over five years show.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, pages 113–114; 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 139; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C03; calculation C05

### Capital intensity and FCF conversion

Gross capital expenditure was USD 246.1 million in 2025 and USD 158.8 million in the nine months of 2026, with 2026 net spending expected at USD 200.0 million to USD 235.0 million, and cash interest paid was USD 392.3 million over twelve months. Free cash flow was USD 168.0 million over twelve months, 19.99% of adjusted EBITDA and 23.18% of reported EBITDA, against USD -365.9 million in 2025. Part of the improvement is working-capital float from film rental paid after admissions and deferred revenue of USD 452.6 million, and part is lease incentives; capex is the flexible item and could be cut in a downturn, but the cuts would weaken the premium offering that supports ticket prices.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, page 79; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, page 82; calculation C04; calculation C12

### Porter's Five Forces

Rivalry is intense with other circuits and streaming, but the three largest U.S. exhibitors held about 54% of 2025 box office and dense urban sites are hard to replicate, which protects share but not demand. The company believes attractive sites are scarce in major cities, which limits entry there. Substitutes (streaming, premium video on demand, other leisure) are the main risk because shorter release windows would shift attendance away from theatres. Buyer power is dispersed and weak individually. Supplier power is high: seven studios provide about 83% of U.S. admissions revenue, and reliance on fewer tent-pole films may raise licensing fees. The creditor consequence is that supplier and substitute forces, not rivalry, set the downside.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, pages 21–22; 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, pages 39–40; calculation C09

### PEST analysis

PEST factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver based on the provided materials: foreign currency effects are small, as a hypothetical 10% rise in translation rates would change the International net loss by about USD 9.6 million, and the regulatory item identified is disability-access compliance.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 108; calculation C11

### SWOT

Strengths are the largest circuit in the United States and Europe by the company's description, loyalty membership reaching about 51% of U.S. attendance, a recovered earnings base and longer maturities. Weaknesses are fixed rent, studio dependence, cash-only liquidity, a larger secured stack and a history of liability management. Opportunities that could improve credit are conversion or repayment of the remaining exchangeable notes and sustained free cash flow. Threats are a weak release slate, shortened release windows, equity-market closure with limited authorized shares and further priority-shifting transactions.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, pages 39–40; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C06; calculation C08

### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale and market position are strong in revenue and footprint terms but not a protection against slate risk; competitive advantage rests on location and loyalty and is average; diversification is weak because one product, film exhibition, with seven suppliers drives almost all revenue; cost and capex flexibility is average because capex can be cut but rent cannot; margin stability is weak on a through-the-cycle view, though 2026 shows high incremental margins; free cash flow stability is weak given USD -365.9 million in 2025 and USD 168.0 million over twelve months; ability to refinance is average after the October refinancing but depends on equity access; liquidity is average with USD 751.9 million of cash and no facility; and financial policy and governance is weak on documented behaviour with equity funding as the offset. Trend is improving from a low base, the main support is operating leverage on a strong slate, the main constraints are 5.17x gross leverage and 1.85x cover, and the missing data are interest cost after closing and forward slate evidence.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C01; calculation C04; calculation C07

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

For the next twelve to twenty-four months the tailwinds are the maturity extension, positive free cash flow and equity-funded deleveraging, which support liquidity and refinancing confidence; the headwinds are a limited authorized share count, a larger stack with USD 1,120.0 million at a fixed second lien rate, and exposure to the release schedule. Over three to five years the sector headwind is North American box office still about 22% below 2019 and the competition from streaming and shorter windows, and the company-specific headwind is the 2031 concentration of 68.92% of principal, with 2033 for the second lien. None of these can be quantified from the documents, so the outlook is directional.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, pages 39–40; 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 97; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 90–91; calculation C06; calculation C09

### Qualitative downside scenario

The initiating condition is a weak or delayed release slate, or shorter exclusive windows, that cuts attendance toward 2025 levels. The first operational break is in admissions and food and beverage sales, while 86.90% of theatre rent and most staffing cost stay in place, so adjusted EBITDA falls faster than revenue. The financial transmission is lower cover than the 1.85x now earned on cash interest of USD 392.3 million, negative free cash flow of the kind seen in 2025, and a reversal of the deferred revenue float. With cash of USD 751.9 million and no undrawn facility, liquidity would be consumed first, management's levers being capex cuts, closures and equity issuance, and the last is limited by the authorized share count and share price. The observable triggers are quarterly attendance and revenue against the prior year, free cash flow, cash balance and the interest cost on the new first lien term loans; resilience appears sufficient to avoid near-term liquidity distress only while attendance holds, because scheduled principal before 2031 is USD 150.1 million and cash is USD 751.9 million, although a repeat of the 2025 free cash flow outflow of USD 365.9 million would equal 48.66% of that cash.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 79–82; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amc-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 90–91; calculation C04; calculation C05; calculation C06; calculation C07

### Overall credit view

Overall, AMC presents an adequate business profile, supported by scale, loyalty and a recovered earnings base, but credit quality is constrained by fixed rent, studio dependence, thin cover and a governance record of moving priority between creditor classes. Cash-flow durability appears weak to adequate: free cash flow turned positive on a strong slate and is modest relative to adjusted EBITDA. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on sustained attendance, continued equity access with limited authorized shares and the cost of the new first lien term loans, which is not printed. The primary downside path is a weak release slate meeting fixed rent and cash-only liquidity. Further analysis would require interest cost and carrying value after the October 2026 refinancing, the executed financing documents, the proxy statement and forward slate evidence. Short credit snapshot completed. Top Risk: attendance shortfall against fixed rent with cash-only liquidity.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C01; calculation C04; calculation C06; calculation C07

### Strengths and weaknesses

The strengths are, first, the recovered earnings base: nine-month adjusted EBITDA of USD 707.2 million against USD 254.3 million came from higher attendance and fixed rent, which lowered leverage and gives creditors more cushion if it holds. Second, maturities were extended: only USD 150.1 million of principal is due before 2031, which removes the 2029 wall and lowers near-term refinancing risk. Third, the circuit has scale and loyalty, with members making up about 51% of U.S. attendance, which supports repeat visits. Fourth, equity has replaced debt: gross equity of USD 350.0 million was raised in the nine months and exchangeable notes were converted, which lowers principal without cash. Fifth, free cash flow turned positive at USD 124.7 million in the nine months, which for the first time in the periods shown funds capital expenditure internally.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_amc-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 12; calculation C03; calculation C04; calculation C06; calculation C08

The weaknesses are, first, fixed rent: 86.90% of theatre rent is fixed, so a fall in attendance cuts earnings faster than revenue. Second, thin cover and high leverage: gross leverage of 5.17x and cash interest cover of 1.85x leave little room for a weak slate. Third, liquidity is cash only at USD 751.9 million with no undrawn facility, against a working capital deficit of USD 792.8 million. Fourth, the stack is larger and layered after the refinancing, with principal of USD 4,086.1 million, USD 1,120.0 million of second lien debt and 68.92% of principal due in 2031. Fifth, governance and financial policy: documented transfers to unrestricted subsidiaries and a non-pro-rata exchange moved priority between creditor classes, rated high risk by Business & governance, so loss given default depends on the claim held.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C05; calculation C06; calculation C07
