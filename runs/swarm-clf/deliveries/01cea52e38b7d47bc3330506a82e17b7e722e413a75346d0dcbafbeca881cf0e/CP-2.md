## Findings

### Bottom line

Cleveland-Cliffs is a scaled, integrated steelmaker whose credit quality is constrained by net leverage of 7.87x and negative free cash flow, so creditors depend on steel prices staying high through 2027 to rebuild coverage before the ABL Facility matures in June 2028.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C8; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

The business profile is adequate: scale, own iron ore and an automotive franchise support cash generation, and the price rise of 2026 lifted nine-month adjusted EBITDA to USD 993 million from USD 37 million for all of 2025. The financial profile is weak at present: interest cover is 1.82x, capital spending and cash interest together exceed EBITDA, and liquidity of USD 3,246 million depends on a borrowing base. Maturities are back-ended, so the near-term question is cash flow, not refinancing dates.
Basis: calculation C4; calculation C6; calculation C9; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_clf-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16

Net debt of USD 7,568 million is 7.87x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA of USD 962 million and 7.79x the adjusted figure of USD 972 million, against 18.90x at the end of 2024, yet net debt is USD 372 million higher than at the end of 2025. Interest cover of 1.82x was 0.94x at June 2026, and only one quarter separates the two, so the cushion above 1.0x is recent.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is the pace of recovery and the company's expectations. Adjusted EBITDA of USD 612 million in the third quarter beat the guide, and the company expects about USD 715 million in the fourth quarter, more than USD 4 billion in 2027, a major debt reduction in the fourth quarter from free cash flow and property sale proceeds, and substantial proceeds if the POSCO transaction proceeds. The 2025 comparison is also less severe than it looks: at the 2025 average hot-rolled coil price of USD 851 per net ton the company earned only USD 37 million of adjusted EBITDA, but that year carried an unprofitable slab contract that has since ended and the company has since idled mines and finishing lines, so the same price could now earn more. The view would improve if the fourth quarter delivers positive free cash flow and net debt starts to fall, and it would worsen if the hot-rolled coil price returned toward its 2025 level, if free cash flow stayed negative after the fourth quarter, or if the repurchase authorisation were used before debt falls.

### Limitations

- No agency ratings, trading levels or peer financials were supplied, so the profile is judged against the issuer's own history and not against rated peers, which means the adequate and weak labels describe absolute metrics and not a rank within the sector.
- The downside path rests on two reference points, the 2025 average hot-rolled coil price and the 2025 free cash flow, and no sensitivity was run, so the share of liquidity it uses illustrates scale and is not a forecast of the liquidity runway.
- Per-ton costs and the effect of the 2026 labour renewals are not quantified in the documents, so margin resilience is judged from price and the observed movement of cost with price alone.
- Interim figures are unaudited and the 2026 multiples sit on a low earnings base, so the direction of the leverage trend is firmer than any single level quoted for 2026.

### Company and ownership

Cleveland-Cliffs is a vertically integrated flat-rolled steel producer focused on automotive sheet steel, with seven blast furnaces and four electric arc furnaces in the United States and Canada and mostly self-supplied iron ore. Integration stabilises the main raw material cost, which supports margin when prices rise, but the Stelco acquisition of 2024 added Canadian capacity with more spot sales and a market that weakened in 2025, which adds earnings volatility. It has no identified sponsor or controlling shareholder, the chairman, president and chief executive roles are held by one person, and directors and share ownership sit in a proxy statement that was not supplied, so financial policy is read from behaviour: debt-funded growth and share repurchases in 2024, then equity and notes in 2025, and no repurchases since.

### Revenue durability

Revenue is cyclical and price-led. Direct automotive was 30% of 2025 revenue, infrastructure and manufacturing 29%, distributors and converters 28% and steel producers 13%, and 35% to 40% of flat-rolled shipments are on fixed-price contracts that are typically annual or multi-year, with the rest on spot or index prices. Most volume therefore reprices with the hot-rolled coil index, so revenue moves with price while shipments stay near the same level, and revenue is not a stable base for fixed charges, though the automotive contracts add some visibility. The annual report flags concentration of customers in the automotive industry as a risk, but concentration by individual customer is not established.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_clf-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-12-31_clf-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_clf-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 35

### Margin resilience

Much of the cost base does not move with price: from the first to the third quarter of 2026 steelmaking revenue rose by USD 690 million while cash cost of goods sold rose by USD 141 million, and depreciation, depletion and amortization is guided at about USD 1.1 billion for 2026 and selling and administrative expense at about USD 575 million. The same asymmetry works in reverse when price falls, so margin swings are larger than revenue swings: adjusted EBITDA fell from USD 773 million in 2024 to USD 37 million in 2025 on a revenue decline of 3.00%, and the last-twelve-months adjusted margin is 4.84%. Management says cost per ton has fallen each year since 2023, but the latest quarterly report cites higher cost per ton in the third quarter from mix and repair expense, so margin resilience is not demonstrated.

### Free cash flow conversion

EBITDA has not converted into free cash flow: free cash flow was USD 1,023 million negative in 2025 and USD 485 million negative over the last twelve months, because capital expenditure, cash interest and working capital absorb more than EBITDA when prices are low and receivables rebuild as prices recover. Capital expenditure is guided at about USD 700 million for 2026, mostly sustaining, cash interest is USD 529 million, and cash pension and other postretirement payments are guided at about USD 125 million, so only EBITDA that stays above this combined burden with stable working capital produces cash for debt reduction.

Competitive rivalry is set by trade policy. The domestic hot-rolled coil price rose 42% from a year earlier to USD 1,195 per net ton on subdued imports, 50% Section 232 tariffs and a 15% fall in imports through August 2026, so pricing power comes from protection and not from a cost advantage, and removal of the tariffs would reverse the earnings recovery that supports cover.

New entrants are a limited threat. Integrated blast furnace capacity is costly to build, and the electric arc furnace capacity that is growing in the United States depends on busheling scrap that averaged USD 465 per long ton in the third quarter against a ten-year average of about USD 400, whereas the company sources most of its iron feedstock from its own mines, which holds its cost steadier.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

For the next twelve to twenty-four months the company expects about USD 715 million of adjusted EBITDA in the fourth quarter and more than USD 4 billion in 2027 on 2027 contract changes and prevailing price forecasts; these are management expectations and would support cover and deleveraging if met. Tailwinds are tariff protection, low imports, the end of the slab contract and the Canadian tariff-rate quotas. Headwinds are the exposure to price, the 2026 labour renewals, the weak Canadian market for value-added sheet products that led to an indefinite idle of finishing operations at Hamilton Works, the USD 1 billion Middletown Works investment of which the company funds half under a Department of Energy framework that is not final, and the option for Stelco to buy a quarter of the MinnTac mine for USD 500 million, exercisable until January 2027. Over three to five years the company points to rare-earth exploration and its electrical steel business, neither of which is quantified.

### Downside scenario

The issuer-specific downside is a return of domestic hot-rolled coil toward its 2025 average of USD 851 per net ton while shipments hold near the 2026 level. Because only 35% to 40% of flat-rolled shipments are on fixed-price contracts, most revenue per ton would fall with the index while cost does not, so adjusted EBITDA would move toward the USD 37 million earned at that price in 2025 and free cash flow toward the USD 1,023 million outflow of that year. As an illustration of scale, one such year would use 31.52% of today's USD 3,246 million of liquidity, and the borrowing base would shrink as receivables and inventory fall, so usable liquidity would fall by more than the cash outflow alone. Management's levers are cutting the capital plan of about USD 700 million, which is mostly sustaining, selling assets and borrowing under the ABL Facility. Liquidity covers at least a year of such outflows by a wide margin, so distress is not indicated within the next year, but the cover shortens if the borrowing base shrinks, leverage would rise well above 7.87x and the June 2028 ABL maturity would arrive with weak coverage. The 2025 outcome also carried the slab contract that has since ended, so the loss could be smaller. The observable triggers are the hot-rolled coil price, quarterly free cash flow and borrowing-base availability.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Cleveland-Cliffs presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, own iron ore and an automotive franchise, but credit quality is constrained by net leverage of 7.87x, interest cover of 1.82x and negative free cash flow. Cash-flow durability appears weak because most volume reprices with the market and costs are fixed. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on holding the 2026 price level through the June 2028 ABL maturity and on the debt reduction management expects, since the 2029 notes follow soon after. The primary downside path is a fall in hot-rolled coil toward its 2025 level while the borrowing base shrinks. Further analysis would require the debt agreements, ratings, customer concentration and a forward model. Top risk: price-driven earnings against a high fixed charge burden.
