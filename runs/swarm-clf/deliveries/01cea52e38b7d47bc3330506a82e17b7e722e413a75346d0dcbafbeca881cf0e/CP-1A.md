## Findings

### Bottom line

Cleveland-Cliffs is an exchange-listed, vertically integrated North American steelmaker with no sponsor identified in the documents, so creditor protection rests on its own financial policy and operating recovery rather than on owner support.
Basis: Source readiness (CP-0); 10-K_2025-12-31_clf-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2025-12-31_clf-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 169

Revenue follows the hot-rolled coil price: about 30% of 2025 revenue came from direct automotive customers and 28% from distributors and converters, and 35% to 40% of flat-rolled shipments are sold on fixed-price contracts, so earnings fell in 2025, when adjusted EBITDA was USD 37 million, and recovered with the 2026 price rise. Debt-funded growth in 2024, then equity and new notes in 2025, extended maturities and cut revolver drawings but leave the company relying on management's plan to repay debt from free cash flow and asset sales that no document quantifies.

### Key findings

The business is a cyclical, price-taking steelmaker whose earnings, not owner support, carry the credit. Adjusted EBITDA was USD 1,893 million in 2023, USD 773 million in 2024 and USD 37 million in 2025 and reached USD 993 million in the first nine months of 2026, while the average hot-rolled coil price of USD 1,195 per net ton in the third quarter of 2026 was 42% above the year before and the highest quarterly average since 2022. Only 35% to 40% of flat-rolled shipments are on fixed-price contracts, so earnings follow price quickly in both directions, and creditors' coverage depends on the price holding. Direct automotive, which the company says earns higher through-the-cycle margins, was 28% of steelmaking revenue in the third quarter of 2026 against 34% for distributors and converters, whose revenue rose 42% on the year, so the revenue mix is shifting toward the distributor market and away from automotive.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_clf-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 93; 10-Q_2026-09-30_clf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47; 10-Q_2026-09-30_clf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 28; 10-K_2025-12-31_clf-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_clf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40

Management's claim of falling unit costs is only partly borne out by the quarterly cost discussion, so margin gains rest on price rather than cost. The report says cost per ton has fallen year over year since 2023, yet the same report attributes a USD 380 million rise in third-quarter cost of goods sold partly to higher cost per ton from mix and repair and maintenance, and a USD 300 million rise over nine months to energy, repair and maintenance and mix. If price falls, creditors cannot assume a lower cost base will cushion it.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_clf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-09-30_clf-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 41

No sponsor or controlling shareholder is identified, so there is no sponsor-willingness question, but share ownership and board control are not established. The documents show a listed company with a combined chairman, president and chief executive role and an auditor's clean opinion on internal control, while directors, pay and share ownership sit in a proxy statement that was not supplied. Without a sponsor there is no sponsor-fee or dividend-recapitalisation channel to read, and also no equity backstop beyond what the public market provides, as the 2025 equity issue showed.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious reading is the speed of the recovery and management's guidance. Third-quarter adjusted EBITDA of USD 612 million was USD 37 million above the USD 575 million guided in July, the company expects about USD 715 million in the fourth quarter and more than USD 4 billion in 2027, and the hot-rolled coil price is far above its 2025 average of USD 851 per net ton. If those expectations hold, leverage would fall quickly without any asset sale, and no sponsor is identified that could extract cash from the company. The view would improve on a signed POSCO transaction with proceeds applied to debt and worsen if the hot-rolled coil price fell back toward its 2025 level, if Stelco's contribution turned negative again after the galvanizing idling, or if the repurchase authorisation were used before debt is reduced.

### Limitations

- The proxy statement was not supplied, so ownership percentages, board independence and related-party terms are not established, which limits the no-sponsor conclusion to what the filings show and leaves open whether any holder has influence over capital allocation.
- No indentures, credit agreement or guarantor schedule were supplied, so restrictions on distributions, repurchases and asset transfers are not established, which means legal capacity for value transfer is unassessed and the view on creditor alignment rests on behaviour alone.
- Customer concentration is not disclosed in the documents read, so the automotive exposure is stated by revenue share only and the durability of the largest customer relationships cannot be judged.
- Management's expectations for the fourth quarter, 2027 and debt reduction are management language and are not tested here, so the recovery view depends on guidance the documents give no way to verify.

### Governance view

Overall, Cleveland-Cliffs presents medium sponsor and governance risk. The key supported behaviour is the combination of debt-funded growth and share repurchases in 2024 followed by an equity-funded repair in 2025, which matters because it raised leverage before earnings fell and shows the company will use equity to cure it, and it implies refinancing risk that depends on earnings recovery rather than on any owner commitment. Countervailing evidence is the clean opinion on internal control, steady quarterly reporting with reconciliations of non-GAAP measures, and no repurchases in 2025 or the first nine months of 2026. Further analysis requires the proxy statement, the indentures and the credit agreement. Governance assessment completed with limitations; sponsor and governance risk level: medium.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Two transactions shaped the balance sheet. The Stelco acquisition closed on November 1, 2024 for total purchase consideration of USD 3,208 million, made up of USD 2,450 million in cash, USD 343 million in shares and USD 415 million of debt consideration, and it roughly doubled the company's exposure to the North American spot market while adding Canadian operations whose results have been weak. The 2025 refinancing then issued USD 850 million of senior notes due 2031 in February 2025 and USD 1,125 million of senior notes due 2034 in September and October 2025, redeemed all USD 685 million of the remaining 2027 notes on October 3, 2025, and used net proceeds of USD 951 million from an equity issue on October 30, 2025 to repay borrowings under the ABL Facility.
