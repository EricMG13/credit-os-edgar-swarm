## Findings

### Bottom line

Precision has an adequate but cyclical credit profile, with moderate leverage and no maturity before October 2028, so creditors face a margin and cash-flow squeeze rather than a liquidity event unless buybacks and capital spending outrun operating cash flow.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B12

Net debt was CAD 606 million, or 1.28x last-twelve-months EBITDA, with liquidity of CAD 462 million, but free cash flow was only CAD 56 million in nine months, EBITDA margins are below last year and the CAD 569 million notes mature in 2029. The primary downside path is a weak winter drilling season while buybacks continue, which at the weakest recent quarter's run-rate would lift net leverage to 1.56x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B2; calculation B1; calculation B13

### Key findings

Business quality is average: scale is real, but earnings are cyclical and only partly contracted. Precision averaged 73 active Canadian rigs and 43 U.S. rigs in the third quarter, 35.96% of the Canadian industry count, but term contracts covered only 43.90% of active rigs and 59.33% of nine-month revenue came from Canada, where seasonality limits utilization.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B12

Margins are the weakest dimension. EBITDA margin was 23.53% in the third quarter against 25.45% a year earlier and 27.70% in the first quarter of 2025, because U.S. operating cost per utilization day rose 14.67% on rig reactivations while Canadian cost per day was flat to lower.
Basis: calculation B1; calculation B9; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4

Cash conversion is thin and falling. Free cash flow was 16.09% of nine-month EBITDA against 28.83% a year earlier, capital expenditures rose to 14.82% of revenue from 13.34%, and cash from operations less capital expenditures was negative CAD 12 million in the third quarter, so little cash is left for debt reduction after buybacks.
Basis: calculation B3; calculation B4; calculation B13; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Leverage and liquidity are the main support. Total debt was 1.33x and net debt 1.28x last-twelve-months EBITDA, liquidity was CAD 462 million, and the company reports senior debt at 0.14x covenant EBITDA against a limit of 2.50x and covenant interest cover of 10.27x against a floor of 2.50x, so near-term covenant and liquidity stress is remote on the reported figures.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13; calculation B13

Financial policy is mixed rather than creditor-aligned. Buybacks were between 62.27% and 83.65% of free cash flow on three definitions against a guideline of up to 50%, while net debt rose and the stated target of net debt below 1.0x EBITDA is not met; the debt reduction target of CAD 100 million has CAD 75 million done.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B12

Refinancing risk sits in 2028 and 2029. The Senior Credit Facility matures in October 2028 and the notes in January 2029 are 89.69% of principal, and the notes' disclosed fair value of about CAD 569 million is close to their principal, a neutral signal, but no ratings, prices or peers are supplied to test market access.
Basis: calculation B5; Financial foundation (CP-1); 6-K-EX-99.2_2026-10-27_exh_992.htm.jsonl, page 11

The tax dispute is the largest contingent claim. The company states a maximum liability of CAD 155 million excluding interest and must pay half, up to CAD 77.5 million, once reassessments are issued, which is 3.34x its cash and 16.76% of liquidity.
Basis: calculation B6; 6-K-EX-99.2_2026-10-27_exh_992.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13

Near-term outlook is constructive and medium-term outlook is not established. Management guides fourth-quarter operating margin per utilization day to CAD 13,500 to CAD 14,500 in Canada and US$9,500 to US$10,500 in the U.S., up 5.33% to 13.13% and 24.13% to 37.20% on the third quarter, but gives no multi-year projections.
Basis: calculation B7; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 7

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against the cautious view is that the company can fund its policy and still delever: liquidity of CAD 462 million, covenant ratios far from their limits, a contract book that grew to an average of 54 term rigs in the third quarter from 43 in the first, customer upgrades backed by long-term contracts, and fourth-quarter margin guidance that is 24.13% to 37.20% higher per U.S. day. Oil prices also rose, with WTI up 32.28% and WCS up 34.65% year on year. The view would improve if fourth-quarter U.S. margins reach guidance, cash is rebuilt while the debt reduction target is met and net leverage moves below 1.0x. It would worsen if margins stay at third-quarter levels, buybacks continue at the current pace, capital spending reaches CAD 285 million, or tax reassessments are issued.
Basis: calculation B7; calculation B8; Business & governance (CP-1A); 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 7–9

### Strengths and weaknesses

The first strength is moderate leverage with ample liquidity. Net debt of CAD 606 million is 1.28x LTM EBITDA, liquidity of CAD 462 million sits against a maturity wall that starts in October 2028, and secured debt is small at 0.14x covenant EBITDA, so near-term refinancing and covenant risk is low.
Basis: calculation B12; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The second strength is market position with a growing contract book. Precision holds 35.96% of Canadian industry rigs and a rising share in the U.S., and average term rigs rose to 54 in the third quarter from 43 in the first, which adds revenue visibility and supports customer-funded upgrades.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B12

The third strength is disclosure. The company prints quarterly statements, a facility-by-facility liquidity table and covenant results, which lets creditors monitor leverage and headroom as events develop.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The first weakness is margin pressure. EBITDA margin fell from 27.70% in the first quarter of 2025 to 23.53% in the third quarter of 2026 as U.S. reactivation costs and international mix offset higher volumes, which cuts the cash available to repay debt.
Basis: calculation B1; calculation B9

The second weakness is thin free cash flow against policy. Free cash flow conversion fell to 16.09% of EBITDA while buybacks took most of it, so net debt rose and cash is CAD 23 million, which leaves the debt reduction target dependent on a strong fourth quarter.
Basis: calculation B3; calculation B12

The third weakness is the 2028 and 2029 refinancing concentration, with the notes 89.69% of principal, combined with an unresolved tax dispute of up to CAD 155 million excluding interest, half of it payable on reassessment.
Basis: calculation B5; calculation B6

### Company description

Precision is an Alberta-based land driller and well-service provider with 184 contract drilling rigs and 150 service rigs, earning 59.33% of nine-month revenue in Canada, 32.09% in the United States and 8.58% internationally, almost all from day-rate and hourly services. Revenue is cyclical and discretionary to oil and gas producers, customers are not named, and term contracts cover 43.90% of active rigs, so cash-flow visibility is partial and debt service capacity depends on utilization and day-rate margins. Variable operating costs and customer-funded upgrades preserve liquidity in a downturn, which supports refinancing confidence, but a mid-cycle slowdown would pass quickly to cash flow.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 8

### Ownership and group structure

Precision is publicly listed in Toronto and New York with no sponsor or controlling shareholder disclosed, so no sponsor support or extraction is evidenced and financial policy is set by the board. Subsidiaries, obligors and guarantors are not named, so structurally senior claims are not established and the secured facility and unsecured notes cannot be ranked beyond the company's own labels. Policy is mixed: up to half of free cash flow before debt repayments goes to buybacks, the company targets CAD 100 million of 2026 debt reduction and net debt below 1.0x EBITDA, and an unexplained director list change and an auditor naming difference reduce confidence in governance facts.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 8

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue follows rig utilization days and revenue per utilization day. Canadian days rose 15.8% and U.S. days 18.8% in the third quarter, U.S. revenue per day rose to US$33,270 from US$31,040 on day rates and technology revenue, Canadian revenue per day eased on rig mix and international revenue per day fell to US$46,304 from US$53,811. Third-quarter WTI was up 32.28% and WCS up 34.65%, which drives customer cash flow and demand. Revenue is therefore cyclical and activity-driven, not recurring, with limited pass-through evidence, which keeps pricing power average and leaves earnings exposed to a customer capital pullback.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 8–9; calculation B8

### Cost structure and margin resilience

The company states a variable operating cost structure with maintenance capital that responds to activity, but cost per day moved against it in 2026: U.S. cost per utilization day rose 14.67% as rigs were reactivated and international crew costs stay elevated, while share-based compensation moved EBITDA by CAD 19 million in the first quarter and a recovery in the second. Margins can be defended under volume growth with reactivation costs absorbed, but under volume pressure the fixed crew and reactivation costs would deleverage EBITDA faster than revenue, as the margin range of 21.43% to 27.70% across seven quarters shows.
Basis: calculation B9; calculation B1; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4, 7, 10

### Capital intensity and FCF conversion

Capital expenditures were 14.82% of nine-month revenue, with CAD 139 million on maintenance and CAD 85 million on customer-driven upgrades, and the 2026 budget was raised to CAD 275 million to CAD 285 million with CAD 143 million of commitments to 2028, so capex flexibility is limited to the maintenance portion and the uncommitted growth portion. Free cash flow of 16.09% of EBITDA falls to 12.52% after lease payments, working capital absorbed cash seasonally, cash interest took 13.44% of EBITDA and cash taxes were small. EBITDA therefore converts only partly into free cash flow for deleveraging, and debt reduction depends on a seasonally stronger fourth quarter.
Basis: calculation B4; calculation B3; calculation B10; calculation B11; 6-K-EX-99.2_2026-10-27_exh_992.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3, 11

### Porter's five forces

Rivalry matters most: Precision's U.S. share is only 7.48% of industry rigs, so it is a price taker in a fragmented market, while its 35.96% Canadian share gives scale but not pricing control. Buyer power is high because producers focus on capital discipline and return, which keeps day rates tied to oil prices and limits margin recovery. New entrants are limited by capital intensity of rig upgrades, and substitutes and supplier power are not evidenced beyond delivery delays and long-lead items noted in the capital budget. These forces cap margin durability and so cap deleveraging speed.
Basis: calculation B12; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 6, 19

### PEST

Political and economic factors are material to credit. The Middle East conflict raised international crew costs and oil prices, tariffs between Canada and the United States are noted as a source of volatility, and U.S. dollar debt is designated as a hedge of the net investment in U.S. operations, which limits the equity effect of exchange rates but not the Canadian dollar value of debt, which rose CAD 23 million in the year. Social and technological factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver on these materials.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.2_2026-10-27_exh_992.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 9; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 13–14

### SWOT

Strengths are moderate leverage, CAD 462 million of liquidity, a growing contract book and quarterly covenant disclosure. Weaknesses are margins down from earlier quarters, thin free cash flow after capital spending and buybacks, notes concentrated in 2029 and a tax dispute. Opportunities that could improve credit are the guided fourth-quarter margin recovery, customer-funded upgrades and contracted rigs through 2029. Threats that could weaken credit are an oil-price or customer capital pullback, international cost pressure, unexpected reassessments and higher interest costs at refinancing.
Basis: calculation B12; Business & governance (CP-1A); 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 7

### Financial profile and credit quality

On a ratings-style lens, scale and market position are average, competitive advantage is average because technology claims are unproven, business diversification is average with three regions but one commodity, cost and capex flexibility is average, margin stability is weak, free cash flow stability is weak, ability to refinance is average with no market data, liquidity is strong in amount but thin in cash, and financial policy and governance is average with mixed alignment. The main supports are leverage of 1.33x and liquidity of CAD 462 million, the main constraints are margins and cash conversion, the trend is flat in net leverage and down in margin, and the missing datapoints are audited fiscal 2025 statements, legal terms and market prices.
Basis: calculation B12; calculation B1; calculation B3

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

For the next 12 to 24 months, management expects North American activity to stay resilient through the end of 2026 with growth potential emerging early in 2027, Canadian Super Series rigs nearly fully used in winter, and U.S. oil-directed activity strengthening, which supports utilization and the margin guidance for the fourth quarter. Egress capacity from LNG Canada and heavy oil demand are tailwinds; customer capital discipline, Middle East costs, margin lag and a CAD 143 million commitment profile are headwinds. A three to five year outlook is not established because the sources give no multi-year projections. Each tailwind supports revenue and margin recovery but does not by itself lower net debt.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 6–8

### Downside scenario

The issuer-specific downside begins with a weak Canadian winter season or lower oil prices that make customers defer drilling, which first idles spot rigs and cuts utilization days in Canada and the U.S. while term rigs, 54 of 123 active in the third quarter, keep earning. Revenue and EBITDA fall faster than costs because crew and reactivation costs are sticky; at the weakest reported quarter's run-rate of CAD 388.22 million EBITDA, total debt would be 1.62x and net debt 1.56x, against 1.33x and 1.28x now. Free cash flow would turn negative if capex stays at budget, so management would need to cut buybacks and uncommitted upgrades; liquidity of CAD 462 million and covenant ratios far from limits suggest the company can avoid liquidity distress, but a cash call on half of the CAD 155 million tax claim would use CAD 77.5 million, and the 2029 notes would face refinancing at higher leverage.
Basis: calculation B2; calculation B6; calculation B12; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 8, 12–13

### Overall credit view

Overall, Precision presents an adequate but cyclical business profile supported by moderate leverage, liquidity and a growing contract book, but credit quality is constrained by margin pressure, thin free cash flow after buybacks and capital spending, and refinancing concentrated in 2028 and 2029. Cash-flow durability appears adequate in good conditions and weak in a downturn due to sticky costs and uneven conversion. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on fourth-quarter margins, the pace of net debt reduction and market conditions. The primary downside path is a weak drilling season with continued buybacks. Further analysis would require audited fiscal 2025 statements, the credit agreement and indenture, ratings and market prices. Fundamental credit analysis completed. Top risk: net leverage rising with thin cash.
Basis: calculation B12; calculation B2; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)
