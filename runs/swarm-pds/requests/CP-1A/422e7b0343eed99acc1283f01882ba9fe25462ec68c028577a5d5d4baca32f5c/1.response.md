## Findings

### Bottom line

Precision is a cyclical North American land driller with no sponsor or controlling shareholder disclosed, so creditors depend on rig margins and the company's own cash discipline, and that discipline is mixed: buybacks continued in 2026 while net debt rose.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 8; 6-K-EX-99.2_2026-10-27_exh_992.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation A8

Revenue is concentrated in Canada at 59.33% of nine-month revenue and the United States at 32.09%, with 43.90% of third-quarter active rigs on term contracts, so earnings follow utilization and day-rate margins. International work is small and contracted into 2027 and 2028. Governance disclosure is thin because the annual information form, ownership and related-party detail were not supplied.
Basis: calculation A1; calculation A5; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8; 40-F_2025-12-31_pds-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 4

### Key findings

Revenue is North American and activity-driven, with limited contractual cover. Canada produced 59.33% and the United States 32.09% of nine-month 2026 revenue and international 8.58%, and term contracts covered 43.90% of third-quarter active rigs; Canadian term rigs earn only 250 days to 300 days of utilization a year because of seasonal access, so earnings swing with activity and weather.
Basis: calculation A1; calculation A5; 6-K-EX-99.2_2026-10-27_exh_992.htm.jsonl, pages 6–7; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 8

Margin pressure is a U.S. execution issue rather than a pricing issue. U.S. revenue per utilization day rose to US$33,270 from US$31,040, but operating margin per day fell to US$7,653 from US$8,700 because six reactivated rigs carried reactivation costs of US$2,125 per day; management guides to US$9,500 to US$10,500 in the fourth quarter, which has not yet been reported.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 2–4, 7

International is small, fixed-term and concentrated in two countries. Seven active rigs in Saudi Arabia and Kuwait sit on five-year contracts into 2027 and 2028 and produced 8.58% of nine-month revenue, but revenue per utilization day fell to US$46,304 from US$53,811 on rig mix and recertification days, and crew costs stay elevated while regional tension persists.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 4, 7; calculation A1

The policy is shareholder-return led with a stated leverage cap, and ownership is public. No sponsor or controlling shareholder is disclosed; the company allocates up to 50% of free cash flow before debt repayments to buybacks, targets CAD 100 million of debt reduction in 2026 and a net debt to EBITDA ratio below 1.0x, and repaid CAD 75 million net of new borrowings in nine months.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 8; calculation A10; calculation A13

Buybacks have run ahead of the stated pace while net debt rose, which makes creditor alignment mixed. Repurchases of CAD 47 million over nine months were between 62.27% and 83.65% of free cash flow on three simple definitions, against the up to 50% guideline, and CAD 31 million of them fell in a third quarter in which cash from operations less capital expenditures was negative CAD 12 million; the company's own free cash flow definition is not shown in the sources.
Basis: calculation A8; calculation A7; calculation A9; calculation A12

Audit governance looks formal but board and auditor facts are inconsistent. The annual report describes a five-member audit committee of independent financial experts, yet one of those directors is listed in the annual report and the first quarter report but not in the second and third quarter reports with no stated reason, and the auditor is named differently in the annual report and in the interim reports.
Basis: 40-F_2025-12-31_pds-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; 6-K-EX-99.2_2026-03-31_exh_992.htm.jsonl, page 10; 6-K-EX-99.2_2026-06-30_exh_992.htm.jsonl, page 13; 6-K-EX-99.2_2026-10-27_exh_992.htm.jsonl, page 13

The company is contesting a Canada Revenue Agency reassessment of intercompany dividend deductions, which is a financial-policy fact to watch. It states a maximum tax liability of CAD 155 million excluding interest, recognises no liability and says it will file a Notice of Objection.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.2_2026-10-27_exh_992.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13

Technology is a management claim, not a demonstrated margin advantage. The company says its Alpha digital platform and EverGreen products differentiate it and cites technology revenue in higher U.S. day rates, but no technology revenue amount is stated.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 6, 19; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-07-28_exh_991.htm.jsonl, page 3

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against caution is operating strength and explicit policy limits. Precision held about 35.96% of Canadian industry rigs against 35.80% a year earlier, and its U.S. share rose to 7.48% from 6.86%, with customer upgrades to 25 rigs backed by long-term contracts and a pending upgrade opportunity that would put multiple rigs under contract through 2029 for a large Canadian producer; the contract book is also growing, with average term rigs of 54 in the third quarter against 43 in the first quarter, and the buyback guideline is capped as a share of free cash flow and tied to a debt reduction target. The view would improve if fourth-quarter U.S. operating margin per day reaches the guided range, buybacks slow to the stated pace and the board changes are explained. It would worsen if repurchases are funded by borrowing, if the credit facility is amended or waived, if further reassessments are issued, or if the audit committee loses members without disclosure.
Basis: calculation A6; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-10-27_exh_991.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 8–10; 6-K-EX-99.2_2026-10-27_exh_992.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

No acquisition, sponsor transaction, leveraged buyout, recapitalisation or new debt issue is stated in the sources for 2026. The financing changes are repayments: the company repaid CAD 75 million of Senior Credit Facility borrowings net of new draws over nine months, and it still has the secured facility and the unsecured senior notes due 2029 outstanding. Capital transactions are customer upgrades to 25 drilling rigs backed by long-term contracts, a pending upgrade opportunity for a large Canadian producer that would have multiple rigs contracted through 2029, and a second five-year Kuwait contract for an existing rig, none of which adds debt in the sources. Share repurchases of CAD 47 million were made under the normal course issuer bid with an automated share purchase plan.
