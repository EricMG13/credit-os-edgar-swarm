## Findings

### Bottom line

Dycom's fundamental credit profile is adequate: liquidity of USD 1,086.5 million, no maturity before April 2029 and backlog of USD 12,242 million support it, but debt-funded acquisitions lifted leverage and a carrier spending deferral is the main downside.

Net debt was 2.73x reported EBITDA at 2026-08-01 against 2.99x at the year end, yet earnings include the acquired businesses only part of the period and cash interest cover of 10.49x is flattered; on run-rate interest it is nearer 6.17x. The Communications margin fell 134 bp in the second quarter while mix lifted the group margin, and free cash flow was USD -54.2 million in the first half.

### Key findings

Liquidity and the maturity profile are the main support. Cash plus the undrawn revolver gave USD 1,086.5 million at 2026-08-01, the first maturity is the USD 500.0 million of 2029 notes in April 2029, and the revolver and term loan A fall due in December 2030, so refinancing is not a pressure within two years; only USD 4.0 million of term loan B amortization is itemized for fiscal 2027. Cash did fall 52.05% from the year end after acquisitions, capital expenditures and buybacks, so the cushion is thinner than it was.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 78; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 19, 21; EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation C23; calculation C24; calculation C26; calculation C43; calculation C25; calculation C45

Leverage rose with the acquisitions and the improvement so far comes from earnings. Net debt was 2.73x and total debt 3.11x reported EBITDA at 2026-08-01, down from 2.99x and 4.00x at the year end, while carrying-value debt was almost unchanged, and the acquired businesses are in last-twelve-months earnings only from their purchase dates. The fall in leverage is therefore real but partly a timing effect, and a full-period figure is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C03; calculation C04

Cover of 10.49x uses cash interest that only partly reflects the larger borrowings; first-half interest expense, net, gives 7.64x, and doubling it gives USD 147.0 million of run-rate interest and cover of 6.17x. About 82.39% of principal is variable-rate and the sources show no interest rate hedge, so each 100 bp rise adds USD 23.4 million of interest.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better credit is that demand is running ahead of capacity: backlog rose to USD 12,242 million with organic book-to-bill of 1.4x, organic revenue grew 16.7% in the second quarter, the issuer guides fiscal 2027 revenue to USD 7.48 billion to USD 7.66 billion with a higher group margin, free cash flow was USD 435.3 million in fiscal 2026, and run-rate interest cover is still 6.17x. The strongest evidence for a worse credit is that all of the margin gain is mix, the Communications margin and first-half free cash flow are falling, and net debt rose in the half as cash funded acquisitions and buybacks.

The view would improve if the quarter ended 2026-10-31, which falls after the supplied filings, shows net debt falling with Adjusted EBITDA inside the issuer's outlook of USD 281 million to USD 302 million and the revolver undrawn. It would weaken on any revolver draw to fund buybacks or acquisitions, a Communications margin below 13.6%, further program deferrals beyond USD 150 million, or a Building Systems margin that settles below the high-teens.
Basis: EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 4, 5; calculation C36; calculation C37; calculation C32; calculation C39; calculation C14; calculation C19; calculation C33; calculation C40; calculation C42

### Limitations

- No peer set, ratings or market data were supplied, so the judgement that the business profile is adequate rests on the issuer's own filings and its standing against other leveraged contractors is not established; a peer comparison could move the profile up or down.
- The issuer does not state how much of the Building Systems margin came from favorable changes in cost estimates, so the normalized margin is not established; a smaller underlying margin would weaken the margin-quality conclusion.
- Run-rate interest is the first half doubled at balances that were close to flat, so it ignores seasonality and any rate move; it supports the view that cash interest cover is flattered but not an exact cover figure.
- The sources split neither maintenance from growth capital expenditure nor working capital by contract type, so how much spending could be cut in a downturn is not established and the downside scenario assumes only that capital expenditure can be slowed.
- The downside is qualitative because covenant definitions and a pro forma earnings base are not available; it shows the order in which pressures arrive, not how far earnings could fall before a covenant is tested.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The main strength is liquidity with a long runway: USD 1,086.5 million of liquidity, of which USD 746.4 million is undrawn revolver, and no maturity before the USD 500.0 million of 2029 notes mean a spending shock can be absorbed without a refinancing. A second strength is visible demand, with backlog of USD 12,242 million, organic growth of 16.7% and organic book-to-bill of 1.4x, and a third is the Building Systems segment, whose 21.1% six-month margin and 19.8% share of revenue add a higher-margin earnings stream. Customers supplying most materials keeps working capital and input-cost exposure lower than for a self-supplied contractor.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 21; EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4; calculation C23; calculation C24; calculation C36; calculation C32; calculation C37; calculation C47; calculation C30

The main weakness is the leverage step-up on a mostly floating-rate base: debt of 3.11x reported EBITDA, 82.39% variable-rate principal and cash interest cover that is flattered, with run-rate cover of 6.17x. Next is customer concentration, 35.8% of revenue in three names with cancellable agreements. Third is margin quality, where the Communications margin fell 134 bp and the group gain is mix, and fourth is first-half cash conversion of 0.14x. A fifth weakness is discretionary cash use, with acquisitions of USD 238.3 million and buybacks while the capacity of the debt documents to restrict them is not established.

### Company, ownership and revenue drivers

Dycom is a United States specialty contractor that builds and maintains fiber, wireless and utility networks for telecommunications carriers and, since December 2025, electrical and related systems for data centers. Revenue is mostly recurring work under multi-year master service agreements, 86.6% of fiscal 2026 contract revenues, but customers can end most agreements for convenience and are not committed to volumes, so backlog of USD 12,242 million at 2026-08-01, of which USD 6,472 million falls in the next twelve months, signals demand rather than secured revenue. Visibility is good for about a year and exposed to carrier budget decisions beyond that.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 9, 49; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 55; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C31; calculation C36

Ownership adds little structural risk. No sponsor or controlling holder is identified, no dividend has been paid since 1982 and sellers received a minority share of the purchase prices in stock, but the company keeps acquiring businesses and buying back shares, so Business & governance rates governance risk medium and the capacity of the debt documents to restrict payments is not established.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A)

Revenue growth is demand-led but acquisition-assisted: reported revenue grew 45.57% in the second quarter against organic growth of 16.7%, so acquired businesses supplied most of the increase. Most contracts price each task discretely, do not let the contractor adjust prices for higher fuel costs during a term, and may not reflect rising costs on renewal, so volume rather than price drives revenue, and growth in the larger Communications segment depends on carriers' multi-year programs.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 26; EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 3; calculation C09; calculation C32

### Cost structure, margins and cash conversion

The group Adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 15.7% in the second quarter, 81 bp above a year earlier, only because Building Systems, with a 24.5% margin, is now 19.8% of revenue; the Communications margin fell 134 bp to 13.6%. The issuer expects a slight decline in the Communications margin for the year, citing investment to scale operations and the effect of deferred wireless revenue, and it attributes part of the Building Systems margin to favorable changes in cost estimates, so margins are being defended in the smaller segment, not the larger one.
Basis: EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 4, 5; calculation C34; calculation C33; calculation C35; calculation C30

The cost base is labor-led and partly fixed in a downturn. Customers supply most materials and keep the financial risk of them, which limits working capital and input-price exposure, but each task is priced discretely, the issuer says it may be unable to pass on higher labor and training costs, and tariffs on steel, aluminum, fuel and vehicles are a stated risk. Field labor and fleet are held for backlog, so a volume fall would be expected to lower revenue faster than cost.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 18, 25, 26

Cash conversion is seasonal and was thin in the first half of fiscal 2027. Operating cash flow was 0.14x reported EBITDA and free cash flow USD -54.2 million, against USD 435.3 million, 61.84% of EBITDA, in fiscal 2026, because receivables and contract assets absorbed cash as revenue grew. Capital expenditure was 4.34% of fiscal 2026 revenue and 3.52% in the first half; the sources do not split maintenance from growth spending, so the amount that could be cut is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 49–50; calculation C07; calculation C13; calculation C11; calculation C14; calculation C16; calculation C17

### Competitive forces and external factors

Rivalry and substitution cap pricing power. The industry is fragmented, with large multinational and numerous regional and private competitors, customers can use their own employees, and master agreements have mostly been awarded through competitive bidding. Barriers to entry appear to rest on skilled-labor scale and customer relationships, but the issuer gives no market share, so its standing against competitors is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 12, 41

Buyer power is high and supplier power moderate. Three named customers supplied 35.8% of second-quarter revenue, four customers hold 44.2% of receivables and contract assets, and agreements can be terminated for convenience. Labor is the key input and cannot always be passed through; materials are mostly customer-supplied, though tariffs on steel, aluminum, fuel and vehicles could raise costs.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 25, 26; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 25; calculation C29; calculation C28

External factors matter because they set customer budgets. Demand depends on carriers' and hyperscalers' capital programs, government infrastructure funding, tariff policy and whether artificial-intelligence-driven data-center demand persists; the Building Systems growth case rests on the last of these. Interest rates matter through the variable-rate debt, where each 100 bp rise adds USD 23.4 million of interest.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 18, 24; calculation C22

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over 12 to 24 months the issuer guides fiscal 2027 revenue to USD 7.48 billion to USD 7.66 billion, now net of about USD 150 million of wireless revenue deferred into fiscal 2028, expects a higher group margin, a slightly lower Communications margin and a Building Systems margin in the high-teens to low-twenties, and guides the third quarter to USD 1.90 billion to USD 1.98 billion of revenue and USD 281 million to USD 302 million of Adjusted EBITDA. These are management's outlook, not evidence, but they imply earnings growth that would lower leverage if net debt stays flat; continued buybacks and acquisitions are the way it would not.
Basis: EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation C39; calculation C40; calculation C41; calculation C42

The refinancing order is the USD 500.0 million of notes in April 2029, USD 1,540.0 million of term loan A and the revolver in December 2030 and USD 800.0 million of term loan B in January 2033, so refinancing capacity depends on leverage falling before the 2029 notes and on continued access to secured and unsecured debt markets.

### Downside scenario

The most plausible downside starts with a large carrier or hyperscaler customer deferring fiber and wireless programs, as about USD 150 million of wireless revenue has already been deferred. Work orders fall while field labor and fleet are held for backlog, so the Communications margin, already down 134 bp, falls further and the Building Systems margin has to carry the group. EBITDA declines, reported net leverage rises from 2.73x, and first-half free cash flow, already USD -54.2 million, stays negative for longer because receivables are collected from carriers that are also slowing spending.
Basis: EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C40; calculation C33; calculation C01; calculation C13

The liquidity effect is bounded by USD 1,086.5 million of liquidity and no maturity before April 2029, and a revenue fall would release receivables and let capital expenditure slow, but about 82.39% variable-rate debt keeps interest running at USD 147.0 million a year and the stated covenant levels are a net leverage ceiling of 4.50x and an interest cover floor of 2.50x on the credit agreement's own definitions. Management levers are capital expenditure, buybacks and acquisitions, and the revolver. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress within two years, but covenant headroom is not calculable. Primary downside path: a carrier spending deferral that cuts Communications margin and cash conversion while buybacks or acquisitions are funded from the revolver.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation C23; calculation C21; calculation C18

### Overall credit view

Overall, Dycom presents an adequate business profile supported by backlog, liquidity of USD 1,086.5 million and a Building Systems growth segment, but credit quality is constrained by leverage that rose with two debt-funded acquisitions, customer concentration and a weakening Communications margin. Cash-flow durability appears adequate but seasonal, with first-half free cash flow of USD -54.2 million. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on earnings growth lowering leverage before the 2029 notes and on financial policy staying within covenants. The primary downside path is a carrier spending deferral against a larger, mostly floating-rate debt load. Further analysis would require a full-period earnings base for the acquired businesses, the covenant definitions and a peer comparison. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: carrier spending deferral.
