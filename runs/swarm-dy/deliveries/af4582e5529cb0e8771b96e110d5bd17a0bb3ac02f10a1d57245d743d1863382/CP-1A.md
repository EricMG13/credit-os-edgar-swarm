## Findings

### Bottom line

Dycom is a publicly traded, customer-concentrated contractor whose creditors face acquisition-driven releveraging and continuing buybacks rather than sponsor extraction, so governance risk is medium and turns on payout limits that were not supplied.

No sponsor or controlling holder is identified and no dividend has been paid since 1982, but funded debt nearly tripled to buy Power Solutions for USD 2,008.7 million, a new USD 150.0 million buyback was authorized on 2026-08-26, and three customers still supplied 50.2% of fiscal 2026 revenue.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 39, 40, 46; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 30; calculation C01; calculation C13; calculation C14; calculation C17

### Key findings

Governance and sponsor risk is medium because the evidence on behavior is good but the legal limits are only summarized. The company has no identified sponsor or controlling holder, has paid no dividend since 1982 and states that it meets its financial covenants, yet it chose debt-funded acquisitions and kept repurchasing shares, and the filings give no basket sizes for payments under the credit agreement or the indenture.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 39, 40, 46, 78; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 21; Source readiness (CP-0)

Customer concentration is the largest business risk but is easing. The three largest customers supplied 50.2% of fiscal 2026 revenue, and three named customers supplied 35.8% of second-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue against 52.6% a year earlier, as other customers including Building Systems grew. Because mass markets fiber revenue moved from Lumen to AT&T in fiscal 2027, the comparable measure is the two combined, which fell from 38.4% to 25.4%. Most contracts can be ended for convenience, so one carrier's budget decision can still move revenue sharply.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 25; calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C03; calculation C04; calculation C05

Building Systems is new and acquisition-built, so its contribution to credit strength is not yet proven. It supplied 20.0% of first-half fiscal 2027 revenue after two purchases, against 1.7% of fiscal 2026 revenue, and its Adjusted EBITDA margin of 21.1% is above the 12.9% of Communications, but no pro forma EBITDA for the acquired businesses is stated and Power Solutions was outside the internal control assessment at the fiscal 2026 year end.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 25, 45; EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 122, 128; calculation C16

The acquisitions were paid mainly in cash, funded for Power Solutions by the larger secured credit agreement and a bridge loan, with the sellers taking shares for the rest. Power Solutions cost USD 1,995.9 million at closing, of which 82.41% was cash, and the total was later adjusted to USD 2,008.7 million; National Technology Integrators cost USD 271.4 million, paid with USD 226.4 million of cash and USD 45.0 million of shares. Secured term loans of USD 1,540.0 million and USD 800.0 million now rank ahead of the USD 500.0 million of unsecured 2029 notes, which adds secured claims in front of the notes.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 76, 99; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 19; calculation C06; calculation C08; calculation C09; calculation C17

Shareholder payments are modest next to acquisition spending but continue. USD 66.1 million of the 2025 repurchase authorization had been used by 2026-08-01, shares were bought at an average USD 359.63 per share in the first quarter of fiscal 2027 against USD 150.93 per share in fiscal 2026, and a new USD 150.0 million program was authorized on 2026-08-26. With the revolver undrawn and covenants stated as met the payments are not straining liquidity today; they would matter if borrowing funded them.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 119; 10-Q_2026-05-02_dy-20260502.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 21, 22, 30; calculation C12

Reporting supports monitoring, with gaps around the new businesses. The auditor reports critical audit matters on revenue recognition and on the valuation of customer relationships acquired with Power Solutions, that business was excluded from the year-end control assessment and National Technology Integrators from the latest quarter's, and the Adjusted EBITDA add-back set changed in fiscal 2027, which weakens comparability.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 70, 128, 130, 131; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 44, 56

Backlog supports revenue visibility but is not committed, and results are seasonal. Backlog was USD 12,242 million at 2026-08-01, of which USD 6,472 million falls in the next twelve months, yet customers are not committed to volumes; the weakest quarters end in January and April, and days sales outstanding eased to 101 days from 108 days a year earlier on a pro forma basis.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 49, 55

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a lower risk level is that no sponsor, controlling holder, dividend or related-party fee is described in the filings read, sellers took shares worth USD 351.0 million of the Power Solutions price, the revolver is undrawn with USD 746.4 million of availability, and covenant compliance is stated each quarter. The strongest evidence for a higher level is that Power Solutions was bought mainly with new secured debt, a second acquisition followed within the year, buybacks continued at a much higher share price and were renewed after the larger deal, and three carriers that can end contracts for convenience still supply more than a third of revenue.

The view would move toward low if the credit agreement and indenture show tight payment baskets and the proxy statement confirms no controlling holder or fee arrangements. It would move toward high if the baskets prove wide, if the next quarterly report shows revolver draws funding buybacks or further deals, or if a controlling holder emerges.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 39, 40, 46, 78; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 21, 22; calculation C02

### Limitations

- The credit agreement and the indenture were not supplied beyond the issuer's summaries, so the sizes of the dividend, repurchase and acquisition baskets are not established; wide baskets would move the governance level from medium toward high and tight ones toward low.
- The proxy statement was not supplied, so holder percentages, board composition, pay and related-party dealings are not established; a controlling holder or a fee arrangement would change the ownership and leakage conclusions.
- No pro forma EBITDA or integration results for the acquired businesses are stated, although pro forma revenue and net income are, so the judgement that the acquisitions are being absorbed rests on reported segment results and could change when a full year is reported.
- Customer attribution changed in fiscal 2027, so only the combined AT&T and Lumen share is comparable across years; the concentration trend for any single customer is not established.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Power Solutions, LLC, a building infrastructure contractor serving data centers in the Washington D.C., Maryland and Virginia area, was bought on 2025-12-23 after an agreement signed on 2025-11-18. The headline price was USD 1.95 billion; at closing USD 1,644.9 million was paid in cash, shares worth USD 351.0 million were issued to the sellers and USD 64.8 million of seller debt was assumed, for total consideration of USD 1,995.9 million. A working capital adjustment of USD 12.8 million raised the total to USD 2,008.7 million. The deal created the Building Systems segment and is the cause of the rise in funded debt.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 99; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 10; calculation C06; calculation C17

The purchase was financed by amending and restating the credit agreement on 2025-12-23: term loan A rose from USD 440.0 million to USD 1,540.0 million, the revolver from USD 650.0 million to USD 800.0 million, and a USD 600.0 million bridge loan was added, with the revolver and term loan A maturing on 2030-12-23. On 2026-01-27 a USD 800.0 million term loan B maturing on 2033-01-27 refinanced the bridge. The only other debt is the USD 500.0 million of unsecured 2029 notes issued in 2021, so all of the period's new debt is secured bank debt.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 76; 10-Q_2025-10-25_dy-20251025.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 19

National Technology Integrators, a low-voltage engineering and construction firm based in Maryland, was bought on 2026-07-02 for a stated price of USD 275.0 million and total consideration of USD 271.4 million: USD 226.4 million in cash, shares worth USD 45.0 million and USD 12.3 million of seller debt assumed. It joins Building Systems and the revolver stayed undrawn. On 2026-08-26 the board replaced the remaining USD 83.9 million of the repurchase authorization with a new USD 150.0 million program running to February 2028.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 30; calculation C09
