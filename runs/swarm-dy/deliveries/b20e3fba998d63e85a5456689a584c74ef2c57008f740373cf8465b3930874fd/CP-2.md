## Findings

### Bottom line

Dycom's credit profile is adequate: scale, backlog and interest cover support its debt, but customer concentration and acquisition-funded leverage leave creditors dependent on continued earnings growth and a still-unproven new segment.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 54–55

Net debt was 2.73x trailing reported EBITDA at August 1, 2026, up from 1.50x at January 2025, with liquidity of USD 1,086.5 million and no bond maturity before April 2029. The ratio is not pro forma for the acquired earnings and covenant headroom is not calculable, so the profile rests on earnings growth continuing while customers hold their programs.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 19–21; EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 4

### Key findings

- Scale and contracted visibility are the strongest supports. Contract revenues were USD 5,545.9 million in fiscal 2026 and backlog reached USD 12,242 million at August 1, 2026, with 86.6% of fiscal 2026 revenues under multi-year master service agreements, so earnings have a visible base even though customers are not committed to volumes.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 54–55; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 49

- Leverage is moderate but not yet proven. Net debt over trailing reported EBITDA was 1.50x at January 2025, 2.99x at January 2026 and 2.73x at August 1, 2026, and 82.39% of drawn principal is secured term debt that ranks ahead of the 2029 notes. The trailing ratio holds acquired earnings for only part of the period, so the fall since year end is partly mechanical.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 19–21; calculation C05

- Cash interest cover of 10.49x flatters the position. Trailing cash interest holds the acquisition debt for only part of the year, and cash interest paid in the first half of fiscal 2027 annualised gives cover of 8.71x, against a covenant minimum of 2.50x that the company reports as met.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation C02

- Free cash flow is the weakest dimension. Free cash flow was 61.84% of reported EBITDA in fiscal 2026, 24.28% in fiscal 2025 and negative in the first half of both fiscal 2027 and fiscal 2026, because receivables and seasonal working capital absorb cash before earnings convert. A supplier finance float of USD 367.4 million inside payables funds part of that cycle.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 29

- Liquidity is adequate but is being spent. Liquidity fell from USD 1,455.5 million at January 31, 2026 to USD 1,086.5 million at August 1, 2026 as acquisitions, a free cash outflow and share repurchases outweighed earnings, with the revolver undrawn and maturing in December 2030, so creditors have cushion but the next acquisition or buyback draws on it.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 19–21; EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation C03

- Customer concentration is the primary downside path. AT&T, Verizon and Lumen supplied 50.2% of fiscal 2026 revenues, agreements are terminable for convenience, and a program deferral worth about USD 150 million of wireless revenue already moved into fiscal 2028; an EBITDA fall of 39.24% on trailing earnings would take net debt to the 4.50x covenant maximum on an illustrative reported basis.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation C01

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better view is organic revenue growth of 16.7% in the second quarter, record backlog, a Building Systems margin of 21.10% against 12.92% in Communications, and a bank refinancing of USD 2,340.0 million of term loans completed within five weeks in December 2025 and January 2026. If trailing earnings absorb the acquired businesses while debt stays flat, net leverage would fall without any repayment, and creditors would be better placed than the unadjusted ratios suggest. The view would improve on pro forma leverage below the January 2026 level of 2.99x with positive second-half free cash flow, and would weaken on a top-three customer cut, a second wave of debt-funded acquisitions or repurchases before leverage falls, or a revolver draw to fund working capital.
Basis: EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 3; EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 8; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 76

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Scale and visibility come first: contract revenues of USD 5,545.9 million, backlog of USD 12,242 million and multi-year agreements for 86.6% of revenues mean fixed work flows continue through a capital-budget wobble, which supports debt service. Second, the capital structure has been extended: secured term debt matures in 2030 and 2033, the revolver is undrawn and the first bullet maturity is the 2029 notes, so refinancing pressure is distant. Third, cash cover is wide: reported EBITDA covered cash interest 10.49x on trailing earnings. Fourth, the new Building Systems segment earns an adjusted margin of 21.10% and no cash dividend has been paid since 1982, which supports deleveraging if cash is retained.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 54–55; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 46

Second, free cash flow is unstable, with conversion of 61.84% in fiscal 2026 and a first-half outflow in each of the last two years, because working capital absorbs cash; the supplier finance float of USD 367.4 million would add to that if withdrawn. Third, leverage has risen since January 2025 on acquisition debt while shares are repurchased and a further USD 150.0 million is authorised. Fourth, secured debt of USD 2,340.0 million ranks ahead of the notes and the new segment's customer base and controls are unproven.

### Company and ownership

Dycom is a United States specialty contractor to telephone companies, cable operators, wireless carriers and utilities, with a Building Systems segment for data-center electrical work since December 23, 2025. It is publicly listed with no sponsor or controlling holder disclosed, so financial policy is set by management rather than an equity owner, and creditors cannot rely on sponsor support or fear sponsor extraction; the missing proxy statement leaves holders above 5% open. The debt sits at the parent with guarantees from substantially all domestic subsidiaries, so the structure is flat and structural subordination is limited to non-guarantor subsidiaries not identified in the filings.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Demand follows telecommunications and data-center capital budgets, with fiber-to-the-home, long-haul fiber and wireless programs the stated drivers. Work is priced per unit under master service agreements awarded competitively, with customers able to terminate for convenience, so revenue is recurring and visible but not committed and pricing power is limited. The risk mechanism is a customer cutting or deferring a program, as the wireless deferral shows, which lowers billable volume faster than Dycom can resize labor; the implication for creditors is volatile earnings around a large backlog rather than structural decline.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 47; EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 5

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Labor and subcontractors are the main costs, customers supply most materials, and tasks are priced per unit, so management states it may be unable to pass labor and training cost increases to customers. Margins can be defended under moderate volume pressure through subcontractor flexing, but operating leverage works both ways: the group adjusted margin rose to 14.6% in the first half of fiscal 2027 on volume and mix, while the Communications margin fell in the second quarter to 13.6% from 14.9%. The implication is that margin, not volume, is the first line to give under wage inflation or a program loss.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 13–14; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 25; EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 4

### Capital intensity and free cash flow conversion

Capital expenditure is mainly fleet replacement and growth, with a fiscal 2027 net outlay guided at USD 210.0 million to USD 220.0 million, so it can be flexed in a downturn. Free cash flow conversion has swung from 24.28% in fiscal 2025 to 61.84% in fiscal 2026 and turned negative in each first half, because receivables and seasonality absorb cash in the January and April quarters. Cash interest, cash taxes and acquisition spending then compete for what remains, so EBITDA translates into durable free cash flow only across the full year and only if working capital normalises. Operating lease liabilities of USD 228.725 million and performance and surety bonds of USD 1,215.8 million lie outside the debt figure, so claims on cash flow are larger than the leverage ratios show.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 72; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 29

### Market forces

Rivalry is high in a fragmented industry with few barriers to entry and some customers self-performing work, which limits pricing and keeps agreements competitively bid. Buyer power is strong because three customers hold half of revenue and can terminate for convenience; supplier power is low because customers supply most materials and subcontractors are small, though skilled labor is the binding input. Substitution through network technology change is a long-dated risk. Policy matters through state and federal broadband spending, which management cites as a demand driver; this is a tailwind to volume but not a credit protection. The credit translation is that bargaining power sits with customers, so margin protection depends on execution rather than market structure.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 47

### Outlook

Over the next 12 to 24 months management has raised its fiscal 2027 revenue outlook to USD 7.48 billion to USD 7.66 billion, with Communications now expecting a slight margin decline because of the wireless deferral and Building Systems a high-teens to low-twenties margin. These are non-GAAP management expectations and are not reconciled to reported results. Tailwinds are record backlog, customers extending contract durations and data-center electrical demand; headwinds are customer program timing, labor cost, integration of two acquisitions and the seasonally weak cash profile of the quarters ending in January and April. Over three to five years, fiber and data-center build programmes support volume, but concentration and acquisition-funded leverage remain the credit constraints.
Basis: EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 5; EX-99.2_2026-05-27_q12027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 47

### Downside scenario

The initiating event is a top customer deferring or cutting a large fiber or wireless program, as the wireless deferral already shows on a small scale. The first operational break is lower billable work in Communications against a largely fixed local labor base, so margin falls before revenue. EBITDA then declines, receivables days rise from 101 days toward the 108 days of a year earlier while the supplier finance float may shorten, and free cash flow turns negative. On reported EBITDA, trailing earnings would have to fall by 39.24% before carrying-basis net debt reached the 4.50x covenant maximum, and cash interest cover would stay above 2.50x until trailing EBITDA fell 76.17%; both are illustrative and not covenant tests. Liquidity of USD 1,086.5 million, an undrawn revolver and discretionary capital expenditure and buybacks are the management levers, so resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress in a single-program shock, while a simultaneous loss of two large customers combined with further acquisitions is not tested here.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation C01; calculation C02

### Overall credit view

Overall, Dycom presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, multi-year contracts and record backlog, but credit quality is constrained by customer concentration, acquisition-funded leverage and weak free cash flow stability. Cash-flow durability appears adequate over a full year but weak within it, because working capital absorbs cash seasonally. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on continued earnings growth and bank-market access, since the first bullet maturity is the April 2029 notes and the secured term loans mature in 2030 and 2033. The primary downside path is a top-customer program cut reaching margin and then working capital while debt stays at its acquisition level. Further analysis would require covenant calculations, pro forma earnings and market data. Credit assessment complete. Top risk: customer concentration.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1)
