## Findings

### Bottom line

Dycom is a concentrated, contract-based telecom construction business now widening into data-center electrical work, and its debt-funded acquisitions and share repurchases, not any controlling holder, are the governance risk creditors should watch.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 51

Three customers supplied 50.2% of fiscal 2026 contract revenues, yet 86.6% of revenues came from multi-year master service agreements and backlog reached USD 12,242 million at August 1, 2026, so visibility is good but not committed. No controlling holder or sponsor is disclosed, and the proxy statement that would show holders above 5% is not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 48; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 49; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 54–55; calculation C01

### Key findings

- Customer concentration is the dominant business risk. AT&T, Verizon and Lumen supplied 50.2% of fiscal 2026 contract revenues, and AT&T and Verizon alone supplied 31.8% of first-half fiscal 2027 revenues, so one customer's capital budget can move earnings and receivables quickly. Agreements are generally terminable for convenience and customers are not committed to specific volumes, so backlog measures awarded work rather than guaranteed work. Wireless program revenue that management defers into fiscal 2028 shows how timing, not demand, can break the quarter.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 48; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 25; EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation C01

- Contract structure gives revenue visibility but limited price protection. Multi-year master service agreements were 86.6% of fiscal 2026 revenues against 79.3% a year earlier, and next-twelve-month backlog is 52.87% of total backlog. Work is priced by unit with discrete task pricing, and management states that it may be unable to pass labor and training cost increases to customers, so margin, not volume, is the exposed line when costs rise.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 49; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 54–55; calculation C02

- Acquisitions have changed the business mix faster than the track record supports. Building Systems supplied 20.0% of first-half fiscal 2027 revenues at an adjusted EBITDA margin of 21.10% against 12.92% in Communications, but it rests on one large acquisition completed on December 23, 2025 and a second on July 2, 2026, and goodwill and intangible assets are now 38.75% of total assets. The new customers are described as highly concentrated hyperscaler and data-center names, internal controls over the first acquisition were excluded from the annual assessment, and purchase accounting is preliminary, so margins and customer durability in the new segment are not yet proven through a cycle.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 25; EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 133; calculation C03; calculation C04

- Governance risk is medium, driven by financial policy rather than control. No sponsor or controlling holder is disclosed, the company has paid no cash dividend since 1982 and intends to retain earnings, and the audit opinions and internal control opinion are unqualified. Against that, management is funding acquisitions with secured term debt and issued shares, repurchased shares in each of the last three fiscal periods, and on August 26, 2026 authorised a further USD 150.0 million repurchase program while carrying debt is about three times its January 2025 level, so shareholder returns compete with deleveraging.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 46; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 83; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 127; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 30

- Working-capital funding behaves like debt but sits outside it. A third-party supplier finance program was introduced in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 and held USD 367.4 million of confirmed obligations at August 1, 2026, and the company also sells receivables without recourse for one customer. Seasonal outdoor work leaves the January and April quarters weakest, so withdrawal of either arrangement would show first in operating cash flow.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 56; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 10

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is the combination of record backlog of USD 12,242 million, organic revenue growth of 16.7% in the second quarter, management's statement that customers are extending contract durations, and a Building Systems margin well above Communications. If those hold while debt stays flat, trailing earnings absorb the acquisitions and the concentration risk is outweighed by contracted visibility. The view would turn more negative if a top-three customer cut or deferred a large program beyond the wireless deferral already announced, if further debt-funded acquisitions or repurchases were made before leverage fell, if the shares issued to the Power Solutions seller were sold in size, or if the new segment's margin fell back toward the Communications level. A proxy statement showing a holder above 5% or board changes could also move the control and governance view in either direction.
Basis: EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 3; EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 5; EX-99.2_2026-05-27_q12027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 4; EX-99.2_2026-08-26_q22027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 30

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Dycom completed the acquisition of Power Solutions on December 23, 2025 for a stated price of USD 1,950 million on a cash-free, debt-free basis, with total consideration later stated at USD 2,008.7 million after a working-capital adjustment. The consideration was USD 1,644.9 million of cash and 1,011,069 newly issued Dycom shares valued at USD 351.0 million, plus assumed seller debt of USD 64.8 million, so the buyer financed most of the price with new secured borrowing, and holders of the 2029 notes now rank behind USD 2,340.0 million of secured term loans that stood at USD 450.0 million a year earlier.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 50; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2025-10-25_dy-20251025.htm.jsonl, page 25; calculation C05; calculation C09

The financing was reshaped between signing and closing. The November 2025 commitment letter offered a USD 1,000 million term loan A, a USD 700 million bridge and a USD 445 million backstop, but the executed structure on December 23, 2025 was a USD 1,540.0 million term loan A, a USD 800.0 million revolver and a USD 600.0 million bridge, and on January 27, 2026 a USD 800.0 million term loan B refinanced the bridge and added cash to the balance sheet. No bond was issued, the 4.50% notes due 2029 remain the only unsecured debt instrument listed, and the new facilities mature in 2030 and 2033, so refinancing risk moved out in time while secured debt moved ahead of the notes.
Basis: 10-Q_2025-10-25_dy-20251025.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-K_2026-01-31_dy-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 76; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 18–21

The second acquisition, National Technology Integrators, was agreed on May 22, 2026 and closed on July 2, 2026 for total consideration of USD 271.4 million, mostly cash from balance sheet funds, with USD 45.0 million paid in shares and USD 12.3 million of seller debt assumed. It adds low-voltage and structured cabling work to Building Systems and, with the first acquisition, shows an acquisition cadence of two deals in seven months, the first funded by secured debt and the second by cash on hand, and neither by new bonds.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-05-02_dy-20260502.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-Q_2026-08-01_dy-20260801.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11; EX-99.2_2026-05-27_q12027presentationmateri.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation C06; calculation C09
