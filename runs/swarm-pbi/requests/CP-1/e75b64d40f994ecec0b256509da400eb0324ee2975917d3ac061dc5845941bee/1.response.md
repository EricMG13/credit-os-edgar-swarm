## Findings

### Bottom line

Net leverage on reported EBITDA fell to 3.25x at September 30, 2026 from 3.77x at year-end 2025, and EBITDA covered cash interest 3.65x, so creditors have moderate headroom that depends on continued debt paydown.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; calculation C61; calculation C52; calculation C62.

Operating cash flow funded the paydown but also had to carry USD 246.32 million of buybacks and dividends in the first nine months, 70.74% of operating cash flow, so a pause in returns or a weaker cash year would stall deleveraging. Adjusted-EBITDA leverage cannot be stated because no supplied document gives the issuer's full-year 2025 adjusted EBITDA.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_ex991q32026earningspress.htm.jsonl, page 9; calculation C63.

### Analysis by dimension

Leverage peaked at 4.44x total debt to LTM reported EBITDA at March 31, 2026, when the 2029 notes had been increased but the 2027 notes were still outstanding, and fell to 3.79x by September 30, 2026 after a term loan upsizing repaid the 2027 notes and a tender retired part of the 2037 and 2043 notes. The fall is debt, not earnings: LTM reported EBITDA slipped from USD 503.26 million at June 30, 2026 to USD 497.69 million at September 30, 2026, because third-quarter reported EBITDA of USD 127.28 million was below the USD 132.85 million of a year earlier and pension non-service cost was USD 8.63 million higher, so further deleveraging depends on cash paydown rather than EBITDA growth.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; calculation C54; calculation C60; calculation C21; calculation C22.

Cover improved from 3.19x in FY2025 to 3.65x on the last twelve months to September 30, 2026 as cash interest paid fell and EBITDA rose, and the cut of 0.75 percentage points in the margin on the 2032 term loan lowers the run-rate further. Both EBITDA and cash interest are consolidated, so the cover blends the finance and industrial perimeters and cannot be read as industrial cover.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26; calculation C53; calculation C62.

Cash flow quality is flattered by working capital and by the run-off of the finance book: nine-month 2026 operating cash flow was USD 348.21 million against USD 161.56 million a year earlier, helped by a smaller payables outflow and by a USD 87.50 million fall in finance receivables, so LTM operating cash flow of USD 569.91 million exceeds LTM reported EBITDA of USD 497.69 million. Receivable run-off is cash the business can use once, and it shrinks the financing revenue line, so the surplus over EBITDA is not a recurring cushion.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation C31; calculation C22.

Liquidity at September 30, 2026 was cash of USD 268.51 million plus stated remaining revolver capacity of USD 427.00 million, with USD 64 million of the cash at foreign subsidiaries, against a current portion of debt of USD 24.73 million and no final maturity before March 2029. Two springing tests tie the term loan and revolver to the bonds: if the 2029 notes are not redeemed by December 14, 2028 and liquidity is below their redemption amount plus USD 100 million, those facilities fall due on that day, and a similar test applies to the convertible notes before May 16, 2030. That definition is not supplied, so covenant and springing-test headroom is not calculable here.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37; calculation C46.

Customer deposits at The Pitney Bowes Bank of USD 647.95 million at September 30, 2026 are a non-debt funding float equal to 34.33% of carrying debt. They stood at USD 653.63 million at year-end 2025 and are repayable to depositors, and the annual report names significant withdrawals as a liquidity risk, so an outflow would have to be replaced by cash or debt; the deposits sit outside Total Debt and outside every ratio above, so those ratios understate claims on the group.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 66; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; calculation C49; calculation C65.

After the balance sheet date the issuer bought back shares for USD 11 million between October 1 and October 23, 2026, which would lift net leverage from 3.25x to 3.27x, and declared a dividend payable on December 8, 2026 whose total is not stated. U.S. pension lump-sum payments of USD 66 million were made from plan assets in October 2026 and a buy-out of the remaining plan is intended before year end; neither is stated as a company cash outflow, and none of these events is blended into the period figures.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 39; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_ex991q32026earningspress.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation C41; calculation C42.

The issuer's own figures differ from the definitions used here in four places, and each is logged: it states only adjusted EBITDA, its income statement shows interest net of the amount allocated to cost of financing, its adjusted free cash flow adds back restructuring payments of USD 35.59 million in nine months, and from 2026 its adjusted measures exclude pension expense that earlier periods carry. The tender offer is a multi-figure event: USD 46 million of notes were redeemed at a discount for a gain of USD 5 million, while the year-to-date cash flow statement shows USD 402.03 million of principal payments, so gross repayment and net debt reduction are different figures.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_ex991q32026earningspress.htm.jsonl, page 9; calculation C50; calculation C64.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an improving credit is that the improvement came from paydown financed by operating cash flow that benefited from finance-receivable run-off, while shareholder returns absorbed most of that cash and reported EBITDA fell year on year in the third quarter. A further buyback programme, a smaller receivable release or a weaker Presort quarter would reverse the move; sustained net leverage under 3.25x with returns below operating cash flow less capital expenditures would support it.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation C63; calculation C44.
