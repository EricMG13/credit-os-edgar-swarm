## Findings

Fundamental credit quality is average: a cash-generative but slowly shrinking mail business has cut net leverage to 3.25x, or 3.27x pro forma for October repurchases, yet creditors depend on a running-off finance book and on a board that spends most free cash flow on shareholders. Cash flow before the finance receivable release covered buybacks and dividends only 1.05x over the last twelve months, leaving USD 17.56 million for debt reduction.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better credit is that leverage fell and cover rose without a distress event: net debt to EBITDA (Reported) fell from 3.77x to 3.25x, or 3.27x pro forma for the October repurchases with the dividend effect not established, cover rose from 3.19x to 3.65x, Presort volume returned to growth of 4% in the third quarter after a competitor's exit, 2026 revenue guidance was raised at the low end to USD 1,830 million to USD 1,860 million, and debt was refinanced with no final maturity before 2029 and a 75 bp lower margin on the largest loan. If operating cash flow before the release stays near USD 404.62 million while returns are held below it, net debt could fall faster than the base case and the credit would merit a stronger assessment. ### Business model and operating profile

The issuer provides shipping and mailing technology, mail sortation and equipment financing to clients that include more than nine in ten of the Fortune 500, and earns its cash flow from long-lived service, maintenance, subscription and sortation contracts and from a finance book. That model supports debt service while installed equipment stays in use, but it weakens as physical mail volumes and meter populations fall, because services and supplies revenue follow the installed base and the finance book follows new equipment placements. Contract terms of one to five years with annual renewals give visibility, and no client exceeded 10% of net sales.

### Competitive forces and sector factors

Rivalry is moderate and pressures price at the margin, because SendTech competes with other mail equipment providers and online shipping platforms and its finance arm competes with banks and leasing companies, while Presort faces regional providers and large mailers that can sort in-house; the consequence is price actions at Presort and a need to keep equipment sales and leases competitive. The threat of new entrants is low in regulated meter and sortation lines because postal authorities must approve many new offerings, but high in digital shipping where platforms negotiate carrier rates, which limits growth. The threat of substitutes is the central risk because electronic communication replaces physical mail, and buyer power is meaningful at Presort, where client losses in the first half of 2025 cut volume; supplier power is meaningful through single-source suppliers and through the postal service as regulator and counterparty.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 14; EX-99.1_2026-05-05_q12026earningspressrelea.htm.jsonl, page 2; Business & governance (CP-1A).

Regulatory and economic factors are material to credit: postal rules and rates, bank regulation of the Utah industrial bank, tariffs on SendTech components (offset by a refund in 2026) and the level of interest rates all change cash flow or funding cost, so floating-rate term loans of USD 878.48 million expose annual interest to USD 8.78 million for each 100 bp. Social and technological shifts are the same substitution risk already described.

Opportunities that could improve credit quality are a lasting Presort volume recovery after a competitor's exit and further repayment from the finance book's run-off. Threats that could weaken it are an adverse postal rule or rate change, faster mail decline, a leveraged transaction arising from the strategic review and an adverse outcome in the Kingsbridge and Trilogy suit.

Business diversification is weak, because both segments and the finance book depend on physical mail and the postal service, and 84.27% of 2025 revenue is earned in the United States.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over 12 to 24 months the issuer guides to 2026 revenue of USD 1,830 million to USD 1,860 million, adjusted EBIT of USD 450 million to USD 470 million and adjusted free cash flow of USD 380 million to USD 420 million, which implies a weaker fourth quarter than the third, and total net leverage covenant levels step down from 4.75x in 2026 to 4.50x in 2027 and 4.25x in 2028. The tailwinds are cost reductions already taken, a lower margin on the 2032 term loan, Presort client wins and the removal of pension volatility; the headwinds are the finance book run-off, higher transport costs, new equipment sales deferred by lease extensions and the expiry of one-off benefits. The net effect is stable earnings and continued paydown only if returns stay below free cash flow.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-28_ex991q32026earningspress.htm.jsonl, page 3; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_ex991q32026earningspress.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; calculation F11.

Over three to five years the 2029 notes, the 2030 convertible notes and the 2031 and 2032 term loans mature in close order, the springing tests bring the 2029 refinancing forward to December 2028, and mail volumes and the meter base keep eroding, so the sector headwind compounds while the finance book shrinks; deleveraging before 2028 is the main protection against a refinancing in a weaker earnings base.

### Qualitative downside scenario

The downside starts with a faster fall in physical mail volumes or an adverse postal rule, which first breaks the Presort network economics because its margin fell 5.94 percentage points on a 4.18% revenue fall, and then erodes SendTech services and supplies revenue as the meter base shrinks. EBITDA falls and the finance book releases less cash, so operating cash flow loses part of the USD 100.00 million release and of the payables benefit; if buybacks and dividends continue at the last-twelve-month rate of USD 387.07 million, net debt stops falling and net leverage rises, to 3.61x after a 10% EBITDA fall and 4.07x after 20% at constant net debt. Rising leverage tightens the stepped-down total net leverage covenant and the revolver, and the springing tests make a refinancing of the 2029 notes necessary by December 2028 unless liquidity exceeds the notes plus USD 100 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; calculation F03; calculation F06; calculation F07; calculation F12; calculation F13.

Management has levers: it can stop buybacks and dividends, which are discretionary and were USD 246.32 million in nine months, let the finance book run off, defer restructuring and use the revolver, and the debt maturities leave time to act. On the figures available, resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress through 2027 if returns are cut at the first signal, but not if returns are held while volumes fall; the observable triggers are a Presort quarter with less volume than the second quarter of 2026, a lower receivable release, revolver use at a quarter end and repurchases above cash flow.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_pbi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pbi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F14.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Pitney Bowes presents an adequate business profile supported by contracted SendTech services, a national Presort network and low capital intensity, but credit quality is constrained by a structurally shrinking mail base, a finance-book release that flatters cash flow and shareholder returns that absorb most free cash flow. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because operating cash flow before the release was USD 404.62 million after capital expenditures, though it is not stable. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on deleveraging before December 2028, on covenant headroom that is not calculable and on lender appetite for notes whose security is not stated. The primary downside path is faster mail erosion with continuing buybacks. Further analysis would require the executed credit agreement, the proxy statement, an industrial-versus-finance split and the Form 10-K/A. Top risk: shareholder returns and mail erosion absorbing the cash that would repay the 2029 notes.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0); calculation F06; calculation F07.
