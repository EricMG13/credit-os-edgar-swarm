## Findings

### Bottom line

Cars.com has no sponsor or controlling holder in the supplied filings, so the creditor question is how the board allocates cash, and the record shows buybacks of USD 159.6 million since the start of 2025 against only USD 15.0 million of debt repaid. Credit risk is moderate and steady while earnings hold, but policy favours shareholders over deleveraging.
Basis: calculation BB1; calculation DP1; 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 38, 55; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10

The operating base is recurring and spread across many dealers, but the audience and OEM media revenue are shrinking, so the 2026 earnings gain rests on cost cuts. A different chief executive signed the 2025 annual report and an 11% workforce reduction followed, which adds execution risk ahead of the November 2028 notes.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 19; 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 98–99; calculation OE1

### Key findings

Customer risk is low and revenue is recurring. About 80% of revenue comes from subscription solutions, Dealer revenue is 90% of the total and no customer exceeded 10% of revenue in any of the last three years, so cash flow is visible and no single customer loss is a credit event.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 91; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 22, 24

Capital allocation favours shareholders over creditors. Buybacks of USD 159.6 million since the start of 2025 are more than ten times the USD 15.0 million of principal repaid, they took 76.39% of free cash flow in the first nine months of 2026, and USD 100.2 million of the programme remains; the company pays no dividend and funds buybacks mainly from operating cash flow.
Basis: calculation BB1; calculation DP1; calculation BB2; calculation FC4; 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 26

Governance is conventional and creditor-neutral. There is a separate non-executive chairman, charter defences against takeovers and no named controlling holder, but board independence, holders above any threshold and executive pay are not established because the proxy statement is not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 39, 95, 99

Management change raises execution risk. A different chief executive signed the 2025 annual report than the 2024 one, an 11% workforce reduction cost USD 8.5 million in March 2026, and a further executive offer letter dated September 2026 gives no role, so policy continuity on buybacks and leverage is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_cars_cars-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 108; 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 98–99; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 19, 30

Acquisitions are small and mostly cash-funded, but one contingent claim is open. DealerClub cost USD 25.3 million in cash in January 2025, with up to USD 88.0 million more payable through 2028 in cash or shares, none of it expensed to date; settled in cash it would compete with debt repayment.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 26

The audience is the operating risk to watch. Average monthly unique visitors fell 10% and traffic 7% over nine months, dealer customers slipped 1% year on year and OEM and National revenue fell 11.18%, while revenue per dealer rose 3%, so price and mix rather than volume carry Dealer revenue.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 22, 24; calculation OE1

Disclosure is good for monitoring but thin on lender terms. Audited statements, quarterly covenant ratios and a stated compliance position are available, while the debt documents, compliance certificates and proxy statement are not, so willingness evidence can be read but legal capacity cannot.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 61–63; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 26

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a moderate, steady view is the pace of shareholder returns: buybacks took 76.39% of nine-month free cash flow, USD 100.2 million of the programme remains and USD 305.0 million of the revolving loan is undrawn, so management could fund further repurchases or the DealerClub earnout with debt, with no sponsor involved. The view would worsen if repurchases were funded by revolver borrowings, if the earnout were paid in cash or if the audience decline reached Dealer revenue; it would improve if repurchases slowed in favour of repayment or the notes were refinanced early. Against that, debt principal fell through 2026, no dividend or debt-funded buyback is documented, and liquidity is ample.
Basis: calculation FC4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10, 26

### Limitations

- The proxy statement is not supplied, so board independence, holders above any threshold and executive pay are not established; the conclusion that no sponsor or controlling holder exists rests on their absence from the filings and would change if a large holder were disclosed.
- The credit agreement and indenture are not supplied, so capacity for restricted payments, debt and liens is not established; the conclusion that buybacks are a policy choice rather than a covenant-bound limit cannot be tested.
- The roles under the December 2025 letter agreement and the September 2026 offer letter are not stated, so the conclusion on leadership change rests only on the change of chief executive signatory.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cars_cars-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 98; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 30
- The settlement mode and likelihood of the DealerClub performance consideration are not stated, so the size of the possible cash call is a ceiling and not an estimate.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cars_cars-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 8
