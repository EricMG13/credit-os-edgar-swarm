## Findings

### Bottom line

Fundamental credit quality is adequate: net leverage of 2.17x, 2.19x pro forma for the dividend payable on 2026-10-15, and interest cover of 9.79x are moderate, but shareholder returns have redrawn the credit facility ahead of a concentrated 2027 refinancing.

Earnings are spread across 11 states and committed availability is USD 1,834.9 million, yet operating cash flow of USD 313.4 million in nine months covered neither capital spending of USD 456.0 million nor shareholder returns of USD 518.4 million. The primary downside path is weaker Las Vegas locals earnings with continued repurchases as the springing maturity of 2027-09-01 approaches, which would leave creditors exposed to board policy more than to operations.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 21; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B1; calculation B3; calculation B6

### Key findings

Leverage is moderate but has been rebuilt in 2026. Net leverage on reported EBITDA is 2.17x at 2026-09-30 and 2.19x pro forma for the dividend of USD 14.2 million payable on 2026-10-15, against 1.61x at the end of 2025 and 2.37x at the end of 2024; net debt is USD 2,377.9 million, or USD 2,392.2 million pro forma. Net leverage on house-adjusted EBITDA, which deducts master lease rent from the issuer's Adjusted EBITDAR, is 1.97x, and Financial foundation states no pro forma figure on that basis. The ratios use carrying-value debt and exclude operating lease liabilities, and the covenant maximum of 4.50x is defined on a lender basis that the filings do not print, so covenant headroom is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 23; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B1

Cash flow no longer covers the spending programme. Operating cash flow of USD 313.4 million in the first nine months of 2026 left a shortfall of USD 142.6 million after capital expenditures of USD 456.0 million, and the trailing twelve months show a shortfall of USD 15.7 million against free cash flow of USD 388.5 million in 2025. The USD 364.2 million paid for tax credits bought in 2025 more than explains the gap; without it nine-month free cash flow would have been USD 221.6 million and cash conversion 87.1% of trailing EBITDA instead of 53.8%. After shareholder returns of USD 518.4 million the nine-month shortfall was USD 661.0 million, met largely by net facility borrowings of USD 625.9 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 58–59; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B2; calculation B3

Earnings are eroding faster than revenue, but the third quarter shows stabilisation. Revenue is flat at USD 3,022.2 million for nine months, yet house-adjusted EBITDA on trailing revenue fell from 32.5% in 2024 to 29.6%, as market access fees fell away and the earnings of Las Vegas Locals and Downtown Las Vegas declined 5.7% and 13.8% in nine months. In the third quarter alone total Adjusted EBITDAR was down 0.7%, with Online earnings up 16.0% as the fee change laps, while Downtown Las Vegas fell 20.5% and Las Vegas Locals 5.1%. Operating income fell USD 22.4 million as depreciation rose USD 65.4 million, which does not reduce EBITDA but does reduce earnings available after reinvestment.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 42–44, 48; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B4; calculation B5; calculation B6

Refinancing is the main credit event and it falls in one window. USD 1,800.9 million of principal, 66.7% of the total, matures in 2027: the facility at its springing maturity of 2027-09-01 and the notes due 2027-12-01, so all USD 800.9 million drawn is classified as current. Committed availability of USD 1,834.9 million covers it only if the term loan is used: USD 800.0 million is a delayed-draw term loan that can be drawn only until 2027-07-01, and revolver availability of USD 1,034.9 million alone would leave USD 34.9 million after retiring the USD 1,000.0 million notes. The issuer states that availability suffices to retire the notes; doing so lifts the floating-rate share of debt from 29.7% to 66.7% and each 100 bp of interest-rate change would cost USD 18.0 million a year against USD 8.0 million today.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 19–21, 61, 67; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B7; calculation B8

Interest cover is high but flattered by timing. Trailing cash interest of USD 111.7 million, which gives cover of 9.79x, includes only quarters after the 2025 repayment, when the facility balance was lower than the USD 800.9 million now drawn. Annualising the third-quarter interest expense of USD 32.5 million gives USD 130.0 million and cover of 8.42x, and retiring the 2027 notes with facility draws at 5.1% would lift annual interest to about USD 133.7 million and cover to 8.18x. Cover remains adequate but is falling as facility borrowing grows, and it would fall further if the facility replaced the fixed-rate notes.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 55, 61; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B9

Financial policy is the swing factor, and a family-influenced board sets it. Shareholder returns of USD 518.4 million were 1.65x operating cash flow, the share count fell 6.7% from the end of 2025 to the end of September, and USD 387.1 million of repurchase authorization remains, limited only by restricted-payment terms whose size the filings do not state. Business & governance rates sponsor and governance risk medium: no sponsor and an unqualified audit, offset by this pace of distributions. Creditors should read the 2026 pace as policy that the board can reverse in one quarter, which is both the risk and the mitigant.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 27, 63; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B3

Structure and claims outside the debt figure matter for priority. The credit facility is secured on substantially all assets other than gaming licenses and could be effectively senior to other debt to the extent of collateral value, while the notes due 2027 and 2031 are unsecured and guaranteed by wholly owned subsidiaries. Undiscounted master lease payments were USD 537.1 million at the end of 2025 and master lease rent was USD 86.4 million in nine months, and the Norfolk project adds USD 750.0 million of spending with USD 275.0 million in 2026. Legal priority and recovery are not assessed here.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_byd_bgc20251121-10k.htm.jsonl, pages 31, 65; 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 23, 26, 62, 66; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B12

Business risk is moderate and competition is stated but not measured. The company competes with other casino operators, Native American casinos that in some cases pay lower or no taxes, online operators and lotteries, and gaming regulation and taxation can change by state. Midwest and South properties produce 56.7% of segment earnings and no segment exceeds 60%, which limits dependence on one market, but the filings give no market share, customer concentration or peer comparison, so the business profile against typical leveraged-credit issuers is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_byd_bgc20251121-10k.htm.jsonl, pages 13–14, 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, page 48; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B4

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a better view is that the 2026 cash gap is mostly one-off and reversible. Without the USD 364.2 million tax-credit payment nine-month free cash flow would have been USD 221.6 million, third-quarter Adjusted EBITDAR was down only 0.7%, the issuer states that availability can retire the notes due 2027, and repurchases and growth spending can be cut: maintenance spending is about USD 250.0 million of a USD 650.0 million plan and USD 325.0 million of the plan is growth and Norfolk. That case wins if repurchases slow after the 2027 notes are addressed, if third-quarter stabilisation holds and if the term loan is drawn or the notes refinanced before 2027-07-01.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 21, 48, 64; calculation B2; calculation B5; calculation B10

The case for a worse view wins if repurchases continue at the 2026 pace while the facility stays drawn, if Las Vegas Locals and Downtown Las Vegas keep falling, or if Norfolk costs exceed USD 750.0 million. A downside in which those two segments lose 10% of fiscal 2025 earnings and the remaining USD 387.1 million authorization is spent on borrowing takes net leverage to 2.65x and leaves USD 447.8 million of committed availability after the notes are retired; that is not distress, but it is thin liquidity just before the springing maturity. The executed credit agreement, the indentures and a refinancing of the notes due 2027 would each move the view.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 26–27; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B11

Committed availability of USD 1,834.9 million funds the 2027 maturities if the term loan is drawn in time. Repurchases and USD 325.0 million of growth and Norfolk spending can be cut. The company is publicly held with an unqualified audit opinion and no related-party leakage beyond compensation.

### Company description

Boyd Gaming operates 26 gaming properties in 11 states, the online business Boyd Interactive and, under a management agreement, the Sky River Casino. Gaming is 65.8% of revenue and the filings call most revenue cash-based, which supports short cash cycles; the loyalty programme, Boyd Rewards, links the properties and the online offer. The business is discretionary and location-driven, so durability depends on visits and spending. For creditors this supports visibility of cash flow and liquidity preservation in a downturn, while the lack of any disclosed customer concentration or market share leaves refinancing confidence resting on the earnings record.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_byd_bgc20251121-10k.htm.jsonl, pages 3–5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 41, 45; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Ownership and group structure

The company is publicly held with the Chairman and her immediate family owning about 30% of the shares and directors and executive officers about 31%, and it has no sponsor. The guarantors of the notes and the credit facility are wholly owned domestic subsidiaries, while the Norfolk entity is a consolidated variable interest entity tied to a tribal gaming authority, with a company option over a percentage of its membership interests that the filings do not size, and the Sky River Casino is managed under an agreement with Wilton Rancheria. The notes and facility therefore sit at the parent and guarantor level, and the filings do not state whether cash at those projects is available to the borrower group. Dividends and repurchases are limited by restricted-payment terms that are not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_byd_bgc20251121-10k.htm.jsonl, pages 34, 39; 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 24–26, 62; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B12

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is mostly slot-driven gaming volume and win, plus rooms, food and beverage and online fees; revenue is not contracted, so it is discretionary and visit-based. Gaming revenue rose 1.1% in nine months on slot win up 1.7% and slot handle up 0.8%, room revenue fell 3.2% on a 1.8% lower average daily rate, and online reimbursements of USD 383.0 million carry no margin. Pricing power is limited in the locals and regional markets, so revenue durability rests on volumes: flat revenue of USD 3,022.2 million with falling Las Vegas earnings means durability is adequate rather than growing.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 43, 45–47; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B4

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Property costs include fixed elements such as master lease rent of USD 86.4 million in nine months, so a revenue shortfall can flow quickly into earnings: gaming margin fell to 60.5% from 61.1%, room margin to 57.9% from 59.8% and corporate expense rose to USD 76.7 million from USD 71.4 million. House-adjusted EBITDA margin on trailing revenue is 29.6% against 32.5% in 2024. Margins can be defended only with cost action; the filings describe no cost programme, so margin pressure is a live downside.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4, 45–47, 48; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B4

### Capital intensity and cash flow conversion

Capital intensity is high: maintenance spending is about USD 250.0 million a year, with USD 75.0 million for hotel renovation, USD 50.0 million of growth projects and USD 275.0 million for Norfolk in 2026, a USD 650.0 million plan of which USD 456.0 million was spent in nine months. Cash conversion of trailing EBITDA fell from 92.9% in 2025 to 53.8%, or 87.1% without the tax-credit payment. Capex is flexible beyond maintenance, so free cash flow can recover if growth spending is paused, but Norfolk is committed until it opens in November 2027.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 58, 64; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B2; calculation B10

### Porter's five forces

Competitive rivalry is the force that matters: properties compete with other operators in each state and in adjacent states, and expansion by competitors or new openings could reduce volumes. Licensing requirements limit new entrants, but Native American casinos and online operators already compete, and some competitors pay lower or no taxes. Substitutes include online gaming and lotteries; buyer power looks high because customers can choose among nearby casinos and the filings describe loyalty rewards as the retention tool; supplier power is not a stated credit factor. The net effect is limited pricing power and margin pressure rather than a collapse in volumes.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_byd_bgc20251121-10k.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 27

### PEST factors

Regulation and tax are the only PEST factors that materially affect credit: gaming licenses require continued suitability, tax changes and proposals for a federal tax on casino revenues could reduce earnings, and licenses are excluded from the collateral of the credit facility. Macroeconomic, social and technological factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver based on the provided materials, beyond the visit and spending sensitivity already described.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_byd_bgc20251121-10k.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, page 23

### SWOT

Strengths are the spread of earnings across 11 states, moderate leverage, committed liquidity and discretion over spending. Weaknesses are shareholder returns above cash generation, the 2027 maturity concentration and falling Las Vegas earnings. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are the opening of Norfolk in November 2027, the Cadence Crossing replacement of Jokers Wild and Boyd Interactive revenue growth of USD 14.2 million. Threats that could weaken it are a Las Vegas locals downturn, a failed or costly refinancing of the notes due 2027, a Norfolk cost overrun and tax or regulatory change.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 34, 43, 64; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Financial profile and credit quality

On a ratings-style lens the profile is adequate: leverage is moderate at 2.17x (2.19x pro forma), cover is 9.79x and falling to 8.42x on a run-rate basis, liquidity is committed but time-limited, access to markets is demonstrated by the 2019 and 2021 note issues and the January 2026 facility, and financial policy is the weakest dimension. The trend is up for leverage and down for cash conversion. Missing datapoints are the covenant ratios, the lender definition of EBITDA and the restricted-payment capacity.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_byd_bgc20251121-10k.htm.jsonl, pages 116–117; 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, page 20; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B1; calculation B3; calculation B9

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

For the next 12 to 24 months the headwinds are the refinancing window to 2027-09-01, higher floating-rate exposure, lower market access fees and Las Vegas weakness; tailwinds are third-quarter stabilisation, the lapping of the online fee change, the Norfolk opening in November 2027 and discretionary capital spending. Over three to five years the 2031 notes of USD 900.0 million are the next maturity and the master leases run to 2031-04-30; the filings give no sector forecast or management projection, so the medium-term outlook is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_byd_bgc20251121-10k.htm.jsonl, page 116; 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 25, 47–48; calculation B5; calculation B7

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a Las Vegas locals downturn in which Las Vegas Locals and Downtown Las Vegas lose 10% of fiscal 2025 earnings, a loss of USD 50.1 million, while the board spends the remaining USD 387.1 million repurchase authorization on facility borrowing. The first operating break is lower gaming volume against fixed property costs and master lease rent, which cuts trailing reported EBITDA to USD 1,044.0 million. Net leverage rises from 2.17x to 2.28x on the earnings loss alone and to 2.65x with the borrowing; retiring the notes due 2027 with facility draws leaves USD 447.8 million of committed availability, interest rises to about USD 154.8 million and cover falls to 6.75x. Management levers are stopping repurchases, deferring growth spending of USD 325.0 million and drawing the term loan before 2027-07-01. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress, but only if the term loan is drawn or the notes are refinanced before its availability lapses.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_byd_bgc20260930-10q.htm.jsonl, pages 21, 27, 61; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B11

### Overall credit view

Overall, Boyd Gaming presents an adequate business profile supported by earnings spread across 11 states, moderate leverage and committed liquidity, but credit quality is constrained by a shareholder-return pace above cash generation and a concentrated 2027 refinancing. Cash-flow durability appears adequate: conversion of EBITDA to operating cash flow was 92.9% in 2025 and 87.1% in the trailing year before the tax-credit payment. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on drawing the term loan or refinancing the notes before 2027-07-01 and on the board holding back repurchases. The primary downside path is weaker Las Vegas locals earnings combined with continued repurchases. Further analysis would require the executed credit agreement, the indentures, the proxy statement and a management refinancing plan. Fundamental credit analysis completed. Top risk: the 2027 refinancing window.
