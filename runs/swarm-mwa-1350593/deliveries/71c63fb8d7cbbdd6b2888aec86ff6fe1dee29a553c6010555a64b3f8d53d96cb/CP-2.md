## Findings

### Bottom line

Mueller Water's fundamental credit profile is strong: total debt is 1.34x reported EBITDA and liquidity is 1.46x the notes, so creditors face little near-term default risk, while price-led margins, two customers at 37% of sales and one 2029 maturity are the constraints.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation M2; calculation M23

Reported EBITDA was USD 337.2 million for the twelve months to 2026-06-30, net debt is negative USD 42.4 million (negative USD 31.5 million pro forma for the dividend declared on 2026-07-28) and liquidity is USD 659.0 million against USD 450.0 million of notes due 2029-06-15. A return to FY2022 earnings would lift total debt to 2.57x without threatening liquidity.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation M1; calculation M3; calculation M4; calculation M19

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against the strong view is that the earnings base is price-led and partly non-recurring: an incremental margin of 74.17% on 15.96% sales growth, unquantified third-quarter tariff refunds, guidance that implies a softer fourth quarter, and free cash flow at 59.14% of adjusted net income against guidance of above 70%. It would weaken if fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA lands below USD 90.3 million, if free cash flow stays below 70% of adjusted net income, if the unnamed transaction is debt funded, or if either large customer is lost. ### Company description

Mueller Water makes the valves, hydrants, pipe repair products, gas products, metering systems and leak detection products that water utilities use to move and measure water in the United States and Canada, with FY2025 net sales of USD 1,429.7 million split 57.70% Water Flow Solutions and 42.30% Water Management Solutions. Municipal repair and replacement drives 60% to 65% of sales and utilities specify the products from a large installed base, so cash flow has more visibility than most industrial manufacturers; but 91.89% of sales are in the United States and two customers hold 37% of gross sales, so that visibility rests on one country and a few channel relationships. The seasonal pattern leaves the first half weakest, which is why the undrawn facility matters for liquidity preservation.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-09-30_mwa-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8; 10-K_2025-09-30_mwa-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 15; calculation M10; calculation M9

### Ownership and group structure

No sponsor or controlling holder is named, so no owner can extract value or step in with support, and policy is set by the board: dividends and repurchases took 43.67% of free cash flow in nine months 2026 and none was debt funded. Substantially all U.S. subsidiaries borrow under the facility and guarantee the notes, which rank behind secured facility borrowings on U.S. inventory and receivables, and the put at 101% on a change of control applies only with a ratings decline whose definition is not supplied, so takeover protection for noteholders is thin; legal capacity for distributions and added debt is not established.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-06-30_mwa-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15; calculation M9

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is predictable in its municipal core and cyclical at the edges. Water and sewerage rates rose 4.6% in the twelve months to 2025-09-30, which supports replacement budgets, while total housing starts fell 1.1% in FY2025 with single-family starts down 5.2%, and management expects a slowdown in new residential construction. Nine-month 2026 net sales grew 4.73% on pricing with slightly lower volumes, so pricing power is demonstrated but volume growth is not, and the backlog of USD 320.7 million, 6.02% above a year earlier, gives some visibility; pass-through works through price actions that the issuer says only mostly offset tariff costs.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

A significant portion of costs is fixed, so margins depend on volume and price. Purchased parts are about 35% and raw materials about 8% of cost of sales, tariffs add about 3% of cost of sales and inflation about 4%, and 42% of the United States workforce is under agreements expiring between December 2025 and October 2027. The EBITDA margin rose from 14.60% to 22.79% with a 74.17% incremental margin, and falling back to FY2022 sales would remove EBITDA at 69.51% of the lost sales, so margins can be defended only while pricing holds and volume does not fall far.

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a volume shortfall: slower residential construction and municipal deferral coincide with a distributor destocking or repricing, while tariffs and about 4% inflation keep costs rising. The first operational break is the price-cost spread, because fixed foundry and labour costs do not flex, and EBITDA then falls at about 69.51% of lost sales; FY2022 is the supplied precedent, with net sales 15.68% below the latest twelve months and EBITDA 47.81% below.
Basis: 10-K_2024-09-30_mwa-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 94–95; 10-K_2025-09-30_mwa-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 34; calculation M12; calculation M17; calculation M18

Financial transmission runs through working capital. In FY2022 inventories absorbed USD 98.3 million, free cash flow was negative USD 2.4 million and USD 71.5 million was still returned, so cash fell USD 81.0 million. Applied to today's debt, FY2022 EBITDA of USD 176.0 million gives total debt of 2.57x and covers the note coupon 9.78x, and a cash fall of that size leaves liquidity of USD 578.0 million, USD 128.0 million above the notes, so resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress; this is a precedent-based illustration, not a default case.
Basis: 10-K_2024-09-30_mwa-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 95; calculation M21; calculation M7; calculation M19; calculation M20; calculation M22

Management levers are repurchases, which fell from USD 35.0 million to USD 10.0 million after FY2022, possible deferral of capital expenditures, whose flexibility cannot be measured, and the undrawn facility. The observable triggers are a guidance cut, backlog below USD 320.7 million and free cash flow below 70% of adjusted net income, and the scenario worsens materially only if the unnamed transaction adds debt or the facility is not extended before it ends 91 days ahead of the notes.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Mueller Water presents a strong business and financial profile supported by low leverage, USD 659.0 million of liquidity and entrenched municipal demand, but credit quality is constrained by price-led margins, two customers at 37% of gross sales and weakening cash conversion. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because municipal replacement anchors demand while earnings and cash conversion rest on pricing. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on whether cash is kept against the notes, which it covers 1.10x, and on extending the facility before 2029-03-16. The primary downside path is a price-led margin reversal with a customer loss and an inventory-driven cash drain, which at FY2022 earnings would leave total debt at 2.57x. Against typical leveraged credit issuers the financial profile looks stronger than the business profile, but that comparison is qualitative because no peer set is supplied. Further analysis would require the fiscal fourth quarter, customer terms, the executed debt documents and peer data. Fundamental credit completed with limitations. Top risk: reversal of price-led margins with two customers at 37% of sales.
