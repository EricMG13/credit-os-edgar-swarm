## Findings

### Bottom line
Kodiak's sponsor has fully sold out and its fixed-fee compression contracts carry USD 2.6 billion of remaining revenue, so creditors now rely on management's financial policy, which funds both shareholder payouts and a large move into power generation.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 63; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 37

Contract terms, investment-grade customers and a utilization rate of 98.2% support the compression cash flows, but the power business has one quarter of history and its leases sit outside the remaining-revenue figure. Shareholder payouts of USD 263.5 million in 2025 exceeded issuer-defined free cash flow of USD 229.6 million, and the executed credit documents that would show how much further the company may distribute were not supplied.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 61; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_kgsq22026earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 77–78; 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 102; 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 128; calculation C01

### Key findings

- The compression business is the credit anchor: customers pay a fixed monthly fee under terms of one to seven years, only 9.0% of revenue-generating horsepower is on month-to-month terms, and remaining contract revenue grew from USD 1.6 billion at the end of 2025 to USD 2.6 billion at June 30, 2026. Contract revenue visibility for compression is therefore multi-year, although it excludes the power leases.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 117; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 17; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_kgsq22026earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 6
- Concentration is the main business risk: the four largest customers, all investment-grade, supplied about 30% of revenue in the first half of 2026, and 82.8% of compression assets sit in the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford Shale, so a regional drilling downturn or the loss of one large customer would hit utilization of 98.2% with little idle fleet to cushion it.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 61; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_kgsq22026earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 6
- The power acquisition changes the business model more than the balance sheet: the purchase of Distributed Power Solutions closed on April 1, 2026 for consideration of USD 726.4 million, power revenue was USD 32.9 million in the second quarter, 8.4% of revenue, and the one disclosed lease has an initial term ending April 1, 2028. Power revenue is lease-based, runs from short-term to multi-year and sits outside the remaining-revenue figure, while purchase commitments of USD 2.5 billion include a multi-year Baker Hughes power generation arrangement and the company announced a one-gigawatt turbine order for delivery by 2030, so the build-out has to be financed before it earns.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 37; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_kgsq22026earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 2; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_kgsq22026earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 6; calculation C04
- The sponsor is gone, which removes sponsor extraction and sponsor support at the same time: the EQT AB affiliates held about 43.1% at the start of 2025 and nothing from December 2, 2025, and the company itself bought back USD 93.3 million of its shares from them during 2025. Who now controls the company is not established: the supplied files name no holder, and the proxy statement was not supplied.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 63; 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 128; 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 89; calculation C02

In 2026 the company paid USD 92.6 million of dividends in the first half and raised USD 836.1 million of equity in May 2026, more than the cash paid for the acquisition, which limited the rise in debt, while the remaining repurchase authorization of USD 31.7 million at the end of 2025 expires on December 31, 2026.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a lower-risk view is that payouts were covered by discretionary cash flow, the share issue raised more than the cash price of the acquisition, the 2029 notes were replaced by 2031 notes at a lower coupon with no collateral release, priming or exchange, and the customers are investment-grade. That evidence would win if the third quarter 2026 report shows free cash flow recovering as power capital spending is delivered and no new debt-funded distribution or acquisition.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 77; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 61

The view would move the other way if the company authorizes a larger repurchase program, funds power growth with the credit facility rather than equity or cash flow, signs power contracts materially shorter than its build-out financing, or discloses a loss from the Mexican matter; any one of these would change the sponsor-and-governance read from mixed to adverse.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 30; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_kgsq22026earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 6

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The power acquisition is the transaction that matters most for creditors. Kodiak agreed on February 5, 2026 to buy Distributed Power Solutions in an equity and cash deal valued at about USD 675.0 million at signing, and closed on April 1, 2026 with consideration of USD 726.4 million, made up of cash of USD 587.3 million and 2.4 million shares worth USD 139.0 million at the closing price; the purchase price allocation is preliminary and goodwill of USD 344.8 million may change. The agreement carried a termination fee of USD 37.1 million payable by Kodiak under certain circumstances.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 69; 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 139; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13

Financing moved in several steps. In September 2025 the company issued USD 1.2 billion of senior unsecured notes due 2033 and 2035, topped up by USD 200.0 million, and amended its asset-based facility to USD 2.0 billion with a September 2030 maturity. On March 20, 2026 it issued USD 1.0 billion of senior unsecured notes due 2031 and redeemed the 7.25% notes due 2029 on April 10, 2026 at a premium that cost USD 27.2 million. On May 13, 2026 it sold shares in an offering that closed on May 15, 2026 for net proceeds of USD 836.1 million, and on April 2, 2026 it amended the asset-based facility again to change how leverage is calculated.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 121; 10-K_2025-12-31_kgs-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 123–124; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 21–23; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 38

Events after the quarter are dated and kept out of the June 30, 2026 position: in July 2026 the company bought out leased compression equipment of about 43,000 horsepower for about USD 32.8 million, and on August 5, 2026 it declared a dividend of USD 0.49 per share payable on August 27, 2026.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-08-06_kgsq22026earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-06-30_kgs-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 29
