## Findings

### Bottom line

Trinet is adequate for creditors near term but exposed on earnings: debt service is well covered and nothing borrowed matures before 2029, yet earnings are an insurance spread on a shrinking client base, so repayment depends on claims discipline.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, pages 25–26; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 28; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

Net debt of USD 568 million was 1.58x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA at 2026-09-30, and USD 581 million or 1.62x pro forma for the October 2026 dividend, with cash interest cover of 7.33x. Free cash flow equalled shareholder payments in FY2025, so net debt falls only while buybacks stay below free cash flow. Covenant headroom is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, pages 94–95; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01; calculation C18

### Key findings

Debt service capacity is strong and the maturity profile is clean, but the first test is the sequence of refinancing. Net debt of USD 568 million is 1.58x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA of USD 359 million, or USD 581 million and 1.62x pro forma for the October 2026 dividend, and cash interest cover is 7.33x. Both notes are fixed rate and senior unsecured, and the first maturity is USD 500 million in March 2029. The USD 700 million revolver has no borrowings but matures in August 2028, before those notes, so it must be extended or replaced to remain a backstop when the notes turn current; its maturity date is stated only in the 2024 annual report.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, pages 25–26; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 28; 10-K_2024-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 126; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01

Earnings are the weakest dimension because they are an insurance spread, not a service fee. Adjusted EBITDA fell from USD 697 million in FY2023 to USD 425 million in FY2025, a fall of 39.02%, while the insurance cost ratio rose from 84% to 91%, and each point of that ratio is worth USD 42.24 million on FY2025 insurance service revenues. The ratio of 86% for the nine months to 2026-09-30 includes a one-time recovery of USD 21 million, the third quarter ratio was 89%, Adjusted EBITDA in the third quarter was USD 89 million against USD 100 million a year earlier and the last-twelve-months ratio was 88.29%, so the recovery to USD 460 million of last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA is not yet a trend.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 71; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 74; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 93; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 11; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C01; calculation C03; calculation C16; calculation C04

Revenue durability is weak because the client base is shrinking and can leave on short notice. Total worksite employees were 10.99% below a year earlier at 2026-09-30, mainly in technology and professional services clients and partly because of health benefit repricing, and total revenues fell 4.86% in the nine months despite 8% higher monthly revenue per worksite employee. Clients can generally cancel on 30 days' notice, and five states account for about 64% of paid wages, which concentrates both revenue and insurance pricing. No client exceeded 10% of revenue in 2023 to 2025 and bad debt was USD 3 million a year, so the risk is broad attrition, not the loss of one large client.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 112; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C15

Cash conversion is good, but shareholder payments, not operations, decide whether leverage falls. Free cash flow was USD 234 million in FY2025 against shareholder payments of USD 235 million, and USD 243 million against USD 134 million in the nine months to 2026-09-30, so payments took 100.43% of free cash flow in FY2025 and 55.14% in 2026. The board added USD 336 million of repurchase authority in February 2026 and USD 311 million remained at 2026-09-30, and the revolver's limits on payments to stockholders cannot be read, so the pace is a board choice that no readable term constrains.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 83; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, pages 94–95; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 29; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C01; calculation C18

Interest income was 19.76% of FY2025 reported EBITDA and 14.76% over the last twelve months, and it fell to USD 39 million in the nine months to 2026-09-30 from USD 53 million because refund interest and average balances fell. Receivables from the IRS for employee retention credits fell from USD 831 million at FY2024 to USD 310 million at 2026-09-30, so part of the shrinking client balances is refunds being processed, not clients leaving, which qualifies the Financial foundation's reading of the fall as a shrinking funding float; the filings do not say how much. Refinancing the 2029 notes will probably cost more but looks manageable. ### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against the earnings concern is the 2026 recovery: nine-month operating cash flow was USD 317 million against USD 242 million, the insurance cost ratio was 86% against 90%, management calls the reset of health benefit rates substantially complete, and net debt was 1.58x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA against 1.79x at FY2025, with USD 581 million and 1.62x pro forma for the October 2026 dividend. If the ratio holds between 86% and 89% for four more quarters while worksite employees stabilise, earnings stability would move from weak to average and leverage would fall without a change in policy. The case is weaker than it looks: the nine-month ratio includes the USD 21 million one-time recovery, the third quarter ratio was 89%, USD 43 million of the USD 75 million rise in operating cash flow came from smaller working-capital outflows that can reverse, and the client base was still 10.99% smaller than a year earlier.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 29; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01; calculation C19

The view would weaken if the insurance cost ratio returned to the FY2025 level of 91% from the 88.29% implied for the last twelve months: that is worth USD 111 million, or 30.92% of last-twelve-months reported EBITDA, and would take net leverage to 2.29x, or 2.34x on USD 581 million pro forma for the October 2026 dividend, and cash interest cover to 5.06x before any repricing offset. It would also weaken if shareholder payments exceeded free cash flow again, if carrier collateral rose above USD 299 million, if the revolver were drawn, or if another debt-funded repurchase appeared. It would strengthen if the debt documents showed tight limits on payments to stockholders, or if the covenant level and letters of credit showed wide headroom.
Basis: calculation C04; calculation C05; calculation C07; calculation C08; calculation C02

### Strengths and weaknesses

The strongest support is low, fixed-rate leverage with a distant first maturity. Net debt of USD 568 million is 1.58x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA, or USD 581 million and 1.62x pro forma for the October 2026 dividend, all debt is two unsecured fixed-rate notes, and the first maturity is USD 500 million in March 2029. Rate rises do not lift interest cost while the revolver is undrawn, and an earnings shock has more than two years before a refinancing, which lowers the probability of default over that period.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, pages 25–26; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 28; Financial foundation (CP-1)

A second support is cash conversion. Free cash flow was USD 286 million over the last twelve months after USD 49 million of cash interest, so debt service is covered comfortably and leverage can fall quickly if buybacks slow, which supports deleveraging capacity.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 29; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01

A third support is that client money does not fund the company. Clients prefund payroll and related taxes, and the USD 941 million of payroll funds collected and USD 40 million held in the trust for administrative services users at 2026-09-30 are restricted and matched by liabilities to clients and tax authorities, so working capital does not draw on corporate cash and the USD 328 million of unrestricted cash is available for debt service; pro forma cash after the October 2026 dividend is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, pages 21–22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 28; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 35; Source readiness (CP-0); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01

A fourth support is a simpler structure. The revolver was repaid in 2024 and 2025, the two notes are the only debt, and the 2023 debt-funded repurchase has not been repeated, which removes floating-rate exposure and leaves the next test to 2028 and 2029.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 83; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 115; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 119; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The weakest dimension is earnings stability. Adjusted EBITDA fell USD 272 million from FY2023 to FY2025 as the insurance cost ratio rose from 84% to 91%, and each point is worth USD 42.24 million, so a return to the FY2025 ratio would remove USD 111 million, or 30.92%, of last-twelve-months reported EBITDA, and fees fixed in advance leave no time to react.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 71; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 74; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C03; calculation C05; calculation C16

A second weakness is a shrinking client base with short contracts. Total worksite employees were 10.99% lower year on year, clients can cancel on 30 days' notice, and the repricing that lifts margin contributed to the losses, so revenue has to be won back at the same time as margin is defended.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 16; Business & governance (CP-1A)

A third weakness is that shareholder payments absorb free cash flow at the board's discretion. Payments of USD 235 million matched free cash flow of USD 234 million in FY2025, the largest stockholder holds about 38%, and the limits in the debt documents are not readable, so leverage is a policy variable, not a result.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 62; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, pages 94–95; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C18

A fourth weakness is that part of EBITDA is yield on client funds. Interest income was 19.76% of FY2025 reported EBITDA and fell to USD 39 million from USD 53 million in nine months, so lower client balances or short-term rates reduce EBITDA without any change in operations.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 93; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 27; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01

### Company and ownership

Trinet is a co-employment professional employer organization and administrative services provider for small and medium-size businesses, and its cash flow follows insurance claims experience more than service volume. Insurance service revenues were 84.31% of FY2025 revenue and are billed at rates set before the benefit period, professional service fees are paid per worksite employee, and interest is earned on client funds. Prefunding by clients supports liquidity, but claims arrive after the price is fixed and clients can leave on 30 days' notice, so cash-flow visibility is short.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 74; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Control rests on board discretion rather than on terms a creditor can read. An Atairos affiliate holds about 38% and may nominate a director while it holds 15% or more, and directors and officers with affiliates hold about 39% in total. In 2023 the company repurchased about USD 1,000 million of shares with USD 600 million of new borrowings, which lifted debt by USD 597 million in the year; the revolver was repaid by July 2025. The guarantors of the notes and the revolver borrower are not named, so how the notes rank against claims at operating subsidiaries, including client-fund and carrier obligations, is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 88; 10-K_2024-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 126; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 62; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 115; Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)

### Revenue durability

Revenue is recurring in form but falling in practice. Professional service revenues were 14.11% and insurance service revenues 84.80% of nine-month 2026 revenue, and both follow volume: total worksite employees fell 10.99% year on year and nine-month revenue fell 4.86%, with 8% higher monthly revenue per worksite employee offsetting only part of the loss. Clients showed modest staffing reductions in every vertical in 2025 and net hiring was low, so same-client volume is not a source of growth. Revenue will keep falling unless attrition slows, and repricing that lifts revenue per employee may itself feed attrition, which management links to the technology and professional services losses.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 16; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C15

Revenue visibility is short and concentrated. PEO clients renew annually but may cancel on 30 days' notice, administrative services clients are monthly or annual on the same notice, and five states account for about 64% of paid wages. No client exceeded 10% of revenue in 2023 to 2025 and bad debt net of recoveries was USD 3 million in each year, so concentration in single clients is small, but revenue by vertical and client size is not disclosed and the breadth of attrition is not established.

### Margin resilience

Margins are fragile to claims experience and only partly flexible on cost. Insurance costs were USD 3,835 million of USD 4,793 million total costs and expenses in FY2025, and because fees are set ahead of claims a rise in the insurance cost ratio falls straight through to earnings, as the move from 84% to 91% did. The Adjusted EBITDA margin fell from about 14% in FY2023 to 8.48% in FY2025 and was 9.53% over the last twelve months; the FY2023 margin is printed as 14.2% in one annual report and 14.0% in the other. Operating expenses of USD 902 million carry the cost flexibility: restructuring costs of USD 49 million in 2024, USD 11 million in 2025 and USD 13 million in nine months 2026 show that management can resize costs, but savings are not quantified, so margin recovery rests mainly on repricing and claims, not cost.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 75; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 70; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 71; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, pages 129–130; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 41; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Cash conversion

Capital intensity is low and cash conversion is high, but part of the 2026 improvement is timing. Capital expenditures including capitalized software were USD 69 million in FY2025 and USD 74 million in nine months 2026 against USD 51 million a year earlier, and free cash flow was USD 234 million in FY2025 and USD 286 million over the last twelve months after cash interest of USD 49 million. ### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense and caps repricing. Direct competitors include large professional employer organizations such as the units of ADP, Paychex and Insperity and many smaller providers, and Trinet's offer must be priced competitively with what clients can buy themselves, so recovering margin through rate rises risks the client losses already seen.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 10

The threat of new entrants is moderate. Payroll agents, other human capital providers and insurance brokers that integrate third-party HR systems can offer parts of the service, and the administrative services offer, where Trinet does not co-employ, competes with a wider field; the filings describe state insurance licensing as complex, which is a barrier mainly for the core PEO offer.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 22

The threat of substitutes is real. Companies can run payroll, benefits and HR in-house or through payroll processors, and clients may buy insurance through brokers, so the benefit-scale advantage has to stay visible in price; substitution would show first as worksite employee attrition, already 10.99% year on year.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 13; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Buyer power is high at the margin. Small and medium-size clients can cancel on 30 days' notice, were cutting staff in 2025 and can compare offers, so price increases meet low switching cost and weak revenue visibility.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 18

Supplier power sits with insurance carriers. Trinet relies on carriers for health and workers' compensation cover, carriers set the fixed cost of guaranteed-cost plans, about 88% of group health costs are risk-based plans in which Trinet reimburses claims within a deductible layer, and carriers set collateral. A change in carrier terms or a loss of a key carrier is therefore a direct credit event for margin and for restricted cash.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 80; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 86; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 104

### External factors

Economic and regulatory factors are material to credit; social and technological factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver based on the provided materials. Net hiring by small and medium-size clients was low in 2025 and short-term interest rates fell, which cuts interest income on client funds and would lower the cost of any revolver borrowing, so the net rate effect is negative for earnings and mildly positive for debt cost. The IRS is examining some employee retention credit claims filed for clients, Trinet may seek reimbursement from clients if claims are found ineligible but the IRS holds professional employer organizations responsible for repaying rejected credits and a contract does not guarantee recovery, and state insurance licensing and benefit rules apply; an adverse finding or fine would hit cash directly, but no amount is stated.

### SWOT

Strengths in credit terms are low fixed-rate leverage, a distant first maturity, strong cash conversion and prefunded client money. Weaknesses are an insurance-spread earnings base, a shrinking client base, shareholder payments at the level of free cash flow and a part of EBITDA that is yield on client funds.

The opportunities that could improve credit quality are completed repricing that holds the insurance cost ratio near its 2026 level, stabilising worksite employees through the broker channel and new software and leave-management tools, and lower interest cost on any future revolver use as short-term rates fall. The threats that could weaken it are health cost inflation and utilisation, further client losses, an adverse IRS finding on retention credit claims, a debt-funded repurchase and carrier collateral calls.

### Outlook

Over twelve to twenty-four months the insurance cost ratio decides the outcome. Management says the reset of health benefit rates is substantially complete, the ratio was 86% in nine months and 89% in the third quarter, and claims costs rise later in the year, so the last-twelve-months figure will move with the fourth quarter, when the implied 2025 ratio was 94.18% against 89.65% for the first nine months and implied Adjusted EBITDA was only USD 57 million against USD 368 million. Interest income is likely to keep falling with client balances and rates, and the filings give no quantified outlook for worksite employees or earnings. No debt matures, but the revolver's August 2028 maturity falls inside the window and the 2029 notes turn current in early 2028.

Over three to five years the credit is a question of policy and refinancing. USD 500 million falls due in March 2029 and USD 400 million in August 2031, and a refinancing of the 2029 notes at a higher coupon would raise interest cost. With moderate leverage and free cash flow, refinancing capacity looks adequate if earnings hold, but it will depend on the leverage the board allows through buybacks, the insurance cost ratio and the worksite employee trend at the time. Sector tailwinds are the scale advantage in benefits for small businesses and lower short-term rates on revolver cost; headwinds are health cost inflation, low small-business hiring and competition from larger providers.

### Downside scenario

The issuer-specific downside is a claims shock layered on continued client losses. The initiating condition is health utilisation above the rates fixed for the plan year, as in 2024 and 2025, and the first operational break is the insurance cost ratio rising while worksite employees keep falling, because fees cannot be reset until the next benefit period. The ratio implied for the last twelve months is 88.29%, and the fourth quarter is the seasonal weak point: the implied FY2025 fourth-quarter ratio was 94.18% against 89.65% for the first nine months. If the ratio returned to the FY2025 level of 91%, the extra insurance cost on last-twelve-months insurance service revenues would be USD 111 million, which would cut reported EBITDA from USD 359 million to USD 248 million and Adjusted EBITDA from USD 460 million to USD 349 million before any repricing offset.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 93; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 74; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 11; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C04; calculation C05; calculation C06; calculation C21

The financial transmission is meaningful but is not a solvency event. Net debt of USD 568 million would be 2.29x the reduced reported EBITDA, or 2.34x on USD 581 million pro forma for the October 2026 dividend, and cash interest cover would fall to 5.06x. Free cash flow would fall to USD 175 million before tax relief, which still covers dividends of USD 52 million 3.37x but leaves USD 123 million for buybacks against USD 183 million in FY2025. Carrier collateral could draw on liquidity as well, since USD 299 million is held now and carriers may require more. Nothing matures before 2029, so resilience appears sufficient to avoid a liquidity event even if buybacks continue at a reduced pace and the revolver stays available, but leverage would not stay at present levels while paying shareholders as in FY2025, and a larger or longer rise in claims or further client losses would take it well beyond this sensitivity.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 28; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 80; 10-K_2025-12-31_tnet.htm.jsonl, pages 94–95; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C07; calculation C08; calculation C09; calculation C10; calculation C02

Management levers are stopping buybacks, which were USD 94 million in nine months 2026, further repricing, restructuring and revolver use of up to USD 700 million before letters of credit. The observable triggers are a quarterly insurance cost ratio above the same quarter a year earlier, when the fourth quarter ran at an implied 94.18% and the third quarter at 90%, worksite employees below their latest level, collateral balances above USD 299 million, any revolver borrowing and a debt-funded repurchase.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tnet.htm.jsonl, page 35; calculation C02; calculation C21

### Overall credit view

Overall, Trinet presents an adequate business profile supported by scale in small-business HR outsourcing, with total revenues of USD 5,010 million in FY2025, an operating record since 1988 and prefunded client money, but credit quality is constrained by an insurance-spread earnings base, a shrinking client base and shareholder payments at the level of free cash flow. No peer set was supplied, so the comparison with typical leveraged-credit issuers in the sector is not established. Cash-flow durability appears adequate for debt service, because capital spending is modest and debt is low and fixed rate, but weak as a measure of recurring earnings, because the insurance cost ratio and client losses can remove a large share of EBITDA within a year. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the insurance cost ratio and worksite employee trend at the time, the leverage the board allows through buybacks, and the extension of the revolver before it matures in August 2028. The primary downside path is claims costs rising above rates set in advance while clients leave, with cash drawn into carrier collateral and shareholder payments continuing. Further analysis would require the credit agreement and indentures, the covenant level, letters of credit outstanding, the proxy statement, a peer set and market quotes. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: the insurance cost ratio.
