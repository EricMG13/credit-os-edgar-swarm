## Findings

### Bottom line

OPENLANE's fundamental credit is solid for a high yield issuer: the corporate term loan is lightly levered and well covered by liquidity, but the finance arm's securitization funding and large buybacks mean group-level gearing and cash leakage are higher than the headline.

Total debt is 1.18x twelve-month EBITDA and 1.40x adjusted EBITDA, liquidity is USD 650.2 million, and no corporate debt matures before 2028 apart from scheduled term loan amortization. Net debt is USD 289.3 million, or 0.64x, and USD 300.4 million, or 0.67x, pro forma for USD 11.1 million of repurchases in October 2026. Including USD 2,064.1 million of securitization, total debt is 5.75x EBITDA.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C1.1; calculation C1.3; calculation C1.5; calculation C1.7; calculation C2.1

### Key findings

The corporate balance sheet is the strongest support. Total debt is 1.18x twelve-month EBITDA, liquidity is USD 650.2 million, and the term loan amortizes only USD 5.5 million a year to 2032, so debt service does not depend on refinancing in the near term.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17; calculation C1.1; calculation C1.5

Structural gearing is the main constraint. Securitization funding of USD 2,064.1 million supports finance receivables of USD 2,763.8 million, and with it total debt is 5.75x EBITDA. The facilities recycle and were extended to January 2030, but they are only as good as dealer credit and the bank purchasers' continued commitment.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 15; calculation C1.7

Earnings are rising across two segments. Adjusted EBITDA was USD 382.7 million over twelve months against USD 332.6 million in 2025, of which the marketplace contributed USD 197.7 million and the finance arm USD 185.0 million, and management raised its 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance to USD 390 million to USD 400 million.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.3_2026-11-04_q32026openlaneearningssl.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation C1.3; calculation C2.2

Cash conversion is good but flattered by settlement timing. Nine-month operating cash flow of USD 309.5 million includes a USD 155.8 million rise in payables, largely offset by a USD 117.0 million rise in receivables, so working capital added USD 38.8 million. After capital expenditure of USD 40.7 million, loan book growth net of its securitization funding, and buybacks of USD 91.5 million, cash generation was USD 132.6 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation C3.1; calculation C3.2; calculation C3.3; calculation C3.4

Financial policy is the swing factor. The 2025 preferred take-out was paid for with USD 550.0 million of secured term loans, the preferred has since converted, and governance risk is rated Medium. Buybacks of USD 91.5 million and a larger authorization show that shareholder returns, not debt reduction, are the first call on spare cash.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27

The primary downside path starts with dealer stress. Delinquent receivables are 0.6% of receivables managed against 0.3% a year earlier, so a further rise would lift provisions in a segment that earned USD 117.0 million of operating profit in nine months and could tighten securitization terms, while falling volumes would shrink fee revenue and the settlement float. Liquidity of USD 650.2 million and an undrawn revolver give room to absorb the first quarters of such a shock.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1.5

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a solid view is that the headline leverage depends on a perimeter choice: reported EBITDA adds back securitization interest, adjusted EBITDA deducts it, and including securitization funding lifts total debt to adjusted EBITDA to 6.79x. The issuer's own covenant leverage of 0.9x cannot be reproduced from the statements, so the comfort it implies rests on definitions that are not supplied.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 54; calculation C1.8

The view would improve if delinquency stays near 0.6%, buybacks stay below cash generation after loan growth, and the 2028 revolver maturity is extended before it becomes current. It would weaken if net debt rose on a debt-funded return, if the revolver were drawn at a quarter end, or if the amendment dated 2026-06-30 proved to have loosened restricted payment limits.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strength one is low corporate leverage with ample liquidity. Corporate total debt is 1.18x twelve-month EBITDA and liquidity is USD 650.2 million, so a downturn would have to be deep before the term loan came under pressure, and the revolver covenant is tested only when it is drawn.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; calculation C1.1; calculation C1.5

Strength two is a growing, two-segment earnings base. Adjusted EBITDA of USD 382.7 million over twelve months is above the USD 332.6 million of 2025, with the marketplace and finance arm contributing similar amounts, so a weak quarter in one segment is partly cushioned by the other.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1.3; calculation C2.2

Strength three is a long funding runway. The term loan matures in 2032, the securitization facilities in January 2030 and the revolvers in 2028, so near-term maturities are limited to small term loan instalments and the funding of the loan book is committed.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17

Strength four is the cleared sponsor overhang. The preferred stock converted to common stock in May 2026 and its cash dividend fell from USD 38.6 million in 2025 to USD 5.3 million in nine months, which reduces the fixed claims ahead of common equity.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22

Weakness one is structural gearing. Securitization of USD 2,064.1 million sits outside the corporate debt line, and including it total debt to EBITDA is 5.75x, so creditors of the parent have a residual claim on a finance arm whose loan book is pledged to its own lenders.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1.7

Weakness two is credit exposure to independent dealers. The finance arm lends to about fifteen thousand independent dealers, delinquent receivables have doubled to 0.6% of receivables managed, and loan growth consumed USD 367.6 million of cash in nine months, so a downturn would raise provisions while the loan book still needs funding.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4

Weakness three is distribution policy. Buybacks of USD 91.5 million in nine months and a larger authorization compete with debt reduction, and the 2025 take-out showed that senior debt can be raised to fund returns to holders.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5

Weakness four is dependence on settlement float and a few seller programs. Accounts payable of USD 827.6 million include consignor proceeds, and commercial volume growth of 46% in the third quarter falls to 17% without the latest commercial customer, so volume and cash flow could both fall if a large program is lost.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34; EX-99.3_2026-11-04_q32026openlaneearningssl.htm.jsonl, page 4
