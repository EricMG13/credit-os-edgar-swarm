## Findings

### Bottom line

OPENLANE is a scaled digital wholesale vehicle marketplace with a captive floorplan lender, and the sponsor-linked preferred that once ranked ahead of common has converted, which lowers governance risk for term loan lenders but leaves buybacks as the main call on cash.

Marketplace earns 78% of revenue and the finance arm 22%, so credit quality rests on wholesale volumes, which rose 24% in the first nine months of 2026. In October 2025 the company used USD 550.0 million of new secured term loans to buy back preferred stock for USD 559.3 million, a debt-funded take-out of the sponsors that shows willingness to add senior debt for shareholders.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_opln-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 45; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22; calculation C1.1

### Key findings

The business is two linked franchises: a marketplace that earns fees on volume, and a floorplan lender that depends on the same independent dealers. The marketplace generated 78% of 2025 revenue, and its vehicles sold rose 24% in the first nine months of 2026, with commercial vehicles up 37% on a new private label customer and more off-lease supply.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_opln-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 45; calculation C1.1; calculation C2.1

Customer concentration is moderate but not fully disclosed. No single customer exceeded 10% of revenue in any year shown, yet commercial volume growth in the third quarter of 2026 was 46% and only 17% without the latest commercial customer, so a few seller programs drive the volume gains.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_opln-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 138; EX-99.3_2026-11-04_q32026openlaneearningssl.htm.jsonl, page 4

The sponsor overhang has largely cleared. Preferred cash dividends fell from USD 38.6 million in 2025 to USD 5.3 million in the first nine months of 2026, and all remaining preferred stock converted into common stock in May 2026, which removes a claim that ranked ahead of common stockholders and the cash dividend that came with it.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_opln-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 81; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 62; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22

The October 2025 take-out was funded with debt. Preferred stock repurchased for USD 559.3 million was paid for mainly with USD 550.0 million of incremental secured term loans, so senior claims on the group rose to buy out junior capital. That is the clearest evidence of how the board treats creditors when a holder wants liquidity, and it is the reason governance risk is rated Medium rather than Low.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_opln-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 128; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18; calculation C3.1

Capital returns now come from cash flow rather than new debt, but they are large. The company repurchased USD 91.5 million of shares in nine months, including about USD 25 million bought from an underwriter in an August 2026 secondary sale by an Apax affiliate, and in October 2026 the board added USD 150 million to the authorization.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 66; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27

The finance arm is the main source of asset risk. It lends short term to roughly fifteen thousand independent dealers, delinquent receivables rose to 0.6% of receivables managed from 0.3% a year earlier, and securitization lenders fund most of the loan book, so stress at independent dealers would reach group cash flow through lower fees and a larger credit loss provision.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest argument for rating governance risk above Medium is that the 2025 take-out added USD 550.0 million of secured debt to repay holders whose preferred stock had a 7% cumulative dividend, and the company has since bought shares in an Apax affiliate's secondary sale. If the board repeats a debt-funded return, or if the amendment dated 2026-06-30 loosened restricted payment limits, the risk rating would need to be reconsidered upward.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_opln-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 128; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 62; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 66; 10-Q_2026-06-30_opln-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 65

The argument for Low risk is that the preferred holders have converted, an Apax affiliate is selling, buybacks are funded from operating cash flow of USD 309.5 million over nine months, and no new debt-funded distribution has occurred since October 2025. It would win if the remaining sponsor stake falls to a minority and the next annual report shows buybacks below free cash flow.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 66

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The October 2025 Second Amendment to the Credit Agreement added USD 550.0 million of incremental term loans due 2032-10-08, priced at Adjusted Term SOFR plus 2.50% and amortizing at 0.25% of the original principal each quarter from 2026-03-31. The proceeds financed the repurchase of 334,028 shares of Series A Preferred Stock from affiliates of Apax and Periphas for about USD 559.3 million. The loans are guaranteed by certain domestic subsidiaries and secured by substantially all assets of the Company and the guarantors.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_opln-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 123; 10-K_2025-12-31_opln-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 128; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18

No further new debt was issued in 2026. In September 2026 the U.S. and Canadian securitization facilities were amended to extend maturity to 2030-01-31, which raised borrowing by about USD 58 million and cost about USD 12.5 million of capitalized fees.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_opln-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 57
