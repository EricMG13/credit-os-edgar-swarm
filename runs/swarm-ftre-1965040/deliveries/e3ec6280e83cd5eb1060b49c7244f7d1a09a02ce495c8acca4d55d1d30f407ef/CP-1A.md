## Findings

### Bottom line

Fortrea is a single-segment clinical research provider spun out of Labcorp with no disclosed controlling holder, so creditors rely on management execution and customer demand rather than sponsor support, and that reliance looks fragile today. Backlog fell to USD 7.3 billion at September 30, 2026 after a USD 558 million reduction, one customer supplied 17.2% of nine-month revenue, and the finance chief role was disrupted by a court order in July 2026.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 28; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-06-30_ftre-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; calculation C3; calculation C5

Sponsor and governance risk is Medium: the filings show no dividend, notes repurchased under an asset sale covenant and a covenant amendment bought with a fee, but also an activist cooperation agreement, a staggered board until 2028 and compensation changes approved while backlog was being written down. The evidence supports caution on management stability, not a finding of creditor-adverse conduct.

### Key findings

The business is a scaled but thin-margin services model: many contracts are fixed-price or fee-for-service with a cap, and more than a third of revenue is pass-through cost. Fortrea sells clinical development, clinical pharmacology and consulting services under full service, functional service provider and hybrid models, earned 47.35% of 2025 revenue in North America, 31.27% in Europe and 21.37% elsewhere, and passed through costs equal to 37.73% of revenue that earn no margin, leaving segment operating income at 3.21% of 2025 revenue. Margin is therefore set by staff utilisation and pricing discipline on the remaining work.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ftre-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6; 10-K_2025-12-31_ftre-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_ftre-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 79; 10-K_2025-12-31_ftre-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 152; 10-K_2025-12-31_ftre-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 184; calculation C8; calculation C16

Most contracts can be ended on 30 to 90 days notice, so the USD 7.3 billion of backlog is a pipeline, not a claim on cash.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against caution is operating momentum and the absence of creditor-adverse conduct. Segment operating income more than doubled in nine months, backlog of USD 7.3 billion is still 2.74x last twelve months revenue, management states that the USD 558 million reduction reflected scope and delivery estimates and not any recent material customer communication and expects no material effect on near-term revenue, the company reported compliance with all covenants at September 30, 2026, and the court case over the finance chief settled with no material impact while the securities class action was dismissed with prejudice on September 28, 2026. No dividend has been paid and the only acquisition was small and paid in cash.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 28; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20; 10-K_2025-12-31_ftre-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 99; calculation C3

The view would improve if the next quarterly report shows backlog stabilising and the share of obligations due within 12 months back above 29.3%, if the largest customer's share of revenue falls without a loss of revenue, and if the proxy statement shows independent directors and no holder above 10%. It would worsen if backlog falls again, if the largest customer cuts or ends work, if the receivables agent's rating-change rights are triggered, or if the finance chief role changes hands again.

### Limitations

- The Medium sponsor and governance rating assumes no holder with influence beyond the activist agreement, but the sources print no holdings, director independence or committee structure, so a holder near the 10% level that triggered the former rights plan would raise the risk level.
- The concentration finding rests on percentages alone: no customer is named and no contract term, renewal date or termination right is stated, so a long-dated contract with the largest customer would lower the risk and a near-term expiry would raise it.
- Revenue by delivery model and by therapeutic area is not printed beyond one oncology share, so the statement that weaker functional service provider demand drives the decline rests on management wording and could prove wrong.
- The reasons for the 2025 and 2026 officer changes, the August 2026 settlement terms and the June 2026 current report are not in the sources, so the management stability finding rests on dates and exhibit titles and could be harsher or milder once they are read.
- The acquired division's revenue, earnings and purchase price allocation are not stated, so whether the October 2026 deal changes the operating model or adds earnings is not established.
- Backlog is defined by management and no net new business or book-to-bill figure is printed, so whether the USD 558 million adjustment signals customer attrition rather than estimation is not established; attrition would lower the view on revenue visibility further.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The founding transaction is the June 30, 2023 spin-off from Labcorp, a pro-rata distribution of one Fortrea share for each Labcorp share. Fortrea incurred USD 1,640.0 million of term loans and notes at the spin, namely USD 570.0 million of 7.50% senior notes due 2030 issued on June 27, 2023, a USD 500.0 million first lien term loan A due 2028 and a USD 570.0 million first lien term loan B due 2030, alongside a USD 450.0 million revolving facility, and it paid USD 1,595.0 million to Labcorp. The separation agreement leaves Fortrea responsible for substantially all liabilities of its business and for the Labcorp obligations it assumed, Labcorp has indemnified Fortrea for certain liabilities without assurance that the indemnity will suffice, and the transition services agreement ended on June 30, 2025.
