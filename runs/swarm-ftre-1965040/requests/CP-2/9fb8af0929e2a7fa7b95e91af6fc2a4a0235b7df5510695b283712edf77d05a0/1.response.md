## Findings

Pro forma for the October 19, 2026 acquisition, net debt is USD 873.7 million, or 9.55x.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a weak view is recovery and time. Reported EBITDA rose from USD 67.5 million at June 30, 2026 to USD 91.5 million at September 30, 2026, cash rose by USD 46.0 million in nine months with no revolver borrowing, house-adjusted net leverage fell to 6.73x from 18.53x at the end of 2025, before any effect of the acquisition, backlog of USD 7.3 billion is still 2.74x last twelve months revenue, the issuer states compliance with all covenants at September 30, 2026 and no principal above USD 25.0 million falls due before 2028. Capital spending of 1.26% of nine-month revenue also leaves little to fund, so a stable top line would let cost savings reach cash.

The view would improve if the fourth-quarter report shows backlog stabilising, free cash flow before working capital rising above USD 23.5 million without a further working capital inflow, the issuer printing its covenant ratio with headroom at the 5.50x and 5.30x steps, and an extension of the 2028 maturities. It would worsen if the largest customer cuts work, if unearned revenue falls below USD 525.5 million, if the receivables agent's rating-change rights are triggered, or if the 2028 maturities remain unaddressed when they enter the 12-month window on June 30, 2027.

### Limitations

- The covenant requirement of USD 145.17 million of EBITDA at 5.75x assumes a net debt test and no netting limits, so a gross debt test or a different EBITDA definition would move the implied add-backs and could show real headroom or none.
- The issuer states no EBITDA and no guidance, so leverage rests on derived and house-adjusted measures; an issuer-adjusted figure above USD 124.0 million would lower leverage and weaken the conclusion that no supported measure removes it.
- The downside scenario halves the largest customer's work and holds selling costs fixed, both assumptions without a management plan to test them, so the contribution lost, USD 45.99 million, could be materially higher or lower.
- No rating, share price, bond quote or peer data is supplied, so the refinancing conclusion is not tested against market pricing, and a par-priced bond or a strong equity value would lower the 2028 refinancing risk.
- The last twelve months free cash flow depends on a fourth-quarter 2025 working capital inflow whose reversal or repeat in the fourth quarter of 2026 is not visible, so the finding that cash flow is undependable could change with the year-end report.
- The interest rate swap's maturity and the loans it hedges are not stated, so the 100 bp sensitivity of USD 4.22 million could be understated if the swap expires or the notional covers other debt.

### Company description

Fortrea is a global contract research organization that runs phase I to IV clinical trials, clinical pharmacology and consulting for pharmaceutical, biotechnology and medical device customers under full service, functional service provider and hybrid models, in one reporting segment. It was spun out of Labcorp on June 30, 2023 and sold its Enabling Services segment in 2024, so the issuer is a three-year-old entity whose operating history before mid-2023 is carve-out data. Revenue was USD 2,666.7 million over the last twelve months, split about 47% North America, 31% Europe and 21% elsewhere in 2025, and 37.73% of 2025 revenue was pass-through cost that earns no margin.

It weakens refinancing confidence because earnings depend on a few customers' research budgets, and it supports liquidity because customers pay in advance, with unearned revenue of USD 525.5 million.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue follows the conversion of backlog into work, the volume of customers' trials, and pass-through costs that move revenue without moving profit. Revenue was USD 2,842.5 million in 2023, USD 2,696.4 million in 2024 and USD 2,723.4 million in 2025, and nine-month 2026 revenue fell 2.7% to USD 2,006.2 million, but USD 48.3 million of the USD 56.7 million decline was lower pass-through, so revenue excluding it slipped 0.66%. Management attributes the 2026 decline to lower clinical development revenue and weaker demand for functional service provider work, partly offset by clinical pharmacology.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Costs are mainly people and pass-through: direct costs, including pass-through, were 79.9% of nine-month 2026 revenue against 81.9% a year earlier, and segment selling and administrative costs fell 9.33% as restructuring actions took effect. The filings do not split costs into fixed and variable, but the stated contribution after direct costs of 20.05% of revenue, with selling costs largely fixed, means each USD 100 million of lost revenue removes about USD 20.05 million of EBITDA before any cost response.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ftre-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 30; calculation C9

Margins can be defended against a modest volume fall because restructuring has already cut selling costs and capital spending is flexible, but not against the loss of a large customer, wage inflation or adverse mix: merit wage increases and higher variable compensation partly offset savings in 2026, and restructuring charges of USD 32.5 million over the last twelve months show the cost base is still being reset. The implication is that margin resilience depends on volume, which is the weakest part of the credit.
