## Findings

### Bottom line

Alta is a stretched credit with a durable core: total debt is 4.65x EBITDA and EBITDA covers cash interest only 2.02x, so creditors depend on refinancing USD 692.9 million that falls due on one day in 2029, not on deleveraging.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 17–18; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C7

Parts and service supply 53.39% of gross profit, no customer exceeds 1% of revenue and no principal falls due before 2029, but EBITDA has fallen 20.46% since FY2023 while total debt rose 6.55%, and the USD 64.2 million of last-twelve-month free cash flow comes from lower capital expenditure and a swing in floor plan payables, not from earnings. Cash plus reported availability of USD 435.3 million covers 62.82% of the 2029 principal, and stockholders' equity is a deficit of USD 47.1 million with no sponsor to support it.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5–8, 17–18, 55; Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C2; calculation C5; calculation C7; calculation C9; calculation C10

### Key findings

The business profile is adequate: exclusive OEM territories and a spread of customers support an aftermarket-heavy earnings stream, but equipment sales and rental income are cyclical and supplier-dependent. Parts and service were 29.83% of FY2025 revenue and 53.39% of gross profit after rental depreciation, no customer exceeds 1% of revenue, and Construction Equipment produced 60.83% of FY2025 revenue. In the first nine months of 2026 organic revenue fell 1.0% and Adjusted EBITDA fell 1.78%, with parts margins down 140 bp and service margins up 150 bp as management cut technician headcount and non-billable labor. Five OEMs supplied 49% of purchases and their finance arms renew floor plan lines each year, so the core is durable and the edge is exposed to supplier decisions.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11, 19, 30; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 35, 37–39; calculation C8; calculation C9

Leverage is stretched and has risen since FY2023 while earnings fell. Total debt to calculated EBITDA rose from 3.47x at FY2023 to 4.50x at FY2025 and 4.65x for the last twelve months to September 30, 2026, and EBITDA cover of cash interest fell from 3.58x to 2.02x, because EBITDA fell 20.46% to USD 152.8 million and total debt rose 6.55% to USD 710.7 million. Counting floor plan payables of USD 325.7 million as debt lifts leverage to 6.78x, while the issuer's own Adjusted EBITDA gives 4.38x; a committee should read 4.65x as the measure consistent with the issuer's debt definition and 6.78x as the conservative bound. At the last twelve months' pace of EBITDA less cash interest and capital expenditure, USD 48.7 million, repaying total debt would take 14.59 years, so deleveraging from cash flow is not a credible route to the 2029 maturity.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2024-12-31_altg_altg-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 70; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5, 18; calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C6

Free cash flow is not yet durable: the last twelve months turned positive on lower spending and the end of a floor plan paydown, not on earnings. Operating cash flow less capital expenditure was an outflow of USD 16.2 million in FY2023, USD 13.5 million in FY2024 and USD 18.0 million in FY2025, then USD 64.2 million positive over the last twelve months. The USD 82.2 million swing is USD 59.1 million from manufacturer floor plan payables, which moved from a USD 55.4 million outflow in FY2025 to a USD 3.7 million net inflow, and USD 22.5 million from lower capital expenditure, leaving USD 0.6 million for everything else; operating cash flow before floor plan changes was USD 88.4 million in FY2025 and USD 89.0 million in the last twelve months. This sharpens Financial foundation, which stressed the nine-month floor plan inflow of USD 30.8 million: over twelve months the net floor plan change is small, and the gain is the end of FY2025's paydown, not new floor plan borrowing.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 73–74; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C5

Liquidity is adequate until 2029 but cannot retire the 2029 debt, and how much of it is drawable is not established. Cash was USD 21.8 million and management reports USD 413.5 million of available borrowings under the ABL and floor plan facilities combined, up from USD 373.8 million at June 30, 2026 and USD 400.2 million at March 31, 2026 but below USD 424.4 million at FY2025. Printed limits less borrowings and letters of credit imply USD 530.9 million, USD 117.4 million more than reported, and the borrowing base is not printed, so reported availability is the figure to use. Cash plus reported availability covers 62.82% of the USD 692.9 million due in 2029. Management says cash flows, cash and borrowings will be adequate for the foreseeable future, and each one percentage point rise in variable rates would cut annual pre-tax earnings by USD 3.5 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18, 55, 57; 10-Q_2026-06-30_altg_altg-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 55; 10-Q_2026-03-31_altg_altg-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 42; 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 64; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C7; calculation C11

Refinancing capacity is the central uncertainty, because both main facilities fall due on 2029-06-01 with no equity cushion and no committed sponsor. The USD 192.9 million ABL and the USD 500.0 million second lien notes mature on the same day and are 95.59% of contractual principal including finance leases, so one refinancing, priced on 2028 earnings and market conditions, decides the credit. Equity is a deficit of USD 47.1 million, the notes rank behind the ABL and the first lien floor plan facility, and no covenant level, borrowing base or lien capacity is printed, so the lender protections that would shape a refinancing are not established. The debt would normally become current from mid-2028, which is when a delayed refinancing would first show in reported liquidity.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 17–18; 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 92–93; Source readiness (CP-0); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C7; calculation C10

Margins are stable but the mix inside them is shifting, and operating leverage is high. The consolidated gross margin was 26.9% for nine months 2026 against 26.8%, operating expenses were flat, and the Adjusted EBITDA margin was 9.33% in both periods, helped by service margins of 61.1% after technician headcount cuts. Parts margins fell to 33.1% from 34.5%, rental equipment sales margins fell 450 bp as aged fleet was sold, and healthcare costs rose. From FY2023 to FY2025 revenue fell 2.18% while calculated EBITDA fell 15.04%, so a small revenue miss moves earnings several times as much.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 38–40; 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 71–72; calculation C3; calculation C8; calculation C9

Financial policy and governance risk is medium, and recent actions support creditors without committing to them. Distributions of USD 41.6 million over FY2023 to FY2025 exceeded the cash left after investment in every year, so they leaned on borrowing; since then the common dividend was halved and stopped, repurchases stopped and disposal proceeds of USD 25.9 million went to senior debt, but the preferred dividend of USD 2.2 million for nine months 2026 continues. Two officers hold an interest in OneH2, to which Alta paid USD 5.3 million for a hydrogen plant and which entered creditor assignment in June 2026. Ownership percentages, board detail and the terms of the 2026 investor agreements are not supplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 40–41, 73–74, 103; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 14, 24; calculation C10

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a better view is the direction since June 2026: total debt to EBITDA fell from 4.91x to 4.65x, reported availability rose from USD 373.8 million to USD 413.5 million, cover improved from 1.96x at FY2025 to 2.02x, organic revenue fell only 1.0%, and management has cut capital expenditure, stopped dividends and repurchases and sold fleet. No principal is due until 2029, which leaves time to repair earnings. The strongest case for a worse view is that EBITDA has not recovered, the free cash flow gain is not earnings-driven, drawable availability may be smaller than printed limits because the borrowing base is unknown, and cash plus availability covers only 62.82% of the 2029 principal. The view would improve if leverage keeps falling toward the 3.47x of FY2023 with cover above 2.02x and operating cash flow before floor plan changes rises above the USD 89.0 million of the last twelve months. It would worsen if reported availability fell below USD 373.8 million, leverage rose above 4.91x, cover fell below 1.96x, or the borrowing base proved smaller than the printed limits.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18, 55; 10-Q_2026-06-30_altg_altg-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 55; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C5; calculation C7

### Strengths and weaknesses

Aftermarket earnings carry more than half of gross profit, which cushions the cycle. Parts and service were 29.83% of FY2025 revenue but 53.39% of gross profit after rental depreciation, because service earns a margin of 59.45% against 14.07% on new and used equipment, so a fall in equipment sales removes less profit than revenue and supports debt service through a soft year.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 71–72; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C9

Customers are spread and territories are exclusive, which limits revenue loss from any one buyer. No customer exceeds 1% of revenue and the top ten are about 6%, while Alta is the exclusive distributor of new equipment and replacement parts in substantially all territories, so losing a customer is immaterial to cash flow and parts demand follows the installed equipment base.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 13; Business & governance (CP-1A)

No principal falls due before 2029, which removes near-term refinancing pressure. The ABL and second lien notes both mature on 2029-06-01, and reported availability of USD 413.5 million gives room to fund seasonal working capital meanwhile, so a default before 2029 would need an operating shock large enough to break the borrowing base.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18, 55; calculation C7

Actions since mid-2025 are creditor-supportive. The common dividend was halved and stopped, repurchases stopped, capital expenditure fell 51.96% in nine months 2026, USD 25.9 million of disposal proceeds went to senior debt, and management cut rental fleet gross cost by USD 84.3 million, which lowers cash outflow and borrowing and shows that levers exist, although none is a stated policy.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 40–41, 44, 103; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 24, 27; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C10

Margins have held. The consolidated gross margin was 26.9% for nine months 2026 against 26.8%, and the Adjusted EBITDA margin was 9.33% in both periods, so cost actions have so far offset weaker volumes and keep cover from falling faster.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26–27, 38–39; calculation C8; calculation C9

The 2029 maturity is the main weakness. The USD 692.9 million of ABL and note principal falls due on one day against cash plus reported availability of USD 435.3 million, or 62.82%, so the credit depends on market access in 2028 and 2029 and on lenders accepting earnings that are lower than in FY2023.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18, 55; calculation C7

Leverage is high and cover is thin. Total debt is 4.65x EBITDA, 6.78x with floor plan payables, and EBITDA covers cash interest 2.02x, so a modest earnings fall cuts quickly into cash after interest and leaves little for debt reduction.

Free cash flow depends on spending cuts and on floor plan timing. Free cash flow was negative in each of FY2023 to FY2025, and the positive last-twelve-month figure of USD 64.2 million is explained by lower capital expenditure and the end of a floor plan paydown, so it can reverse if spending or inventory financing normalizes.

### Market forces

Supplier power is the force that matters most to creditors, and buyer power matters least. Five OEMs supplied 49% of purchases, suppliers may appoint other distributors, sell direct or end agreements, and their finance arms renew floor plan lines each year, so supplier decisions reach earnings and liquidity. Buyers are fragmented, with no customer above 1% of revenue, and new entrants face OEM exclusivity and a shortage of skilled technicians, so rivalry and substitution are the pressures that show in margins: OEMs discount to win share, which is visible in the 14.07% margin on new and used equipment, and falling rental utilization makes renting a weaker offer for Alta's own fleet.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 13, 19, 30, 43–44, 71–72; calculation C9

Economic and political factors change the credit view, and social and technological factors do not on the evidence supplied. Variable-rate debt makes earnings sensitive to interest rates, tariff volatility and elevated channel inventories pressured equipment pricing in 2025, and public infrastructure funding supported construction demand, so these factors act through margins and the cost of the 2029 refinancing.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 43; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 57; calculation C11

Opportunities that could improve credit quality are limited. The lift truck industry expects bookings to strengthen in the second half of 2026, the annual report cites signs of stabilization in construction late in 2025 and management says Material Handling bookings improved with healthy backlog, and territory in-fill acquisitions could add parts and service, though acquisitions would add debt. Threats are tariff and rate volatility, supplier termination or non-renewal of floor plan lines, parts availability constraints in Material Handling and a refinancing window that opens in 2028.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 43; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 38

### Outlook

Over the next 12 to 24 months earnings look flat to slightly lower, with cost actions offsetting soft volumes, and no maturity falls due. Interest expense was USD 7.7 million lower in nine months 2026 on smaller inventories and fleet and lower benchmark rates, operating expenses were flat, and management has reduced headcount, which supports margins; organic equipment sales are still falling and rental revenue falls as the fleet shrinks, so growth in cash flow would have to come from fleet and inventory release, which is finite.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 37–40; calculation C8

Over three to five years the outlook is set by the 2029 refinancing: if EBITDA recovers toward the FY2023 level the debt can be refinanced at a leverage nearer 3.47x, and if it stays near USD 152.8 million the refinancing meets 4.65x leverage and cover near 2x with no equity cushion. Sector headwinds are tariff volatility, high channel inventories and rate sensitivity; tailwinds are infrastructure funding and an expected recovery in lift truck bookings, and none is quantified in the filings.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 43; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1

### Downside scenario

The downside path runs from equipment demand to earnings and then to the 2029 refinancing, and not to near-term liquidity. Softer construction and lift truck demand would cut equipment deliveries and rental utilization, shrink the inventory and receivables that support ABL advances and compress margins; if calculated EBITDA repeated its FY2023 to FY2025 fall of 15.04% from the last-twelve-month level it would be USD 129.8 million, cash interest cover would be 1.72x and total debt to EBITDA 5.48x, or 7.98x counting floor plan payables. The scenario is an assumption, not a forecast.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_altg_altg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 43–44; 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 55; calculation C3; calculation C4

Management levers are real but largely used. Capital expenditure is already only USD 28.5 million over twelve months, dividends and repurchases are stopped, and fleet sales and inventory release can raise cash, but they also reduce earning assets; EBITDA less cash interest and capital expenditure would fall to USD 25.7 million. Liquidity appears sufficient to avoid distress before 2029, because no principal falls due and USD 435.3 million of cash plus reported availability remains, but it would not protect an orderly refinancing of USD 692.9 million at that leverage.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_altg_altg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18, 55; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C4; calculation C7

### Overall credit view

Overall, Alta presents an adequate business profile supported by aftermarket-heavy earnings, spread customers and exclusive OEM territories, but credit quality is constrained by leverage of 4.65x, cover of 2.02x, a deficit in equity and a single maturity of USD 692.9 million in 2029. Cash-flow durability appears weak because free cash flow was negative for three years and the recent gain comes from spending cuts and floor plan timing. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on EBITDA stabilizing near the current level, on the borrowing base holding and on supplier floor plan lines being renewed. The primary downside path is softer equipment demand that cuts earnings and collateral before the 2029 refinancing. Further analysis would require the credit agreements, the borrowing base, covenant levels, peer comparison and the proxy statement. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: the single 2029 maturity of the ABL and second lien notes.
