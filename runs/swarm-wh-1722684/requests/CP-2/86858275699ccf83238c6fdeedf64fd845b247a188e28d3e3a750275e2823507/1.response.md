## Findings

### Bottom line

Fundamental credit is adequate: fee income is recurring and liquidity is USD 988 million, but net leverage of 5.48x on unadjusted EBITDA and shareholder returns above free cash flow leave little room, so creditors should underwrite to the unadjusted figure and watch distributions.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C4

The strongest support is a franchise fee stream with free cash flow of 44.59% of adjusted EBITDA and only USD 23 million of debt due within a year. The weakest point is leverage of 4.09x even after removing the Revo charges, with distributions of 151.55% of free cash flow in the nine months to 2026-09-30. The primary downside path is a 10% fall in royalty income while buybacks continue, which lifts unadjusted net leverage to 6.16x ahead of the 2028 refinancing and hedge expiries. Covenant headroom is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 15; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C1; calculation C4; calculation C7

### Key findings

Leverage depends on which EBITDA the committee accepts, and the defensible range is 4.09x to 5.48x, not the issuer's 3.59x. Net debt of USD 2,656 million is 5.48x unadjusted EBITDA of USD 485 million and 3.59x the issuer's adjusted EBITDA of USD 740 million; removing only the Revo charges of USD 164 million gives 4.09x, because the other add-backs of USD 91 million, mainly stock-based compensation and development advance note amortization, recur every year. The creditor consequence is that the headline 3.59x understates the burden, and the reported first-lien covenant ratio of 2.0x against a 5.0x maximum, which cannot be rebuilt without the credit agreement, does not offset that.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40; 10-K_2025-12-31_wh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 118; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1

Near-term refinancing risk is low, but rate and refinancing risks cluster in 2027 and 2028. Liquidity of USD 988 million covers the USD 542 million of debt due within 24 months 1.82x, and only USD 23 million falls due within a year, with the USD 500 million notes due in August 2028 the first large maturity. Interest rate swaps of USD 475 million expire in the fourth quarter of 2027 and USD 950 million in the second and third quarters of 2028, and they hedge over 95% of the term loan B; once they lapse, each 100 bp on USD 1,500 million would cost USD 15.00 million a year, or 4.55% of last-twelve-months free cash flow. The creditor consequence is that the 2028 notes and the swap roll-off fall in the same window, so a refinancing plan and a hedging decision are due together.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C3; calculation C8

Cash conversion is adequate, but shareholder returns, not operations, decide whether leverage falls. Free cash flow was USD 330 million over the last twelve months, 44.59% of adjusted EBITDA, while dividends and repurchases were USD 368 million, or 111.52% of free cash flow, and 122.43% in FY2025 and 151.55% in the nine months to 2026-09-30. Had the USD 100 million by which returns exceeded free cash flow in those nine months been kept, net leverage would be 0.21x lower on unadjusted EBITDA and 0.14x lower on adjusted. The creditor consequence is that the board can restore deleveraging quickly by cutting buybacks, and has not yet done so.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C4

Revenue is recurring, but the fee base is not growing in dollars: rooms grow and royalty income does not. System rooms grew 2.57% year on year to 2026-09-30, or 3.74% excluding Revo, yet royalties and franchise fees fell 2.62% in nine months 2026 to USD 409 million from USD 420 million, because Revo fees are deferred, all net room growth came from international rooms that earned a 2.6% royalty rate in the third quarter against 4.7% in the United States, and global RevPAR is flat over the nine months. Net revenue growth of USD 17 million came from ancillary income of USD 24 million, mainly the co-branded credit card programme, and USD 9 million of management and other fees, mainly from two owned hotels, less USD 11 million of lower royalties and franchise fees and USD 5 million of lower marketing revenue. About USD 8 million of the USD 22 million rise in adjusted EBITDA is a swing in the marketing, reservation and loyalty margin from a loss of USD 1 million to a surplus of USD 7 million, which the issuer links to the absence of the 2025 franchisee conference and to timing of spend. The creditor consequence is that recent earnings growth leans on partner fees and a better marketing margin rather than on the core fee base.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C5; calculation C6

Franchisee credit is the main source of loss outside the debt figure. The Revo insolvency wrote off USD 160 million of USD 197 million of book value, while development advance notes of USD 383 million equal 80.63% of stockholders' equity and the issuer guarantees a share of some franchisee construction loans in an amount it does not state. A second failure of Revo's size would cut unadjusted EBITDA by USD 160 million and lift unadjusted net leverage to 8.17x, although the adjusted figure would stay at 3.59x because the charge is added back. The creditor consequence is that growth funded by advances turns franchisee failures into balance-sheet losses, and the add-back that hides them is the one a committee should test first.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_wh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-K_2025-12-31_wh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 118; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 12; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C7; calculation C9

Financial policy is the weakest dimension and the one creditors can least control. Business & governance rates sponsor and governance risk Medium: no controlling holder is disclosed, so the board alone chooses returns, it added USD 400 million of repurchase capacity in September 2026, and finance leadership changed in 2026. The issuer states ratings of Ba1 from Moody's and BB+ from S&P and Fitch, which this analysis does not rely on or replace. The creditor consequence is that policy can change faster than the credit agreement protects against, and the restricted-payment baskets were not supplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 45; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is that the issuer's own leverage is moderate and the business is cash generative. Net leverage is 3.59x on adjusted EBITDA, the reported first-lien ratio is 2.0x against a 5.0x maximum, free cash flow is positive after development advances, United States RevPAR rose 6% in the third quarter of 2026, the development pipeline is a record, the first large maturity is the USD 500 million notes in August 2028 with only USD 23 million due within a year, and the buybacks that hold leverage up are discretionary. The view would improve if distributions stayed at or below free cash flow for four consecutive quarters with net debt falling below USD 2,656 million, if royalties and franchise fees rose in dollars with rooms, and if the add-backs other than Revo proved small against cash flow. It would worsen if a second large franchisee failed, if RevPAR fell in two consecutive quarters, if the reported first-lien ratio moved towards 5.0x, or if the 2028 notes and the swap expiries approached without a refinancing and hedging plan.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wh-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C4

### Limitations

- Unadjusted EBITDA is calculated because the issuer states none, and the covenant EBITDA is defined in an agreement that was not supplied, so the range of 4.09x to 5.48x is not a covenant measure and the 2.0x covenant ratio cannot be reconciled to it; this limits the leverage conclusion.
- The floating interest rate in force is not stated, so the cost of the term loan B after the 2027 and 2028 swap expiries is shown only as a USD 15.00 million sensitivity per 100 bp and not as a forecast; this limits the refinancing conclusion.
- The downside scenario assumes a 10% fall in royalties and franchise fees and no change in costs or tax; the filings give no management projections, so the scenario sizes a sensitivity and is not a forecast.
- No peer or sector benchmark was supplied, so the business profile is judged against the issuer's own history and the sector risks it describes; relative strength against other franchisors is not established.
- Cash interest paid fell in 2026 for reasons the filings do not give, so cover on cash interest is treated as flattered and net interest expense is used for the coverage conclusion.
- The amount of franchisee loans the issuer guarantees is not stated, so exposure to franchisee failure beyond the development advance notes and the Revo write-off is not established.

Agreements run 10 to 20 years, more than six thousand franchisees pay royalties, none exceeds 2% of hotels, and free cash flow of USD 330 million over the last twelve months was 44.59% of adjusted EBITDA after development advances. This supports debt service through a slow year.

### Analysis: company and operating model

Wyndham franchises 25 hotel brands, mostly in the economy, midscale and upper midscale chain scales, and earns royalties of about 5% of gross room revenue, marketing and reservation fees, loyalty income, licence fees from the former parent and partner fees under agreements of 10 to 20 years. Only 53.39% of FY2025 net revenues is profit-bearing fee income, because the marketing, reservation and loyalty stream is passed through at close to break-even. The credit link is that cash flow is visible and recurring, but it is earned on a revenue line that overstates fee income and rests on room revenue at hotels that others own.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_wh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-12-31_wh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_wh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 80; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C11

### Analysis: ownership and group structure

No sponsor or controlling holder is disclosed, so Wyndham Hotels & Resorts, Inc. is a widely held public company and the borrower. Certain wholly owned domestic subsidiaries guarantee the credit facilities, which carry first-priority liens over substantially all assets and make up USD 1,574 million, or 57.72%, of the USD 2,727 million of debt, while the notes are unsecured and their guarantors are not named in the sources. The former parent pays licence fees equal to 7.07% of FY2025 net revenues and bears two-thirds of separation liabilities, and Business & governance rates sponsor and governance risk Medium. The credit link is that the unsecured notes sit behind the secured facilities and that the owner of the cash flow is the board, not a sponsor.
