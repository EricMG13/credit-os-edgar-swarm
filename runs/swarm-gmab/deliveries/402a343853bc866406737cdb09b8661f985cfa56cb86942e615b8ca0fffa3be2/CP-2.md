## Findings

### Bottom line

Genmab is an adequate but concentrated credit: one royalty stream carries USD 4,992 million of Merus acquisition debt, and it begins to erode in 2029, so creditors depend on deleveraging well before then. Net debt fell to USD 3,132 million at 30 September 2026 and nine-month EBITDA covered cash interest 4.53x, yet total debt is still 4.78x last-twelve-months operating profit, a proxy that sits below EBITDA.
Basis: calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C6; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The primary downside path is a fourth-quarter and 2027 launch-spending step-up that outpaces royalty growth, because guidance implies fourth-quarter adjusted operating profit between USD -43 million and USD 277 million against USD 452 million in the third quarter. The profile would be called stable only once gross leverage is on a path to the management target of below 3x by December 2027, which the nine-month repayment pace alone does not achieve.
Basis: calculation C14; calculation C16; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-05_genmabinterimreportq32026.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 6–7

### Key findings

Revenue quality is high today but finite, because one product family generates most of it and its patents expire from 2029. Revenue was USD 3,238 million in nine months, up 21.64%, and DARZALEX royalties were USD 2,057 million, or 63.53% of revenue, up 17.95% on the prior year. Johnson & Johnson develops, sells and bears the costs of the product, so Genmab earns the royalty without selling or manufacturing cost, which is why it converts to cash and also why Genmab cannot steer it. US patent rights end in 2029, European rights in 2031 and Japanese rights begin to expire in 2030, and the annual filing expects royalties to fall materially from 2029, so the debt has to be paid down before the royalty base shrinks.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-05_genmabinterimreportq32026.htm.jsonl, pages 39–40; 20-F_2025-12-31_gmab-20251231_d2.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11; calculation C1

Leverage is the main financial constraint and cannot be stated on EBITDA, because 2025 depreciation and amortization are not supplied. Total debt of USD 4,992 million is 4.78x last-twelve-months operating profit of USD 1,044 million and net debt of USD 3,132 million is 3.00x; on the company's adjusted operating profit of USD 1,356 million the multiples are 3.68x and 2.31x. Management targets gross leverage below 3x within two years of the December 2025 closing. At the nine-month repayment pace of USD 288 million, debt would be about USD 4,512 million by the end of 2027, which is 3.33x adjusted profit and 4.32x reported profit at today's levels, so the target needs profit growth of 10.91% on the adjusted measure or 44.06% on reported profit, or faster repayment.
Basis: calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C16; 6-K-EX-99.1_2025-11-06_genmabinterimreportq32025.htm.jsonl, page 3; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Cash conversion is uneven, and the nine-month result is flattered by a low tax charge. Operating cash flow was USD 574 million against USD 885 million a year earlier, with only USD 3 million in the first quarter and USD 520 million in the third, because receivables, inventories, contract liabilities and other payables together absorbed USD 354 million. Taxes paid fell to USD 97 million from USD 317 million and the estimated tax rate dropped to 3.4%, which the company says is not indicative of future periods; at 18.9%, the prior-year rate, tax expense would have been USD 111.65 million higher over the nine months. Free cash flow of USD 555 million was 51.97% of EBITDA.
Basis: calculation C7; calculation C17; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-05_genmabinterimreportq32026.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 28, 35; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-05-07_genmabinterimreportq12026.htm.jsonl, page 3; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Debt service is covered with room to spare today, but part of the room is being used for share purchases. Free cash flow, which is after interest, covered nine-month principal repayments of USD 288 million 1.93x and left USD 171 million once USD 96 million of share purchases were paid, and cash rose to USD 1,860 million. A buy-back that runs alongside repayment while leverage is above the management target is a financial-policy choice, and the restricted-payment limits that govern it sit in debt documents that are not supplied.
Basis: calculation C9; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-05_genmabinterimreportq32026.htm.jsonl, pages 35, 44–45; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Liquidity is adequate for the next year, but the refinancing profile cannot be placed. Cash of USD 1,860 million, all bank deposits, is 5.85x current borrowings of USD 318 million, which are the current portions of the Term A and Term B loans, and the notes have no current portion. A USD 500 million revolving facility was arranged in December 2025, but the borrowings note shows no revolver balance and its availability, maturity and conditions are not stated, and the annual filing also refers to an unsecured three-year revolving facility whose size is not stated. Maturity dates, coupons and covenant levels of the term loans and notes are not supplied; the company states that it complied with the Term A covenants at 30 September 2026.
Basis: calculation C10; calculation C22; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-05_genmabinterimreportq32026.htm.jsonl, pages 35, 45–46; 20-F_2025-12-31_gmab-20251231_d2.htm.jsonl, pages 126, 131

The interest burden is manageable and market access looks open. Nine-month cash interest was USD 236 million, covered 4.53x by EBITDA of USD 1,068 million, and the implied cost of debt is about 6.65% on average borrowings. Four interest rate swaps of USD 406 million each, running to 30 June 2028, fix 62.46% of the term loans at carrying value and leave USD 976 million floating, so a rise of 100 bp adds about USD 9.76 million a year. The June 2026 cut in the Term B margin from 3.00% to 2.00% saves about USD 19 million a year on USD 1,900 million and suggests lenders accepted a lower spread six months after closing.

Margins are under pressure from launch preparation, and management's levers are real but costly. Revenue rose 21.64% but operating profit fell to USD 986 million from USD 1,007 million, an operating margin of 30.45% against 37.83%, because adjusted operating expenses rose 27.33% (research and development 24.09%, selling, general and administrative 35.65%) as petosemtamab and Rina-S trials and launch teams were funded, and acquisition and integration charges added USD 87 million. Capital expenditure was only USD 19 million, so flexibility sits in research, selling and administrative spend, and cutting it would weaken the pipeline that has to replace DARZALEX royalties.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C20; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-05_genmabinterimreportq32026.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 23–24, 34

Either the range is conservative or launch-related spending steps up sharply.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a better view is that the royalty base is still growing fast and the debt is being repaid from cash flow. DARZALEX royalties rose 17.95%, Kesimpta royalties rose to USD 408 million from USD 320 million, EPKINLY global net sales rose 47% to USD 491 million, net debt fell USD 427 million in nine months, the Term B margin was cut from 3.00% to 2.00%, and three Phase 3 trials read out positively in October 2026. The leverage proxies also overstate leverage, because operating profit is struck after depreciation and amortization of USD 82 million in nine months, which EBITDA adds back, and management's adjusted profit gives 3.68x gross, within reach of the target with moderate growth.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C3; calculation C5; calculation C13; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-05_genmabinterimreportq32026.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 20

The view would improve if the debt documents show maturities beyond 2029, covenant headroom and tight restricted-payment limits, if fourth-quarter operating cash flow returns to the third-quarter level of USD 520 million, or if the launches of epcoritamab, Rina-S or petosemtamab add royalty-independent profit before 2029. It would worsen if growth in DARZALEX net sales falls below the 19% of nine months, if biosimilar or patent events reduce royalties before 2029, if first-quarter-style operating cash flow of USD 3 million recurs, if share purchases continue while leverage stays near today's 4.78x, or if the AbbVie or Chugai cases are decided against Genmab.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C4; calculation C7; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-05_genmabinterimreportq32026.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 33–34; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Limitations

- Maturity dates, coupons, guarantor structure and covenant levels for the term loans and notes are not supplied, so the conclusions that liquidity is adequate for a year and that refinancing cannot be placed rest on the borrowings note alone; a maturity inside 2028 would turn the liquidity view negative and wide covenant headroom would lift it.
- The Annual Report 2025 is not supplied, so EBITDA for 2025 and for the last twelve months cannot be built; the conclusion that leverage is the main financial constraint rests on operating-profit proxies, and true EBITDA leverage is lower by an amount that is not established.
- The first-half 2026 report is not supplied, so second-quarter cash flow is derived by subtraction; the conclusion that cash conversion is uneven depends on the derived USD 51 million for the second quarter, and a different figure would change the quarterly pattern but not the nine-month total.
- The fourth-quarter conclusions are arithmetic on company guidance, so the conclusion that spending steps up sharply in the fourth quarter would fall away if the range proves conservative.
- No agency rating, market price or peer data is supplied, so the conclusion that market access looks open rests on one margin cut, and the comparison with typical leveraged issuers is qualitative.
- The drawn amount and availability of the USD 500 million revolving facility are not stated, so liquidity could be higher by up to that amount if it is undrawn and available, which would strengthen the liquidity conclusion.

Basis: calculation C6; calculation C9

Near-term liquidity is sound: cash of USD 1,860 million is 5.85x current borrowings and no note has a current portion, so scheduled repayment in the next twelve months is small against cash on hand.

Royalties were USD 2,699 million, or 83.35% of nine-month revenue, and net product sales were USD 410 million, mostly EPKINLY in the US and Japan at USD 381 million. ### Revenue, cost and cash conversion

Revenue is recurring, partner-controlled and finite. DARZALEX net sales by J&J were USD 12,410 million in nine months, up 19%, and the company guides 2026 DARZALEX royalties at about USD 2.83 billion at the midpoint. The royalty tiers run from 12% to 20% of annual net sales and are reduced country by country on patent expiry and on the first biosimilar sale, so pricing power and the pace of decline sit with J&J, not with Genmab. Kesimpta and TEPEZZA add diversification, and EPKINLY net sales grew 47% on demand in later-line lymphoma and second-line follicular lymphoma, but none of them yet offsets DARZALEX.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-05_genmabinterimreportq32026.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 6, 20; 20-F_2025-12-31_gmab-20251231_d2.htm.jsonl, page 10; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Costs are mostly discretionary research, selling and administrative spend, which was 58.55% of nine-month revenue against 55.94% a year earlier. The variable costs are the AbbVie profit share and royalty expense of USD 29 million, which grow with EPKINLY sales, and operating leverage is currently negative because launch preparation for Rina-S and petosemtamab and the Merus integration add cost before revenue. Foreign exchange was a net loss of USD 10 million in nine months, so currency is not a main driver of margins.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-05_genmabinterimreportq32026.htm.jsonl, pages 23–24, 26; calculation C20

Capital expenditure was USD 19 million in nine months, while interest on borrowings took USD 229 million of cash, which did not exist a year earlier, taxes took USD 97 million, and working capital took USD 354 million as receivables from collaboration partners rose to USD 992 million from USD 907 million. Free cash flow of USD 555 million, 51.97% of EBITDA, therefore depends on partner collection timing and on a tax rate that is not durable.

### Market forces

Rivalry and substitution are the main forces. DARZALEX competes in multiple myeloma with CAR-T therapies and bispecific antibodies, including J&J's own TECVAYLI and TALVEY, on which Genmab also earns royalties that were not material in nine months, so substitution inside the J&J portfolio offsets little; epcoritamab competes with Regeneron's odronextamab and Roche's mosunetuzumab and glofitamab. Because royalties fall country by country once a biosimilar is sold, biosimilar entry, not only patent expiry, can end the royalty.
Basis: 20-F_2025-12-31_gmab-20251231_d2.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 83–84; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-05_genmabinterimreportq32026.htm.jsonl, page 20

Buyer power and policy are the second force. Payers set coverage, the Inflation Reduction Act lets Medicare negotiate prices, DARZALEX was not among the first ten drugs negotiated, and most-favoured-nation targets apply to biologics without biosimilar competition; the effect on royalties is uncertain, but a lower net price lowers the royalty in proportion.
Basis: 20-F_2025-12-31_gmab-20251231_d2.htm.jsonl, pages 45, 49

Supplier and partner power matter more than usual. J&J controls DARZALEX, AbbVie shares EPKINLY profits and is also litigating against Genmab over Rina-S, and the contract manufacturer for Rina-S is exposed to US action against Chinese biotechnology companies, which could delay a launch. New entrants matter little within the debt tenor.
Basis: 20-F_2025-12-31_gmab-20251231_d2.htm.jsonl, page 20; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-05_genmabinterimreportq32026.htm.jsonl, pages 23, 33–34

Other macro factors are not a primary credit driver: tariffs and exchange rates are cited, but the net foreign exchange loss was USD 10 million in nine months, so political and economic factors matter through drug pricing and manufacturing only.
Basis: 20-F_2025-12-31_gmab-20251231_d2.htm.jsonl, page 16; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-05_genmabinterimreportq32026.htm.jsonl, page 26

The opportunities that could improve credit quality are the potential launches in 2027 of epcoritamab in earlier lines, Rina-S in ovarian cancer and petosemtamab in head and neck cancer, after positive topline results in October 2026. The threats are earlier royalty erosion, pricing regulation, the AbbVie and Chugai litigation and the integration of Merus, which was 59.6% of total assets at 31 December 2025.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-05_genmabinterimreportq32026.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 4; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Downside scenario

The downside that matters is a slowdown in DARZALEX royalties while launch spending is already committed. The initiating condition is growth in partner net sales slowing from 19% toward zero through competition from CAR-T therapies and bispecifics, price regulation or an early biosimilar. The first operational break is margin: royalties carry almost no cost, so each 10% fall in guided DARZALEX royalties of about USD 2.83 billion removes about USD 283 million of operating profit, which is 26.01% of the guided 2026 midpoint of USD 1,088 million, while spending for Rina-S and petosemtamab continues.
Basis: calculation C15; calculation C18; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-05_genmabinterimreportq32026.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 6

The financial transmission is leverage and cash: gross debt of USD 4,992 million would be 6.20x and net debt 3.89x the reduced profit of USD 805 million, and free cash flow, USD 555 million in nine months, would shrink toward principal repayments of USD 288 million. Liquidity holds for a year because cash of USD 1,860 million is 5.85x current borrowings, but refinancing capacity would then depend on pipeline value and lender appetite rather than on royalty growth. The levers are real, since research, selling and administrative spend of USD 1,896 million over nine months is discretionary and share purchases of USD 96 million can stop, but using them delays the launches that must replace DARZALEX royalties. Resilience looks sufficient to avoid a liquidity crisis within a year and insufficient to meet the leverage target.
Basis: calculation C9; calculation C10; calculation C18; calculation C20

A failure of petosemtamab would not move cash, yet in-process research and development of USD 8,237 million exceeds shareholders' equity of USD 6,333 million, so a full write-off would eliminate equity; whether any covenant tests equity is not established.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-05_genmabinterimreportq32026.htm.jsonl, pages 34–35, 40–41; calculation C19

### Overall credit view

Overall, Genmab presents an adequate business profile supported by growing royalty income that carries no Genmab cost, interest cover of 4.53x and cash of USD 1,860 million, but credit quality is constrained by concentration in DARZALEX royalties, leverage of 4.78x last-twelve-months operating profit and unverified debt documents. Cash-flow durability appears adequate rather than strong because operating cash flow fell to USD 574 million and guidance implies a heavy fourth quarter. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on debt maturities that are not supplied, on the lender appetite shown by the June 2026 margin cut and on launch success before 2029. The primary downside path is a DARZALEX royalty slowdown while launch spending is committed. Further analysis would require the Annual Report 2025, the credit agreement and indentures, the first-half 2026 report, agency views and peer data. Top risk: DARZALEX royalty erosion from 2029 before leverage has fallen below the management target.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C4; calculation C6; calculation C7; calculation C13; Financial foundation (CP-1)
