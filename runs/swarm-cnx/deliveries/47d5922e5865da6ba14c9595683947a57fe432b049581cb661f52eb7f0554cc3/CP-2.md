## Findings

### Bottom line

CNX's credit profile is sound for a gas producer, with low leverage, wide interest cover, ample undrawn liquidity and no principal due before 2029, so near-term creditor risk is low; the main constraint is capital-return policy that has outrun free cash flow.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C3

At 2026-09-30 net debt was 1.80x issuer-adjusted EBITDA and 1.25x reported EBITDA, adjusted EBITDA covered cash interest 7.99x, and USD 1,673.05 million of revolver capacity was undrawn against USD 1.98 million of cash. Nine-month share repurchases of USD 432.78 million exceeded issuer-defined free cash flow of USD 402.61 million, which is the path by which leverage could rise without an earnings change.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C4; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Key findings

Leverage and cover are low on every measure the sources support, but reported ratios swing with hedge marks. Net debt was 1.25x reported EBITDA and 1.80x adjusted EBITDA at 2026-09-30, against 4.16x on reported EBITDA at FY2024, when derivative losses depressed earnings. The adjusted measure is the steadier guide to debt service, and on it cash interest is covered 7.99x.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C2; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Liquidity is strong and rests on undrawn revolvers, not cash. Cash was USD 1.98 million, and undrawn capacity was USD 1,673.05 million, of which USD 533 million belongs to the midstream subsidiary's facility alone; both revolvers mature on 2029-05-17 and no principal falls due before FY2029. The consequence is that near-term obligations are covered by committed lines, with the 2029 revolver maturity the first test.
Basis: calculation C3; calculation C9; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Earnings are tied to one commodity in one basin, hedged most heavily for 2026. Natural gas is 92% of sales volumes and the issuer states that 82% of expected 2026 gas volumes are hedged, so cash flow is best protected in 2026 and more exposed later. Fixed gas transportation and processing commitments of USD 1,237.86 million raise operating leverage if prices or volumes fall.
Basis: calculation C6; calculation C7; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Capital return is the policy risk. Repurchases of USD 432.78 million in the nine months exceeded issuer-defined free cash flow of USD 402.61 million, and net revolver borrowings of USD 67.10 million covered the excess; no dividend is paid. The consequence is that leverage depends on a board choice, with USD 1,991.71 million of repurchase authority still open.
Basis: calculation C4; calculation C8; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Market access was demonstrated in 2026 and maturities were extended. The company issued USD 500 million of senior notes due March 2034 and repurchased USD 500 million of senior notes due January 2029, and settled the convertible notes in shares. The consequence is a ladder with USD 299.85 million due in FY2029 and the notes beginning in FY2030.
Basis: calculation C5; calculation C9; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a sound profile is that earnings quality is weaker than the ratios suggest: reported EBITDA of USD 1,820 million over the last twelve months includes unrealised derivative gains of USD 595 million that the issuer removes to reach USD 1,262 million, FY2024 reported leverage of 4.16x shows how fast the ratio moves when hedge marks reverse, and the hedge book thins after 2026 while fixed transport commitments stay. The view would weaken if repurchases keep exceeding free cash flow while revolver borrowings rise, if gas prices fall as hedges roll off, or if Core stops honouring its indemnity on the coal liabilities. It would strengthen if repurchases fall below free cash flow and the executed debt documents show no limit that repurchases approach.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C5; calculation C6; calculation C7; calculation C10; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

  Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1)
- No agency rating, bond price or spread is supplied, so the conclusion that market access is strong rests on the 2026 issuance alone and not on observed pricing.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 40; Source readiness (CP-0)

  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 182–183
- The downside scenario is qualitative and issuer-specific, so it supports a view on liquidity and leverage direction but no probability of default or recovery estimate.
  Basis: calculation C10

### Strengths and weaknesses

The first strength is low leverage and wide cover, because net debt of 1.80x adjusted EBITDA and cash interest cover of 7.99x leave room to absorb an earnings fall before debt service is stressed.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C2; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The second strength is liquidity depth and distant maturities, because USD 1,673.05 million of undrawn revolver capacity and no principal before FY2029 mean obligations are covered by committed lines.
Basis: calculation C3; calculation C9; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The third strength is demonstrated market access, because the 2026 issue of notes due March 2034 and the repurchase of notes due January 2029 extended the ladder, and the convertible notes were settled in shares, not cash.
Basis: calculation C5; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The fourth strength is hedge-protected 2026 cash flow and a modest unit cost, because 82% of expected 2026 gas volumes are hedged and lifting cost was $0.15 per Mcfe against a realised price of $2.75 per Mcfe in FY2025.
Basis: calculation C7; 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 15; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The fifth strength is an owned gathering system and an operated reserve base, because 99.1% of reserves are operated and 72.2% proved developed, which gives control over development timing and takeaway.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 17; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The first weakness is single-commodity, single-basin exposure with earnings that swing on hedge marks, because natural gas is 92% of volumes and FY2024 reported leverage of 4.16x fell to 1.25x as hedge marks moved from losses to gains.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C7; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The second weakness is capital return above free cash flow, because USD 432.78 million of repurchases exceeded USD 402.61 million of free cash flow and the shortfall was met from the revolver.
Basis: calculation C4; calculation C8; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The third weakness is fixed commitments, because USD 1,237.86 million of gas transportation and processing obligations persist if volumes fall.
Basis: calculation C6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 65

The fourth weakness is legacy coal liabilities that depend on Core's indemnity, because a USD 75 million settlement is reimbursed by Core and a Coal Act suit remains with no accrual.
Basis: calculation C11; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The fifth weakness is incomplete evidence on priority and control, because the debt documents, proxy statement and ratings are not supplied and USD 467.00 million of midstream debt sits outside the parent's guarantee.
Basis: calculation C12; Source readiness (CP-0); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Company description

CNX is an independent natural gas producer in the Appalachian Basin with owned gathering, producing from Marcellus and Utica shale and Virginia coalbed methane; 92% of sales volumes are natural gas and 94% come from Shale. Its cash flows are therefore a function of regional gas prices, volumes and the hedge book.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10

### Ownership and group structure

The common stock is listed on the New York Stock Exchange and its holders are not established, because the proxy statement is not supplied. Debt sits at two levels: the parent's revolver and notes are guaranteed by most subsidiaries, while the midstream subsidiary's USD 467.00 million of notes and revolver are outside the parent's guarantee and outside the parent's covenant ratios.
Basis: calculation C12; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Substantially all gas is sold at market prices under short-term contracts, so CNX has no pricing power beyond basis and marketing choices; realised price including hedge settlements was $2.75 per Mcfe in FY2025 against $2.66 per Mcfe in FY2024, and three buyers each took more than 10% of customer revenue in FY2025. The creditor consequence is price-taker revenue with some buyer concentration.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 164–166

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Lifting cost was $0.15 per Mcfe in FY2025 against a realised price of $2.75 per Mcfe, but transportation, gathering and compression of USD 383.10 million and fixed firm transport commitments mean a large share of cost does not fall with price. Margins therefore move mainly with price and hedge settlements, which turned from a gain in FY2024 to a loss in FY2025.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 114; calculation C6

### Capital intensity and free cash flow conversion

Capital expenditure of USD 451.06 million in the nine months to 2026-09-30 took more than half of operating cash flow of USD 810.56 million, leaving USD 359.50 million before asset sale proceeds and issuer-defined free cash flow of USD 402.61 million after them. Conversion is adequate, and the capital programme is guided inside USD 571 million to USD 586 million for 2026.
Basis: calculation C4; Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-10-29_ex991supplementalq326.htm.jsonl, page 7

### Business risk frameworks

Competition in a commodity market is on cost and takeaway, where CNX's owned gathering and low lifting cost are advantages; regulation of emissions and pipelines and the climate policy path are the political and environmental pressures the filings name; buyer power is moderate, because gas is sold at market prices to many marketers and utilities, although three buyers each took more than 10% of FY2025 customer revenue. In SWOT terms the strengths are cost, liquidity and access, and the weaknesses are commodity concentration, capital return and legacy liabilities, which the ranked lists above translate into credit terms.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 37–39; 10-K_2025-12-31_cnx-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 55–57; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Financial profile and credit quality

On the nine dimensions the evidence supports these readings: scale and market position average, because output is concentrated in one basin and no peer comparison is supplied; competitive advantage average, resting on lifting cost and owned gathering; business diversification weak, because 92% of volumes are natural gas and 94% come from one segment; cost and capex flexibility average, because capital expenditure is guided inside a range while USD 1,237.86 million of transport commitments are fixed; margin stability average, because hedge marks and price swing reported earnings; free cash flow stability average, because repurchases and asset sales move it; ability to refinance and access capital markets strong, because 2026 issuance extended maturities and no principal is due before FY2029; liquidity position strong, because USD 1,673.05 million is undrawn; and financial policy and governance average, because repurchases exceeded free cash flow while holders are not established.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C6; calculation C7; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months the issuer's 2026 guidance of 612.5 Bcfe to 617.5 Bcfe and hedged volumes support cash flow, while headwinds are the roll-off of hedges, with hedged volumes lower in each year from 2027 to 2029, normal production declines that cut nine-month volumes, and a repurchase pace above free cash flow. Beyond that horizon no source states a plan, so the medium-term view is not established.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-29_ex991supplementalq326.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 54; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 75; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a sustained fall in Appalachian gas prices as hedges roll off. Hedged volumes are largest for 2026 and lower in each later year, so the first operational break is lower realised prices on unhedged volumes from 2027. Fixed transport commitments and interest do not fall with price, so EBITDA and free cash flow fall faster than revenue, and leverage rises further if repurchases continue to be funded from the revolver. Liquidity would absorb the shock because USD 1,673.05 million is undrawn and no principal is due before FY2029, so resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress; headroom against the covenant maximum cannot be tested without the executed documents. The observable triggers are realised price below the FY2025 level, free cash flow below repurchases and undrawn capacity below its current level.
Basis: calculation C3; calculation C6; calculation C7; calculation C10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnx-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 75

### Overall credit view

Overall, CNX presents a sound business and financial profile supported by low leverage, undrawn liquidity and demonstrated market access, but credit quality is constrained by commodity concentration, capital return above free cash flow and legacy coal liabilities. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because hedges cover 2026. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on gas prices and the revolver maturity in 2029. The primary downside path is a gas price fall as hedges roll off while repurchases continue. Further analysis would require the executed debt documents, the proxy statement and agency ratings. Top risk: capital return funded by the revolver.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C3; calculation C4
