## Findings

### Bottom line

Encompass Health is a scaled, government-paid inpatient rehabilitation operator with no sponsor or controlling shareholder identified, so creditors face payment-policy and capital-allocation risk rather than ownership-extraction risk, though governance visibility is only moderate on the filings supplied.

Medicare and Medicare Advantage supplied 81.8% of net operating revenues in the nine months to 2026-09-30, and 92% of patients are referred from acute-care hospitals, so a payment-rule or referral shock would reach revenue directly. Growth depends on opening new capacity, and shareholder returns of USD 261.6 million in those nine months exceeded the cash left after capital spending and joint-venture distributions. We rate sponsor and governance risk Medium because disclosed behaviour is mixed and the proxy statement and executed debt documents are not supplied.

### Key findings

Payment policy, not demand, is the main business risk. Medicare and Medicare Advantage supplied 81.8% of net operating revenues in the nine months to 2026-09-30, with Medicare alone at 65.7% for the nine months and 66.3% in the third quarter. Rates are reset each year by the federal payer, and the final rule for fiscal 2027 adds a net 2.3% to Medicare payment rates from October 2026, which supports pricing. The same payer is widening claim review: a demonstration that puts claims through pre-claim or post-payment review reached Texas in March 2026 and California in May 2026, and from January 2027 will cover about 63% of the company's Medicare claims. Denied claims of USD 39.0 million were under review at 2025-12-31, and revenue reserves for bad debt rose to 2.7% of revenue in the third quarter from 1.9%, so slower or lower collection is where a payment-policy shock would first reach cash flow.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ehc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 30, 31, 33, 35; 10-K_2025-12-31_ehc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 124, 161; calculation C1

Referral and partner relationships concentrate operating risk in counterparties the filings do not quantify. About 92% of patients are admitted from acute-care hospitals, and 37.85% of hospitals are joint ventures with health systems that also refer patients. Partners hold noncontrolling interests of USD 821.4 million plus redeemable interests of USD 58.6 million, can require the company to buy certain interests at fair value, and received USD 141.8 million of distributions in the nine months to 2026-09-30. The company lists partner operating or financial strain as a risk, so a partner problem would be both a volume risk and a possible cash call.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ehc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 187; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ehc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 10, 12, 19; EX-99.2_2026-10-28_ehcq32026earningsslides_.htm.jsonl, page 8; calculation C2

Growth is capital-hungry, so cash flow is committed to expansion before it is available for debt reduction. The network added 16 hospitals between the end of 2023 and 2026-09-30, and new capacity contributed 2.9 percentage points of the 5.2% discharge growth in the nine months, which is more than half of it. The 2026 capital plan is USD 920 million to USD 995 million, of which USD 225 million to USD 240 million is maintenance, with about USD 535 million more to finish projects now under construction. The non-maintenance part is discretionary, which gives management a lever in a downturn, but it also means leverage falls only if growth spending slows or earnings keep outrunning it.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ehc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 36, 43; 10-K_2025-12-31_ehc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 8; EX-99.2_2026-10-28_ehcq32026earningsslides_.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 11; calculation C9; calculation C7; calculation C17

No sponsor, fund or controlling holder is identified, so sponsor-exit and fee-extraction risks are absent from the record, but the concentration of ownership is not established. The shares are listed on the New York Stock Exchange, non-affiliate market value was about USD 12.2 billion at 2025-06-30, the board chair is a director separate from the chief executive, related-party transactions were not material in 2025, 2024 or 2023, and the share count fell 1.30% between 2026-02-12 and 2026-10-22. The beneficial-ownership table sits in the 2026 proxy statement, which was not supplied, so a large holder with influence over distributions cannot be excluded.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ehc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 3, 132, 151, 157; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ehc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation C13

Shareholder returns are the creditor-relevant behaviour to watch, and they are rising while evidence of the legal room for them is not supplied. The quarterly dividend rose from USD 0.17 to USD 0.19 per share in July 2025 and to USD 0.21 in July 2026, repurchases rose from USD 31.1 million in FY2024 to USD 158.0 million in FY2025 and USD 203.7 million in the nine months to 2026-09-30, and the board raised the repurchase authorization to USD 1.0 billion in July 2026. Dividends and repurchases took 19.49% of operating cash flow in FY2025 and 27.32% in the nine months, and in the nine months they exceeded the USD 198.7 million left after capital spending and joint-venture distributions by USD 62.9 million. The March 2026 credit agreement also relaxed limits on investments, debt, liens and restricted payments, which widens the room for such returns, while no related-party fee, sponsor payment or creditor-adverse transaction is disclosed.

Disclosure supports monitoring but not yet a full governance read. The statements are audited by the same firm since 2003 with an opinion on internal control, the auditor named the valuation of patient receivables, including denied claims, as a critical audit matter, and the quarterly reports and slides reconcile the adjusted measures. Covenant compliance is stated without the ratios achieved, and directors' independence, compensation and beneficial ownership are in a proxy statement that was not supplied, so the Medium rating reflects the limits of the evidence as well as the behaviour observed.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ehc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 151, 160, 161, 162; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ehc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 40, 46

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against reading Medium as benign is that shareholder returns have begun to exceed the cash left after growth spending and partner distributions, in the same year lender restrictions were loosened and the repurchase authorization was raised to USD 1.0 billion. If the executed documents show wide restricted-payment capacity, that pattern would be a deliberate policy of leaning on the revolver and bond market, and the view would move to higher risk, although the arithmetic is thin, because the shortfall of USD 62.9 million compares with operating cash flow of USD 957.7 million. The view would move to Low if the proxy statement shows independent oversight and dispersed ownership, returns fall back below free cash flow and leverage keeps falling. A partner asking the company to buy its interest, or claim review delaying collections beyond the bad-debt reserve already taken, would also change the view because either reaches cash flow without any change in policy.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ehc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 18, 44; calculation C11; calculation C10

### Limitations

- Board independence, committee structure, compensation design and beneficial ownership are not established because the 2026 proxy statement was not supplied, so the Medium governance rating could move either way once it is read.
- The executed credit agreement and indentures were not supplied, so legal capacity for dividends, repurchases, investments and liens rests on the company's own summaries, which limits how firmly distributions can be called a policy choice rather than a constraint.
- Geographic concentration in Florida and Texas is measured by hospital and bed counts, not revenue, so the concentration finding could be understated if those hospitals earn more than the average hospital.
- The effect of expanded claim review on collections cannot be quantified from the filings, so the payment-policy risk is stated as a direction and is not sized.
- Growth targets, opening dates and guidance are management statements and tentative, so the conclusion that growth is funded from cash flow and the revolver describes a plan, not an outcome.
- Partner put-right terms and timing are disclosed only as a fair-value formula, so the size and timing of any cash call from a partner exit is not established.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

No sponsor transaction, buyout, merger or acquisition is described in the sources, and none is pending; the 2026 activity is refinancing and a small disposal. On March 9, 2026 the company entered a new credit agreement with Truist Bank as agent that replaced the 2022 agreement, moved the revolver maturity to March 2031, lowered the fee on undrawn commitments by 5 bp, relaxed limits on investments, debt, liens and restricted payments, and raised the swingline sublimit from USD 25 million to USD 40 million. It drew USD 250.0 million on the revolver that month to retire the old facility.
