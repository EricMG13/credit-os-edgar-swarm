## Findings

### Bottom line

Hni Corp is an average-quality industrial credit in transition: a larger, cash-generative furniture and hearth business whose leverage is still elevated after Steelcase and whose free cash flow after dividends is thin, so creditors depend on delivered synergies to reduce debt.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B16; calculation B5

Net debt was USD 1,244.2 million at 2026-10-03, or 3.07x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA of USD 404.7 million, backed by USD 490.3 million of cash and undrawn revolver and scheduled principal of no more than USD 22.5 million a year until 2029. Free cash flow of USD 132.7 million leaves only USD 39.5 million after dividends, and the maximum covenant ratio falls from 4.25x to 3.50x within seven full quarters of closing, so the next four quarters of margin recovery and debt paydown decide whether the view improves.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B5; calculation B2; calculation B12; calculation B32; calculation B14; calculation B16; calculation B48

### Key findings

The business is large, spread across dealer and distributor channels and light on customer concentration, which supports revenue durability but does not remove cyclicality. Last-twelve-months net sales were USD 5,301.3 million and the five largest customers were about 13% of net sales, yet demand follows corporate office spending, which hybrid working keeps below historic occupancy, and housing activity for the hearth business. The company says backlog orders are typically cancellable and fulfilled within a few months and gives no backlog amount, so revenue visibility is short and rests on channel mix and management comment.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2026-01-03_hni-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 16–18, 85; calculation B1

Leverage is elevated and its fall so far comes from the earnings window, not from debt repayment. Net debt over reported EBITDA fell from 6.19x at 2026-04-04 to 3.07x at 2026-10-03, but net debt itself is USD 160.3 million above year-end and the window replaces pre-acquisition quarters with Steelcase quarters; on EBITDA before acquisition costs, restructuring and the inventory step-up, an analyst judgement, the ratio is 2.13x. The year-end ratio of 4.65x is the less reliable because it set full acquisition debt against under four weeks of Steelcase.

Operating cash flow is seasonal, with an outflow in the first quarter and USD 195.3 million in the third quarter alone, so a single quarter overstates or understates the run rate.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better view is that reported leverage on EBITDA before one-offs is 2.13x, that the third-quarter margin has returned to the prior-year level, that the equity is 58.07% of book capitalization and that no debt matures in size before 2029. Against that, goodwill and intangible assets are 36.08% of total assets and leave tangible equity of only USD 76.7 million, the added-back items recur in part, with restructuring and impairment charges of USD 51.9 million in the last twelve months, and the Q3 margin includes tariff refunds of unstated size.

The view would improve if net debt fell below the year-end level of USD 1,083.9 million while last-twelve-months EBITDA held above USD 404.7 million, if free cash flow after dividends rose well above USD 39.5 million and if the 2029 notes were refinanced early. It would weaken if net sales fell year on year in the legacy business for two quarters, if the quarterly EBITDA margin dropped below the prior-year quarter, if repurchases were funded with revolver borrowings above the USD 169.0 million seasonal peak, or if the executed credit documents showed covenant headroom thinner than the house measures suggest.

### Limitations

- No credit rating, market price, peer set or lender presentation was supplied, so the comparison with typical leveraged issuers in the sector and any market-implied view are not established, and the Average overall reading rests on absolute measures of scale, margin, cash flow and liquidity.
- The credit agreement's Consolidated EBITDA and covenant calculations were not supplied, so the stress case compares house leverage with covenant levels only directionally and neither a breach nor comfortable headroom can be concluded.
- Steelcase results before 2025-12-10 and any pro forma EBITDA were not supplied, so last-twelve-months EBITDA mixes perimeters and true run-rate leverage may be better or worse than 3.07x.
- The size of third-quarter tariff refunds is not stated, so how much of the margin recovery will repeat is not established and the second-quarter estimate stays an analyst inference.
- No backlog amount, order intake or book-to-bill was stated, and the annual report says backlog is cancellable and shipped within a few months, so revenue durability beyond the current quarter rests on channel mix and management comment and could change if order data showed a different trend.

### Company description and credit linkage

HNI makes workplace furnishings, sold mainly through independent and company-owned dealers and office product distributors, and hearth products, sold through dealers, distributors and company-owned outlets; the company describes itself as the market leader in workplace furnishings and the leading hearth maker in North America, which is management language. After the Steelcase acquisition on 2025-12-10, Workplace Furnishings was 88.99% of nine-month net sales and Residential Building Products 11.01%, so cash flow now depends mostly on office spending, which is discretionary and project-based, while hearth earns 31.19% of segment operating income on housing and remodel demand.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-03_hni-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 3–9, 16, 85; 10-Q_2026-10-03_hni-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 25, 30; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B55; calculation B56; calculation B22

For creditors the mix means cash flow visibility is moderate: sales are project-based and dealer-led, the filings give no backlog amount and say backlog is typically cancellable and fulfilled within a few months, and the integration of Steelcase through 2026 and the planned network optimization to 2028 will absorb cash before they add to it. The facilities are concentrated, with some production sites, people and key management located in a small area of eastern Iowa near the Mississippi River and several single-site plants, which makes a flood or storm a risk to production and cash flow.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-03_hni-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 20–22; 10-Q_2026-10-03_hni-20261003.htm.jsonl, page 30

### Ownership, group structure and financial policy

HNI is a listed company with no identified sponsor or controlling holder, one executive holds the Chairman, President and Chief Executive Officer roles with a named Lead Director, and Business & governance rated governance and financial-policy risk Medium. Debt sits at the parent with certain domestic subsidiaries as guarantors, while USD 99.0 million of Steelcase notes remain unsecured obligations of a subsidiary and USD 351.0 million were exchanged into HNI senior secured notes, so the two groups of holders have different claims and ranking against the bank debt is not established.

The financial policy evidence is mixed: acquisitions were repeated, the dividend and repurchase authorization were widened while net debt was higher than at year-end, but the 2024 repayment of USD 100 million of acquisition debt and the 55.03% share funding of the Steelcase price point to a tolerance for leverage that the Board has so far corrected after deals.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is cyclical and partly discretionary. Workplace furnishings sells through a contract channel of large, customised, lengthy projects and a small and medium-sized business channel driven by price and delivery, and hybrid working has left office occupancy below historic levels, which the company says has and could continue to reduce demand. Legacy volume in Workplace Furnishings was lower in the first half of 2026, when the company cited soft volume and geopolitical uncertainty, and legacy Workplace Furnishings net sales rose 5.86% in the third quarter, so the trend has turned but is one quarter old.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-03_hni-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 16; 10-Q_2026-04-04_hni-20260404.htm.jsonl, pages 24–25; 10-Q_2026-07-04_hni-20260704.htm.jsonl, pages 29–30; 10-Q_2026-10-03_hni-20261003.htm.jsonl, pages 30, 34; calculation B23

Pricing power is limited rather than absent: competitors offer similar products, price competition from lower-cost entrants constrains increases, and large customers and dealer consolidation add bargaining power, yet favourable price-cost was cited in the first and third quarters of 2026. In hearth, remodel-retrofit sales rose while new-home sales fell, so a housing slowdown would cut the higher-margin segment's volumes first.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins are defensible in the legacy business but not yet in the combined one. The last-twelve-months gross margin of 41.29% and selling and administrative expenses of 33.50% of third-quarter net sales leave little room if volume falls, and Workplace Furnishings operating margin was 9.29% in Q3 2026 against 12.09% a year earlier after USD 24.8 million of purchase accounting amortization and USD 10.0 million of restructuring. Management levers are lean manufacturing, the Mexico plant, network optimization to 2028, Steelcase synergies focused on the Americas, and variable compensation, which rose in 2026.

The filings give no split of fixed and variable cost, so operating leverage is bounded only by the gross margin: a lost dollar of sales removes at most about 41 cents of gross profit before any cost response, which is the basis of the downside case below.

Capital intensity is moderate and partly discretionary. Conversion of reported EBITDA to free cash flow was 32.79% over the last twelve months.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense and is the force that matters most to margins: large competitors named in the annual report sell similar products and compete on price, delivery and service, and installed bases give them repeat orders, so share loss or price concessions would hit gross margin directly. Buyer power is moderate because the five largest customers are about 13% of sales, but dealer consolidation and dealer insolvency, which the company says can cause credit losses, add working capital risk. Supplier power is limited by multiple sources of steel, aluminium, lumber and textiles, while tariffs and freight cost raise input costs; substitutes include remote work and, as the company notes, uncertain effects of artificial intelligence on work patterns and specification.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-03_hni-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 5–9, 17–20, 31–32

### Macro, policy and technology factors

These factors are material here. Office demand tracks employment, corporate profits and occupancy; hearth demand tracks housing starts, rates and affordability; the 2026 Supreme Court ruling against the emergency tariffs opened refunds that lifted second-quarter margins but make trade policy a source of volatile earnings; and the company states inflation, geopolitical uncertainty and housing softness shaped the third quarter. Each acts through lower volume or lower margin on a cost base that is larger after Steelcase, which is why they sit inside the downside case.

### Strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats

Strengths are scale, low customer concentration, a high-margin hearth business, positive free cash flow through the cycle and liquidity of USD 490.3 million. Weaknesses are elevated leverage, thin free cash flow after dividends, a margin still reset by purchase accounting and a heavy intangible balance. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are delivered Steelcase synergies, lower interest cost after the term loan B repricing and normalising capital expenditure. Threats are a demand slowdown before deleveraging, rate and tariff volatility, covenant step-downs and a refinancing of the 2029 notes into a weaker market.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over 12 to 24 months the tailwinds are Steelcase synergies that management calls slightly ahead of expectations, favourable price-cost, network optimization savings through 2028, the lower term loan B rate, and a Q3 margin already at the prior-year level. The headwinds are soft legacy volumes in the first half of 2026, housing affordability, geopolitical uncertainty, tariff refunds that will not repeat, net interest of USD 63.8 million in nine months 2026 against USD 18.9 million a year earlier and covenant step-downs. Over three to five years, management expects synergies to add to earnings over five years, while the 2029 notes, 2030 facilities and 2032 term loan must be refinanced and demand for offices remains uncertain.

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a slowdown in corporate office orders, hearth demand from housing, or both, on top of tariff and input-cost shocks. The first operational break is volume: with a gross margin of 41.29% and no stated fixed-cost split, a 10% fall in last-twelve-months net sales of USD 530.1 million would remove USD 218.9 million of gross profit if costs did not respond, cutting reported EBITDA to USD 185.8 million, lifting net debt over EBITDA to 6.70x and reducing EBITDA over net interest to 2.31x. If management offset half the loss through cost actions, EBITDA would be USD 295.2 million, leverage 4.21x and interest cover 3.67x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B34; calculation B35; calculation B36; calculation B37; calculation B38; calculation B39; calculation B40; calculation B41; calculation B42

The financial transmission then runs through cash: free cash flow after dividends of USD 39.5 million would turn negative, the revolver would be drawn from USD 380.0 million of undrawn capacity, and floating-rate interest on about 66.37% of principal would add to the burden if rates rose. The covenant maximum falls toward 3.50x on credit agreement definitions that were not supplied, so a covenant conversation would be the observable trigger, with dividend and repurchase cuts, capital expenditure of USD 125 million to USD 130 million and restructuring as the levers. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid a liquidity crisis in the half-offset case because liquidity of USD 490.3 million is large against scheduled principal, but protection of the 4.25x to 3.50x covenant path cannot be shown without action, because the covenant definitions were not supplied.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Hni Corp presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, low customer concentration, a high-margin hearth segment and consistent free cash flow, but credit quality is constrained by leverage that has not fallen in absolute terms, thin free cash flow after dividends and earnings still reset by Steelcase. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow was positive in FY2024, FY2025 and the last twelve months, though it is seasonal and thin after dividends. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on delivering EBITDA growth and net debt reduction before 2028, when the 2029 notes and springing maturities come into view. The primary downside path is a demand slowdown before synergies and paydown arrive. Further analysis would require the credit documents, a company EBITDA bridge, ratings, market prices and a peer set; a comparison with typical leveraged issuers in the sector is not established. Top risk: leverage staying near the covenant path while demand softens.
