## Findings

### Bottom line

Hertz's fundamental credit profile is weak: scale, matched vehicle funding and improving 2026 pricing support it, but negative adjusted EBITDA, negative free cash flow and thin corporate liquidity mean creditors depend on fleet collateral and refinancing access rather than on earnings.

Net debt was USD 17,462 million at 2026-09-30 (USD 17,381 million pro forma for the cash received in the October 2026 Oro Mobility joint venture), cash and available facilities were USD 946 million against USD 1,475 million of maturities for the rest of 2026, and free cash flow was USD -1,026 million in the nine months. The primary downside is a further fall in used-vehicle values, which would raise depreciation and collateral calls while corporate liquidity is already below its FY2024 and FY2025 levels.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 93; calculation F1; calculation F3; calculation F5; calculation F6

### Key findings

Earnings do not yet cover the cost of capital: Adjusted Corporate EBITDA was USD -1,541 million in FY2024 and USD -339 million in FY2025 on the earlier definition, giving interest cover of -1.93x and -0.34x, and although the revised definition shows USD 153 million for the nine months of 2026 against USD -82 million a year earlier, no comparable twelve-month figure exists, so leverage multiples are not meaningful.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F7; calculation F8

Cash generation is positive before fleet spending but negative after it: operating cash flow was USD 1,628 million in FY2025 and USD 1,000 million in the nine months of 2026, yet free cash flow was USD -366 million and USD -1,026 million, and net debt rose USD 973 million in the nine months, so the business consumes capital while it rebuilds earnings.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F5; calculation F9

The debt structure is more collateralised than the headline suggests: vehicle debt of USD 11,958 million is 0.92x the fleet carried at USD 12,967 million, so the earnings-dependent exposure is the non-vehicle debt of USD 6,035 million, which has risen from USD 5,104 million and was refinanced in 2026 with first-lien exchangeable notes at an effective rate of about 17.5%.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F10; calculation F11

Liquidity is the binding constraint: cash of USD 531 million and availability of USD 415 million total USD 946 million, down from USD 1,842 million at FY2024, against USD 1,475 million of maturities in the rest of 2026 and USD 2,038 million in 2027, nearly all vehicle debt, and the Class B vehicle commitment extended in November 2026 funds only if the senior notes due 2026 are repaid by 2026-12-31.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 60; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 93; calculation F3; calculation F4

Governance adds a creditor-alignment risk without clear offsetting controls: the Plan Sponsors hold more than 50% of the parent's common stock, the issuer's board is three executives with no committees, all six executive officers joined in 2024 or 2025, and 2026 brought related-party dealings with sponsor-affiliated funds and securities litigation over the June capital raise.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 70; 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 213–214

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a weak view is the 2026 operating turn: revenue rose 8.35% in the nine months, quarterly Adjusted Corporate EBITDA was USD 233 million in Q3 2026 against USD 203 million in Q3 2025, the vehicle funding programs were renewed with four new series, and fleet rotation cut the Americas holding period to 18 months. The view would improve if the fourth-quarter 2026 report gives a positive twelve-month measure on the revised definition, if used-vehicle prices hold so that depreciation per unit stays near USD 301 a month, and if the 2026 senior notes are repaid without new secured debt; it would worsen if corporate liquidity falls below USD 946 million, if collateral calls arise, or if further value passes to sponsor-affiliated parties.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 71; calculation F2; calculation F8

### Limitations

- No credit rating, agency report or dated debt quote is supplied, so the weak overall assessment is not tested against rating or market views and could be harsher or more lenient than they imply.
- No twelve-month earnings measure on one definition exists, so the 2026 improvement cannot be annualised and the conclusion that earnings do not cover the cost of capital would soften if a positive twelve-month figure appears.
- Executed financing documents are not supplied, so rank, security and covenant headroom of each instrument are not established and the collateral-cover conclusion rests on the filings' own summaries.
- The October 2026 joint venture and the segment revision from the fourth quarter of 2026 are not in period figures, so the business perimeter assessed here may differ from the one reported next.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. The franchise is large and long-established, with three brands, an extensive airport and off-airport network and 34% of rental revenue from off-airport locations that reduce reliance on air travel, which supports revenue durability through cycles. Vehicle debt is secured on a fleet carried at more than the debt and has been extended, which gives funding stability that earnings alone would not. Pricing drove revenue up 8.35% in the nine months of 2026, and Adjusted Corporate EBITDA turned positive on the revised definition.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 15; calculation F2

Weaknesses. Earnings measures were negative in both fiscal years, free cash flow is negative after fleet spending, corporate liquidity of USD 946 million is small against near-term maturities, and funding has become more secured and more expensive, with an effective rate of about 17.5% on the 2026 first-lien exchangeable notes. Sponsor control above 50%, a three-executive issuer board and related-party transactions raise creditor-alignment risk, and used-vehicle price softness and securities litigation add event risk.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 49

### Business and ownership assessment

The business model supports cash-flow visibility only partly: rental demand is seasonal and tied to airline travel, concessions carry fixed minimums, and the fleet must be financed ahead of revenue, but the brands, the network and off-airport rentals of 34% of rental revenue give a broad base, and the issuer is wholly owned by a parent in which the Plan Sponsors hold more than 50%, so the owner group can steer distributions and related-party dealings even though the issuer's own board has no independent oversight.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 20; 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 70; 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 213–214

### Revenue durability and pricing

Revenue durability is moderate: price rather than volume drove 2026, with revenue up 8.35% in the nine months while transaction days rose by about one percent in each segment, so the recovery depends on pricing holding as used-vehicle values and demand evolve; revenue had fallen in FY2025 to USD 8,504 million from USD 9,049 million.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 68; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 71; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 73; calculation F2; calculation F12

### Cost structure, margin resilience and cash conversion

Margins are exposed to fleet depreciation and fixed concession and site costs: depreciation and lease charges were USD 3,611 million in FY2024 and USD 1,927 million in FY2025, which shows how large the swing is, and cash conversion is weak because fleet expenditure net of disposals exceeds operating cash flow, giving free cash flow of USD -366 million in FY2025 and USD -1,026 million in the nine months of 2026.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F5; calculation F13

### Competitive forces

Competitive rivalry is intense with Avis Budget Group, Enterprise Holdings and SIXT, and with ride share and peer-to-peer platforms, which limits pricing power; manufacturers supply the fleet and program vehicles shift residual risk to them at a higher purchase cost; airports and insurers act as powerful counterparties through concessions and reimbursement terms; and the move into Oro Mobility with ride share partners adds both a growth option and execution risk.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 14; 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 21; 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 95; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Financial profile and credit quality

The financial profile is weak on earnings and cash flow, average on collateral and market access, and weak on policy and governance. Earnings and coverage are negative, liquidity is below its prior levels, and leverage cannot be expressed on earnings, but vehicle debt is matched to collateral and asset-backed programs were extended to 2028 and beyond, which has kept market access open at a rising cost.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25; calculation F7; calculation F10

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next one to two years the tailwinds are the 2026 pricing recovery, the extended vehicle programs and the joint venture cash, and the headwinds are the USD 1,475 million of 2026 maturities, the 2026 senior notes of USD 199 million tied to the Class B condition, used-vehicle softness and the segment reorganisation; over three to five years the 2028 maturity wall of USD 7,118 million, which holds the revolver, term loans and vehicle programs, is the main refinancing test.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 60; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 66; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 93; calculation F4; calculation F12

### Qualitative downside scenario

The most credible downside starts with a further fall in used-vehicle values, which raises depreciation and losses on vehicle sales, triggers collateral top-ups in the asset-backed facilities and absorbs cash that corporate liquidity of USD 946 million cannot easily replace; the first break would appear in depreciation per unit per month rising above its 2026 level of USD 301 in Americas RAC, the financial transmission would be lower availability and higher secured borrowing at rates near 17.5%, and the observable trigger would be a shortfall in repaying the 2026 senior notes or further first-lien exchangeable issuance.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 66; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 49; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 60; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 71; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F3; calculation F14
