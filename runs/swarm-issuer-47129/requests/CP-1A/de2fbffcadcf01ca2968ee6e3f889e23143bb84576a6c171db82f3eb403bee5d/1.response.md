## Findings

### Bottom line

Hertz is a scaled, long-established vehicle rental operator whose creditors depend less on the franchise than on fleet-cost control, asset-backed funding and a sponsor-controlled owner, so governance and fleet economics are the swing factors for the credit.

The Plan Sponsors hold more than 50% of the parent's common stock and can decide matters put to stockholders, the whole executive team joined in 2024 or 2025, the issuer's own board is three executives with no independent directors, and in 2026 the company raised first-lien exchangeable debt at an effective cost of about 17.5% and agreed an Oro Mobility joint venture in which sponsor-affiliated funds are counterparties. Revenue rose 8.35% in the nine months of 2026, which supports the operating franchise.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 70; 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 78–79; 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 213–214; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 52; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 58–59; calculation R7

### Key findings

The business model is broad but capital intensive: two segments, three brands and a large airport and off-airport network, with Americas RAC at 79.48% of FY2025 revenue and off-airport rentals at 34% of rental revenue, while the fleet is financed by asset-backed programs and concession fees carry fixed minimums, so volume or residual-value shocks hit cash before earnings recover.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 11; 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 20; calculation R1

Control sits with the Plan Sponsors rather than with public stockholders or independent directors: they held more than 50% of the parent's common stock at 2025-12-31, the issuer's three-person board consists of executive officers, and the issuer has no audit, compensation or nominating committee, so creditors rely on contractual covenants and public-company controls at the parent more than on board independence at the issuer.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 70; 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 213–214

Management is new in role: all six executive officers joined and took their current roles between April 2024 and October 2025, including the chief executive and chief financial officers, so the strategy has a short track record under this team.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 77–79

Capital allocation toward owners has been restrained recently but was not in 2023: the issuer paid USD 13 million of dividends to its parent in 2025 and USD 7 million in 2024, against USD 321 million in 2023 that funded share repurchases, no repurchases were made in 2025 or 2024, and USD 874 million of the repurchase authorization remains, so the policy can change quickly.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 80; 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 210

Sponsor-related transactions are growing in 2026: sponsor-affiliated funds contributed about USD 39 million of the USD 81 million paid for Oro units and agreed to sell about USD 51 million of principal of the Exchangeable Notes Due 2029 for about USD 21 million, a discount that benefits the issuer but is struck with related parties, while investors affiliated with CK Amarillo, an affiliate of the parent, had earlier bought about USD 44 million of those notes and received a consent fee of about USD 1 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 52; 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 199; calculation R9

Funding in 2026 moved toward more expensive and more secured instruments: the first-lien exchangeable notes of USD 380 million carry an effective interest rate of about 17.5% and the second-lien exchangeable notes about 17.0%, which adds secured claims ahead of the unsecured notes and raises cash interest, and a securities class action alleges the June 2026 capital raise followed misleading liquidity statements.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 49; calculation R8

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is the operating recovery and the access to funding: revenue rose 8.35% in the nine months of 2026, Americas RAC total revenue per day rose to USD 61.24 from USD 56.75, the average holding period in Americas RAC fell over 25% to 18 months with a younger fleet, and the company renewed asset-backed programs and issued four new vehicle series in 2026. The view would turn more favourable if the proxy statement showed independent committees at the parent, if the Oro and repurchase terms were shown to be approved by disinterested directors, and if the fourth-quarter 2026 report confirmed that the joint venture reduces net debt; it would turn less favourable if sponsor-affiliated parties received further value from the issuer ahead of creditors.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 71; 10-K_2025-12-31_47129-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 20; calculation R7

### Limitations

- The proxy statement is not in the set, so the parent's board composition, committee independence and the sponsors' board seats are not established; this limits the conclusion that governance is sponsor-led to the issuer's own board and the stated stock ownership.
- Individual percentages of Apollo, Knighthead and Certares and of public stockholders are not stated, so the strength of any single sponsor's control and the scale of public float are not established.
- The Oro Mobility joint venture terms are summarised only in the quarterly report; the Hertz remaining stake, consolidation treatment and board approval process are not stated, which limits any conclusion on whether the repurchase discount was arm's length.
- Customer, manufacturer and business-versus-leisure mix appear only in charts that the supplied text does not carry, so no concentration conclusion is drawn.
- Evidence ends on 2026-11-05, so ownership, management and financing events after that date are not established.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The 2026 financing program moved toward secured and exchangeable instruments. In June 2026 the issuer sold USD 350 million of exchangeable first-lien secured PIK notes due 2030 and added USD 30 million under the greenshoe in July 2026, for USD 380 million at 6.75%, half paid in cash and half in kind, with an effective interest rate of about 17.5%; the parent lent a block of its shares to an underwriter under a share lending agreement, whose initial fair value of USD 85 million the parent recorded as a debt issuance cost. The notes are first-lien secured by their stated name and so rank ahead in security of the unsecured exchangeable notes of USD 425 million issued in September 2025, but the filings do not state their rank against the first-lien senior secured notes of USD 1,250 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 22; 10-Q_2026-06-30_47129-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 56; calculation R8

Vehicle funding was renewed rather than reduced: in 2026 the vehicle financing subsidiary issued four new series, USD 500 million and USD 500 million in May and USD 375 million and USD 500 million in August, added USD 221 million of Class E notes in April, and extended the maturity of the Series 2021-A Class A notes to May 2028. Amendment No. 3 on 2026-11-02 extends the Class B commitment to November 2028 at a fixed 12.46%, but funding of those notes requires the senior notes due 2026 to be paid off before 2026-12-31, which links asset-backed capacity to a corporate maturity.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 60; calculation R10

The Oro Mobility joint venture is the main ownership transaction: in October 2026 outside investors, including funds affiliated with Certares or Knighthead, agreed to buy Class A common units of Oro from Hertz for about USD 81 million, which gives them 50% of the voting units and about 16% of the common units, while Hertz contributed about USD 60 million of cash, assigned a 5% interest to an affiliate and keeps three of six board seats; Hertz also agreed to buy back about USD 51 million of principal of the Exchangeable Notes Due 2029 for about USD 21 million and will lease Oro vehicles at about USD 14 million a month on an average term of about 4.5 years.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 52; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 58; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 59; calculation R9

Asset sales are a second funding source: sale-leasebacks of operating sites and an airport location gave gains of USD 64 million in the nine months of 2026 against USD 128 million in the nine months of 2025, with the buildings portions carried as financial liabilities, and the company expects to continue them through 2026.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_47129-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 15
