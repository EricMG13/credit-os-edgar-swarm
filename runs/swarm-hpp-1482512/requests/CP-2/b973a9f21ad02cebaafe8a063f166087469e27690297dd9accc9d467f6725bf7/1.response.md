## Findings

### Bottom line

Hudson Pacific is a weak, highly leveraged office and studio landlord whose liquidity does not cover its 2027 maturities, so unsecured noteholders depend on refinancing the Hollywood Media Portfolio loan and the 2027 notes.

Cash plus the undrawn revolver of USD 955.78 million at 2026-09-30 covers 0.65x of the USD 1,479.77 million of principal due in 2027, and 0.42x once USD 333.3 million of revolver commitments mature on 2026-12-21. That is before the October 2026 tender offer, whose cash cost is not established, and the USD 30.0 million revolver draw reported at 2026-11-06. Cash interest cover is 2.60x on trailing EBITDA but 2.07x on annualised third-quarter EBITDA over trailing cash interest, because fourth-quarter 2025 carried lease termination income. Same-store office leasing is recovering and every covenant is reported as met, which supports the next twelve months but does not retire the 2027 wall.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation M09; calculation M13; calculation M04; 10-Q_2026-09-30_hpp-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27–29, 47, 73

### Key findings

Leverage is the binding weakness, and the trailing figure understates it. Net debt of USD 3,253.06 million at 2026-09-30 is 9.27x trailing EBITDA of USD 351.01 million and 11.61x annualised third-quarter EBITDA of USD 280.30 million. Pro forma net debt is USD 3,177.56 million (9.05x) after the Skyport Plaza sale on 2026-10-15 and USD 3,243.66 million (9.24x) after the Albuquerque studio sale on 2026-10-15, neither of which removes the sold property's earnings from EBITDA; the effect of the tender offer of 2026-10-14 is not established because the price is not stated. Trailing EBITDA holds fourth-quarter 2025 EBITDA of USD 140.57 million, USD 70.50 million above the latest quarter. The 2025 annual report attributes a USD 39.72 million increase in non-same-store office rental revenue to the Element LA early termination fee but does not size the fee or two other termination fees, which refines the statement in Financial foundation that no amount is given. Removing only that increase, as an Analyst Judgement, gives 10.45x and cash interest cover of 2.30x.

Liquidity is adequate for the next twelve months but is smaller than the headline and covers less than half of 2027 principal once the December revolver tranche lapses. ### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against the weak view is that the 2027 wall is two refinancings with large asset cover behind them. Unencumbered assets are 301.5% of unsecured debt against a 150% minimum, secured debt is 21.4% of total assets against a 40% maximum, same-store NOI is growing at 8.45%, the revolver was undrawn through September 2026, equity markets were open in June 2025, and ratings were unchanged between 2025-12-31 and 2026-09-30. The Hollywood Media Portfolio loan is secured by eight properties that include buildings leased to Netflix to 2031, the company extended it in 2026 without a paydown, and the October 2026 sales and tender offer show disposals being turned into debt reduction. Equity capitalization of USD 1,195.54 million, including the Series C preferred, sits beneath USD 3,363.15 million of debt, which is 73.8% of total market capitalization.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation M24; calculation M47; 10-Q_2026-09-30_hpp-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 29, 54–55, 74

The view would improve if the Hollywood Media Portfolio loan and the 3.95% notes are refinanced early at an all-in cost near 6.16% rather than 7.65%, the December 2026 revolver tranche is replaced, and quarterly EBITDA holds above USD 70.07 million without termination income. It would worsen if refinancing prices near the Office Portfolio spread, which adds USD 32.63 million of annual interest and cuts annualised cover to 1.67x, if the Office Portfolio extensions are not met, if tenants fail as Weil, Gotshal & Manges announced a wind-down on 2026-10-28, if disposals stop before leverage falls, or if fixed charge cover moves toward its 1.5x minimum.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths, in order of weight. Same-store office leasing is recovering: ending occupancy rose 9.8 pp to 84.9% and same-store NOI rose 8.45%, which supports debt service in the assets that remain and shows tenants will sign in these markets. Anchor tenants are long-dated: Google, the City and County of San Francisco, Netflix and Amazon provide 28.6% of office rent on leases that run to between 2028 and 2067, which gives the rent roll visibility through the refinancing. Policy has favoured creditors: USD 689.3 million of equity was raised in June 2025, common dividends are suspended, and sales have repaid debt, so deleveraging has not been paid for by lenders. Asset-based covenants are far from their limits, which keeps default triggers on those tests distant before 2027. No principal other than small amortisation falls due before 2027, and the Hollywood loan rate is fixed by swap to its maturity, which gives a stable interest bill until then.

### Company description

Hudson Pacific Properties, Inc. is a Maryland real estate investment trust headquartered in Los Angeles that owns, leases, develops and operates Class-A office and studio properties in Los Angeles, the San Francisco Bay Area, Seattle, New York and Vancouver, with a studio development in Greater London. At 2026-09-30 the portfolio held 51 properties: 38 consolidated office properties, three consolidated studio properties and five consolidated development sites, plus five properties and sites held through unconsolidated ventures. Office space was 85.6% occupied on a perimeter of 35 in-service properties after sales and repositioning. Office provided 81.74% of revenue and studio 18.26% in nine months 2026, but studio provided only 2.98% of segment profit, and Quixote, the production services business, is being wound down after operating losses in 2025.

### Ownership and group structure

There is no controlling owner, which removes sponsor extraction risk and also any sponsor backstop. The charter caps any holder at 9.8% of shares unless the board exempts it, 75 holders of record were reported on 2026-02-18, and the board alone sets investment and financing policy, with no limit on debt in the organizational documents and the power to change the borrowing policy without a stockholder vote. Creditors therefore rely on covenants rather than on policy statements, and the management aim of conservative leverage is not a binding limit while debt is 73.8% of total market capitalization.

Hudson Pacific Properties, Inc. is the sole general partner of Hudson Pacific Properties, L.P. and owns about 96.7% of it including unvested units. The operating partnership issues the unsecured notes and borrows under the revolver, and Hudson Pacific Properties, Inc. and certain subsidiaries guarantee its unsecured debt; the secured loans sit in separate property-owning subsidiaries that are not obligors of other debt, so unsecured holders rank behind secured lenders on the pledged properties. The equity beneath the debt is USD 770.54 million of implied common equity at the 2026-09-30 closing price and the USD 425.0 million Series C preferred, which ranks ahead of the common, costs USD 20.19 million a year and became redeemable by the company on 2026-11-16, so a redemption would use cash that supports debt service. The Ferry Building venture is 55.0% owned with PIMCO, which holds put rights and is owed USD 66.14 million that sits in consolidated debt.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is mostly recurring, contractual office rent, and it is falling with a smaller portfolio. Office rental revenue was USD 439.57 million of USD 551.04 million of total revenue in nine months 2026, total revenue fell 4.18%, and studio service revenue of USD 60.86 million is volatile because it depends on production activity. The recovery is led by occupancy, not price: same-store ending occupancy rose 9.8 pp while the average annual rental rate per square foot fell to USD 56.28 from USD 57.08, and the company states that its office rents are generally below current market quotes without giving the quotes. The portfolio's in-place rent is USD 54.23 per square foot and rises contractually to USD 62.65 at expiry; that is a scheduled rent, not a market rent, so it does not show rollover upside, which corrects the reading in Business & governance. The 2028 expiries are in place at USD 71.11 per square foot, the highest of any expiry year and well above the portfolio average, so renewals that year are the most exposed to any gap between in-place and market rent, which the filings do not quantify.

Competitive rivalry limits pricing. Same-store rent per square foot fell while occupancy rose and leasing cost per square foot rose, so recovery is being bought with concessions, which limits how much of the NOI gain reaches free cash flow.

### PEST analysis

Two categories alter credit quality. The economic category matters most: borrowing costs reset when the swapped Hollywood Media Portfolio loan and the 2027 notes refinance, and the company's own risk statement says its growth depends on external capital outside its control, so interest rates and access to the CMBS and bond markets act directly on liquidity and refinancing capacity. The social and technological category works through tenant demand: a significant portion of rental revenue comes from technology and media and entertainment tenants, so layoffs, relocations and production slowdowns in those industries feed vacancy, concessions and studio use.

Political and regulatory factors, mainly local property taxes and entertainment labour agreements, are secondary and not quantified in the filings. Foreign exchange is minor: the Bentall Centre loan is in Canadian dollars and held in an unconsolidated venture with a company carve-out guarantee.

The short-term outlook, over 12 to 24 months, is dominated by the refinancing and by the trailing figures rolling over. The revolver tranche of USD 333.3 million lapses on 2026-12-21 and the two November 2027 maturities approach, and the fourth quarter of 2025, whose EBITDA was USD 140.57 million, leaves the trailing window at 2026-12-31. ### Downside scenario

The downside scenario starts with the refinancing market for office-weighted and studio collateral staying expensive or closed as the November 2027 dates approach, while the company still relies on asset sales. The first operational break is that sales slow or the 2027 and 2028 expiries, which cover 32.3% of office rent, roll at lower occupancy or rents, so same-store NOI stops rising while sold assets have already removed income. The financial transmission runs through interest: refinancing the Hollywood Media Portfolio loan at a rate USD 16.69 million to USD 32.63 million a year higher than today's swap-fixed cost takes cash interest cover on annualised EBITDA from 2.07x to between 1.85x and 1.67x, and fixed charge cover, reported at 1.7x against a 1.5x minimum, falls by a comparable proportion of its base. If the three conditional extensions of the Office Portfolio loan were also unavailable, 2027 principal would be USD 1,721.85 million.
Basis: calculation M20; calculation M21; calculation M19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_hpp-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27–29, 58–59

The liquidity effect is that cash plus revolver commitments of USD 622.53 million after the December expiry would meet only part of USD 1,479.77 million of 2027 principal, leaving USD 857.23 million to be refinanced, sold or raised before any further draw; the October sales of USD 84.9 million gross cover about a tenth of that. Management levers are further disposals, USD 59.2 million of at-the-market capacity, deferral of uncommitted development, and the dividend discipline already in place; the Series C preferred dividend of USD 20.19 million a year continues. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid a liquidity crisis into the first half of 2027, but it does not appear sufficient to repay the 2027 maturities without access to refinancing, so the scenario does not assume a default and treats the refinancing as the point at which default risk is decided.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Hudson Pacific presents a weak business profile supported by long-dated anchor leases, a recovering same-store portfolio and creditor-aligned policy, but credit quality is constrained by net leverage of 9.27x on trailing EBITDA and 11.61x on run-rate EBITDA, thin cash interest cover and negative free cash flow. Cash-flow durability appears weak due to heavy leasing costs, interest that takes 48.22% of run-rate EBITDA and income removed by sales. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the Hollywood Media Portfolio loan and the 3.95% notes being refinanced before November 2027, on replacement of the December 2026 revolver tranche, and on collateral values in the studio and office assets. The primary downside path is a refinancing at higher spreads while earnings are lower after sales and lease expiries. Further analysis would require the executed credit documents, the size of the termination fee income, the price of the tender offer and cash after the October events, and market data. The strongest credit support is the combination of long-dated anchor tenants and same-store leasing recovery, the weakest material dimension is refinancing capacity against the 2027 wall, and the primary downside path is monitored through cash plus revolver against 2027 principal and through fixed charge cover. Business profile against typical leveraged-credit issuers in the sector is not established because no peer data is supplied. Top risk: refinancing of the November 2027 maturities.
