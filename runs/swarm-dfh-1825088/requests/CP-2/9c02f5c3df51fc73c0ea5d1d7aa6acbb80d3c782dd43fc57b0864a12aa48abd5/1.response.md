### Bottom line

Dream Finders has a weak and weakening credit profile: net leverage reached 4.69x and interest cover 1.23x before the Beazer acquisition, cash is consumed in every period, and creditors depend on a housing recovery and on refinancing in August 2028. Pro forma net leverage after the closing on 20 October 2026 is not established.

### Company description

Dream Finders builds single-family homes for entry-level, move-up and active adult buyers in the Southeast, Mid-Atlantic and Midwest, sells homes to rental investors under built-for-rent contracts and earns mortgage, title and insurance income from the same buyers; it acquired Beazer on 20 October 2026. Revenue is transactional, earned as each home closes, and the order book is mostly spec homes, so cash flow follows closings, mortgage rates and incentives rather than contracts, which weakens visibility for debt service. Financial services are a small second stream and homebuilding dominates earnings.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_dfh.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dfh.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dfh.htm.jsonl, page 48

### Ownership and group structure

The company is founder-led and publicly listed with no sponsor. The founder holds all Class B stock, about 84% of the votes, and the company is a controlled company that could opt out of independent-board requirements; part of his stake is pledged for margin loans and the new Series B stock gives holders a cash redemption right if his holding falls below 50% of votes or economic interest. The parent is a holding company that depends on its operating subsidiaries, whose guarantors are not named, and the preferred stock ranks ahead of common stock but is classified as mezzanine equity, so creditors rely on the operating subsidiaries' cash flow and a stable control position.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_dfh.htm.jsonl, page 48; 10-K_2025-12-31_dfh.htm.jsonl, page 54; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dfh.htm.jsonl, page 26; calculation C15

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue depends on volume, price and mix, and is cyclical and discretionary. In nine months closings rose 1% while the average selling price fell 7.71%, driven by geographic and product mix and more built-for-rent closings, and net sales rose 6% with the help of incentives and mortgage buydowns, with a cancellation rate of 10.2% against 12.7%. Pricing power is weak: management says affordability is acute for entry-level buyers and incentives press on price and margin, and backlog value fell 8.72%, so forward revenue visibility is lower even though closings are at a record for the period.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_dfh.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dfh.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dfh.htm.jsonl, page 48; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C15

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Costs are mostly variable but the largest items are rising. Homesite costs are typically 30% to 35% of a home's cost, materials 30% to 35%, labor 20% to 25% and interest, commissions and closing costs 5% to 10%, and the company reports higher land and financing costs as the main cause of lower margin. Direct cost cuts and cycle-time gains have not offset a 3.2 percentage point fall in homebuilding gross margin, and homebuilding SG&A rose to 12.5% of revenue from 12.0%, so margins are not defended under price pressure; the company can cut compensation and terminate land options, which provides partial flexibility.

### Capital intensity and FCF conversion

Capital expenditure is small, but inventory is the capital need and it is growing. Purchases of property and equipment were USD 16,220 thousand in nine months, while inventories rose 24.19% in the balance sheet since year end and used more cash than a year earlier. In nine months EBITDA less cash interest and cash taxes was negative USD 20.573 million before working capital, and operating cash flow was lower still, so EBITDA does not translate into free cash flow available for debt service or deleveraging while the company grows its inventory and lot position.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense and barriers to entry are low: the company competes with larger national builders and smaller local builders, and builders are using pricing adjustments, mortgage buydowns and incentives to hold sales, which presses margin and cash flow. Substitutes are resale homes and rentals, and rising resale inventory gives buyers alternatives, while buyers are highly payment sensitive. Land developers and land bank partners hold supply-side power over lot terms and fees, and trades and materials suppliers can pass on price and labor pressure, so the main credit effect is margin and cash-flow durability rather than market share.

### Macro and policy factors

Macro and policy factors are material because they move demand and cost together. Mortgage rates rose late in the third quarter, consumer confidence weakened to near-record lows, and management expects higher energy, materials and labor costs and tighter skilled labor availability, including from changes in immigration enforcement. Each pushes down closings or margin at the same time as debt rises, so they bear directly on interest cover and liquidity; technology and social factors are not a primary credit driver on the documents supplied.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months the near-term headwinds are weak affordability, sustained incentives, higher land and financing costs, the integration of Beazer, expected deal abandonment charges in the fourth quarter and the 2027 commitments maturity; the tailwinds are cost reductions already under way, shorter cycle times and a backlog made up mostly of spec homes that close quickly. ### Downside scenario

The scenario starts with mortgage rates staying high through the fourth quarter, so closings miss the guidance and incentives rise. The first operating break is homebuilding gross margin: a further 3.2 percentage point fall, the same as the fall between the first nine months of 2025 and 2026, takes last-twelve-months EBITDA from USD 373.312 million to USD 247.068 million, lifting net leverage to 7.08x and cutting EBITDA cover of cash interest to 0.81x on pre-closing debt. Financially, operating cash outflow continues while inventory is carried, so borrowing on the Credit Agreement rises toward its borrowing base and liquidity falls below the pre-closing runway of 15.42 months; the Series B dividend of USD 20.25 million a quarter could be deferred, which compounds the claim at 12.0%. Management levers are terminating land options, cutting overhead and slowing starts. On pre-closing figures liquidity would cover about 15.42 months of the outflow, so distress in 2027 is not indicated, but the post-closing position that decides it is not established, and resilience depends on refinancing the August 2028 cluster. The observable triggers are fourth-quarter closings, gross margin, liquidity after closing and any sale of pledged founder shares.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Dream Finders presents an adequate business profile supported by an asset-light land model, regional breadth and integrated financial services, but credit quality is constrained by net leverage of 4.69x, interest cover of 1.23x, negative free cash flow and compressed margins before a large acquisition. Cash-flow durability appears weak because inventory growth absorbs earnings. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on housing demand and on market access in 2027 and 2028, when USD 300.0 million of commitments and then 60.07% to 75.10% of pre-closing debt mature. The primary downside path is a further fall in homebuilding gross margin on a larger debt and preferred stock base. Further analysis would require the closing balance sheet, the Credit Agreement drawing, covenant definitions and Beazer's earnings. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: refinancing the August 2028 maturities after margin compression and a debt-funded acquisition.
