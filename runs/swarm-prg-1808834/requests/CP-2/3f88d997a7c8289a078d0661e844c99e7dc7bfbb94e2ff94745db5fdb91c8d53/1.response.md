## Findings

### Bottom line

PROG's fundamental credit is adequate but weakening: low recourse debt, short-duration leases and USD 458.9 million of liquidity protect creditors, while the core leasing profit pool is shrinking under consumer stress and cash is again being returned to shareholders.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C15

The recourse claim is USD 600.0 million of unsecured notes due in 2029, on which the company reports net leverage of 1.5x; on the reported basis, which also counts USD 272.0 million of non-recourse asset-backed debt, net debt to EBITDA is 2.73x at 2026-09-30 and 3.09x pro forma for the USD 100.0 million share repurchase announced on 2026-10-28. The main downside is further erosion of Progressive Leasing, which earned 91.73% of nine-month pre-tax profit, and no principal is scheduled to mature before 2029.

### Company description and credit linkage

PROG is a financial technology holding company whose profit comes mostly from one business. Progressive Leasing leases durable goods to near-prime and subprime consumers through about 24,000 partner locations and websites, and generated 91.73% of nine-month 2026 pre-tax profit and 77.70% of revenue. Purchasing Power, acquired on 2026-01-02, sells goods to employees of employer clients through payroll deduction, and Four offers buy now pay later; with the small Other segment they supplied the remaining 22.30% of revenue. Leases run for a fixed term of up to 12 months and the customer may cancel at any time by returning the merchandise, so revenue visibility is about one lease term and earnings follow approvals and partner volume quickly in both directions. The working asset is lease merchandise of USD 511.293 million and capital expenditure is only 0.59% of revenue, so cash generation depends on keeping approvals, losses and partner volume stable; that supports debt service while volume is steady and weakens it quickly when it is not.

### Revenue durability

Progressive Leasing revenue is recurring within each lease term but discretionary, cyclical and concentrated. Volume grew 1.20% in nine months and 2.3% in Q3 on a larger e-commerce share, yet revenue fell 4.37% over nine months and 0.69% in Q3 because smaller lease portfolios entered each period; active customers were flat at 781 thousand against 784 thousand. Management attributes the pressure to cost of living, fuel prices and the war in Iran, expects tighter decisioning to keep pressuring volume, and has reduced its 2026 revenue and earnings outlook ranges. Purchasing Power and Four are growing but small: consolidated revenue ex Purchasing Power fell 0.86% in nine months, so the group's growth is acquired.

### Competitive forces, macro and regulation

Competitive rivalry is high: the annual report describes the virtual lease-to-own market as highly competitive with new payment products, and Four competes with other buy now pay later providers, cards and digital wallets, so pricing power is limited and approvals, not price, are the lever management uses. Buyer power sits with the large retail partners, who can renegotiate rebates, exclusivity and merchandise prices; incentive payments were USD 25.2 million in nine months, so any worse terms reduce margin directly. Substitutes are plentiful for the same customer, which limits pricing power. Entrants matter less than partner concentration. Of the macro and policy factors only two change the credit view. Economic: inflation, fuel prices, tariffs and unemployment fall on near-prime and subprime customers first and show up in write-offs within a lease term. Regulatory: state attorneys general have acted against rent-to-own disclosure, a state demand arrived in October 2026, and seven states opened a coordinated inquiry into buy now pay later providers that did not name Four, so enforcement is a recurring cost and practice risk to the core segment.

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a further consumer shock from fuel prices and unemployment, or the loss of another large retail partner. The first operational break is at Progressive Leasing: delinquencies rise, write-offs move above 8% of revenue and approvals tighten again, so volume falls and lease revenue follows with a lag of a lease term, as it already did in Q3 when volume grew and revenue did not. Financially, one extra point of write-offs on last-twelve-months Progressive Leasing revenue costs USD 22.45 million, 8.10% of reported EBITDA, and a 5% fall in that revenue at the Q3 gross margin removes USD 35.70 million of gross margin, 12.88% of reported EBITDA, before any cost response. Cash is cushioned first because a shrinking lease book releases inventory cash, which hides the earnings fall for several quarters, then the cushion fades as purchases fall with volume. On the reported basis a one point rise in write-offs lifts net debt to EBITDA from 2.73x to 2.97x, and to 3.36x with the full repurchase; on the company definition the same two events give 1.99x, at the top of the 1.5x to 2.0x range, and cover on reported EBITDA falls to 4.65x. Liquidity of USD 458.9 million, no maturity before 2029 and USD 36.0 million of annual note interest mean distress is unlikely within 12 to 24 months; management levers are to cancel the repurchase, cut lease merchandise purchases, sell charged-off receivables and cut costs. The observable triggers are write-offs above 8% for a second quarter, a partner exit, a revolver draw, a rising notes discount and any change in covenant compliance language.

### Overall credit view

Overall, PROG Holdings presents an adequate business profile supported by short-duration leases, low capital intensity, a recourse structure limited to USD 600.0 million of notes and liquidity of USD 458.9 million, but credit quality is constrained by a shrinking and concentrated core earnings engine, rising write-offs and shareholder returns that are partly revolver funded. No peer data were supplied, so the profile cannot be benchmarked against typical leveraged consumer finance issuers. Cash-flow durability appears adequate rather than strong because recent conversion was helped by inventory release and a tax refund. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on Progressive Leasing earnings holding up until the notes and revolver mature in November 2029, on the covenant definition and on whether the repurchase is revolver funded. The primary downside path is partner loss or rising write-offs at Progressive Leasing feeding through to leverage while distributions continue. Further analysis would require the credit agreement and covenant definitions, the adjusted EBITDA reconciliation, partner-level concentration and the proxy statement. Top risk: erosion of Progressive Leasing earnings through partner loss or rising write-offs.
