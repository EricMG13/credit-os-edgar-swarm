## Findings

### Bottom line

ATS is a publicly listed automation builder with no sponsor or controlling owner identified in the filings, so creditors depend on management's financial policy, which is in transition: a new chief executive, an interim finance chief, an unremediated control weakness and a restructuring program.
Basis: 40-F_2026-03-31_ats-20260331_d2.htm.jsonl, pages 7–9; 6-K-EX-99.1_2025-12-28_ats-mdaxfy26q3.htm.jsonl, page 3; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 36–38; 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 4

Demand is mixed: second-quarter bookings of CAD 827 million rose 12.7% year on year but revenue fell 8.6%, and management no longer expects its fiscal 2027 goals of modest revenue growth and margin expansion. The company spent CAD 61.3 million on share repurchases in the quarter while its own leverage measure sat above its target range, and the sale of two U.S. facilities for CAD 71.2 million is meant to fund restructuring. Ownership percentages, guarantor entities and sponsor economics are not established.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 1–3; 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 11; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 6

### Key findings

ATS builds and services automated manufacturing systems, and its revenue is project-driven with a growing service share. Of fiscal 2026 adjusted revenue of CAD 2,970.1 million, construction contracts were 53.84%, services 24.37% and goods 21.78%, and life sciences was 51.24% by market and North America 52.48% by customer location. Services were 26.90% of second-quarter revenue and management expects recurring revenue of 25% to 35% on a trailing basis, which matters because service revenue is described as margin-accretive and less tied to customer capital cycles.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-03-31_ats-pressreleasexfy26q4.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12; 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 9; 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 13; calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C4; calculation C5

Demand has softened outside life sciences. Order backlog of CAD 2,009 million is 2.9% below a year earlier, and 61.62% of it is life sciences at CAD 1,238 million, while industrial and consumer backlog fell to CAD 312 million from CAD 431 million. Management attributes weaker non-life-sciences orders to timing and to tariffs that took effect in late August 2026 and were modified at the end of October to remove the effect on most covered products, and it guides third-quarter revenue to CAD 640 million to CAD 680 million against CAD 760.7 million a year earlier. Revenue therefore steps down before bookings can recover, so cash and leverage, not backlog, are the near-term constraint for creditors.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 3; 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 15; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 31; calculation C6

Leadership and controls are in transition. The chief executive joined on 12 January 2026 and the chief financial officer's resignation took effect on 15 February 2026, since when the corporate controller has acted as interim finance chief. The company states that its disclosure controls were not effective at 27 September 2026 because of a material weakness in internal control over financial reporting first reported in fiscal 2026, and an ERP upgrade that went live in the quarter is still stabilising. For creditors the risk is the reliability of reported figures and of add-back classification while earnings fall.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2025-12-28_ats-mdaxfy26q3.htm.jsonl, page 3; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 36–38; 40-F_2026-03-31_ats-20260331_d2.htm.jsonl, page 3

Financial policy is partly creditor-aligned and partly not. The company brought its net debt to pro forma adjusted EBITDA measure down to 2.8x at fiscal 2026 year end from 3.9x a year earlier and extended its credit facility to 4 December 2029, but in the second quarter of fiscal 2027 it bought back CAD 61.3 million of shares at an average of CAD 27.32 per share while the same measure was 3.1x against a stated target range of 2.0x to 3.0x. Management says it expects to stay moderately above the range through fiscal 2027 and will pursue such uses only where there is a clear path back to range. The filings do not disclose the restricted payment limits that would cap further repurchases, so capacity cannot be separated from willingness.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-03-31_ats-pressreleasexfy26q4.htm.jsonl, page 15; 6-K-EX-99.1_2025-12-28_ats-mdaxfy26q3.htm.jsonl, page 20; 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 2; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 6; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 23–24

Restructuring is now continuous and sizeable. The Fixed Cost Transformation Program announced on 6 August 2026 targets annualised savings of CAD 65 million to CAD 75 million, about 250 basis points of margin and half of the 500 basis points still needed to reach the 15% long-term margin target. Implementation is expected to run about 15 more months with workforce reductions of several hundred including subcontractors, and CAD 21.3 million of restructuring was charged in the second quarter against nil a year earlier. The charges are added back to adjusted measures but are cash costs, and the sale of two U.S. facilities for CAD 71.2 million after the quarter end is intended to fund them.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-06-28_ats-pressreleasexfy27q1.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 3; 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 4; 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 11; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 5

The filings name no sponsor, controlling shareholder or ownership percentage, so neither sponsor support nor sponsor extraction can be evidenced; the shares are listed on the TSX and NYSE and the audit committee has three independent directors. The debt was shaped by two placements of Canadian dollar notes due 2032 in 2024, US dollar notes due 2028 and a secured credit facility extended in December 2025, and guarantees come from subsidiaries that are borrowers or guarantors under the credit facility, whose names are not listed. Overall, ATS presents medium governance risk for creditors: the key supported behavior is share repurchases above the leverage target during a restructuring, which matters because it uses liquidity ahead of an earnings recovery, and the countervailing evidence is the fiscal 2026 deleveraging and the extended facility.
Basis: 40-F_2026-03-31_ats-20260331_d2.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 26–28; 6-K-EX-99.1_2025-12-28_ats-mdaxfy26q3.htm.jsonl, page 20; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-03-31_ats-pressreleasexfy26q4.htm.jsonl, page 15

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against caution is operational and financial. Bookings rose 12.7% in the second quarter, life sciences backlog ended the quarter at the highest level in the company's history, the end-of-October change to tariffs removes the effect on most covered products, and the company states CAD 966.7 million of unutilised credit alongside a leverage measure that was 2.8x at fiscal 2026 year end. Management also expects restructuring payback in under one year. This view would win if revenue stays inside the guided third-quarter range and non-life-sciences bookings convert, which would let earnings recover without further share repurchases.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 1–4; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 26; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-03-31_ats-pressreleasexfy26q4.htm.jsonl, page 15

A more positive view would follow leverage returning inside 2.0x to 3.0x, no further repurchases until it does, remediation of the control weakness and restructuring charges ending on the stated timetable. A more negative view would follow third-quarter revenue below CAD 640 million, a further bookings delay outside life sciences, repurchases or acquisitions while the measure exceeds 3.0x, or covenant levels that prove tight once disclosed.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 6; 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 37–38

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The supplied filings report no new bond issue after December 2024; recent debt activity is the extension of the secured credit facility and use of the revolver. On 4 December 2025 the company amended the facility, moving maturity to 4 December 2029 and setting a CAD 900.0 million committed revolver and a fully drawn CAD 150.0 million term facility, with CAD 2.6 million of costs deferred and amortised. The notes outstanding are US dollar notes of USD 350.0 million due 15 December 2028 and Canadian dollar notes of CAD 600.0 million due 21 August 2032, the latter placed in two steps on 21 August 2024 and 19 December 2024, the second at a premium of CAD 1.3 million.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2025-12-28_ats-mdaxfy26q3.htm.jsonl, page 20; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 26–28

Other transactions bear on creditors. After 27 September 2026 the company completed the sale of two U.S. facilities for CAD 71.2 million, with an estimated gain of about CAD 12.2 million to be recognised in the third quarter of fiscal 2027 and an agreement to lease one facility back; a German facility remains held for sale. The transportation reorganization announced in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 was completed in the second quarter of fiscal 2027, and the Fixed Cost Transformation Program, announced on 6 August 2026 with a European footprint consolidation as its first phase, is in progress. Heidolph and Paxiom were acquired before fiscal 2026, with about CAD 179 million of cash acquisitions printed for fiscal 2025 and none since.
