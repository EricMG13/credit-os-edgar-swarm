## Findings

### Bottom line

ATS is an adequate credit under pressure: reported earnings are falling faster than revenue, so net leverage of 3.76x, or 3.51x pro forma for the completed facility sale, depends on restructuring savings arriving before the 2028 notes mature.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 4

Liquidity is the strongest support, with cash of CAD 231.0 million and CAD 860.0 million undrawn on the committed revolver, and no large principal falls due before December 2028. The weakest dimension is margin stability: the second-quarter revenue decline of 8.6% produced a 22.2% fall in adjusted EBITDA, and CAD 103.3 million of last-twelve-months add-backs separate reported from adjusted EBITDA. The sale of two U.S. facilities was completed after 27 September 2026 on a day the filings do not state. Covenant levels are not supplied, so headroom is not established.

### Key findings

The strongest support is liquidity and visibility. Cash of CAD 231.0 million and CAD 860.0 million undrawn on the CAD 900.0 million committed revolver sit against near-term debt maturities of only CAD 2.4 million of bank indebtedness and a negligible current portion of long-term debt. Backlog of CAD 2,009 million, with 61.62% of it in life sciences, and second-quarter bookings that rose 12.7% give revenue visibility into fiscal 2027, although management guides third-quarter revenue to CAD 640 million to CAD 680 million, below the CAD 760.7 million of a year earlier.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 1–3; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 31

The weakest dimension is margin stability. Second-quarter revenue fell 8.6% but adjusted EBITDA fell 22.2%, and the adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 12.1% from 14.2%, so fixed costs are not flexing with volume. Reported EBITDA is lower again: CAD 277.8 million over the last twelve months against CAD 381.1 million adjusted, because CAD 103.3 million of add-backs, CAD 99.8 million of it restructuring or reorganisation cost, is excluded from the adjusted measure. Creditors should underwrite reported earnings until the program's savings are visible.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest contrary evidence is that life sciences backlog and bookings are rising while tariffs have been eased, the company states CAD 966.7 million of unutilised credit, its own leverage measure was 2.8x at fiscal 2026 year end, and management expects restructuring to pay back within a year. This view would win if third-quarter revenue lands inside the guided range, the adjusted EBITDA margin recovers toward the 13.3% of the first quarter, and the facility sale proceeds fund restructuring so that net leverage returns to its target range without new repurchases.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 1–4; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 26; 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-06-28_ats-pressreleasexfy27q1.htm.jsonl, page 4; Business & governance (CP-1A)

It would lose if third-quarter revenue falls below CAD 640 million, if add-backs persist beyond the roughly 15-month program horizon, or if covenant tests prove tight once disclosed. Conviction therefore moves on two observables: the leverage measure at the next two quarter ends and the pace at which restructuring charges decline. Further repurchases while leverage exceeds 3.0x would weaken the view.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strength one is liquidity. Cash of CAD 231.0 million and an undrawn committed revolver of CAD 860.0 million, with only CAD 2.4 million of near-term bank debt, mean the company can fund restructuring and working capital from available resources, which lowers the chance that earnings weakness becomes a liquidity event before the December 2028 notes.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 24–28

Strength two is the order book. Backlog of CAD 2,009 million, led by life sciences at 61.62%, and a trailing book-to-bill of about 1.05 times give partial coverage of revenue while non-life-sciences orders recover, which supports cash generation through the guided third quarter.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 15; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C6

Strength three is a self-help program with funding. Annualised savings of CAD 65 million to CAD 75 million, about 250 basis points of margin, and CAD 71.2 million from the facility sale, which management intends to use for restructuring, address the fixed-cost base that is currently amplifying revenue declines.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 11; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Strength four is a deleveraging record and a longer runway. The company's leverage measure fell to 2.8x at fiscal 2026 year end from 3.9x and the credit facility was extended to December 2029, which reduces refinancing pressure and shows past willingness to repay debt.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-03-31_ats-pressreleasexfy26q4.htm.jsonl, page 15; 6-K-EX-99.1_2025-12-28_ats-mdaxfy26q3.htm.jsonl, page 20; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Strength five is service content. Services were 26.90% of second-quarter revenue and management expects recurring revenue of 25% to 35%, which stabilises cash flow through the capital cycle if the share rises.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 9

Weakness one is operating leverage. Revenue fell 8.6% and adjusted EBITDA 22.2% in the second quarter, so a further volume shortfall cuts earnings faster than revenue and pushes leverage and interest cover toward weaker levels.

### Financial profile

The nine-dimension profile is assessed from the evidence above. Scale and market position is average: revenue of CAD 2,867.2 million over the last twelve months across several end markets, but no market share data. Competitive advantage is average, resting mainly on management statements about differentiated technology. Business diversification is average: four end markets, but life sciences is 51.43% of first-half revenue and customer concentration is not quantified. Cost and capex flexibility is average: capital expenditure is low and guided to CAD 60 million to CAD 80 million, yet the fixed-cost base is large enough to need a multi-year reduction program. Margin stability is weak because a revenue decline of 8.6% produced an adjusted EBITDA decline of 22.2%. Free cash flow stability is average: conversion is high but swings with working capital and one-off collections. Ability to refinance is average, helped by the extended facility and a first large maturity in December 2028, and limited by leverage above target. Liquidity position is strong. Financial policy and governance is average: deleveraging in fiscal 2026 is offset by repurchases above target and a control weakness.

### Revenue durability and ownership

ATS earns about 54% of adjusted revenue from construction contracts, which are project-timed and discretionary for customers, and about a quarter from services, which are steadier. Customers are primarily large multinationals, and customer concentration is named as a risk but not quantified, so revenue durability is average rather than strong. The order book converts to revenue over several quarters and includes long-duration enterprise programs in life sciences, which explains why revenue lags bookings in both directions; this makes the third-quarter guide, not the second-quarter bookings, the better indicator of near-term cash. Sales into the U.S. from Canadian and European operations are just over 20% of adjusted revenue and exposed to tariffs and the exchange rate, although management says tariff changes at the end of October remove the effect on most covered products.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 29; 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 12; calculation C4

Ownership is public with no sponsor or controlling shareholder identified, so there is neither sponsor support nor sponsor extraction to evidence; the debt sits with the listed company, guaranteed by subsidiaries that are borrowers or guarantors under the credit facility, whose names are not listed. Financial policy is therefore a board and management matter, set out in a leverage target range of 2.0x to 3.0x that management has said it will exceed through fiscal 2027.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 27

### Margin resilience and cash conversion

Margins are exposed to operating leverage. In the second quarter gross margin fell to 28.5% from 29.9% and adjusted EBITDA margin to 12.1% from 14.2% on lower volumes and mix, while selling, general and administrative costs fell less than revenue. The program targets about CAD 65 million to CAD 75 million of annualised savings, with payback under a year, but lower revenues and mix are temporarily offsetting its benefits, so margins are more likely to defend through cost action than through volume in the next two quarters. The sensitivity to volume is the central credit risk.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 17; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Cash conversion is supported by low capital intensity: capital expenditure was about 2.65% of last-twelve-months revenue and is guided lower, and free cash flow equalled 0.93x reported EBITDA. Restructuring cash costs, cash taxes and lease principal sit on top, and working capital was close to neutral in the first half of fiscal 2027, which produced only CAD 36.5 million of free cash flow after restructuring and a CAD 25.8 million tax payment, so durable cash available for debt service is lower than conversion implies.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-09-27_ats-mdaxfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 25; 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, page 21

### Industry forces and outlook

Industry forces matter for credit mainly through buyer power and cycle timing. Buyers are large and decide timing of capital projects, so orders swing and revenue follows with a lag; supplier power is limited, as supplier lead times are generally acceptable and supply chain effects of trade changes have been largely mitigated through alternative sourcing and pricing. Evidence on rivalry, new entrants and substitutes beyond management's claim of differentiated technology is not in the supplied files, so those forces are not assessed. Political and trade factors are material: the USMCA review process and Section 338 tariffs delayed packaging orders in the second quarter. Exchange-rate factors also matter because the Canadian operations earn significant U.S. dollar revenue that exceeds the natural hedge.

Credit-translated, strengths are scale across end markets, backlog, service content and a funded cost program; weaknesses are operating leverage, add-back dependence, repurchases above target and reporting reliability; opportunities are nuclear and radiopharmaceutical demand and tariff relief; threats are customer deferrals, further tariff changes and a failure to deliver savings on the stated timetable.

### Downside scenario

The downside path starts with a further delay in non-life-sciences orders, for example if tariff uncertainty persists, so that third-quarter revenue lands below the guided range. The first operational break is fixed costs: in the second quarter an 8.6% revenue decline produced a 22.2% fall in adjusted EBITDA. As an analyst illustration anchored on the 15.54% first-half fall in adjusted EBITDA, applying that fall to last-twelve-months reported EBITDA of CAD 277.8 million gives about CAD 234.63 million, which lifts net leverage to 4.46x, or 4.15x pro forma for the facility sale, and lowers cash interest cover to 2.57x. Liquidity would still hold: the company would have cash of about CAD 231.0 million and CAD 860.0 million undrawn, although the revolver depends on covenant tests whose levels are not supplied. The consequence for refinancing is that the notes due in December 2028 would need to be refinanced with leverage above the target range, so a lender would look for restructuring savings to be visible in reported EBITDA first. Management levers are to stop repurchases, cut capital expenditure toward the bottom of the CAD 60 million to CAD 80 million range, accelerate the program and use the facility sale proceeds. The observable triggers are third-quarter revenue below CAD 640 million, the leverage measure above 3.1x, and any covenant disclosure. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress in this illustration, but not to hold leverage in its target range.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C4; Financial foundation (CP-1); 6-K-EX-99.5_2026-09-27_ats-pressreleasexfy27q2.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4

### Overall credit view

Overall, ATS presents an adequate business profile supported by scale across end markets, a life-sciences-led backlog and ample liquidity, but credit quality is constrained by earnings that fall faster than revenue, adjusted measures that rely on large add-backs, leverage above target and a financial policy that still includes repurchases. Cash-flow durability appears adequate but volatile because fiscal 2026 conversion rested on working capital and a one-off collection. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on leverage returning to the 2.0x to 3.0x range and on the restructuring savings appearing in reported EBITDA before the December 2028 notes. The primary downside path is a further order delay that cuts revenue below the guided range while fixed costs and add-backs persist. Further analysis would require covenant levels, the ranking and security of the notes, restricted payment limits and the audited annual notes. Fundamental credit view completed. Top risk: reported EBITDA falling faster than restructuring savings arrive while leverage is above target.
