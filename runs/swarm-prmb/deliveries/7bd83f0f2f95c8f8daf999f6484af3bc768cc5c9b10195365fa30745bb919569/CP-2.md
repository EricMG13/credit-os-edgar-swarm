## Findings

### Bottom line

Primo Brands is an adequate credit whose improving leverage depends on shareholder payouts staying below free cash flow; creditors should treat financial policy, more than operations, as the swing factor.
Basis: calculation C10; calculation C11; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

Net debt was 3.87x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA at 2026-09-30, or 3.91x pro forma for the dividend declared on 2026-11-03, against 4.51x at 2026-03-31; the gain came from earnings and cash rather than repayment, and 2026 margins are under cost pressure. Liquidity of USD 1,064.0 million, with pro forma cash after the dividend not established, and a term loan now due in 2031 leave the 2028 and 2029 notes of USD 1,930.1 million as the refinancing test.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C05; calculation C06; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_prmb-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_prmb-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 51

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better view is the speed of the improvement and the cash it produced: net leverage fell 0.64x in two quarters, interest cover is 4.05x against a 2.00x covenant minimum, premium water grew 31.66% and management says Direct Delivery returned to growth earlier than it anticipated. Payouts in 2026 are lower against free cash flow than in 2025, and the issuer's integration cash costs fell to USD 146.3 million from USD 206.2 million. If the add-backs prove one-off and margins recover with freight costs, the credit is stronger than adequate.

It would strengthen with filings showing sustained gross margin recovery, debt repayment from free cash flow, a printed maturity schedule after the 2026 amendment, or a sponsor stake confirmed at or above 30%, which would keep its consent rights over payouts in force.

### Business model and credit linkage

Primo Brands sells branded spring, purified, premium and refill water through retail, Direct Delivery, Exchange and Refill, formats that combine consumer retail demand with a recurring delivery and refill base. The recurring formats support cash-flow visibility, but Direct Delivery is also where integration problems surface: a consumer class action alleges missed deliveries after facility closures, and management says only that Direct Delivery returned to growth earlier than it anticipated, with no channel sales supplied. The creditor consequence is that visibility is good in the retail base and unproven in the delivery base.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_prmb-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 6–7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_prmb-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; EX-99.1_2026-08-05_ex991.htm.jsonl, page 1

### Ownership and group structure

The issuer is a listed holding company whose notes are co-issued by two subsidiaries and guaranteed by the parent and substantially all material wholly owned domestic subsidiaries, with the term loans and secured notes sharing one first lien and the 2029 unsecured notes ranking below. The sponsor group held 32.0% at 2026-02-24 and is estimated at 26.51% after August 2026; sponsor-linked repurchases of USD 224.1 million, a quarterly dividend of USD 0.12 per share and USD 12.9 million of original notes that lost covenants and, on two series, guarantees show a financial policy that favours shareholders at the margin without any documented priming.
Basis: calculation C11; calculation C26; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_prmb-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 90–91; 10-K_2025-12-31_prmb-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 98; 10-K_2025-12-31_prmb-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 99

### Revenue durability and pricing power

Revenue is largely a consumer staple with a recurring component, and the annual report states that cost increases have historically been recovered through price increases; 2026 tests that, because net sales grew 2.61% in nine months while the increase in transportation costs equalled 79.39% of it. Retail consolidation and private-label water cap pricing power, and one customer is 21% of net sales, so revenue is predictable in volume but not fully in price. Premium water, up 31.66%, is the one line growing fast enough to lift mix.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

The cost base is commodity- and freight-linked: PET resin, aluminum, glass, fuel and energy, with the group bearing the risk and mitigating part through supplier pricing agreements and derivatives, and the filings do not split fixed from variable costs, so operating leverage is not measured. In nine months 2026 cost of sales rose by transportation costs of USD 105.9 million and materials costs of USD 33.0 million, and gross margin fell from 31.12% to 29.90%, so margins are defended mainly by pricing and productivity. Integration costs are fading, with other integration expenses of USD 55.4 million against USD 97.7 million, which helps reported EBITDA more than operating margin.

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital spending took 63.85% of operating cash flow in 2025 and 48.89% in nine months 2026, 6.52% and 5.83% of net sales, against base capital spending guided at 4% of net sales; the gap is mostly integration and disaster-related capital spending, USD 101.8 million of it integration in nine months, which should fall away as integration ends. Free cash flow converted 22.73% of reported EBITDA in 2025 and 32.69% in nine months 2026, and the group does not split maintenance from growth capital, so flexibility to cut capital spending in a downturn is real but not measured.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is high: national and regional beverage companies, retailer private labels and tap-water alternatives compete on brand, price and distribution, which limits price increases. New entry is limited by the cost of replicating a spring-source and depot network, which management calls difficult to replicate, while substitutes, from filtration to municipal water, cap the premium for bottled water. Buyer power is high because retail is consolidated and one customer is 21% of net sales, and supplier power is moderate because some packaging inputs come from few or sole suppliers; the credit effect is a margin ceiling that makes cost pass-through slower than cost inflation.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_prmb-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-K_2025-12-31_prmb-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_prmb-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_prmb-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 42

### External factors

External factors are a secondary credit driver on the provided materials, with three exceptions that are not quantified in the filings: local bans and taxes on plastic bottles and water extraction, tariff and freight cost pressure, and growing concern about contaminants in public water. They enter the monitoring triggers rather than the base view.

### Credit-translated SWOT

Credit strengths are scale, brands, spring-source redundancy, improving cash conversion and liquidity of USD 1,064.0 million at 2026-09-30. Credit weaknesses are leverage that depends on add-backs, margin pressure, concentration by product and customer, and payout policy. The opportunities that could improve credit quality are premium mix, the roll-off of integration costs and capital spending, and EBITDA growth that lowers leverage without repayment; the threats are freight and input cost inflation, plastic and water regulation, retailer pricing pressure, service-failure litigation and the sponsor's pledged shares.
Basis: calculation C05; calculation C23; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Financial profile and credit quality

The financial profile is adequate and improving. ### Qualitative downside scenario

A realistic downside starts with a freight, resin and aluminum cost spike or a service failure in Direct Delivery that costs volume, while price increases lag. Revenue growth stalls, gross margin falls further from 29.90%, and EBITDA falls; a 10% fall in last-twelve-months reported EBITDA, with net debt unchanged, lifts net leverage from 3.87x (3.91x pro forma for the dividend declared on 2026-11-03) to 4.31x and cuts interest cover from 4.05x to 3.64x. Free cash flow falls more than EBITDA because interest and capital spending are largely fixed, and if payouts continue at the 2026 rate liquidity of USD 1,064.0 million at 2026-09-30 erodes while leverage stalls above 4x, so the 2028 and 2029 notes are refinanced from a weaker position.

Management has levers: the new USD 300.0 million buyback is a ceiling and can be left unused, dividends can be cut from about USD 175.2 million a year, integration capital spending of USD 101.8 million in nine months should end, and base capital spending is guided at 4% of net sales. With those levers resilience looks sufficient to avoid a liquidity problem before 2028; default risk is not assumed from this evidence, and the first reading of stress would be a payout that exceeds free cash flow while net leverage on management's measure is above 3.5x.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Primo Brands presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, brands and a spring-source network, but credit quality is constrained by leverage that rests on add-backs, margin pressure in 2026 and a payout policy that the sponsor's falling stake no longer checks. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow rose to USD 319.6 million in nine months and interest cover is 4.05x, though adjusted free cash flow overstates it by USD 283.3 million. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on EBITDA recovery and on payouts staying below free cash flow before the 2028 euro notes fall due on 2028-10-31, and the primary downside path is cost inflation and service disruption lowering EBITDA while payouts continue. Further analysis would require a printed maturity schedule, covenant definitions, the sponsor's current holding and fourth-quarter results. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: shareholder payouts outpacing free cash flow while margin pressure persists.
