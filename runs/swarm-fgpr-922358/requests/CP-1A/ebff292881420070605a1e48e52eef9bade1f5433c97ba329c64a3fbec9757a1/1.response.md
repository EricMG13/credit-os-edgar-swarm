## Findings

### Bottom line

Creditor governance risk is medium: the general partner is controlled by an employee-owned parent, and fiscal 2026 preferred and Class B distributions equalled 229.27% of free cash flow, while the March 2026 Class B conversion removed a brake on Class A payouts.
Basis: calculation C07; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 154; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The risk is not high because no cash went to Class A unitholders or the general partner in fiscal 2025 or fiscal 2026, the preferred units are paid in cash rather than in kind, the general partner takes no management fee and seven of nine directors are independent. It is not low because the preferred holders may gain a right to force a sale from March 2031, the partnership agreements remove default fiduciary duties, and the executed debt documents that set what may be paid out were not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 48–49; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 95; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 106; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 148–154; calculation C12

### Key findings

Control rests with the general partner, not with unitholders, and the owner holds a small share of the units. Ferrellgas, Inc. manages both partnerships, is wholly owned by Ferrell Companies, and that parent is owned 100% by an employee stock ownership trust whose independent trustee votes the shares. Ferrell Companies and its affiliates beneficially own 10.03% of the Class A units, and the Chairman of the board holds a further 2.23%, so creditors cannot count on unitholder votes to restrain the general partner.
Basis: calculation C04; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 103–105; 10-Q_2026-01-31_fgpr-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 11

The preferred units are the real counterweight and also the largest claim ahead of the equity. The USD 700.0 million issue carries a cash distribution rate that steps up each year to a cap, so at the stated rates the annual cash cost rises by 216.9 bp of the issue between the original rate and the cap, which would take it from 19.51% to 24.24% of fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA. The holders have a board seat, consent rights over change of control, indebtedness and similar actions, and from March 2031 a path to a forced sale of the operating partnership if more than a third of the units are still outstanding and are not redeemed.
Basis: calculation C06; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 148–152; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 36–37

Payouts to holders ranking below creditors were large in fiscal 2026 and were not fully covered by cash generation. Preferred distributions of USD 65.7 million and the final Class B distribution of USD 107.0 million together were 229.27% of free cash flow. The operating partnership, which is the borrower, paid the USD 107.0 million up to Ferrellgas Partners on March 6, 2026 and Ferrellgas Partners paid it to Class B holders a week later. The revolver was drawn by USD 87.5 million in the same year, which the issuer attributes to refinancing and litigation settlement costs, and the filing does not say how the Class B payment was funded.
Basis: calculation C05; calculation C07; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 150–154; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 69; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 73; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 118; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The Class B conversion reduced control risk but also removed a constraint on Class A payouts. Class B holders had received cash equal to the USD 357.0 million principal of the notes they exchanged in 2021, and on March 16, 2026 their units converted into Class A units at the lower conversion factor, 15 days before it rose, and the units issued equal 57.23% of the Class A units now outstanding. The conversion ended their contingent right to acquire the general partner interests and ended a rule that required Class B holders to receive six times any payout to Class A holders or the general partner, which had made such payouts costly.
Basis: calculation C04; calculation C05; 10-K_2025-07-31_fgpr-20250731.htm.jsonl, pages 155–156; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 153–154; 10-Q_2026-01-31_fgpr-20260131.htm.jsonl, pages 27–28

Payouts to Class A holders and the general partner are now limited by contract, and the contracts were not supplied. The Ferrellgas Partners agreement requires quarterly distribution of all available cash within 45 days of quarter end, and that cash depends on what the operating partnership may pay up under net leverage and liquidity tests in the credit agreement, both note indentures and the preferred-unit terms. No Class A or general partner distribution was paid in fiscal 2025 or 2026. Capacity and willingness are separate questions: the tests are described but cannot be worked, and the absence of payouts was also the result of the six-times rule, so it is weak evidence of restraint.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 36–37; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 69; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 139; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 144–146; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 152–154

Related-party leakage is small, but the cost base itself runs through the general partner. The partnership has no employees, and reimbursement of the general partner's costs of USD 347.7 million was 49.98% of operating and administrative expense in fiscal 2026. The general partner takes no management fee, and the disclosed payments to directors' firms were USD 1.2 million for refinancing advice and USD 0.8 million for legal services, against USD 3.0 million of legal fees a year earlier.
Basis: calculation C08; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 95; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 105–106; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 163

The board is mostly independent and reporting is conventional, but board and finance leadership changed within the year. Seven of nine directors are independent and the audit committee is made up of independent financial experts; two directors joined in 2026, one of them a paid adviser who is not counted as independent, and the director designated by Class B holders was removed after conversion. No chief financial officer is listed: the principal financial officer is the Vice President and Corporate Controller.
Basis: calculation C12; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 83–93; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 103–106; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 81

The business is the second-largest US retail propane marketer, with earnings driven by weather rather than by customer concentration. Retail sales were 64.64% of fiscal 2026 revenue and wholesale, mainly portable tank exchange and bulk sales to resellers, 28.78%. A winter 11% warmer than the year before cut volumes by 3.13%, while gross margin rose to 54.93% of revenue from 52.77%, and the fourth quarter contributed only 7.39% of fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA, which leaves the winter quarters decisive.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C03; calculation C10; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 8–11; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 60–62; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 11

The history explains why the structure looks as it does. The 2021 restructuring converted USD 357.0 million of Ferrellgas Partners notes into Class B units and created USD 700.0 million of preferred units, and the 2017 Eddystone lawsuit was settled in January 2025 for USD 125.0 million, paid in three instalments to January 2026, which released a USD 190.0 million appeal bond. Bridger Logistics, a named defendant, remains outside the guarantee group for the credit facility and both note series.
Basis: 10-K_2025-07-31_fgpr-20250731.htm.jsonl, page 168; 10-K_2025-07-31_fgpr-20250731.htm.jsonl, page 155; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 143–147; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 153–154

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a lower risk rating is conduct: no distribution went to Class A holders or the general partner for three years apart from USD 1.0 million to the general partner in fiscal 2024, the preferred units have been paid in cash, the general partner takes no management fee, no loans to management are disclosed, the October 2025 refinancing replaced the 2026 notes with senior unsecured notes on the same guarantee terms as the 2029 notes, and the one legacy lawsuit has been settled and paid. Moody's and S&P reversed their 2025 downgrades in October 2025, lifting the corporate rating from B3 to B2. Disclosure controls were effective, the audit report has no critical audit matters, and the Class B conversion removed the holders' option to take over the general partner.

The view would move to low if the executed documents showed tight payout baskets and the preferred holders' consent rights held firm while leverage fell, and if no Class A or general partner payout were made after conversion. It would move to high if Class A or general partner distributions resumed while the revolver was drawn, if the preferred units were paid in kind, if fees or transactions with directors' firms grew, or if the holders prepared to use the 2031 sale right before the 2029 and 2031 notes were refinanced.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 49–50; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 81–82; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 106; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 114–115; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 145–154; 10-K_2025-07-31_fgpr-20250731.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-01-31_fgpr-20260131.htm.jsonl, page 61

### Limitations

- The executed indentures, credit agreement and partnership agreements were not supplied, so every statement about what may be paid out rests on the issuer's own summaries; if the baskets prove wider than described, the medium risk level should rise.
- The identity and current holdings of the preferred and former Class B holders are not disclosed beyond one 2021 purchase of USD 246.0 million, so who can act as the required holders, and whether control of the preferred consent rights is concentrated, is not established; this could move the 2031 sale-right assessment either way.
- The funding source of the March 2026 Class B distribution is not stated, and the issuer ties the revolver draw to other costs, so the finding that cash generation did not cover it rests on the arithmetic of free cash flow, not on a disclosed link.
- Rating actions are known only from the issuer's own description at month precision, and no agency report, outlook or market price was supplied, so rating pressure from the ownership structure cannot be assessed.
- The preferred redemption amount depends on distributions paid since 2021, of which only three fiscal years are supplied, so the cost of redeeming the preferred units is not calculable and the size of the 2031 event cannot be stated.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The only new debt issue in the supplied period is the senior unsecured notes due 2031. On October 27, 2025 the operating partnership and its finance subsidiary issued USD 650.0 million of 9.25% notes at par and used net proceeds of about USD 637.5 million, with cash on hand, to redeem all USD 650.0 million of the 2026 notes. The 2031 notes mature on January 15, 2031, are guaranteed by the general partner and by domestic subsidiaries other than the named exclusions, and may be redeemed before January 15, 2028 at par plus a make-whole premium, or in part from equity offerings at a premium. On the same day the credit agreement was amended to extend its maturity to October 27, 2028 and to raise the maximum commitment to USD 350.0 million, subject to a borrowing base, with an accordion of USD 50.0 million.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 143; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 145–146

The refinancing lengthened the maturity profile at a higher coupon and raised the secured revolver commitment from USD 308.8 million to USD 350.0 million. The extra coupon is sized in Financial foundation. The credit facility now matures in 2028, before the 2029 notes, so the revolver comes up for refinancing first.
Basis: calculation C13; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 143–146

The equity-side transactions of fiscal 2026 were a final Class B distribution of USD 107.0 million paid on March 13, 2026, the conversion of all Class B units into Class A units on March 16, 2026 at the lower conversion factor, and the expiry of the 20% voting cap on Class A units on March 30, 2026. The Class A unit count rose only through that conversion, so no equity was sold for cash, and no contribution by the owner is disclosed.
Basis: calculation C05; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, pages 153–154

After year end the operating partnership borrowed a further USD 20.0 million under the credit facility on August 27, 2026 at 7.0% for working capital, which lifts short-term borrowings from USD 87.5 million at year end to USD 107.5 million before any repayment. The filing states no other subsequent event, and none of the fiscal 2026 cash flow shows an acquisition.
Basis: calculation C13; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 143; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 168; 10-K_2026-07-31_fgpr-20260731.htm.jsonl, page 118
