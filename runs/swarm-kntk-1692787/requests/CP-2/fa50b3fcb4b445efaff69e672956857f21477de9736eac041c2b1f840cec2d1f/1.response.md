Kinetik's fundamental credit is adequate but constrained: earnings are growing, yet payouts above free cash flow keep adding debt ahead of USD 2,200.00 million of 2028 maturities, so creditors rely on the revolver and on the board holding distributions.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better view is that cash generation is improving and management says it is sufficient. Free cash flow rose to USD 266.62 million in the nine months to 2026-09-30 from USD 104.80 million a year earlier, Midstream Logistics adjusted EBITDA grew 26.90%, adjusted leverage eased and the 2026 capital estimate is 3.71% to 15.04% below FY2025 spending. Management states that cash from operations, pipeline distributions and remaining borrowing capacity will exceed capital expenditure and the planned quarterly dividend over the next 12 months; that statement counts borrowing capacity as a source, so it is consistent with payouts funded by debt rather than a contradiction of it. Kinetik also takes title to products and sells to downstream buyers it describes as large investment grade entities, which limits credit risk from producers.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_kntk-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47; 10-K_2025-12-31_kntk-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 73; 10-K_2025-12-31_kntk-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 104; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C5; calculation C11; calculation C14

The view would improve if free cash flow covered distributions in full for several consecutive quarters, if the board reset the payout, or if the gap between adjusted and ex-gain leverage narrowed as pipeline earnings proved durable. It would worsen if one of the three largest customers cut volumes, if payouts continued while free cash flow fell, if the receivables facility were not extended before 2027-03-30, or if the 2028 refinancing had to be priced at higher leverage than today.

### Limitations

- Covenant levels, ratings and market prices were not supplied, so the liquidity and refinancing conclusions rest on facility size, maturity and the 2025 refinancing alone and would weaken if a covenant binds before the revolver is exhausted.
- No peer or sector data were supplied, so the adequate business profile is a judgement on Kinetik's own facts and is not ranked against other leveraged midstream issuers.
- The split of capital spending between maintenance and growth and the split of costs into fixed and variable are not stated, so the cost and capex flexibility view rests on the 2026 estimate and past spending and would fall if maintenance is a large share.
- The dividend paid on 2026-10-30 has no stated effect on period-end cash, debt or leverage, and the USD 50.0 million increase in receivables facility commitments requested on that date is outside the balance sheet figures, so liquidity and leverage after those events are not established.
- The downside sensitivities cut last-twelve-months adjusted EBITDA by fixed percentages with debt and cash interest held flat, so they show scale rather than forecast and ignore hedges, management actions and covenant tests.

### Company and ownership

Kinetik is a Permian Basin midstream operator that gathers, compresses, treats and processes natural gas, stabilizes and transports NGLs, gathers and disposes of produced water, and gathers and stores crude, with over 2.4 Bcf/d of processing capacity and minority stakes in two long-term contracted pipelines. For creditors the model gives fee-based cash flow tied to producers' drilling on dedicated acreage plus commodity exposure through product sales, so cash-flow visibility is moderate and depends on volumes rather than on long take-or-pay contracts.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_kntk-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_kntk-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 35

The parent is a holding company that owns about 50% of Kinetik Holdings LP, the borrower and note issuer, and guarantees the term loan, the revolver and the 2030 senior notes, so creditor claims sit at the Partnership while only consolidated statements are supplied. Entities controlled by Blackstone and I Squared Capital held a majority of the voting shares when the 2025 annual report was filed, and the Business & governance view of sponsor and governance risk is medium because no adverse conduct is documented while payouts exceed free cash flow.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_kntk-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 125–126; 10-K_2025-12-31_kntk-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 128

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue durability is moderate. The volume driver is producers' completion activity on dedicated acreage, which the company says depends on its customers' development and declines naturally from existing wells, and the price driver is fee rates plus, for product sales, NGL, condensate and gas prices. Committed revenue at fixed price and volume is USD 418.80 million, about 1.05x one year of service revenue, and minimum volume deficiency payments were USD 4.6 million in the nine months to 2026-09-30. Three customers produced 61.80% of 2025 revenue, and the contracts allow customers to suspend, reduce or terminate obligations after non-performance or force majeure events. Revenue is therefore recurring in form but concentrated and cyclical with producer activity, and a drilling slowdown would cut volumes before fees reset, which reaches EBITDA and then payout coverage.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_kntk-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-12-31_kntk-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 27–28; 10-K_2025-12-31_kntk-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_kntk-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C15

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margin resilience is average. Cost of sales moves with product volumes and prices, and operating expenses of USD 213.86 million in the nine months to 2026-09-30, 3% higher, sit almost entirely in Midstream Logistics; the split between fixed and variable cost is not stated. Midstream Logistics adjusted EBITDA was 43.47% of segment revenue in FY2023, 41.73% in FY2024 and 36.23% in FY2025 as product sales raised revenue, then 37.85% in the nine months to 2026-09-30 against 34.88% a year earlier. Margin on revenue therefore does not rise with revenue, and the cost base cannot be tested against volume declines from the filings, so operating leverage is a risk that is assumed rather than measured.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense. The company faces intense competition for gas and NGL volumes from integrated producers, pipelines and other midstream firms, based on location, reputation and pricing, so fees can be pressured when contracts roll and volumes can be lost where acreage is not dedicated; this moderates fee durability.
Threat of new entrants is moderate. Competitors may expand or build gathering systems and customers may build their own, which would cut volumes on the affected acreage; Kinetik's existing processing capacity and dedicated acreage slow but do not prevent this.
Substitutes are a long-term rather than a near-term threat. Other forms of energy compete with hydrocarbons, which would reduce producer activity over time, but no near-term effect on the credit is shown in the filings.
Buyer power is high. Producers can suspend, reduce or terminate obligations after non-performance or force majeure, and three customers account for most revenue, so contract terms give creditors limited protection against a customer's decision to move volumes.
Supplier power is moderate. Kinetik relies on third-party pipelines for residue gas and NGL outlets and faces equipment and skilled-labor shortages; a dispute with Energy Transfer over long-term NGL purchase agreements shows how a counterparty on the NGL chain can affect margin, although its effect is not stated.

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a slowdown in Delaware Basin completions as NGL and condensate prices weaken, which the company lists among its principal risks because volumes depend on customers' drilling on dedicated acreage. The first operational break is lower gathered and processed volumes; committed revenue of USD 418.80 million is only about 1.05x one year of service revenue, so it cushions little, and commodity swaps cover stated notional volumes only for the next 1 to 27 months with the hedged share of production not stated, so protection at lower prices is not established. Financial transmission runs through adjusted EBITDA and through the USD 203.43 million of pipeline distributions received in nine months, which fall if partner-operated volumes fall; a 10% fall in last-twelve-months adjusted EBITDA gives 4.11x leverage and 4.47x cover, and a 20% fall gives 4.62x and 3.98x, with debt and cash interest held flat. Distributions of USD 521.18 million continue until the board acts, so the payout shortfall grows beyond USD 284.92 million and is met from the revolver; liquidity of USD 1,069.42 million covers the current shortfall 3.75x, so a liquidity crisis is not indicated within the next year on these figures, subject to covenants that are not printed.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Kinetik presents an adequate business profile supported by integrated Permian gathering and processing scale, growing segment earnings and adjusted cash interest cover of 4.97x, but credit quality is constrained by payouts above free cash flow, customer concentration and the 2028 maturities. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because earnings are rising and capital spending is discretionary, yet last-twelve-months free cash flow covers only 0.45x of distributions. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on whether the board holds distributions, on covenant headroom and on the Partnership's rating, none of which is in the supplied filings. The primary downside path is producer activity or prices falling while payouts continue. Further analysis would require the credit agreements and indentures, ratings, market prices, peer leverage and customer terms; the business profile against typical leveraged midstream issuers is not established.
