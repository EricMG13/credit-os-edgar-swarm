## Findings

### Bottom line

Ati Inc's credit profile is improving, with total debt at 1.92x and net debt at 1.46x reported EBITDA and USD 1,012.3 million of liquidity, so near-term refinancing risk is low, but shareholder returns exceed free cash flow, so deleveraging relies on earnings.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C9

Aerospace and defense is about 68% of sales and commercial jet engines were 42% of Q3 2026 sales, so one cycle drives cash flow, though long-term agreements and confirmed orders of about USD 4.3 billion cover about a year. The nearest senior note maturity is USD 325.0 million in FY2029, which liquidity covers 3.11 times, and net debt rose by USD 82.5 million since FY2025 while last-twelve-months reported EBITDA of USD 968.5 million is 22.75% above FY2025.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-28_ati_ati-20251228.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-27_ati_ati-20260927.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-27_ati_ati-20260927.htm.jsonl, page 36; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C3

### Key findings

Leverage is falling because earnings are rising, not because debt is being repaid. Total debt over reported EBITDA fell from 2.54x at FY2024 to 2.22x at FY2025 and 1.92x on the last twelve months, but net debt rose from USD 1,332.7 million at FY2025 to USD 1,415.2 million at 2026-09-27, so the gain came from last-twelve-months reported EBITDA reaching USD 968.5 million, not from paydown. On FY2025 EBITDA of USD 789.0 million the same debt would be 2.35x total and 1.79x net, and on the lower FY2024 EBITDA of USD 745.8 million it would be 2.49x and 1.90x, so the ratios depend on the earnings step-up holding.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C5; calculation C6; calculation C7; calculation C8

Liquidity and maturities are the strongest part of the credit. Liquidity of USD 1,012.3 million, made of USD 442.3 million of cash and about USD 570 million of undrawn revolving capacity, covers the next senior note maturity of USD 325.0 million, due in the fourth quarter of FY2029, 3.11 times, and the 2026 refinancing replaced the USD 350.0 million of 2027 notes with USD 450.0 million of unsecured 2033 notes. The only maturity ladder printed predates that refinancing and shows FY2030 as the largest year at USD 633.4 million, consistent with the 2030 notes and the asset-based term loan falling due together, so the test of market access sits in FY2029 and FY2030, not before.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-27_ati_ati-20260927.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-27_ati_ati-20260927.htm.jsonl, page 47; calculation C1

Free cash flow is real but is being fully distributed. Free cash flow was USD 333.7 million in FY2025 and USD 198.0 million in 9M 2026, USD 421.1 million over the last twelve months or 43.48% of reported EBITDA, after inventories absorbed USD 255.1 million and capital expenditures were USD 216.6 million in 9M 2026. Shareholder returns of USD 277.2 million in the same nine months exceeded free cash flow by USD 79.2 million, and treasury stock purchases were 140.85% of free cash flow in FY2025, so cash that could have repaid debt went to holders and net debt rose.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C9; calculation C10

Revenue visibility is good for about a year and improving. Confirmed orders were about USD 4.3 billion at 2026-09-27 against about USD 3.6 billion a year earlier, and about 75% of them are due to be satisfied within the next 12 months; most major engine and airframe makers hold long-term agreements with the company, and no customer exceeded 10% of sales. This supersedes the year-end reading in Business & governance that orders fell from about USD 3.9 billion to about USD 3.7 billion, because the interim figure is later and shows growth over the year, although the backlog eased slightly from about USD 4.4 billion at 2026-06-28.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-27_ati_ati-20260927.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-06-28_ati_ati-20260628.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-28_ati_ati-20251228.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-28_ati_ati-20251228.htm.jsonl, page 11; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Business risk rests on one cycle. Aerospace and defense was about 68% of FY2025 sales and commercial jet engines alone were 42% of Q3 2026 sales, and commercial jet engines were 74% of the engine-component segment's Q3 2026 sales; the annual report calls the commercial aerospace cycle hard to predict, so a fall in engine build rates would reach revenue, margin and cash flow together, and other markets at 31% of Q3 2026 sales soften that only partly.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-28_ati_ati-20251228.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2025-12-28_ati_ati-20251228.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-27_ati_ati-20260927.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-27_ati_ati-20260927.htm.jsonl, page 40

Margins are improving on volume, price and mix, but the gain is concentrated in one segment and exposed to input costs. The issuer's Adjusted EBITDA margin was 23.0% in Q3 2026 against 20.0% a year earlier, and Advanced Alloys & Solutions earned segment EBITDA of USD 181.3 million against USD 90.4 million, a margin of 25.8% against 17.3%, while the High Performance Materials & Components margin eased to 23.2% from 24.2% on the cost of moving a new superalloy into production. Raw material surcharges pass most nickel and titanium moves through, but hedges cover only about 7% of a year's nickel purchases, so tariffs and delays can hit margin before recovery.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-27_ati_ati-20260927.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-Q_2026-09-27_ati_ati-20260927.htm.jsonl, page 43; 10-Q_2026-09-27_ati_ati-20260927.htm.jsonl, page 50; 10-Q_2026-09-27_ati_ati-20260927.htm.jsonl, page 17

Funding outside reported debt flatters leverage. Supplier financing of USD 83.0 million and USD 120 million used under the receivables facility total USD 203.0 million, 14.34% of net debt, and customers' receivable programmes took USD 377.5 million of invoices in 9M 2026. If banks or customers withdrew these programmes, working capital would absorb cash and net leverage would be higher than reported.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C13; calculation C14

Financial policy is the weakest dimension. The company pays no dividend but has USD 425 million of repurchase authority left, the asset-based facility sets no dividend limit while undrawn availability stays above the greater of USD 120 million and 20% of the facility, and the nearest senior note maturity is far enough away that buybacks can continue without an immediate refinancing constraint. Holders above reporting thresholds are not established, so no controlling owner is identified to weigh either way; the 60%-owned Chinese joint venture holds USD 106.1 million of the cash, which is not wholly available to creditors of the parent.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-28_ati_ati-20251228.htm.jsonl, pages 123–124; 10-Q_2026-09-27_ati_ati-20260927.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-27_ati_ati-20260927.htm.jsonl, pages 47–48

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an improving reading is that the fall in leverage rests on one earnings step-up concentrated in one quarter and one segment: Advanced Alloys & Solutions segment EBITDA of USD 181.3 million in Q3 2026 was about double the USD 90.4 million of a year earlier, net debt did not fall, and shareholder returns exceeded free cash flow. If earnings fell back to the FY2025 level with debt unchanged, total debt over reported EBITDA would be 2.35x and net debt 1.79x, above the 1.92x and 1.46x reported now, and 2.49x and 1.90x at the FY2024 level. Revenue growth of 9.29% over FY2025 produced EBITDA growth of 22.75%, so operating leverage works in both directions. The evidence for the view is that backlog is above its level of a year earlier and liquidity covers the next maturity 3.11 times, so a fall to the FY2025 or FY2024 earnings level would raise leverage but not threaten liquidity. The view would weaken if confirmed orders fall below USD 4.3 billion, if the engine-component margin keeps easing, if free cash flow stays below shareholder returns, or if buybacks are funded from the revolving facility. It would strengthen if free cash flow covers shareholder returns and net debt starts to fall while EBITDA holds.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The first strength is the earnings-led fall in leverage: last-twelve-months reported EBITDA of USD 968.5 million puts total debt at 1.92x and net debt at 1.46x, so interest cover and refinancing capacity improve for as long as earnings hold, and the issuer's own ratios of 1.74x and 1.33x on Adjusted EBITDA point the same way.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C3

The second strength is the liquidity and maturity runway: USD 1,012.3 million of liquidity covers the next senior note maturity of USD 325.0 million 3.11 times, so no refinancing is needed before FY2029 and the company can fund working capital and capacity growth from cash and the revolver.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1

The third strength is contract cover: long-term agreements with most major aerospace makers, no customer above 10% of sales and orders of about USD 4.3 billion, about 75% of them due within the next 12 months, fix volume for about a year and steady the cash flow that services debt.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-27_ati_ati-20260927.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-28_ati_ati-20251228.htm.jsonl, page 9; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The first weakness is shareholder returns above free cash flow: USD 277.2 million of returns against USD 198.0 million of free cash flow in 9M 2026, leave a gap of USD 79.2 million, so net debt rises even as EBITDA grows and leverage falls only if earnings keep rising.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C9; calculation C10

The second weakness is concentration in one cycle: about 68% of FY2025 sales are aerospace and defense, so a downturn in engine build rates would cut EBITDA and lift leverage together, and the company's own statement that the cycle is hard to predict leaves no basis for assuming it away.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-28_ati_ati-20251228.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2025-12-28_ati_ati-20251228.htm.jsonl, page 17; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The third weakness is funding outside reported debt: supplier financing and receivables facility funding of USD 203.0 million equal 14.34% of net debt and could reverse, which would raise net leverage above the reported 1.46x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C13; calculation C14

### Company description and credit linkage

ATI Inc. makes specialty materials and components, mainly nickel-based alloys and superalloys, titanium alloys and zirconium, hafnium and niobium products, in two segments of similar size: High Performance Materials & Components, which sells engine and airframe parts mostly under multi-year agreements, and Advanced Alloys & Solutions, which sells flat-rolled products into aerospace, energy, automotive and electronics markets. Qualified products and long-term agreements support visibility of cash flow, so debt service capacity is good while the cycle holds; the concentration in commercial aerospace means a cyclical decline would reach both segments, and the year of covered orders limits how fast it would show in cash flow and liquidity.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is cyclical, concentrated by end market but not by customer, and covered by contracts for about a year. Volume, price and mix drove growth of 26.49% in Q3 2026, led by commercial jet engine products, and about 75% of the USD 4.3 billion backlog is due within the next 12 months. Most products are sold under contracts with raw material surcharges and index mechanisms, which pass metal prices through, but the annual report states that long-term contracts can be renegotiated or re-priced at intervals and that excess capacity and competition have at times cut prices excluding surcharges. The credit implication is predictable revenue in the near term with re-pricing risk at renewals and cyclical risk beyond the order book.
