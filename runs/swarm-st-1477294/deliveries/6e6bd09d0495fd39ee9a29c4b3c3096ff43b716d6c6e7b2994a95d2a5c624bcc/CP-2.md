## Findings

### Bottom line

Sensata's fundamental credit profile is adequate and improving, as cash-funded debt retirement and rising free cash flow have cut leverage sharply, but automotive cyclicality, tariff exposure and add-back-heavy earnings leave creditors exposed to a volume downturn.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

Reported net leverage fell from 4.47x at 2025-12-31 to 2.29x at 2026-09-30, or 2.31x pro forma for the quarterly dividend approved in October 2026, and liquidity of USD 1,199.2 million covers the first maturity, in April 2029, 5.78x. Automotive earned 53.37% of FY2025 segment operating income and 91.39% of note principal falls due in 2030 to 2032, so the profile is adequate and not strong.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C8; calculation C12; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Key findings

Deleveraging is cash-funded and is the main reason leverage no longer constrains the credit. Carrying-value net debt fell from USD 2,276.8 million at 2025-12-31 to USD 1,859.6 million at 2026-09-30, or USD 1,877.1 million pro forma for the dividend approved in October 2026, and on adjusted EBITDA net leverage fell from 2.72x to 2.08x, of which debt reduction explains 0.50x and higher earnings 0.14x.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C3; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_st.htm.jsonl, page 12

The sharper fall on reported EBITDA overstates the repair, because the 2025 goodwill impairment left the trailing window: reported net leverage fell 2.18x, of which debt reduction explains 0.82x and the earnings recovery 1.36x, so the adjusted view is the cleaner guide to progress.
Basis: calculation C3; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Free cash flow is the strongest support and is rising. It was 44.52% of adjusted EBITDA in FY2024, 58.66% in FY2025 and 71.89% over the last twelve months, or USD 641.2 million, which would repay carrying-value debt in 3.76 years; part of the 2026 gain is a USD 53.1 million working-capital inflow that could reverse.
Basis: calculation C4; calculation C5; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_st.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4

Liquidity and the maturity profile give a long runway before refinancing matters. Cash was USD 553.4 million at 2026-09-30, with pro forma cash for the dividend approved in October 2026 not established, and the revolver was undrawn at USD 645.8 million, so liquidity of USD 1,199.2 million is 5.78x the USD 207.3 million of notes due in April 2029; the revolver matures on 2030-09-24, weeks after notes due 2030-09-01, so 2030 is the pinch point.
Basis: calculation C8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_st.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_st.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_st.pdf, page 85; 10-Q_2026-09-30_st.htm.jsonl, page 12

Refinancing is a cost risk more than an access risk. 91.39% of note principal, USD 2,200.0 million, falls due in 2030 to 2032 against a weighted average coupon of 4.93%, and quarter-end fair values were below principal for every series, from 90.29% to 99.38%, so replacement debt would likely carry a higher coupon; the documents give no yield or spread, so the size of any step-up is not established.
Basis: calculation C9; calculation C10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_st.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_st.htm.jsonl, page 12

Earnings are stable on the issuer's adjusted measure but depend on large add-backs and have not been tested by a volume downturn. Adjusted EBITDA margin held between 22.45% and 22.56% from FY2023 to FY2025 and reached 23.18% over the last twelve months, yet add-backs were USD 326.1 million, 63.97% of reported EBITDA, in FY2025 and USD 80.2 million, 9.88%, over the last twelve months, and the FY2025 revenue fall of 5.80% came from divestitures with organic growth of 0.1%.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a better view is that adjusted net leverage of 2.08x is already moderate, free cash flow of USD 641.2 million would repay carrying-value debt in 3.76 years, interest expense of USD 132.5 million over the last twelve months is covered 6.73x by adjusted EBITDA, ratings are stable at BB+ from S&P and Ba2 from Moody's, and the issuer cites an equity value of USD 6.0 billion; the profile would move to strong after a period of lower volumes in which margins and free cash flow hold, with add-backs staying below 10% of reported EBITDA and the 2030 notes refinanced early.
Basis: calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_st.htm.jsonl, page 40; PRESENTATION_Q32026_st.pdf, page 5; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The strongest case for a worse view is that no volume downturn sits in the record, capital expenditure of 2.34% of revenue in nine months is 67.09% of depreciation after 4.55% in FY2023, the working-capital inflow can reverse, and shareholder returns reached USD 173.5 million in the first nine months of 2025; the profile would move to weak on a sharp fall in automotive volumes with margins falling, a revolver draw above the level that triggers the leverage test, a return to heavy repurchases, a debt-funded acquisition, or a failure to address the 2030 maturities well before they fall due.
Basis: calculation C6; calculation C7; calculation C11; calculation C16; 10-K_2025-12-31_st.pdf, page 101; 10-Q_2026-09-30_st.htm.jsonl, page 14; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Limitations

- Cash interest paid is not printed for interim periods, so coverage is judged on interest expense for the last twelve months; the adequate view would weaken if cash interest is materially above expense.
- The 2023 to 2025 record contains no organic volume decline, so margin and cash resilience under a downturn are inferred from adjusted margins and documented cost actions and not observed; the margin finding would change after the first volume decline.
- No peer data or sector benchmark was supplied, so the comparison with typical leveraged-credit issuers in the sector is a judgement from the filings alone and the adequate label has no benchmark.
- Note prices are quarter-end estimates from the filings and no yield or spread was supplied, so the size of the refinancing cost step-up is not established and the refinancing finding could move either way.
- No forward credit model was run, so the outlook rests on one quarter of management guidance and the issuer's own full-year targets; the improving trend is not projected beyond them.
- The proxy statement and executed financing documents were not supplied, so distribution capacity, guarantee coverage and ranking enter this view only as the issuer's own summary and cannot be tested here.

### Company description and credit linkage

Sensata develops, manufactures and sells sensors, electrical protection and power conversion products in three segments, Automotive, Industrials, and Aerospace, Defense, and Commercial Equipment, which were 57.0%, 21.3% and 21.7% of FY2025 revenue. No customer exceeded 10% of revenue and the top ten customers average 36 years of relationship, which supports cash-flow visibility across platform cycles of five to seven years, but the filings state no order backlog and no contracted volumes, so visibility ends at the platform level and automotive and commercial equipment demand remain cyclical. About 61% of revenue is earned by subsidiaries outside the United States, which adds tariff and currency exposure to debt service. The portfolio has been pruned through the 2024 Insights Business sale, the 2025 sale of the Magnetic Speed and Position Business and the exit from Spear, which cut revenue by 6.5% in FY2025 and left a smaller, more focused earnings base.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_st.pdf, page 6; 10-K_2025-12-31_st.pdf, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-31_st.pdf, page 19; 10-K_2025-12-31_st.pdf, page 31

### Ownership and group structure

Sensata is a widely held English public company with no sponsor or controlling shareholder identified, so the sponsor-extraction mechanism is absent but beneficial holdings were not supplied. The plc has no debt of its own: the notes are issued by Sensata Technologies B.V. and Sensata Technologies, Inc., both indirect wholly-owned subsidiaries, and the borrower's distributions to its parents are limited by baskets in the credit agreement. Guarantee coverage is thinner since the August 2023 release of non-U.S. guarantors, and the sponsor and governance risk level is medium because of the acquisition record, wide distribution capacity and a one-year clean internal control record; the executed documents were not supplied, so these terms are the issuer's own summary.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_st.pdf, page 85; 10-K_2025-12-31_st.pdf, pages 88–89

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue follows light vehicle production in Automotive, housing, energy and data center demand in Industrials, and aerospace, truck and construction cycles in the third segment. Pricing power is limited: the annual report states that customers demand annual price reductions and that raw-material increases have historically been hard to pass on, although the USD 40.8 million of FY2025 tariffs was passed through and design-in positions give some protection. Third-quarter revenue growth was 3.7% in Automotive, 12.6% in Industrials and 13.4% in the third segment, with a USD 24.0 million one-time payment for a cancelled electric vehicle program inside Automotive, so revenue is recurring through platform life but exposed to program cancellations and volume swings, and the credit implication is that earnings can be stable in a flat market and uncertain in a falling one.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Cost of revenue was 70.73% of FY2025 revenue and consists mainly of production materials and employee costs, with research and development at 3.61% and selling, general and administrative expense at 9.62%. Manufacturing sits in lower-cost countries, notably China, Mexico, Malaysia and Bulgaria, which helps margins but ties them to tariffs and currency. Adjusted EBITDA margin held at 22.45% to 22.56% in FY2023 to FY2025, and gross margin was 31.41% in nine months of 2026 against 29.27% in FY2025, but the record holds no organic volume decline, restructuring charges recurred every year, and metals hedges are not accounting hedges, so margin defence under a volume shock rests on cost actions and pass-through that have not been tested at scale.

### Capital intensity and free cash flow conversion

Capital expenditure fell from 4.55% of revenue in FY2023 to 3.54% in FY2025 and 2.34% in nine months of 2026, which is 74.46% and 67.09% of depreciation, so part of the cash conversion comes from spending below the asset base's wear, and management expects up to about USD 150.0 million in FY2026. Free cash flow reached USD 641.2 million over the last twelve months, 71.89% of adjusted EBITDA, with cash interest of USD 157.0 million in FY2025 and a nine-month working-capital inflow of USD 53.1 million that may reverse. Capex flexibility and the option to stop repurchases give room to protect cash over the 2029 to 2032 maturity wall, but the conversion gain is part structural and part timing.

### Competitive forces in credit terms

Rivalry is moderate in Automotive, where the company competes with three to four suppliers per application, and sharper in Industrials, where low-cost suppliers in China have entered, which caps pricing and margin. New entrants face high switching costs because customers invest in selecting and testing sensors, but competitors influenced by large vehicle makers limit access to some customers. Substitution risk comes from customers producing in-house and from the shift to electrified platforms, which the electrical protection portfolio partly offsets. Buyers hold the stronger hand through annual price reductions, and suppliers of semiconductors, resins and metals can pass on cost swings that the company has found hard to recover, so the forces most relevant to credit are buyer pricing and input costs acting on margin.

### Policy, macro and technology factors

These factors are material to credit and are therefore assessed. Trade policy matters most: tariffs of USD 40.8 million were passed through in FY2025, most Mexican facilities operate under the Maquiladora program whose changes could raise costs, and the February 2026 ruling against certain tariffs leaves potential refunds uncertain and outside guidance. Economic cycles matter through light vehicle production, expected to fall 2% in 2026 on the issuer's cited S&P forecast, and soft U.S. housing in Industrials. Technology cuts both ways: electrification adds high-voltage content, yet slower electric vehicle adoption or cancelled programs, as in the third quarter of 2026, would reduce demand, while data center products, USD 27 million of revenue in 2025 and USD 48 million estimated for 2026, add a small diversifier.

### Credit-translated strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats

Strengths are cash-funded deleveraging, free cash flow of USD 641.2 million over the last twelve months, liquidity of USD 1,199.2 million with no maturity before 2029, and customer breadth with no customer above 10% of revenue. Weaknesses are the 53.37% share of segment operating income earned in Automotive, add-backs that were 63.97% of reported EBITDA in FY2025, recurring restructuring and goodwill write-offs, and negative tangible equity. Opportunities that could lift credit quality are growth in data center, aerospace and commercial equipment products, further cash retirement of notes, and an early refinancing of the 2030 notes. Threats are a fall in light vehicle production, loss of tariff pass-through, a return to heavy repurchases or debt-funded acquisitions, and a new internal control weakness.

### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale is strong, with USD 3,704.5 million of revenue in FY2025 and operations in many countries, and business diversification is average because three segments and a broad customer base spread the business but Automotive still dominates profit. Competitive advantage is average: switching costs protect incumbent positions while customers demand price reductions and low-cost rivals have entered Industrials. Cost and capex flexibility is average, with spending and distributions that can be cut but restructuring that has recurred every year. Margin stability is average because adjusted margins are steady but untested by a volume decline, and free cash flow stability is average because conversion is rising yet helped by capital spending below depreciation and a working-capital inflow. Ability to refinance is average, with BB+ and Ba2 ratings and no maturity before 2029 but 91.39% of notes due in 2030 to 2032 and quarter-end prices below par. ### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a fall in global light vehicle production beyond the 2% the issuer cites, joined by further electric vehicle program cancellations such as the one that produced a USD 24.0 million payment in the third quarter of 2026. The first operational break is lower Automotive volume, where fixed manufacturing costs are absorbed less and customers keep demanding annual price cuts, so margin falls before revenue restructuring cuts costs. Automotive earned 53.37% of FY2025 segment operating income, so the effect reaches free cash flow quickly, while working capital may reverse the 2026 inflow and tariff refunds are not counted. Liquidity of USD 1,199.2 million, the option to stop repurchases and cut capital expenditure, and no maturity before April 2029 mean the scenario is a leverage and refinancing-cost problem, not a liquidity distress, unless loans and uncollateralized letters of credit on the revolver exceed USD 195.0 million, which would activate the leverage test, or distributions resume. Observable triggers are fourth-quarter revenue below guidance, light vehicle forecast cuts, revolver draws, repurchases and the 2030 refinancing.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Sensata presents an adequate business profile supported by customer breadth, design-in positions and stable adjusted margins, but credit quality is constrained by automotive cyclicality, trade exposure and earnings that rely on large add-backs. Cash-flow durability appears adequate to strong because free cash flow is rising and capital spending and distributions are flexible. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the 2030 cluster of notes and revolver, on how note prices move, and on whether management keeps cash-funded deleveraging ahead of shareholder returns. The primary downside path is a fall in automotive volumes that cuts margin and free cash flow while tariff pass-through weakens and distributions resume. Further analysis would require interim cash interest, executed financing documents, peer benchmarks, market yields and a forward model.
