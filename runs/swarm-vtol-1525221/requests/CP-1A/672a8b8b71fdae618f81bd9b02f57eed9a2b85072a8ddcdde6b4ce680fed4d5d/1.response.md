## Findings

### Bottom line

Bristow is a publicly listed, contract-based aviation operator whose business mix is diversified enough to support its credit, but the Government Services transition is currently loss-making and 2026 capital returns and acquisitions are being funded from cash rather than from free cash flow.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 30 and 36; calculation C11; calculation C9

Offshore Energy Services supplies 66% of revenue and Government Services 26%, with the three largest customers at 36% of revenue; the planned Norway sale and the Berry Aviation purchase shift the mix toward government work. No sponsor or controlling shareholder is identified in the filings supplied, so creditor risk is set by management's own policy, and the legal capacity for distributions is not established because no indenture is supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 7 and 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7 and 10; calculation C1; calculation C3

### Key findings

- Revenue is diversified by segment and geography but concentrated in a few customers and contracts. The largest customer, a government customer within Government Services, was 18.9% of revenue and the three largest were 36.4%, so a single contract transition or loss moves group earnings more than the segment mix suggests, and the ten largest customers were about 63% of revenue.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 12 and 135; calculation C3
- Government Services is the weak point: revenue grew 25.3% in the nine months of 2026 to USD 350.1 million, yet the segment swung to an operating loss of USD 2.6 million from income of USD 6.7 million. Transition costs on the UK and Irish search and rescue contracts, penalties of USD 9.8 million for aircraft availability tied to supply chain delays, and fuel costs above fuel revenue absorbed the new revenue, so margin recovery as the UK transition ends by December 31, 2026 is the main operating driver of deleveraging.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 30–32; 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 8; calculation C11
- Offshore Energy Services carries group earnings: it earned operating income of USD 131.0 million at a 17% margin in the nine months, more than the group total after the segment and corporate losses. Management describes production-support work as less cyclical than exploration, but Europe revenue and flight hours fell in the period, which leaves the segment exposed to North Sea utilisation.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20 and 30–31; calculation C14
- Portfolio actions are reshaping the perimeter before their credit effect is visible. The Berry Aviation purchase at an estimated USD 110.9 million used USD 102.8 million of cash and added only 2.1% of nine-month revenue, while Norway generated 18.5% of fiscal 2025 revenue and the planned sale of its Norway Offshore Energy Services business has no stated terms, proceeds or timing.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 10 and 39; 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 135; calculation C8; calculation C10
- Capital allocation turned shareholder-friendly in 2026 while free cash flow was thin. Dividends began in the first quarter and USD 47.2 million of dividends and buybacks were paid in nine months, with USD 96.0 million of the buyback authority left, so distributions are discretionary and funded from cash until operating cash flow recovers.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 17 and 36; calculation C6
- Governance disclosure supports monitoring but control rights are not established. The auditor issued an unqualified opinion and internal control was effective at the 2025 year-end, revenue from related parties, the Canadian affiliate and its co-owner, was only USD 22.9 million in nine months, and nationality rules require U.S. citizens to hold at least 75% of the parent's voting interests and local nationals to own and control the UK, Irish, Dutch and Norwegian operating companies, yet board composition, shareholder concentration and the covenant and restricted payment terms are not in the filings supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 21–24, 84 and 92; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 12; calculation C7
- Labour contracts are a recurring cost and continuity risk: about 60% of employees are under collective agreements, the UK engineers' agreement expired in December 2025, and several Norwegian agreements were due to expire in March 2026, so wage settlements or disruption could hit the two largest offshore regions.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 19 and 35

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against caution is that Government Services work is on contracts of up to 10 years, the segment's loss is small against group operating income, offshore margins held at 17%, and the UK transition is scheduled to finish by the end of 2026, so the loss may prove a transition effect rather than a structural one. That view wins if segment operating income turns positive in the fourth quarter of 2026 and penalties fall as aircraft availability improves. It loses if the segment stays loss-making after the transitions end, if buybacks continue beyond free cash flow, or if the Norway sale is completed without proceeds applied to debt.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 8 and 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 30–32; calculation C14

### Limitations

- The proxy statement is not supplied, so board independence, shareholder concentration and management ownership are not established, which limits the conclusion that no controlling shareholder or sponsor exists.
- No executed indenture or credit agreement is supplied, so the capacity for dividends, buybacks and further acquisitions is not established, which limits the conclusion that distributions are discretionary rather than constrained.
- Norway results are not printed for the quarters, so the earnings and perimeter effect of the planned sale is not quantified, which limits any view on the group's earnings after it.
- Berry Aviation is consolidated for under three months with preliminary purchase accounting, so its steady-state effect on Government Services margins is not established.
- Transition costs on the UK and Irish contracts are described but not quantified separately from base operating costs, so the pace of margin recovery cannot be dated precisely.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The one completed transaction is the acquisition of Berry Aviation, Inc., now Bristow Special Mission Inc., closed on 2026-07-13 for an estimated purchase price of USD 110.9 million, up from the USD 105.0 million announced in June 2026, subject to purchase price adjustments. It adds special mission, intelligence and surveillance, maintenance, unmanned systems and on-demand cargo operations, was paid in cash with USD 102.8 million of net cash outflow, contributed USD 26.3 million of revenue and USD 2.6 million of net income from the acquisition date, and recorded goodwill of USD 23.4 million, with acquisition costs of USD 7.4 million in nine months.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5 and 10–11; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vtol_vtol-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 18; calculation C8

The new debt issue in the period was the January 2026 offering of USD 500 million of senior secured notes due 2033, which are guaranteed by certain material domestic and foreign subsidiaries and secured by first-priority liens on substantially all assets of the issuer and guarantors, including certain pledged aircraft. Part of the proceeds redeemed the 2028 senior secured notes in full on 2026-03-01, and in January 2026 the ABL Facility was amended to cut commitments from USD 85 million to USD 70 million and extend maturity to 2031. The company also announced plans on 2026-06-23 to pursue the sale of its Norway Offshore Energy Services business, which was not classified as held for sale or as discontinued operations because not all criteria had been met, and sold two AW189 heavy helicopters in sale and leaseback transactions in the nine months.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 13 and 33; 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 112 and 114
