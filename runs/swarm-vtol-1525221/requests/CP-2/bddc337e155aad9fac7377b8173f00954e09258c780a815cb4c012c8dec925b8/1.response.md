## Findings

### Bottom line

Bristow's fundamental credit profile is adequate, with net leverage of 1.99x, interest cover of 5.45x and no large maturity before 2031, but its cash cushion is thinning because free cash flow is small, shareholder returns have started and Government Services is loss-making.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C1; calculation C4

Creditors are supported by a long-dated secured capital structure and liquidity of USD 287.3 million, but the earnings measures are the analyst's because the issuer reports no EBITDA, and covenant headroom and legal capacity are not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33; calculation C4

### Key findings

- Leverage is moderate but has turned up. Net debt was 1.99x trailing twelve-month EBITDA at 2026-09-30 against 1.68x at the 2025 year-end, while total debt stayed near 2.91x, because 2026 cash paid for an acquisition, capital spending and shareholder returns, so deleveraging now depends on EBITDA growth rather than on repayment.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C6
- Coverage, liquidity and maturities are adequate. EBITDA covered cash interest 5.45x, liquidity of USD 287.3 million is about 10.50x current maturities of USD 27.4 million, and the schedule shows only amortisation before 2031, so a refinancing event is distant and the main near-term risk is operating cash flow, not market access.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 114; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4 and 33; calculation C4
- Cash flow is the weak link. Free cash flow was only USD 12.3 million on a twelve-month basis against USD 56.4 million in fiscal 2025, operating cash flow converted 66.98% of EBITDA, and USD 47.2 million of dividends and buybacks were paid in nine months while free cash flow was negative, so distributions are being funded from the cash balance.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C5; calculation C6
- Business risk is moderate and concentrated in one transition. Offshore Energy Services earns the group profit and Government Services holds ten-year contracts, but the government segment made an operating loss of USD 2.6 million in nine months and the three largest customers are 36% of revenue, so a slow end to the UK and Irish transitions would hit group earnings directly.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20 and 30; calculation C7
- Earnings quality limits comparability. The issuer reports no EBITDA, derived EBITDA includes disposal gains of about 2.98% of the total, and adding power-by-hour amortization would lower total leverage to 2.78x, while excluding disposal gains would raise it to 3.00x, so the range of reasonable leverage is wider than one point estimate suggests.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C8; calculation C9
- Financial policy and governance are mixed rather than adverse. There is no sponsor or controlling holder in the filings and audit and reporting are sound, but dividends and buybacks began in 2026 and the restricted payment terms that would constrain them are not supplied, so the discipline of future distributions is not established.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 52–53

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better view is that derived EBITDA grew faster than debt, the secured notes extended maturity to 2033, the ABL Facility is undrawn and the offshore business holds a steady margin, so the nine-month cash drain may be a one-off from the acquisition and transition costs. The strongest evidence for a weaker view is that free cash flow is close to zero before capital returns and the new government contracts are still loss-making. The view improves if Government Services turns profitable after the UK transition ends and buybacks stay within free cash flow, and weakens if free cash flow stays near USD 12.3 million while distributions continue.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 8; calculation C3; calculation C5

### Limitations

- Leverage and cover use the analyst's derived EBITDA, so the view that leverage is moderate could shift, for total debt, from 2.78x to 3.00x with the treatment of power-by-hour amortization and disposal gains.
- The maturity schedule is printed only at the 2025 year-end on a pro forma basis, so the statement that no maturity falls before 2031 beyond amortisation rests on that schedule and the later narrative rather than on a 2026 schedule.
- Stated ABL availability is USD 6.0 million below commitments less letters of credit and the reason is not described, so the liquidity conclusion carries that unexplained difference.
- No forward model, guidance or peer and market data are supplied, so the outlook and the downside path are qualitative and the profile is not benchmarked against comparable issuers.
- Covenant headroom is not established, so the view says nothing about default triggers or the point at which creditors would gain control rights.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strength: a diversified, long-dated contract base. Offshore Energy Services, Government Services and Other Services contributed 66%, 26% and 8% of fiscal 2025 revenue, government search and rescue contracts run for ten years, and the three largest customers were 36% of revenue, so earnings do not hang on one market and revenue visibility is better than for a spot-priced operator, which lowers the chance that a single market shock threatens debt service.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8 and 12; calculation C7

Strength: moderate leverage and strong coverage. Total debt was 2.91x and net debt 1.99x derived EBITDA with cash interest covered 5.45x, so a material fall in earnings could be absorbed before coverage reaches a level that stresses interest payments, which supports the view that the capital structure is serviceable at current earnings.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1

Strength: a lengthened and secured maturity profile with undrawn liquidity. The 2033 secured notes replaced the 2028 notes, the schedule shows only amortisation before 2031, and the ABL Facility is undrawn with letters of credit outstanding, so refinancing risk before 2031 is limited and the revolver provides a backstop.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 114; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 13–14; calculation C4

Strength: audited reporting and no identified sponsor. The auditor gave an unqualified opinion with effective internal control and no sponsor or controlling holder is named, so monitoring is possible from filings and no sponsor extraction behaviour is documented.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 92

Weakness: thin free cash flow against rising claims on cash. Free cash flow was USD 12.3 million on a twelve-month basis while dividends and buybacks of USD 47.2 million, an acquisition and unfunded aircraft commitments drew on cash, so liquidity has fallen and net leverage rose, which leaves less cushion if earnings weaken.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C5; calculation C6

Weakness: the Government Services transition. The segment's operating loss, availability penalties and fuel costs above fuel revenue absorbed new revenue in nine months, so earnings growth depends on the UK transition ending on schedule and costs receding, and delay would hold group EBITDA below what the contract base implies.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 30–32; calculation C7

Weakness: customer concentration and labour exposure. The largest customer is a government customer within Government Services, about 60% of employees are under collective agreements and the UK engineers' agreement has expired, so a contract loss or a labour dispute in the UK or Norway would hit a large share of earnings.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 19 and 35

Weakness: unverified legal and covenant protections. No indenture or credit agreement is supplied and distributions are discretionary, so creditors cannot confirm what limits dividends, buybacks and further secured or other debt, which weakens confidence in the creditor protection behind the structure.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 52–53

### Operating model, margin and cash conversion

Offshore Energy Services supplies most revenue and more than the group's total operating profit, because production-support contracts for offshore platforms carry fixed monthly fees plus flight-hour payments, while Government Services adds ten-year search and rescue contracts whose start-up costs have so far held margins below break-even. Cost flexibility is limited because personnel and maintenance are the largest cost lines and about 60% of employees are under collective agreements, so margin falls quickly when utilisation drops, and the fleet needs continued capital spending that absorbed most operating cash flow over the last twelve months.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20; calculation C5

### Credit-translated frameworks

Competitive rivalry is moderate because tenders are competitive and incumbents hold an advantage, supplier power is high because a few manufacturers supply aircraft and parts and parts delays already cost penalties, and buyer power is meaningful because large energy and government customers can cancel some contracts on notice, so margins are defended mainly by safety record and incumbency. Regulation matters more than macro factors: nationality ownership rules limit foreign control, and fuel price swings are recouped with a lag, which makes fuel a margin timing risk rather than a solvency risk. Credit-translated strengths are contract tenor, diversification and a lengthened maturity profile; weaknesses are thin free cash flow, the government transition and customer concentration; opportunities are the end of the transitions and Berry Aviation; threats are labour disputes, supplier delays and a Norway sale without debt reduction.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 13 and 33; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40

### Outlook

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months the supportive items are the scheduled end of the UK transition and a full year of Berry Aviation, and the headwinds are fuel prices linked to the conflict with Iran, supplier delays, labour negotiations and the cash cost of fleet renewal, so EBITDA growth is likely to be modest and free cash flow will depend on capital discipline. Over three to five years the 2031 and 2033 maturities are the key events and the 2036 UKSAR Debt amortises, so the capital structure should be refinanceable if leverage stays near current levels, but that depends on contract renewals and offshore energy demand that the filings do not forecast.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 8 and 114; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 23 and 40

### Downside scenario

The credit-relevant downside starts with the Government Services transition running past 2026, so penalties for aircraft availability and subcontractor costs persist while revenue grows more slowly than costs, which keeps the segment loss-making and holds group EBITDA flat. At the same time a weaker North Sea or lower utilisation in Europe would reduce offshore margins, and fuel price spikes would be recouped only with a lag, so operating cash flow would fall below capital spending and the buyback and dividend programme. Cash would then fall and the stated ABL availability would be the next source, which would raise net leverage and could shrink liquidity within a few quarters, but management has levers: the buyback authority can be suspended, capital spending can be deferred, and aircraft can be sold, so liquidity distress is unlikely without a combined loss of a large contract and a prolonged offshore downturn. The observable triggers are a continued segment operating loss after 2026, negative free cash flow after distributions, and falling stated availability.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_vtol_vtol-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 14 and 30–32; 10-K_2025-12-31_vtol_vtol-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 8; calculation C6

### Overall credit view

Overall, Bristow presents an adequate business profile supported by diversified long-dated contracts, moderate leverage and a lengthened maturity profile, but credit quality is constrained by thin free cash flow, a loss-making government segment and unverified creditor protections. Cash-flow durability appears adequate rather than strong because conversion fell, and refinancing capacity is likely to depend on offshore demand and on whether the Norway sale reduces debt. The primary downside path is a prolonged Government Services transition combined with continued shareholder returns. Further analysis would require the executed debt documents, issuer EBITDA definitions, a maturity schedule at the latest date and the proxy statement. Top risk: cash flow after capital spending and shareholder returns.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)
