## Findings

### Bottom line

Beazer is a land-light US homebuilder facing a change of control: Dream Finders Homes agreed on August 6, 2026 to buy it for $33.50 per share in cash, and the supplied filings do not say how the notes or revolver are treated at closing.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 34; Source readiness (CP-0)

The operating model spans 13 states and three segments with no mortgage subsidiary, but nine-month revenue fell with closings while the company kept repurchasing shares as net debt rose. Governance disclosure is orderly, yet post-closing financial policy cannot be assessed from these sources.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; 10-K_2025-09-30_issuer_bzh-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 51; 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 44; 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 57–58; 10-K_2025-09-30_issuer_bzh-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 10

### Key findings

The pending acquisition is the dominant fact for creditors, and its terms are only partly known. Dream Finders would pay $33.50 per share in cash, the company would survive as its wholly owned subsidiary, closing is expected in the fourth quarter of calendar 2026 and stockholder and regulatory approval are still required. The company says the interim operating restrictions will not stop it meeting debt service, but the filings give no termination fee amount, no financing plan and no treatment of the notes or revolver on a change of control, so the effect on bondholders is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 34

The revenue base is spread across 13 states but leans on the West. West produced 62.25% of fiscal 2025 revenue and 57.98% of nine-month fiscal 2026 revenue, against 63.43% in the prior nine months, so the mix is moving toward the Southeast, which rose to 17.82% from 12.52%. Net new orders fell over the nine months while the average price in backlog rose, which supports revenue only if closings recover.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C4; calculation C7; calculation C6; calculation C9; 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 42–44

The land-light strategy limits balance-sheet land risk but carries a large contingent commitment. The company controlled 60.0% of its active lots through option agreements, with deposits and pre-acquisition costs of $387.4 million at risk and a remaining purchase price of $1.63 billion that it expects mostly to exercise, so a demand shortfall would hit liquidity through forfeited deposits or cash spent exercising options.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 58

Capital allocation has favoured shareholders while leverage rose. The board approved a $100.0 million repurchase program in April 2025 and the company has bought back $78.7 million since, including $66.2 million in the first nine months of fiscal 2026, paying no dividends. Its stated goal is a net debt to net capitalization ratio in the low 30% range by the end of fiscal 2027, against 52.8% at June 30, 2026 and 39.5% at fiscal 2025 year end.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_issuer_bzh-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-K_2025-09-30_issuer_bzh-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 51; 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 57–58

Governance disclosure is orderly but incomplete. One person is Chairman, President and Chief Executive Officer, the auditor gave unqualified opinions on the statements and internal control for fiscal 2025, and a rights agreement protects tax assets, yet board independence, executive pay and large holders sit in a proxy statement that is not supplied. The company has no mortgage subsidiary, which keeps finance-company debt out of the perimeter.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_issuer_bzh-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-09-30_issuer_bzh-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 114–117; 10-K_2025-09-30_issuer_bzh-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 120; 10-K_2025-09-30_issuer_bzh-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 125; 10-Q_2025-12-31_issuer_bzh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 51

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against reading the change of control as a creditor risk is that the company refinanced and extended before the announcement: it issued $400.0 million of notes at par in June 2026, redeemed the remaining $357.3 million of its 2027 notes, and lifted its revolver commitment to $525.0 million with a March 2030 termination date. Moody's reaffirmed a stable outlook in June 2026, and the company states the merger restrictions will not impair debt service. The view would change toward comfort if the indentures show a change-of-control repurchase right at par or above, or if the acquirer's financing plan leaves the notes outstanding with a stronger parent. It would change toward concern if closing needs the notes or revolver to be amended, if S&P, whose outlook is negative, downgrades again, or if repurchases continue while net debt keeps rising.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 17–19; 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 57

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

On August 6, 2026 the company agreed to be acquired by Dream Finders Homes, Inc. for $33.50 per share in cash. A Dream Finders subsidiary would merge into the company, which would survive as a wholly owned subsidiary and stop being publicly traded. Closing is expected in the fourth quarter of calendar 2026 and needs stockholder approval, regulatory approval and other customary conditions. The agreement restricts the company to ordinary-course operations in the interim, which the company says will not prevent it from meeting debt service, and the risk list notes a possible termination fee without stating an amount.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 34

In June 2026 the company sold $400.0 million of 8.000% Senior Notes due 2032 at par in a private placement to qualified institutional buyers, with the first semiannual interest payment due in January 2027, and used the proceeds to redeem the remaining $357.3 million of its 5.875% Senior Notes due 2027, recording a $0.7 million loss on extinguishment. This extends the nearest note maturity from October 2027 to October 2029 and adds a longer, higher-coupon tranche.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16; 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19; 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 61

In March 2026 the company raised its unsecured revolving facility commitment from $365.0 million to $525.0 million, including up to $100.0 million for letters of credit, and extended the termination date from March 15, 2028 to March 13, 2030. It may request a further increase of up to $100.0 million for a maximum of $625.0 million. At June 30, 2026 it had $340.0 million drawn and $45.8 million of letters of credit, leaving $139.2 million of remaining capacity.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_issuer_bzh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-03-31_issuer_bzh-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 60
