## Findings

### Bottom line

QXO is a founder-controlled consolidator whose creditors are exposed to acquisition appetite rather than to extraction: preferred equity funded Kodiak, but new debt funded 40% of the roughly USD 15,000 million TopBuild purchase. Control sits with the founder, who held about 22.1% of the voting power, and his investment vehicle, which may designate 40% of the board. Preferred stock of USD 4,575 million at liquidation preference ranks ahead of common equity, and the three deals since April 2025 total about USD 27,866 million of price.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_qxo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 34–35; 10-Q_2026-06-30_qxo-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11; calculation C-08; calculation C-11; calculation C-13

Apart from contractual preferred dividends, the filings show no related-party fees, distributions to the founder group or creditor-adverse refinancing, but the proxy statement that would disclose them is not supplied, and ownership percentages are stated only as of the annual report, before the 2026 share and preferred issuances.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_qxo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2025-12-31_qxo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 34–35; 10-Q_2026-06-30_qxo-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 51; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

Control is concentrated in one founder group, which supports continuity of the acquisition strategy and removes outside challenge to it. Brad Jacobs held about 22.1% of the voting power at the date of the annual report, and Jacobs Private Equity II, LLC may designate 40% of the board for as long as the investors in the 2024 equity deal keep at least 30% of fully diluted votes, a share the company put at 39.4% at that date. The board includes a lead independent director, but the independence of the other directors is not stated, and no later filing updates these figures after the 2026 share and Series C issuances, so the headroom above the 30% threshold is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_qxo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 34–35; 10-K_2025-12-31_qxo-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 111; 10-Q_2026-06-30_qxo-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 87; calculation C-17

Acquisition funding follows no fixed policy, so leverage tolerance has to be read deal by deal. Beacon used USD 4,500 million of new debt against a net price of USD 10,644 million, a share of 42.28%, and USD 1,400 million of that debt was repaid within a month from equity proceeds; Kodiak used no new debt; TopBuild used USD 6,000 million against a price of about USD 15,000 million, a share of 40.00%. None of the seven documents states a leverage target, a credit-rating objective or a deleveraging plan after TopBuild; management states a plan to more than double EBITDA by 2030, which is a growth goal without a base year.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_qxo-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 52–55; 10-Q_2026-06-30_qxo-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11; 10-Q_2026-06-30_qxo-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-06-30_qxo-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-Q_2026-06-30_qxo-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-06-30_qxo-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 46–49; EX-99.1_2026-08-13_qxoq2-26earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation C-02; calculation C-03; calculation C-05; calculation C-08

The preferred stack is the largest claim ahead of common equity and behind all debt, and its cash-pay features draw on the same cash as debt service. Liquidation preferences total USD 4,575 million. The Convertible Preferred pays 9%, which is USD 90.0 million a year, and Jacobs Private Equity II, LLC holds 90.00% of it; Series C pays 4.75%, which is USD 142.5 million a year at the full USD 3,000 million; and the Mandatory Convertible Preferred pays 5.50%. The company paid USD 84 million of preferred dividends in the first half of 2026, a half in which operating cash flow was negative. Series C is carried as mezzanine equity because holders can require cash redemption after a fundamental change, and a change of control would separately trigger a 101% repurchase offer on the notes, although the definitions are not supplied.

No extraction, related-party payment or creditor-adverse liability management is documented beyond the contractual preferred dividends, and that finding is limited to what the filings disclose. The only common dividend was USD 17.4 million in June 2024, before any acquisition, and the company says it does not intend to pay another; the only change to existing debt terms described, apart from the new incremental loan, is a cut in the term loan margin from 3.00% to 2.00%. The company calls the limits on restricted payments not significant given that policy, so creditors should not treat those limits as a binding check on distributions.

The business is defensive in demand and seasonal in cash flow. The company states that about 80% of industry revenue is repair and re-roofing, no customer exceeded 1% of 2025 net sales, and the first quarter is expected to bring low profit or a loss. Complementary products reached 37.9% of second-quarter 2026 net sales, with Kodiak contributing USD 595 million of the total, against 22.4% in the second quarter of 2025, which held Beacon for only two months; supplier concentration is not quantified.

Reporting quality is adequate for a listed filer but leaves gaps that matter to a creditor. The auditor's opinion on 2025 is unmodified with one critical audit matter on the valuation of Beacon's customer relationships; management excluded Beacon, which was 86.1% of consolidated assets and 99.1% of revenue, from its internal-control assessment as the rules allow for one year, so no auditor attestation on internal control exists; and the purchase price allocation for TopBuild is not yet available.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is how the first two deals were funded. Debt remaining after the May 2025 prepayment covered 29.12% of the Beacon price, with the rest met from equity and cash; USD 1,450 million raised in May 2025 exceeded the USD 1,400 million prepayment; and Kodiak needed no new debt because Series C preferred stock covered its cash cost with USD 28 million to spare. The only cash flowing to the founder group in the filings is its contractual share of preferred dividends, USD 81.0 million a year, and the preferred investors hold instruments that rank behind every creditor.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_qxo-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-06-30_qxo-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-06-30_qxo-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-06-30_qxo-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-Q_2026-06-30_qxo-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 48; calculation C-03; calculation C-04; calculation C-06; calculation C-17

Three developments would raise the risk assessment: a further acquisition funded mainly with debt, evidence that cash is moved from the borrowing group to the parent to pay preferred dividends outside the restricted-payment limits, or related-party arrangements and board-seat rights for the Series C investors that the supplied documents do not show. Three would lower it: a stated leverage target together with repayment of the incremental term loan, conversion of preferred stock into common shares, which would end the cash dividends but dilute control, and a proxy statement that discloses an independent board majority and no related-party payments.

### Limitations

- The control conclusion rests on voting and designation percentages stated at the annual report date; if the 2026 issuances have taken the investors' fully diluted share below the 30% threshold, the founder group's board designation right would shrink and the concentration finding would weaken.
- The no-extraction finding covers only what the filings and notes disclose; the independence of most directors, related-party transactions, compensation and fees sit in a proxy statement that is not supplied, so a related-party payment would not be visible and the finding could reverse.
- The finding that creditor protections are limited relies on the company's summaries of the debt and preferred documents; if the executed documents define permitted holders or restricted-payment baskets more tightly than summarised, the view that distributions are unchecked would soften.
- TopBuild is described only by its purchase terms, so the operating model and the funding conclusion cover the business before 1 July 2026; the stated price also differs between filings, which makes the 40% debt share an approximation.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Beacon was the first acquisition and turned a software company into a building products distributor. It closed on 29 April 2025 for a net purchase price of USD 10,644 million, made up of USD 7,736 million for the shares, USD 104 million for converted equity awards and USD 2,948 million to repay Beacon's own debt, less USD 144 million of cash acquired. New debt at closing was USD 2,250 million of 6.75% senior secured notes due 2032 and a USD 2,250 million term loan, with an asset-based facility of up to USD 2,000 million alongside; equity raised around the closing came from private placements and public offerings. Beacon was determined to be the predecessor for financial reporting, but the statements supplied show the company's own results before 29 April 2025, which exclude Beacon.
