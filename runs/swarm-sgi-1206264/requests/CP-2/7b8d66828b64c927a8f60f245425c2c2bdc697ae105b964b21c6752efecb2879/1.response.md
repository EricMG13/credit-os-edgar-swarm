## Findings

### Bottom line

Fundamental credit quality is adequate but stretched: cash generation and liquidity support USD 5,381.6 million of debt, yet reported leverage of 4.25x, falling organic sales and unproven Leggett & Platt integration leave little room for another acquisition.

The main support is free cash flow of USD 698.7 million over the trailing twelve months and liquidity of USD 1,794.8 million against USD 600.5 million of debt maturing in 2027. The primary downside is a further fall in consumer demand reaching fixed store and plant costs as the 2027 maturities approach; the issuer's 2.69x covenant ratio does not measure that risk, because it adds USD 475.5 million of EBITDA beyond adjusted earnings.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C6

### Key findings

Scale and channel breadth are real strengths but are being diluted by lower-margin acquired earnings. The group has four segments, direct sales of 54.44% of net sales in the third quarter of 2026 and no customer above 5% of global sales before Leggett & Platt, which spreads demand risk across stores, wholesale partners and now components. Leggett & Platt earns a gross margin of 17.71% against 42.02% for the group, so each acquired dollar of sales carries less margin to service debt.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 46–47; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 35–36; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C5; calculation C11

Cash generation is the strongest support. Trailing operating cash flow of USD 931.3 million less capital expenditure of USD 232.6 million leaves free cash flow of USD 698.7 million, 0.55x trailing EBITDA, and dividends of USD 108.1 million in nine months 2026 took 16.44% of nine-month free cash flow. Capital expenditure and the dividend are the levers management controls, so the cash flow can protect creditors in a downturn if they are used.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 44; calculation C2

Leverage is the weakest dimension, and the range of credible answers is wide. Reported total debt to EBITDA is 4.25x and net debt to EBITDA 3.77x, also 3.77x pro forma for the USD 200.0 million Term B prepayment of 2026-10-01, while the issuer's covenant ratio is 2.69x. The reported figures overstate leverage because Leggett & Platt's debt is counted against 36 days of its earnings; the covenant ratio understates it because its EBITDA is 34.23% above adjusted EBITDA, and no source gives a full-period figure between the two.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 52–54; calculation C1; calculation C6

Liquidity and the debt calendar are comfortable for the next two years. Liquidity of USD 1,794.8 million is made of USD 612.1 million of cash, USD 412.1 million after the USD 200.0 million prepayment of 2026-10-01, and USD 1,182.7 million of revolver availability; it covers the 2027 maturities 2.99 times. The bank debt now matures in 2031, so the next test of market access is the USD 500.0 million Leggett & Platt note due 2027-11-15 and the securitization due 2027-10-07.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 19–21, 47; calculation C3

Interest cost is likely to rise as a share of cash flow, and a rising rate would add to it. The issuer's guide of about USD 240 million of cash interest for 2026 implies USD 68.5 million in the fourth quarter against an average of USD 57.17 million in the first three, and 43.11% of principal is floating rate, so 100 bp costs about USD 24.3 million a year, 3.48% of trailing free cash flow.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 47, 54; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C4; calculation C7

Earnings momentum is negative and is the main reason the credit is stretched. Net sales excluding Leggett & Platt fell 6.80% in the third quarter of 2026, pro forma net sales including it fell 6.30%, and adjusted operating income excluding it fell 5.91%, with Mattress Firm and North America each down 5.8% and International down 12.3%. A fixed cost base of stores, plants and leases, of which operating lease cost alone was USD 489.5 million in FY2025, turns that fall into a larger fall in EBITDA, which is why interest cover and leverage are sensitive to a further weak quarter.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 34–36, 49–51; 10-K_2025-12-31_sgi.htm.jsonl, page 103; calculation C5; calculation C10

Financial policy is acquisitive and the equity cushion is thin, so creditor protection rests on restraint rather than structure. Total debt rose USD 1,571.7 million, 41.25%, since 2024-12-31, goodwill and intangibles exceed stockholders' equity so tangible equity is negative USD 2,974.8 million, and the unsecured share of principal rose to 55.09% with USD 1,500.0 million of Leggett & Platt notes that carry no guarantee. No sponsor exists to inject equity, and the guarantors are not named, so the priority of the new debt against the older creditors is not established.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); Source readiness (CP-0); calculation C8; calculation C9; calculation C4

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against the stretched view is that the issuer reports covenant leverage of 2.69x against a 5.00x maximum, that nine-month free cash flow covers the dividend 6.08 times, that Leggett & Platt was paid for mainly in shares, and that Mattress Firm synergies added 420 bp to North America gross margin in the quarter. The view would move toward strong on a printed full-period EBITDA for Leggett & Platt near the USD 330.5 million pre-acquisition add-back, two quarters of net sales growth excluding acquisitions, and repayment of the 2027 maturities from cash flow. It would move toward weak if third-quarter sales declines persist, if the add-backs rise further, or if another debt-funded acquisition is announced before leverage returns to the issuer's 2.0x to 3.0x target.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 36, 45, 47, 52–53; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C2; calculation C6

### Company description

Somnigroup designs, manufactures, distributes and retails bedding through Tempur Sealy, Mattress Firm and Dreams and, since 2026-08-26, makes engineered components through Leggett & Platt. Direct sales were 63.48% of FY2025 net sales against 24.93% in FY2024 because Mattress Firm is a retailer, and the second acquisition adds a component business with a lower gross margin and different end markets. Store-based sales and component supply are discretionary and cyclical, so cash-flow visibility is moderate: modest seasonality and the absence of customer concentration help, while store leases and plant costs make earnings sensitive to demand, which matters for debt service and refinancing confidence.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_sgi.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-K_2025-12-31_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 6–8, 11, 48; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 30–31; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C11

### Ownership and group structure

The company is listed with no sponsor or controlling holder named in the filings and a fully-owned group of operating businesses plus 50.0% joint ventures that are equity accounted. Sellers of the two acquired companies hold new shares equal to 23.50% of shares outstanding, which links part of the owner base to the integration outcome. Because no sponsor exists, support in a stress cannot be assumed and the creditor claim rests on the group's own cash flow; the guarantor perimeter and the restricted group are not described beyond a general statement, so structural priority between the secured facilities, the Somnigroup notes and the Leggett & Platt notes needs a separate review.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 10; calculation C9

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is driven by consumer traffic and mix in Mattress Firm, wholesale partner orders in North America and International, and by component demand at Leggett & Platt. Third-quarter net sales excluding Leggett & Platt fell 6.80%, led by a 12.3% fall at International after the Dreams system change and a 5.8% fall at Mattress Firm and North America, while gross margin rose in North America on synergies and about USD 20.0 million of tariff refunds. Revenue is cyclical and discretionary rather than contracted, with limited visibility; the group has taken pricing actions against tariffs, but the North America margin gain includes refunds that may not repeat, so the sales trend, not price, decides cash flow.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 35–36; calculation C5

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Cost of sales is 57.4% of FY2025 net sales and selling and marketing 23.3%, with store occupancy and manufacturing depreciation inside cost of sales and operating lease cost of USD 489.5 million, so a large part of the cost base is fixed in the short run. Group gross margin fell to 42.02% from 44.89% in the third quarter because Leggett & Platt's margin is lower, and raw material and tariff exposure remains, although most U.S. sales are of U.S.-made product. Margins can be defended against modest volume pressure through savings already visible at Mattress Firm, but a larger fall in volume would deleverage stores and plants and lower EBITDA faster than sales.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 21, 48, 103; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 31, 35–36; calculation C5; calculation C10

### Capital intensity and FCF conversion

Capital expenditure is 2.92% of nine-month net sales and guided to about USD 255 million for 2026 including USD 75 million of one-time store refresh, so it is moderate and partly discretionary. Trailing free cash flow is 0.55x EBITDA after cash interest of USD 252.7 million, working capital release supplied 16.29% of nine-month operating cash flow and integration costs recur with each deal. EBITDA turns into free cash flow reliably enough to fund the dividend and debt repayment, but part of the conversion rests on working capital and on add-backs that need cash.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 44; calculation C2

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is high: domestic manufacturing is concentrated but retail is fragmented, competition is on price, brand and availability, and online direct-to-consumer entrants and retailer private labels press margins. Buyer power is moderated by direct ownership of the largest specialty retailer and the absence of customers above 5% of sales, while supplier power is limited because the group buys from several suppliers and now makes components itself. For creditors the force that matters is rivalry in a weak market, which caps pricing and makes volume recovery the route to margin repair.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 11–13, 47; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Macro and policy factors

Consumer confidence, housing activity, interest rates and tariffs are primary credit drivers: the issuer says the bedding industry was challenged in 2025 and remains pressured in 2026, interest rates remain relatively high, and tariff policy is uncertain, although the group expects no material tariff effect on 2026 results. These factors reach the credit through consumer traffic and through the floating-rate share of debt of 43.11%, so a weak consumer and higher rates would pressure sales and interest cost together.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sgi.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 31, 54; calculation C4

### Credit-translated SWOT

Strengths are the cash flow, liquidity and tenor, and the breadth of brands and channels. Weaknesses are leverage on reported earnings, falling underlying sales and thin tangible equity. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are Mattress Firm and Leggett & Platt savings, with USD 145.0 million of covenant add-backs still unrealised, and repayment of the remaining Term B Loan from cash flow. Threats that could weaken it are a prolonged consumer downturn, a further debt-funded acquisition, tariff changes and a delay in refinancing the 2027 maturities.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C6

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over 12 to 24 months the issuer expects capital expenditure of about USD 255 million and cash interest of about USD 240 million for 2026 and a leverage target of 2.0x to 3.0x on the covenant measure; the filings give no sales or earnings guidance. Tailwinds are the savings still to come, the lower margin on refinanced debt and, in the issuer's view, long-term industry growth from consumer awareness of sleep and housing formations; headwinds are weak consumer demand, a fourth-quarter interest cost above the first three quarters' average, Leggett & Platt's lower margin and a 2027 maturity cluster. Over three to five years, deleveraging depends on volume recovery and on discipline over further acquisitions, which cannot be assessed from the filings.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 44, 47; calculation C7

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a further weakening of U.S. consumer demand while Dreams recovers slowly. The first operational break is lower traffic and same-store sales at Mattress Firm and lower wholesale orders at North America, where the third-quarter fall of 5.8% in each already shows the direction, and Leggett & Platt's furniture, flooring and automotive customers would add cyclical exposure. Fixed store, plant and lease costs, of which lease cost alone is USD 489.5 million, would turn the sales fall into a larger fall in EBITDA, free cash flow of USD 698.7 million would shrink, and reported leverage of 4.25x would rise. Liquidity of USD 1,794.8 million, the capital expenditure guide of about USD 255 million and the dividend of USD 108.1 million in nine months are the levers, and the next observable tests are quarterly net sales excluding acquisitions, revolver borrowings of USD 516.5 million, the covenant ratio and the refinancing of the securitization and the Leggett & Platt note due in 2027. On the evidence, liquidity distress within 24 months is not indicated because liquidity covers the 2027 maturities 2.99 times, but the leverage reduction the issuer targets would be pushed back.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sgi.htm.jsonl, pages 19–21, 35, 44, 47; 10-K_2025-12-31_sgi.htm.jsonl, page 103; calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C5; calculation C10

### Overall credit view

Overall, Somnigroup presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, brands, channel breadth and strong cash generation, but credit quality is constrained by leverage on reported earnings, falling underlying sales and a thin equity cushion. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow covers dividends and capital expenditure but depends on demand. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the integration of Leggett & Platt, the 2027 maturities and the covenant EBITDA add-backs. The primary downside path is weaker consumer demand acting on fixed store and plant costs. Further analysis would require a full-period EBITDA for Leggett & Platt, the executed debt documents and the proxy statement. Fundamental credit analysis completed with limitations. Top risk: sales decline against a fixed cost base while leverage is high.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0); calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C5
