## Findings

### Bottom line
Everforth's credit quality is weakening: total debt reached 4.66x EBITDA from 2.24x at FY2023 as debt funded buybacks and acquisitions while earnings fell, so creditors depend on cash conversion recovering and on refinancing the 2028 notes.

The business itself is adequate, with revenue of USD 3,986.8 million over the last twelve months, a federal backlog of USD 2,943.4 million and positive free cash flow of USD 195.6 million. But liquidity of USD 374.5 million is mostly the revolver and is smaller than the USD 541.0 million of notes due in 2028, EBITDA covered cash interest only 4.15x against 7.48x in FY2023, and the issuer's covenant ratio of 2.12x against a 3.75x maximum cannot be tested. The primary downside path is weaker Commercial earnings feeding revolver draws before the 2028 maturity.

### Company description
Everforth, known as ASGN Incorporated until 2026-04-24, sells IT consulting, staffing and digital engineering through two segments: Commercial, about 70% of revenues, serving large corporate clients mainly under time-and-materials contracts, and Federal Government, about 30%, serving defense, intelligence, national security and civilian agencies on contracts of three to five years. Consulting is 62% of 2025 revenues, no client other than the federal government exceeded 10% of 2025 revenues, and virtually all revenues arise in the United States. The staffing heritage gives a labour cost that flexes with hours worked and a talent pool shared by short and long contracts, which supports cost flexibility, but the same structure means most Commercial revenue is short-cycle and can be terminated at will, so cash-flow visibility comes from the federal backlog and from client renewals rather than from contracted revenue.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power
Revenue is volume-driven by billable hours and headcount at rates set by competitive bidding and client negotiation, with little contractual protection: about 87% of Commercial revenue is time-and-materials, many agreements are non-exclusive and terminable at will, and the issuer cites a lower mix of permanent placement revenue and a shift toward lower-margin cloud services as 2026 margin drags. Consulting grew 14.35% in FY2025 while assignment fell 13.81%, which management presents as a move to higher-margin work, but Commercial segment operating income fell 23.32% in the first nine months of 2026, so pricing power is not evident. Federal revenue is steadier but flat: contracts last three to five years, backlog is USD 2,943.4 million and 2.5 times revenue, yet awards trail revenue (book-to-bill 0.9 times) and government budget delays, continuing resolutions and a shutdown reduced 2025 revenue. Revenue is therefore cyclical and discretionary in Commercial, budget-dependent in Federal, and not structurally declining but flat after a decline of 10.42% from FY2023.

### Cost structure and margin resilience
Cost of services is mainly compensation of contract professionals, so gross margin of 28.0% moves with rates and utilisation, while selling and administrative cost, which rose to USD 668.3 million in the first nine months of 2026 from USD 643.5 million, is largely fixed in the short run; with revenue 10.42% below FY2023, EBITDA margin fell 2.92 percentage points to 7.51%, which is the operating deleverage a creditor should expect to repeat in a volume decline. The company is spending to change its cost base, through back-office outsourcing to a single vendor, an enterprise resource planning system and strategy work, with acquisition, integration and strategic planning cost of USD 30.9 million in nine months against USD 15.8 million a year earlier, but the savings are not quantified. Margins can be defended against modest volume pressure through hiring and utilisation, but the 2026 record shows them eroding under adverse mix and integration cost, so margin resilience is limited.

### Competitive forces
Rivalry is high: the annual report describes an industry that is highly competitive and fragmented with limited barriers to entry, which caps pricing on assignment work and means a loss of share shows up first in Commercial gross margin. The threat of new entrants is similar for the same reason, so margin cannot rely on barriers. Substitutes matter through artificial intelligence, which the company is both using and investing in; as an analyst inference it could substitute for lower-end engineering and staffing hours, a risk to assignment revenue that is already falling, while the company's own data and AI offerings are a potential support. Buyer power is strong because clients can place orders with competitors and terminate at will, and the federal government awards work by competitive bidding. Supplier power sits with the skilled workforce and a single outsourcing vendor: wage inflation or a vendor failure would raise cost of services or disrupt back-office processing, which feeds directly into margin and receivable collection.

### Downside scenario
The scenario starts with a Commercial demand shock that the filings show is already under way: consulting book-to-bill was 0.9 times in the third quarter, clients can terminate at will, and federal awards are below revenue. The first operating break is lower utilisation and rates in time-and-materials work, which cuts gross margin below 28.27% while the selling and administrative base stays fixed. Financial transmission is lower EBITDA and slower collection, so operating cash flow falls further than earnings, as it did in 2026, and the revolver absorbs the gap together with fixed purchase obligations; revolver availability of USD 227.0 million is already 54.60% below the USD 500.0 million available at FY2024. As an arithmetic illustration only, a 10% fall in trailing EBITDA with debt and interest unchanged would lift total debt to EBITDA to 5.18x and cut cover to 3.73x. The leverage and refinancing consequence is that the covenant maximum steps down to 3.50x from the quarter ending 2027-06-30, the secured lenders' allowance narrows while the 2028 notes approach with liquidity of USD 374.5 million against USD 541.0 million. Management levers are paused buybacks, lower capital spending and savings from outsourcing, and these appear sufficient to avoid liquidity distress in a moderate shock but not to repay the notes without refinancing. Observable triggers are quarterly EBITDA below USD 85.5 million, revolver availability below USD 227.0 million, a book-to-bill ratio below 1.0 in both segments and any resumption of repurchases.

### Overall credit view
Overall, Everforth presents an adequate business profile supported by federal backlog, low capital intensity and positive free cash flow, but credit quality is constrained by total debt to EBITDA of 4.66x, falling earnings in the Commercial Segment and liquidity that is smaller than the 2028 notes. Cash-flow durability appears adequate but weakening because working capital and lower cash earnings cut nine-month operating cash flow. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on earnings stabilising near USD 299.6 million, on the revolver staying available to 2031-07-07 and on the market for the 2028 notes, for which no rating or price is supplied. The primary downside path is weaker Commercial earnings feeding revolver draws and debt-funded shareholder returns ahead of the 2028 maturity and the lower covenant maxima. Further analysis would require the executed credit agreement and indenture, covenant definitions and compliance certificate, the proxy statement, and any rating or market pricing. The fundamental credit analysis is completed with limitations. Top Risk: refinancing the 2028 notes after Commercial earnings erode further.
