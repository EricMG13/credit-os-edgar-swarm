## Findings

### Bottom line
Everforth is a publicly held IT consulting and staffing group with no sponsor or controlling holder named, so creditors depend on management's own capital allocation, which has funded acquisitions and buybacks with debt while earnings fell.

From FY2024 to the first nine months of 2026 the company repurchased USD 547.8 million of stock and paid USD 587.7 million for two acquisitions, while principal debt rose USD 350.2 million to USD 1,399.0 million and operating income fell from USD 364.1 million in FY2023 to USD 230.3 million in FY2025. The company has never paid a dividend, buybacks stopped in the third quarter of 2026 and the July 2026 amendment lowered the covenant maximum from mid-2027, but USD 922.5 million of repurchase authority remains and the proxy statement that would show owners, board independence and pay is not supplied.

### Key findings
Governance risk rests on management's capital allocation, not on an owner: no sponsor, fund or controlling holder is named, the company had 27 holders of record in February 2026 plus beneficial owners through brokers, and the board chair is separate from the chief executive. Because the proxy statement is not supplied, ownership above reporting thresholds, board independence and executive pay are not established, so the assessment reads behaviour only and settles on a Medium risk level that rests on behaviour alone.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_efor-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-K_2025-12-31_efor-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 76; 10-K_2025-12-31_efor-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 81; Source readiness (CP-0)

Shareholder returns and acquisitions were funded partly with debt while earnings fell, which is the main creditor-adverse behaviour in the record. Buybacks of USD 547.8 million and two acquisitions costing USD 587.7 million between FY2024 and the first nine months of 2026 compare with principal debt up USD 350.2 million, and the share count fell about 12% from the end of 2023 to October 2026, so cash that could have repaid debt went in part to shareholders. This reading is an analyst interpretation of the cash flow and debt tables, because the filings do not name the funding source of each use.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_efor-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 46; 10-K_2024-12-31_efor-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 60; 10-K_2025-12-31_efor-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-Q_2026-09-30_efor-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_efor-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_efor-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 6; calculation C04; calculation C05

The operating model is moving toward consulting but is not yet earning more. Commercial consulting grew to 32.41% of FY2025 revenues from 24.61% in FY2023 as assignment work fell from 46.71% to 37.69%, yet Commercial segment operating income fell from USD 344.1 million in FY2023 to USD 248.1 million in FY2025 and by a further 23.32% in the first nine months of 2026. The issuer attributes the 2026 gross margin compression to fewer permanent placements, a weaker currency effect at the Mexican delivery centre and faster growth in lower-margin cloud services, and selling and administrative cost rose on acquisition, integration and strategic planning cost of USD 30.9 million against USD 15.8 million a year earlier.

Commercial revenue visibility is short-cycle. About 87% of Commercial revenues in 2026 come from time-and-materials contracts, and the annual report states that many client agreements are not exclusive and may be terminated at will, so revenue depends on continued client orders rather than contracted backlog; the 70% revenue weight of this segment therefore carries most of the earnings risk.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_efor-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_efor-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-12-31_efor-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-K_2025-12-31_efor-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 12

The Federal Government segment gives steadier but shrinking visibility. It is 30% of revenues on contracts of three to five years with backlog of USD 2,943.4 million, 2.5 times revenue, but awards over the last twelve months fell 9.44% to USD 1,049.2 million, book-to-bill is 0.9 times, backlog is 4.64% below a year earlier, and cost-reimbursable contracts rose to 34.25% of nine-month segment revenue from 30.52%, which carry lower margin. Direct federal prime contracts were 26% of 2025 revenues and the annual report names budget delays, a government shutdown and efficiency efforts as causes of lower 2025 revenue.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest evidence against a Medium risk reading is that management has already moved toward creditors in 2026. Buybacks stopped in the third quarter after small purchases in the second, the company accepted lower covenant maximums from mid-2027, repurchased USD 9.0 million of its notes below par, has never paid a dividend, and the stated covenant ratio of 2.12x sits well inside the 3.75x maximum. Federal backlog of USD 2,943.4 million also supports cash flow even as Commercial earnings weaken.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_efor-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_efor-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_efor-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-06-30_efor-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_efor-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 39

Three things would move the risk level to High: a resumption of repurchases while leverage is rising, a further debt-funded acquisition, or a proxy statement showing a controlling holder or related-party payments. A sustained halt to repurchases, falling debt and disclosure of guarantor and covenant terms showing that distributions are restricted would move it to Low.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_efor-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-K_2025-12-31_efor-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-K_2025-12-31_efor-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 76; calculation C04
