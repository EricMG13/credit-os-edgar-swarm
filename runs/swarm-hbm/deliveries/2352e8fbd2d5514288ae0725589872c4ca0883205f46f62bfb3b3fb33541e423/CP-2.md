## Findings

### Bottom line

Hudbay's credit profile supports creditors today, with debt of USD 860.2 million at 0.68x adjusted EBITDA and no principal due before 2028, but earnings swing with metal prices and two operations, and growth spending is the main threat.

### Key findings

Leverage is low and the maturity profile gives two years of runway, but 94.0% of principal falls due in two maturities five months apart. Total debt of USD 860.2 million is 0.68x trailing adjusted EBITDA and 0.56x reported EBITDA, down from 0.95x adjusted at the end of 2025 after the 2026 notes were repaid. Net debt excluding the USD 334.5 million of cash reserved for Copper World is USD 303.8 million, or 0.24x, and the pro forma effect of the dividend declared on 28 July 2026 on that net debt is not established. The USD 272.0 million revolver draw matures in November 2028, 24 months from the as-of date, and the USD 542.4 million of notes in April 2029, so both need refinancing in the same window, and nothing is due before it.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19; calculation FC-01; calculation FC-12; calculation FC-13

Earnings are volatile and concentrated in two operations, which is the risk a creditor underwrites, not leverage. Adjusted EBITDA has ranged by a factor of 2.96x between the weakest and strongest of 11 quarters, and Peru and Manitoba earned 85.1% of first-half 2026 revenue. In the third quarter of 2025 mandatory wildfire evacuations shut Manitoba for most of the quarter, a temporary mill suspension hit Peru and a USD 60 million concentrate shipment slipped into the next quarter, taking revenue down 35.3% and adjusted EBITDA to USD 142.6 million. In the second quarter of 2026 port closures again deferred a concentrate shipment to July, and a 19.5% fall in copper sold cut adjusted EBITDA 23.9%. Even so, debt would be 0.92x the trailing EBITDA at the end of the third quarter of 2025, the lowest of the last five quarter-ends, 1.05x the 2024 level and 1.51x if four quarters matched the weakest, so interruptions threaten liquidity timing before they threaten solvency.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against the supportive view is that leverage is measured on trailing adjusted EBITDA that the company calls a record, struck at high metal prices, while the cost advantage and the profit after tax both depend on gold. If gold fell below the USD 3,850 per ounce assumed in 2026 guidance while a Peru or Manitoba interruption recurred, quarterly EBITDA could return towards the USD 142.6 million seen in 2025 just as USD 600.0 million of group-funded capital, Copper World and the 2028 and 2029 maturities converge. The view would weaken if the sanction decision commits group cash beyond the USD 200 million estimate, if the revolver is drawn again to fund growth, or if covenant levels prove tight on a definition the company does not print. It would strengthen if the USD 272.0 million draw were repaid from cash flow, if the 2028 and 2029 maturities were refinanced early, and if covenant levels showed wide headroom.

### Business model, ownership and revenue durability

Revenue is durable in volume but not in price: Hudbay sells copper, gold, silver, zinc and molybdenum priced off market quotations, and receivables on provisionally priced concentrate are marked to forward prices each quarter, so revenue moves with metal markets rather than contracts. Copper and gold together were 90.6% of revenue from contracts in the first half, Peru and Manitoba earned 85.1% of revenue, and 38% of 2025 revenue was gold. The sources do not name customers, state offtake terms or show a customer concentration, so buyer concentration is not established. The consequence for creditors is high operating leverage to prices with low contractual visibility, offset by 90.8% terminable commitments and low leverage.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins can be defended against moderate volume shortfalls, since adjusted EBITDA margin was 50.9% in the second quarter of 2026 against 55.7% in the first after revenue fell 16.6%, and they are exposed to input inflation and to gold. Onsite cost per pound rose 25.9% in a year to USD 4.23, on higher fuel and consumable prices and, in British Columbia, equipment maintenance timing, and the second quarter showed operating deleverage as adjusted EBITDA fell 23.9% on a 19.5% fall in copper sold. Three features cushion the margin: employee profit sharing, which reached USD 36.1 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 at high margins and was lower in Peru in the second quarter of 2026; a new Peru power contract and lower treatment and refining charges; and a record British Columbia mining rate. Adjusted EBITDA margin has ranged from 41.1% to 55.7% across the periods supplied.

### Market forces and policy

Five forces matter to credit mainly through price-taking, entry barriers and input costs. Rivalry and substitutes act through metal prices, which Hudbay does not set, and its hedges are small. New entrants face long permitting, as the New Ingerbelle permit and the judicial review application against it show, which protects the existing production base but delays Hudbay's own growth. Buyer power cannot be assessed because customers are not named, and substitutes are not discussed in the sources. Supplier power shows in fuel and consumable inflation, Manitoba labour shortages that required a mining contractor, and equipment failures such as a hoist gearbox, each of which raised unit costs in the second quarter of 2026.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 11–12, 19–20; 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-03-31_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, page 28; calculation FC-06; calculation FC-15

Political and regulatory factors are material for Peru and for permits. ### Outlook

The outlook for the next 12 months to 24 months is supportive but back-end loaded. Tailwinds are the delivery of concentrate deferred into July, the second-half ramp-up in British Columbia, higher Manitoba output, the Peru pebble crushers, copper hedges struck above the copper price assumed in guidance, improved consolidated cost guidance of negative USD 0.45 to negative USD 0.25 per pound, and the higher Constancia mill permit. ### Downside scenario

The realistic downside is a repeat of the 2025 sequence while gold weakens and Copper World spending begins. The initiating condition is a fall in gold below the USD 3,850 per ounce used for 2026 guidance together with an interruption in Peru or Manitoba, both of which occurred within 12 months. The first operational break is deferred shipments or a mine shutdown, as in the third quarter of 2025, when revenue fell 35.3% and adjusted EBITDA fell to USD 142.6 million. Lower gold credits then raise net cash cost directly, since every dollar of gold credit lost falls straight to EBITDA, while the cash tax charge falls only with profit. Free cash flow turns negative for a quarter, as it did in the third quarter of 2025, and the revolver, with USD 153.7 million undrawn, absorbs the gap. Leverage would be 0.92x on the trailing EBITDA at the weakest recent quarter-end, 1.05x on the 2024 level and 1.51x on four weak quarters. Management can cancel USD 183.9 million of commitments, defer Cactus and Mason spending and keep dividends at their small level, so resilience looks sufficient to avoid liquidity distress through 2027 on the stated figures. The observable triggers are quarterly EBITDA below USD 142.6 million, further revolver draws, a Copper World sanction without committed non-group funding, and no refinancing plan by late 2027.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Hudbay presents an adequate business profile supported by low leverage, a gold-driven cost position and cancellable capital commitments, but credit quality is constrained by concentration in two operations, exposure to gold and copper prices and a growth programme that peaks as its debt matures. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because company-defined free cash flow of USD 413.0 million over the last 12 months has absorbed taxes and sustaining capital, but conversion falls as profits rise. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on metal prices in 2027, the Copper World outcome and covenant headroom that the sources do not show. The primary downside path is a gold price fall or a Peru or Manitoba interruption coinciding with Copper World funding and the 2028 and 2029 maturities. Further analysis would require covenant levels, the audited 2025 statements, the Copper World venture and Wheaton agreements, customer terms and market evidence on pricing and ratings.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation FC-01; calculation FC-09; calculation FC-12

Fundamental credit is complete. Top risk: earnings concentration and gold dependence meeting the Copper World funding decision and the 2028 and 2029 maturities.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths, in order of weight. First, low leverage with no principal due before 2028: debt of USD 860.2 million is 0.68x adjusted EBITDA, so a weak year would not by itself threaten debt service. Second, a gold-driven cost position: credits paid for 85.1% of gross cash cost, which kept adjusted EBITDA at 50.9% to 55.7% of revenue in 2026, though it depends on gold. Third, liquidity and flexibility: USD 710.1 million of liquidity excluding Copper World cash and 90.8% of capital commitments that can be cancelled give management levers in a downturn. Fourth, creditor-aligned financial policy: debt fell USD 148.4 million in the first half, the dividend is 1.1% of operating cash flow and no sponsor is identified, which lowers the risk of cash extraction.

Weaknesses, in order of weight. First, concentration and interruption record: 85.1% of revenue comes from Peru and Manitoba and adjusted EBITDA has ranged by a factor of 2.96x between quarters, so cash flow can fall fast.
