## Findings

### Bottom line

Hudbay's business and governance profile supports creditors: a listed producer with no controlling shareholder identified in the sources, three operating regions and a record of debt reduction, but its Arizona growth programme now places the main risk in capital allocation.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 18; 6-K-EX-99.3_2025-12-31_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation BG-01

Peru and Manitoba earn 85.1% of first-half 2026 revenue, so interruptions there matter more than the spread across three regions suggests. The Copper World partnership with Mitsubishi and the all-share Arizona Sonoran acquisition added growth without new group borrowing, though the venture holds USD 334.5 million of cash the group cannot use. The annual information form, the shareholder list and the venture agreement were not supplied, so control rights and restricted-payment capacity are not established.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9, 12, 30–32; 40-F_2025-12-31_form40f.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; calculation BG-01

### Key findings

Ownership gives creditors alignment but little evidence to test it. The sources identify no sponsor or controlling shareholder and print no shareholder percentages, so a sponsor-extraction risk cannot be shown and cannot be ruled out from them. The one outside owner stated is Mitsubishi, which holds 30% of Copper World and has committed USD 600 million in two payments, and dividends paid in the first half of 2026 were only USD 5.7 million against operating cash flow of USD 508.3 million, or 1.1%.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 5, 9, 23; 40-F_2025-12-31_form40f.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation BG-03

The operating base is diversified by metal and region but concentrated in two operations. Peru contributed 45.8% and Manitoba 39.2% of first-half 2026 revenue, with British Columbia at 14.9%, and copper and gold together were 90.6% of revenue from contracts, so a fall in either price or an interruption in Peru or Manitoba moves cash flow far more than the third region can offset. The company states that gold was 38% of second-quarter revenue.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 30–32; 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, page 17; calculation BG-01; calculation BG-02

Operating execution is the near-term swing factor and is currently mixed. In the second quarter port closures deferred concentrate shipments in Peru to July, a hoist gearbox failure and reduced workforce availability held back Manitoba, and Copper Mountain ran with its primary SAG mill constrained, with a cash cost of USD 3.22 per pound against an annual guidance range of USD 1.50 to USD 2.50. Adjusted EBITDA fell from USD 421.9 million in the first quarter to USD 321.2 million in the second, so execution rather than ownership is what moves creditor risk this year.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 5, 10–14; calculation BG-05

Capital allocation has been creditor-friendly so far but is turning toward growth. Hudbay retired USD 102.5 million of the 2026 notes below par in 2025, repaid the remaining USD 472.5 million at maturity in April 2026, funded the Arizona Sonoran acquisition with new shares rather than debt and pays only a token dividend. Growth spending is now under way at Copper World, New Ingerbelle, Cactus and Mason, and British Columbia growth capital for 2026 was raised by about USD 30 million to USD 115 million.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 18; 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 19–20; 6-K-EX-99.3_2025-12-31_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6; calculation BG-04

Copper World is a controlled joint venture whose partner funding protects the parent but whose terms are not visible. Hudbay keeps 70% and consolidates it, Mitsubishi pays a further USD 180 million within 18 months and funds 30% of future equity calls, and Hudbay estimated its own share of remaining equity contributions at about USD 200 million with the first call no earlier than 2028. The joint venture board is chaired by the finance chief, which keeps control with Hudbay management, but the venture agreement was not supplied, so funding obligations, deadlock rights and distribution limits are not established.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 9; 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 18; 6-K-EX-99.1_2025-09-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 13; calculation BG-05

Disclosure is strong on financial reporting and thin on creditor-specific terms. The auditor gave an unqualified opinion on internal control over financial reporting for 2025, no code-of-ethics waiver was granted, and no material off-balance-sheet arrangement exists, but the annual information form, covenant levels and the 2026 management discussion were not supplied and the interim statements are unaudited, so board composition, related-party dealings and restricted-payment capacity are not established.
Basis: 40-F_2025-12-31_form40f.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 19

Management turnover is orderly but concentrated in finance and operations. The finance chief becomes President and Chief Financial Officer, the chief operating officer retires at the end of September 2026 and is replaced from within, and new heads were named for the British Columbia and Arizona business units, which supports continuity but leaves one executive leading both capital markets and the Copper World venture board. No source confirms that the retirement took place by the 5 November 2026 cutoff.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 15–17

Permitting and community relations are an unpriced risk at Copper Mountain. The Lower Similkameen Indian Band applied for judicial review of the New Ingerbelle permit amendment on 23 March 2026, after permits were received on 19 February 2026, and the project is expected to give first production in late 2028; no amount, provision or funding obligation is stated, so the effect on creditors is directional only.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-03-31_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15; 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against the supportive view is that capital allocation, not ownership, now carries the risk. Hudbay has two development projects in Arizona, an expansion in British Columbia and a Nevada project, the Copper World study is expected to show higher capital cost than the 2023 pre-feasibility study, and there is no sponsor to inject equity if a project overruns. The view would weaken if the venture agreement or credit agreements show that Hudbay must fund calls or allow payments out of the group, or if growth spending is funded by revolver draws, and it would strengthen if the sanction decision is paired with committed project financing and continued debt reduction.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19, 22; 6-K-EX-99.1_2025-09-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, page 13; calculation BG-05

A second counterargument is that diversification is narrower than three regions imply, because Peru and Manitoba earn most of revenue and Manitoba depends on a workforce it is still building. A repeat of the 2025 wildfire shutdown, social unrest or a port closure in Peru would test that concentration, and the view would improve if Manitoba and British Columbia deliver the higher second-half production the company guides to.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11; 6-K-EX-99.1_2025-09-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, page 3; calculation BG-01

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Copper World joint venture. Hudbay sold a 30% interest in Copper World to Mitsubishi in January 2026 under a subscription agreement dated 12 August 2025, for USD 600 million in total, of which USD 422.7 million of cash was received at closing and a further USD 180 million is due within 18 months. The transaction was accounted for as an equity transaction, so Hudbay keeps control and consolidates the project, and the cash received is reserved for the project and not available for general group use.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, page 9; 6-K-EX-99.1_2025-09-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 13; 6-K-EX-99.3_2025-12-31_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, page 5

Arizona Sonoran acquisition. Hudbay completed the acquisition of Arizona Sonoran on 24 June 2026 by issuing 46,794,082 shares valued at USD 1,135.6 million and cancelling shares it already held valued at USD 123.1 million, and accounted for it as an asset acquisition of the Cactus project. It brought USD 63.0 million of cash and USD 102.8 million of deferred land payments, and no new group borrowing was used.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 23; 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19

Debt activity. Hudbay repaid the USD 472.5 million 2026 senior notes on 1 April 2026 using cash and a USD 272.0 million draw on its revolving credit facilities, leaving one series of senior notes due in April 2029 with USD 542.4 million of principal. On 24 June 2026 Copper World LLC issued USD 52.0 million of 4.50% solid waste disposal revenue bonds through the Arizona Industrial Development Authority, guaranteed by the parent and its primary operating subsidiaries, with an initial mandatory tender date of 2 July 2036 that the release describes as the due date, and the proceeds are held as restricted cash.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 18–20; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-03-31_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, page 25; 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 18

Other transactions. Hudbay acquired the remaining 25% of Copper Mountain on 30 April 2025 for total consideration of USD 35.9 million, of which USD 4.5 million was paid at closing, agreed amended terms with Wheaton on the Copper World stream in August 2025, with a USD 230 million initial deposit and up to USD 70 million more on a mill expansion, and amended an option agreement with JOGMEC and Marubeni on 22 January 2026 over a 10% interest in three Flin Flon projects.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; 6-K-EX-99.1_2025-09-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 13; 6-K-EX-99.3_2025-12-31_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 25–26
