## Findings

### Bottom line

Hudbay is lightly levered, with total debt at 0.56x reported last-twelve-month EBITDA on 30 June 2026, but USD 334.5 million of its cash is reserved for Copper World, so creditors should anchor on net debt excluding that cash, at 0.24x adjusted EBITDA.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 12, 18; 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 24–25; calculation C-03; calculation C-06; calculation C-11

Total debt fell from USD 1,008.6 million at the end of 2025 to USD 860.2 million after the 2026 notes were repaid, partly with a USD 272.0 million revolver draw, and no long-term debt principal falls due before 2028. The audited 2025 statements were not supplied, so reported EBITDA is calculated and full-year interest cover is not established; on half-year figures EBITDA covered cash interest 23.79x.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 18; 6-K-EX-99.3_2025-12-31_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 40; calculation C-07; calculation C-09; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

Leverage is low on every definition the sources support, and it fell in the first half of 2026 as the 2026 notes were repaid and trailing earnings rose. Total debt to reported last-twelve-month EBITDA moved from 0.74x at the end of 2025 to 0.56x at 30 June 2026, and total debt to the company's adjusted EBITDA moved from 0.95x to 0.68x, as total debt fell by USD 148.4 million and adjusted last-twelve-month EBITDA rose from USD 1,060.9 million to USD 1,271.6 million. Reported EBITDA of USD 1,541.4 million is the higher figure because the USD 322.3 million impairment reversal on Copper World booked in the third quarter of 2025 is still in the trailing year; the adjusted figure removes it, so the adjusted ratio is the better guide to repeatable leverage.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, page 18; 6-K-EX-99.3_2025-12-31_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 40; 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 24–25; calculation C-03; calculation C-04; calculation C-06; calculation C-08; calculation C-19

The headline net cash position rests on money the wider group cannot use. Cash of USD 890.9 million at 30 June 2026 includes USD 334.5 million held by Copper World LLC for the project's exclusive use, and the company also deducts USD 49.8 million of restricted municipal bond proceeds to report net debt of negative USD 80.5 million. Counting only cash available to the wider group, net debt is USD 303.8 million, or 0.24x adjusted EBITDA, against the company's negative 0.1x. The revolver is secured on substantially all assets other than those of the Copper World, Mason and Cactus projects and the notes are not guaranteed by the subsidiaries holding those projects, so Copper World's cash does not support those lenders directly, while the USD 50.7 million of Copper World municipal bonds, guaranteed by the parent, sits inside total debt.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 14, 18–20; 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 24–25; calculation C-05; calculation C-11

The debt stack is short and simple, with no principal due before 2028. It comprises USD 272.0 million drawn under secured revolving facilities that mature in November 2028 and carry a USD 450.0 million commitment, USD 542.4 million of senior notes due in April 2029 and USD 52.0 million of municipal bonds whose initial mandatory tender date is in 2036. The April 2026 repayment of the USD 472.5 million 2026 notes used cash and the revolver draw, so USD 270.1 million of secured bank debt now ranks ahead of the unsecured notes, and cumulative debt through the notes is 0.53x reported EBITDA. The revolver and the notes mature within five months of each other, so the refinancing window opens in 2028. Principal exceeds carrying value by USD 6.2 million of unamortised issuance costs and discounts.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 18–20; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-03-31_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, page 13; calculation C-09; calculation C-15

Liquidity is adequate on the company's definition and thinner on the group's usable basis. Cash of USD 890.9 million and USD 153.7 million of undrawn revolver availability give USD 1,044.6 million, or 1.21x total debt; without Copper World's reserved cash the figure is USD 710.1 million, or 0.83x total debt. Undrawn availability fell from USD 425.2 million at 31 March 2026 to USD 153.7 million because of the USD 272.0 million draw. Outstanding capital commitments of USD 202.6 million are mostly terminable, so most near-term capital calls can be cancelled, and the sources print no covenant levels against which headroom could be measured.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 29; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-03-31_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, page 14; 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 3, 6; calculation C-10; calculation C-11; calculation C-17

Interest cover is very high but rests on half-year evidence. Calculated reported EBITDA of USD 713.6 million covered cash interest paid of USD 30.0 million 23.79x in the first half of 2026, against 18.17x in the first half of 2025, as EBITDA rose 31.3% and cash interest was almost unchanged. The first-quarter cash flow statements print no interest line, which suggests interest is paid in lumps outside that quarter, so quarterly and last-twelve-month cover cannot be worked, and no full-year 2025 cash interest figure is supplied. Interest on the repaid 2026 notes drops out of later periods, partly offset by interest on the revolver draw, whose margin the sources do not print.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; 6-K-EX-99.1_2025-09-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, page 43; calculation C-07; calculation C-14

Cash conversion is moderate and was helped by working capital in the second quarter. Net cash from operating activities was USD 508.3 million in the first half of 2026, including a USD 89.5 million working-capital inflow, after USD 262.8 million of cash taxes. Purchases of property, plant and equipment of USD 293.1 million left free cash flow, defined here as operating cash flow less those purchases, of USD 215.2 million, or 30.2% of reported EBITDA. The second-quarter working-capital inflow of USD 86.9 million partly reflects trade receivables falling to USD 163.1 million as concentrate shipments from Peru were delayed by port closures and delivered in July, so part of it is likely to reverse.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3, 12–13; 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation C-13

Total debt understates the claims on cash flow. Outside the USD 860.2 million total, the company carries lease liabilities of USD 53.0 million, equipment and land financing of USD 131.3 million that rose from USD 92.2 million at the end of 2025, deferred land payments of USD 102.8 million assumed with Arizona Sonoran, deferred and contingent Copper Mountain consideration of USD 29.1 million and a USD 8.3 million Wheaton refund liability, together USD 324.5 million. The land payments are secured vendor carryback loans at 6% with USD 82.2 million due on 28 August 2029, so with all of these items total debt would be USD 1,184.7 million, or 0.93x adjusted EBITDA, and the land loans are secured by deeds of trust on Cactus land. Decommissioning, restoration and similar liabilities of USD 336.7 million and pension and other employee benefit obligations of USD 86.1 million are further long-dated claims outside debt.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 15–17; calculation C-12

Stream deposits are a non-debt claim that creditors should track. Deferred revenue was USD 297.1 million at 30 June 2026, down from USD 317.1 million at the end of 2025, and is settled in precious metals rather than cash, but the company accretes USD 9.0 million of finance cost on it in the half year and carries a USD 8.3 million Wheaton refund liability on the stream for a mine that has finished mining, repayable in cash by 2052 if metal credits fall short. Amortising USD 29.0 million of the balance into revenue lifts reported EBITDA, and the adjusted measure deducts it.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2, 15, 17, 20–21; calculation C-12

Two transactions reshaped the balance sheet in 2026, mostly through equity and cash rather than debt. The Copper World joint venture closed in January 2026 with USD 422.7 million of cash received at closing and USD 169.8 million of deferred consideration recorded as a receivable, and Mitsubishi's 30% interest is a non-controlling interest of USD 463.4 million. The Arizona Sonoran acquisition on 24 June 2026 added USD 1,357.0 million of exploration property for new shares valued at USD 1,135.6 million plus shares already held, and brought USD 102.8 million of deferred land payments. The only later event is a dividend of C$0.01 per share declared on 28 July 2026, which cannot be converted to US dollars from the sources.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9; 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, page 22; calculation C-16

Four data conflicts affect how the figures can be used, though none changes the leverage conclusion. The fourth-quarter 2025 release called USD 1,008.6 million total principal debt, but that is the carrying value, and note principal was USD 1,014.9 million. Free cash flow comparatives differ between releases, for example USD 228.2 million against USD 225.0 million for the fourth quarter of 2025. Fourth-quarter 2025 cash from operations is USD 209.4 million in one release and USD 290.4 million in a trailing table whose own total implies USD 209.4 million. First-quarter 2026 adjusted net earnings are USD 159.1 million in one release and USD 161.0 million in the next. Trailing cash-flow measures should therefore be read with a margin of a few USD million.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.3_2025-12-31_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 22, 41; 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 24–25; 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-03-31_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, page 24; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest contrary evidence is that reported leverage ignores Copper World. The project's cash of USD 334.5 million is spoken for, the sanction decision is expected in late 2026, and the group's own net debt of USD 303.8 million will rise with any group funding of the project, so 0.24x adjusted EBITDA is a starting point rather than a ceiling. The view would weaken if the sanction decision commits the group to fund Copper World from the revolver, whose availability is already USD 153.7 million, and it would strengthen if the USD 272.0 million draw were repaid from operating cash flow.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-06-30_exhibit99-1.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 29; 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 3–4; calculation C-10; calculation C-11

Earnings are also cyclical: adjusted EBITDA fell from USD 421.9 million in the first quarter of 2026 to USD 321.2 million in the second on lower sales volumes, and results lean on high gold and copper prices. A sustained fall in prices or a repeat of operating interruptions would cut EBITDA, and total debt to adjusted EBITDA, now 0.68x, would rise. The audited 2025 statements, the revolver covenant levels and the Copper World feasibility study are the documents that would most change the view.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.3_2026-06-30_exhibit99-3.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 5, 24; calculation C-18
