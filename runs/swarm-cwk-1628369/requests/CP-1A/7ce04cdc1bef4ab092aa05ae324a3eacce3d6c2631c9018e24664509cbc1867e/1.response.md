## Findings

### Bottom line

Cushman & Wakefield is a public services group with no sponsor or controlling shareholder described in the supplied filings, and its recurring contract revenue and light capital needs support creditors, but ownership, board independence and the legal limits on distributions are not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 5–8; 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 140; calculation C1; calculation C4

Documented shareholder behaviour is creditor-aligned: the company pays no cash dividend, says it intends to retain earnings for operations, expansion, debt repayment and possible share repurchases, prepaid USD 300.0 million of term loans in 2025 and redeemed all of its 2028 secured notes by October 21, 2026. The main business risks are the cyclical and seasonal Leasing and Capital markets lines, the USD 177.0 million write-down of its 40% Greystone joint venture in 2025, and litigation and tax claims.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 40; 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 112; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cwk_exhibit991-october212026re-htm.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 77; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 26–29

### Key findings

- Earnings depend more on transaction volumes than the revenue mix suggests: contract Services is 66% of 2025 revenue, but Leasing and Capital markets are 42% of fee revenue, and about 31% of revenue is pass-through reimbursement that the company says carries substantially no margin, so a fall in deal activity, concentrated in the fourth quarter, reaches earnings and cash flow faster than revenue.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 4–8; 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 56; calculation C1; calculation C3; calculation C9

- Ownership and control are not established, so creditor alignment rests on stated policy and not on knowing who decides: no sponsor or controlling shareholder is described, the 2024 annual report records a secondary offering by former shareholders, ten directors including a non-executive chairman signed the 2025 annual report, the board stays declassified until 2028, and the shareholder register and independence of directors sit in a proxy statement that is not supplied.
  Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 40; 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 140 and 148; calculation C10

- Financial policy is creditor-aligned on the evidence supplied: no cash dividend, USD 300.0 million of voluntary prepayments in 2025, and in 2026 a term loan repriced to a lower margin, extended to 2033 and increased by USD 352.5 million, less than the USD 450.0 million of notes redeemed in the second quarter, so secured debt moved from notes to the term loan without new net borrowing; the company also says about 80% of debt is fixed through swaps, which is management language.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 40 and 112; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 22–24; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cwk_exhibit991-october212026re-htm.htm.jsonl, page 2; IR_Third-Quarter-2026_presentation.pdf, page 12; calculation C5; calculation C6

- The joint ventures are the main non-operating exposure: the 40% Greystone venture carries Fannie Mae loss-sharing obligations, was written down by USD 177.0 million in 2025 and is still carried at USD 379.9 million, and together with the 35% Onewo venture the equity-method holdings are 6.89% of total assets; the company states that any further impact on its consolidated statements would be limited to its 40% interest.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 77; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 28–29; calculation C8

- Litigation and tax claims look modest against liquidity but remain open: the federal antitrust claim was settled by consent decree in September 2026 with no monetary damages, state claims are pending, and a payroll tax dispute claims about USD 78.0 million plus interest with a further reasonably possible loss of up to USD 46.0 million net of tax benefit, 3.09% of the September 30, 2026 liquidity of USD 1,489.0 million.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 26–27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 2 and 23; calculation C7

- Disclosure is adequate for monitoring but the reporting basis moved in 2026: the auditor gave a clean opinion and the company concluded its internal control over financial reporting was effective, yet from January 1, 2026 it stopped reporting service line fee revenue and Adjusted EBITDA margin and reallocated corporate costs between segments, which weakens comparison with earlier years.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 75 and 78; 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 139; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 13 and 43

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a creditor-aligned reading is what the supplied documents cannot show: no shareholder register, no board independence detail and no credit agreement, so nothing here proves that distributions, new secured debt or asset transfers are limited, and a sizeable holder could change policy through the board. Within the business, the Greystone exposure is the least bounded item, with 67 loans on the Fannie Mae loss-sharing list at September 30, 2026 and a company statement that additional loan losses could create a potentially material liability for the venture, and the change in reported measures from 2026 makes margin trends harder to verify. The view would weaken if a proxy statement showed a controlling holder with distribution ambitions, if executed debt documents showed wide restricted-payment capacity, if Greystone losses grew beyond the 40% interest the company describes, or if the consent decree obligations or state claims produced monetary outflows. It would strengthen if the documents confirmed broad ownership, independent directors and tight limits on distributions.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 28–29; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 43; 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 36 and 140; calculation C8

### Limitations

- Ownership percentages, director independence, related-party detail and executive pay sit in a proxy statement that is not supplied, so the conclusion that no sponsor or controlling holder exists rests only on the absence of any such description in the filings searched and could be reversed by the proxy statement.
- The credit agreement and the indentures are not supplied, so the legal capacity to pay dividends, incur debt or move assets is not established; the creditor-aligned reading of shareholder behaviour rests on stated policy and actions alone.
- No client concentration figure is printed, only the company's description of its client portfolio as highly diversified, so the revenue durability conclusion rests on management language for the client base.
- Service line fee revenue and Adjusted EBITDA margin are no longer reported from 2026, so the fee-basis split of revenue cannot be updated beyond 2025 and the 2025 split may not describe 2026 mix.
- The company says S&P and Moody's upgraded its ratings, but no rating level, agency commentary, market data or peer comparison is supplied, so the view cannot test how agencies or investors treat the issuer's governance or business position.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

No acquisition, sponsor transaction or new bond issue appears in the supplied documents for the period; the transactions are refinancings and repricings of existing secured debt. On June 12, 2026 the company amended its credit agreement to reprice USD 847.5 million of the term loan from 1-month Term SOFR plus 2.75% to plus 2.25%, extend that loan from January 2030 to June 2033 and add USD 352.5 million, giving a USD 1,200.0 million term loan whose proceeds were used to redeem part of the 2028 notes.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 22–23; 10-Q_2026-06-30_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 69; calculation C6

The 2028 secured notes, originally USD 650.0 million, were redeemed in three steps: USD 450.0 million in the second quarter of 2026, USD 50.0 million in August 2026 and the remaining USD 150.0 million on October 21, 2026, after which no long-term debt matures before 2030. Earlier, in 2025, the company repriced its 2030 term loans three times, prepaid USD 300.0 million of them, and cut and extended its revolver on October 21, 2025 to USD 1.0 billion maturing in October 2030.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 23–24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 44; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cwk_exhibit991-october212026re-htm.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 111–112; calculation C5

The receivables securitization limit was raised by USD 50.0 million to USD 300.0 million on March 18, 2026 and its termination date extended to March 18, 2029; this is off-balance-sheet funding and not a new debt issue, and the effect of the refinancings on net debt is for the Financial foundation analysis.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 38
