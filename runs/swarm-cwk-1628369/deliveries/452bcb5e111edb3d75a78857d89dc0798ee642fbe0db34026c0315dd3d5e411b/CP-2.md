## Findings

### Bottom line

Cushman & Wakefield's fundamental credit profile is adequate and improving: recurring services income, light capital needs and falling leverage support creditors, but earnings that swing with deal activity and reliance on issuer add-backs leave less cushion in a downturn than leverage ratios suggest.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C2; calculation C13

When fee revenue fell 9.89% in 2023, adjusted EBITDA fell 36.57%; the same fall applied to the latest twelve months would put net leverage near 4.66x.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an adequate and improving profile is that the improvement is recent, cyclical and concentrated: Americas adjusted EBITDA margin on total revenue rose to 6.44% from 5.76% in the nine months, while EMEA revenue rose 15.16% and its adjusted EBITDA fell 7.25%, and Leasing and Capital markets are 29.38% of nine-month revenue against 27.49% a year earlier, so a reversal in deal activity would remove most of the gain; cash conversion has also weakened as earnings rose. The view would weaken if quarterly Leasing or Capital markets revenue turned negative, if the fourth quarter failed to restore operating cash flow, if the revolver were drawn at a quarter end, or if the unseen credit agreement or shareholder register showed room to distribute or add secured debt. It would strengthen if the next annual report showed fourth-quarter cash flow restoring free cash flow conversion, add-backs shrinking toward the 2026 nine-month share, and adjusted EBITDA holding in a weaker transaction quarter.

### Limitations

- The stress replay applies the 2022 to 2023 fall in the issuer's own adjusted EBITDA to the latest twelve months, using 2022 comparatives from the former parent's 2024 annual report; the documents do not explain why 2023 fell, so the replayed 4.66x shows sensitivity and not a forecast, and a different cause or cost response would change it.
- Management outlook is the only forward evidence supplied, with no analyst, market or rating-agency forecast, so the outlook conclusion tests the company's targets against history and could move if an independent view were available.
- Service line fee revenue is no longer reported from 2026, so margin resilience is measured on fee revenue only to 2025 and on total revenue and recast segment results for 2026, which weakens the claim that 2026 margins are higher on a like-for-like basis.
- The covenant definition, security ranking and guarantor structure are not in the documents read, so the assessment that refinancing and liquidity risk are low assumes lenders keep the access shown in 2025 and 2026 and could change if the executed terms are tighter than the filings imply.
- The share of cash flow that is seasonal is measured only for 2025, the one year with a derived fourth quarter, so the statement that the fourth quarter restores operating cash flow rests on a single year.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The first credit strength is a recurring contract base that needs little capital: Services is 66% of revenue under multi-year contracts, capital spending was USD 47.4 million or 0.46% of 2025 revenue, and full-year free cash flow rose from USD 101.2 million in 2023 to USD 293.0 million in 2025, so contract income funds fixed costs and interest while transaction fees add earnings, which lowers the chance that a deal slowdown alone exhausts cash. The second is falling leverage with long maturities: net debt over adjusted EBITDA fell from 4.35x at end-2023 to 2.96x, cash interest paid fell from USD 263.9 million in 2024 to USD 202.4 million over the latest twelve months, and no debt matures before January 2030, which lowers interest burden and refinancing pressure. The third is liquidity of USD 1,489.0 million at September 30, 2026, made of USD 489.0 million of cash and an undrawn USD 1,000.0 million revolver maturing October 2030, which is 1.78x the nearest maturity. The fourth is demonstrated market access: in June 2026 lenders agreed to reprice, extend to 2033 and increase the term loan by USD 352.5 million, and the company says its scale places it among the top three providers by revenue and workforce, which is management language.

The first weakness is earnings that move with deal activity while overhead does not: the 2023 fall of 36.57% in adjusted EBITDA against a 6.06% fall in revenue shows operating leverage that raises leverage ratios quickly in a downturn, so creditors carry more cyclical risk than the recurring services share implies. The second is dependence on add-backs: unadjusted net leverage of 4.63x and unadjusted cash interest cover of 2.26x are the thinner measures, and add-backs were 50.99% of reported EBITDA in 2025, so the improvement in adjusted metrics is only as durable as the issuer's judgement that the items do not recur. The third is weak cash conversion in 2026, with nine-month operating cash flow of USD 24.4 million, which leaves the year dependent on a fourth-quarter inflow. The fourth is contract and talent fragility: service agreements can end on short notice and the industry has high turnover of revenue-producing advisors, so revenue and earnings can leave faster than the recurring label implies. The fifth is that legal protection and control are not established, which prevents a firmer view of financial policy and of structural priority.

### Business model and credit linkage

Cushman & Wakefield is a global commercial real estate services firm with revenue of USD 10,288.2 million in 2025, about 53,000 employees in nearly 60 countries and four service lines; the United States is 69% of revenue. Contract Services income is described by the company as mostly recurring and is the predictable base against which fixed costs and interest are met, Leasing earns fees after leases are signed with some visibility from renewal dates, Capital markets fees arrive at deal close, and the reimbursed cost of contract work passes through revenue at substantially no margin, so revenue overstates the earning base and the creditor-relevant earnings pool is fee revenue of USD 7,061.3 million.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 4–6; 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 54; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C1; calculation C14

The group parent is a Bermuda holding company listed in New York that has no debt of its own, with the debt guaranteed jointly by substantially all material operating subsidiaries in the United States and certain in the United Kingdom, each pledging substantially all of its assets; no sponsor or controlling shareholder is described, so creditor alignment rests on stated policy of no dividends and debt repayment and not on knowing who controls decisions. The joint ventures, the 40% Greystone venture and the 35% Onewo venture, sit outside consolidated EBITDA, with Greystone carrying loss-sharing obligations under its lender agreement with Fannie Mae.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18

### Revenue durability

Revenue is partly recurring and partly cyclical, and the cyclical part is growing faster: nine-month revenue rose 9.92%, with Services up 7.13%, Valuation and other up 5.99%, Capital markets up 10.23% and Leasing up 20.32%, which lifted Leasing and Capital markets to 29.38% of revenue from 27.49%. Services contracts have multi-year terms and high switching costs, but many can be terminated for convenience on short notice, and no client concentration figure is printed beyond the statement that the client portfolio is highly diversified. The creditor consequence is that the contract floor under revenue is real but not contractually locked, so a fall in transaction income is only partly cushioned.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6; 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 27 and 29; calculation C6

The 2023 episode shows the cyclical half: revenue fell 6.06% from USD 10,105.7 million in 2022 to USD 9,493.7 million in 2023, and fee revenue fell 9.89%; the documents read do not state why, and the 2025 annual report describes clients delaying transaction decisions when credit is scarce or costly as a recurring risk, so the fall is evidence of sensitivity and not of one cause.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 74; 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 18; calculation C2

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins can be defended only in part under volume pressure. Direct employment cost fell 9.22% in 2023 against a 9.89% fall in fee revenue, consistent with the commission-based pay the company describes for many United States leasing and capital markets professionals, and absorbed USD 373.9 million of the USD 716.7 million of lost fee revenue; but indirect and overhead employment cost rose USD 41.6 million, other direct and overhead costs fell only USD 82.8 million together, other segment items that support adjusted EBITDA fell USD 27.1 million, and the adjusted EBITDA margin on fee revenue fell from 12.40% to 8.73%. Employment cost has since risen to 69.18% of fee revenue from 67.01%, so a decline of the same size would now take a larger share of lost fees.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2024-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, page 92; 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 100–101; calculation C1; calculation C2

The nine-month margin on total revenue improved to 5.90% from 5.66%, entirely in the Americas.

### Capital intensity and free cash flow

Capital spending is small, USD 47.4 million or 0.46% of 2025 revenue and USD 81.5 million or 0.74% of latest twelve month revenue, but it rose to USD 58.5 million in the nine months of 2026 from USD 24.4 million, 2.40 times, a USD 34.1 million increase that the company attributes primarily to relocating its New York office, so the rise is more likely one-off than a new run rate, though that is an analyst reading. Capex flexibility is therefore a minor lever, and the cash drains that matter are working capital and outflows to other non-current assets, which include receivables from advisors and other employees of USD 492.8 million at September 30, 2026: the outflow was USD 24.7 million in 2023, USD 58.0 million in 2024 and USD 121.2 million in 2025, and USD 72.0 million in the nine months of 2026. ### Competitive forces and external factors

Rivalry is high but the group has scale: competitors include several large global peers and many smaller local firms, in-house corporate real estate departments and outsourcing companies, and the company says it is among the top three providers by revenue and workforce, which is management language, so scale helps win multinational mandates but does not remove competition for renewals. New entrants matter more in regional brokerage, where firms are smaller and licensing requirements are one barrier, than in integrated facilities management, where global scale is needed, so the earnings most exposed to entry are the commission lines that also swing with the cycle. Substitutes are in-house teams and technology: the company is investing in artificial intelligence and says AI-driven demand for data centres supports leasing, but it does not quantify any fee erosion from technology. Buyers hold power through short-notice termination rights and the possible growth of large corporate clients, and suppliers matter mainly through third-party contractors whose costs are passed through, and through advisor compensation, where high turnover makes talent the real supplier.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 10 and 15; 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 21–22; 10-K_2025-12-31_cwk.htm.jsonl, pages 27 and 29

Macroeconomic and regulatory factors are a material credit driver because demand for transactions depends on interest rates, credit availability, tariffs and employment, as the company states, and because hybrid working has changed the use of commercial property. Revenue outside the Americas was 27% of 2025 revenue, but the company invoices in the currency of its costs, so currency translation is a secondary risk; the Bermuda corporate income tax under the global minimum tax initiative adds tax uncertainty without a quantified effect.

### Downside scenario

The realistic downside is a transaction slump that repeats the 2023 pattern.
