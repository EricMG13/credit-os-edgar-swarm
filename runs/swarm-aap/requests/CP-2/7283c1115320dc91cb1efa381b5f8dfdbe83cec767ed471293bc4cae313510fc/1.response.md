## Findings

### Bottom line

Advance Auto Parts is a recovering, thinly profitable retailer: USD 3,979 million of liquidity and no maturity before October 2027 protect creditors near term, but 6.67x leverage and supplier-finance reliance leave little room for another earnings setback.

Net debt is USD 303 million, or 0.60x, because cash of USD 3,085 million offsets most of the USD 3,388 million of debt; the pro forma figure after the dividend declared on 2026-10-26 is not established in the financial foundation. Reported EBITDA of USD 508 million covers cash interest of USD 200 million 2.54x. The main downside path is a withdrawal of the rating-linked supplier finance, USD 2,600 million, which would drain cash faster than debt service does. Covenant headroom, ratings and recovery are not established.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C04; calculation C06; calculation C07; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Key findings

Liquidity, not earnings, carries this credit through the next two maturities. Cash of USD 3,085 million plus USD 894 million of undrawn asset-based facility is USD 3,979 million, against the 2027 notes of USD 350 million and the 2028 notes of USD 300 million at year-end principal, so cash alone covers the 2027 notes 8.81x and both notes 4.75x. About USD 2,300 million of the cash sits in accounts under springing control that become restricted on a default or covenant breach, and the facility imposes minimum availability tests, so the usable amount is lower than the headline. The dividend declared on 2026-10-26 is a post-balance-sheet event whose pro forma effect on cash and net debt is not established in Financial foundation. Near-term refinancing risk is therefore low, and the test moves to the 2030 wall of USD 1,475 million.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C03; calculation C12; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 89–92

Leverage is high on reported EBITDA and the fixed-charge load is heavy. Total debt is 6.67x last-twelve-month reported EBITDA, and on the company's own adjusted EBITDAR, gross debt with lease liabilities is 4.82x. Cover is 2.54x on cash interest and 2.38x on accrued interest, and adjusted EBITDAR covers rent plus cash interest only 1.60x. The mechanism is that rent of USD 524 million and interest of USD 200 million come ahead of equity, so a fall in earnings reduces cover faster than the debt ratio suggests. In fiscal 2025, when EBITDA was USD 229 million, leverage was 14.90x, which shows how little earnings erosion the structure absorbs.
Basis: calculation C06; calculation C07; calculation C08; calculation C14; calculation C15; Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-11-05_aap-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 10

Cash generation has turned but depends on payables. Free cash flow was USD 109 million in the forty weeks to 2026-10-10 against USD -277 million a year earlier, yet the last twelve months convert only 17.32% of EBITDA into free cash flow of USD 88 million. Payables swung from a USD 270 million outflow to a USD 94 million inflow, and restructuring cash charges fell from USD 130 million to USD 19 million. The gains are therefore partly timing and one-off relief, and capital expenditure guided at about USD 300 million for fiscal 2026 will absorb a rising share of cash as restructuring costs fade.
Basis: calculation C11; calculation C16; Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-11-05_aap-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 10

Supplier finance is the largest swing factor in liquidity. Confirmed supplier finance obligations of USD 2,600 million are 84.83% of accounts payable, they sit outside debt, and the annual report links bank participation to the company's credit rating. A repeat of the fiscal 2025 payables outflow of USD 469 million would absorb 15.20% of cash, and a wider withdrawal would shorten payment terms across the inventory base. This is why a rating action matters more here than a maturity.
Basis: calculation C10; calculation C17; 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 33, 49, 103; 10-Q_2026-10-10_aap-20261010.htm.jsonl, page 14

Structure and governance leave noteholders behind a secured facility with no sponsor to lean on. The undrawn USD 1,000 million first-lien facility ranks ahead of the unsecured notes on receivables, inventory and cash, the guarantor list and credit agreements were not supplied, and the company is widely held with no controlling holder named. Governance risk is Medium on the assessment in Business & governance: the dividend was cut and no shares were bought back, but dividends continued while free cash flow was negative. The consequence is that creditor protection depends on documents and ratings that are not in the file.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 88–91

The operating base is stabilising rather than growing. Net sales fell 5.4% in fiscal 2025 on store closures and are flat for forty weeks of 2026, comparable store sales are up 0.8%, professional customers are about half of sales and no customer reaches 10%. Gross margin rose to 45.29% from 43.21% and reported EBITDA reached USD 406 million against USD 127 million, but guidance of about USD 8,500 million of net sales for fiscal 2026 implies a slightly lower figure than the fifty-three-week base of fiscal 2025. The credit therefore rests on margin and working-capital discipline rather than on volume.
Basis: calculation C18; calculation C19; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.1_2026-11-05_aap-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 2

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against caution is that net debt is small and the company's own net leverage is 2.2x, down from 2.4x two quarters earlier, with free cash flow guidance raised to about USD 150 million and comparable sales turning positive in the last four weeks of the third quarter. If the recovery holds, reported EBITDA above the last-twelve-month USD 508 million would move leverage below 6.67x without any debt repayment. The case for more caution is that the cash reflects the Worldpac proceeds and the 2025 notes rather than operating cash generation, USD 2,300 million of it is under springing control, and USD 2,600 million of supplier finance is rating-linked. The view would improve with sustained earnings growth and a rating that supports the supplier programs; it would worsen if confirmed supplier finance obligations fall sharply, if cash is used for payouts or deals, or if availability under the facility shrinks toward its stated floors.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C06; calculation C09; calculation C10; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_aap-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2, 10; 10-Q_2026-10-10_aap-20261010.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 14

### Limitations

- No agency rating, market price or peer set was supplied, so the judgement that refinancing access is adequate rests on liquidity and fixed-rate maturities alone; a speculative-grade rating with a negative outlook would lower that judgement, and an upgrade would raise it.
- The executed facility agreement was not available, so usable liquidity is stated as cash plus availability without covenant headroom; a binding springing test would reduce the liquidity buffer judged here.
- Management guidance as of 2026-11-05 is unaudited and is the only forward evidence, so the outlook for 2026 earnings and free cash flow is as reliable as that guidance; a miss would reverse the view that cash generation has turned.
- The downside scenario is built from observed fiscal 2025 flows rather than a forecast, so it shows the order of magnitude of a payables shock and does not give a probability or a timetable.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The strongest credit support is liquidity: USD 3,979 million of cash and availability against USD 650 million of notes due in 2027 and 2028 removes near-term refinancing pressure and gives time for the turnaround. A second support is the recovery in earnings and cash flow, with forty-week reported EBITDA of USD 406 million and free cash flow of USD 109 million, which if sustained lowers the leverage ratio without any repayment. A third is the breadth of the customer base, with half of sales to professional customers on short terms and no customer at 10%, which limits concentration risk. A fourth is the payout record since 2024, with the dividend cut by more than half and no share repurchases, which keeps cash in the business. A fifth is the maturity profile, which is entirely fixed-rate unsecured notes with no scheduled maturity until October 2027.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C03; calculation C11; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

The most material weakness is leverage on thin earnings: total debt is 6.67x reported EBITDA and cover is 2.54x on cash interest, so there is little room for a setback. A second is the supplier-finance float of USD 2,600 million, which is rating-linked and would drain cash if withdrawn. A third is structural: a first-lien facility ranks ahead of the notes and USD 2,300 million of cash is under springing control. A fourth is the fixed claims outside debt, lease liabilities of USD 2,189 million and rent of USD 524 million, which leave EBITDAR cover of rent plus cash interest at 1.60x. A fifth is the shrinking sales base and unfinished restructuring, with net sales down 5.4% in fiscal 2025 and a further USD 5 million to USD 10 million of restructuring cost expected in fiscal 2026.
Basis: calculation C06; calculation C07; calculation C10; calculation C14; calculation C15; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-10-10_aap-20261010.htm.jsonl, page 19

### Company description

The company is a North American aftermarket auto parts retailer and distributor serving professional installers and do-it-yourself customers from company stores, independently owned Carquest stores and online, with net sales of USD 8,601 million in fiscal 2025 and nearly all of them in the United States. Demand is replacement-driven and recurring, which supports cash-flow visibility, but it is discretionary at the margin because elective maintenance can be deferred. Since 2024 the company sold Worldpac for net proceeds of USD 1,440 million, closed 10.90% of its stores and refinanced, so the creditor question is whether a smaller, simpler business can hold earnings while it carries USD 3,388 million of debt.
Basis: calculation C18; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 4–9, 105

### Ownership and group structure

The company is publicly listed with no sponsor or controlling holder named, and the proxy statement that would show large holders and related-party dealings was not supplied. The issuer is the parent and borrower, with subsidiaries that guarantee the notes and the facility, and the guarantor list was not supplied. Financial policy has been to cut the dividend, buy back no shares and keep the cash from the Worldpac sale and the 2025 notes, but dividends were paid while free cash flow was negative. The implication is that there is no sponsor extraction pathway but also no sponsor support, and policy risk sits with the board.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 37, 68

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue comes from many small repeat purchases at the point of need, with parts and batteries at 64% of sales and professional customers about half, so the base is granular and no customer reaches 10% of net sales. Volume depends on vehicles on the road, miles driven and weather, and the filing names fuel costs, inflation and tariffs as pressures on discretionary spending; price increases from suppliers have been partly passed through. Comparable store sales rose 0.8% in fiscal 2025 and in the forty weeks of 2026 but fell 1.2% in the third quarter before turning positive in its last four weeks. Revenue is therefore recurring and cyclical in a mild way rather than contracted, and it is not structurally declining apart from closures.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 32, 40, 76, 105; 10-Q_2026-10-10_aap-20261010.htm.jsonl, pages 20–22; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_aap-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 1

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Costs are largely fixed in the short run: rent is a fixed claim of USD 524 million over the last twelve months, about 80% of stores are leased and most employees work in stores, so volume declines cut margins quickly. Gross margin recovered to 45.29% for forty weeks of 2026 after lower-margin liquidation sales and charges in 2025, and selling and administrative costs excluding restructuring fell to 41.5% of sales in fiscal 2025 from 41.9%. Inventory is largely on last-in first-out accounting, with a reserve of USD 133 million at 2026-10-10 against USD 104 million at year end, so inflation reduces reported gross profit without a cash cost. Margins can be defended against modest volume pressure through store closures and hub conversion, but not against a sharp fall in volume.
Basis: calculation C19; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 43, 49; 10-Q_2026-10-10_aap-20261010.htm.jsonl, page 10

### Capital intensity and FCF conversion

Capital expenditure was USD 252 million in fiscal 2025 and is guided to about USD 300 million for fiscal 2026, and cash taxes paid were only USD 13 million in fiscal 2025, so the drain between EBITDA and free cash flow is capex, working capital, rent already inside EBITDA, interest and restructuring cash. Free cash flow was USD -298 million in fiscal 2025 and USD 109 million in the forty weeks to 2026-10-10, giving a last-twelve-month conversion of 17.32% of reported EBITDA. Capex can be flexed down in a downturn, but store and supply-chain investment is the main source of the recovery, so cutting it would slow the turnaround. Conversion is not yet durable enough to fund repayment of the 2030 wall from internal cash flow.
Basis: calculation C11; calculation C16; Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-11-05_aap-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 10

### Porter's five forces

Competitive rivalry is the force that matters most: the company competes with national chains, independents, internet retailers and dealers on availability, price and service, and the filing warns that the company might not be able to pass higher supplier costs on to customers, so pricing power is limited and gross margin depends on mix and sourcing. Supplier power is the next, because the company buys from several hundred vendors and relies on favourable payment terms and supplier finance, which a downgrade could remove. In the analyst's judgement buyer power is moderate for professionals, who value same-day availability, and higher for do-it-yourself customers, who can switch easily. The threat of new entrants and substitutes is mainly online retail and changes in vehicle use, which are slow-moving credit risks rather than near-term ones.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 9, 23, 28, 32

### PEST analysis

Political and economic factors are material because tariffs led some suppliers to raise prices in fiscal 2025, although the company says the effect has not been material so far, a court decision in February 2026 opened a refund process that produced USD 26 million of benefit in the second quarter, and inflation and fuel costs affect miles driven and customer spending. Regulatory and technological factors, including emissions policy and vehicle electrification, are named in the annual report but are slow-moving and are not a primary credit driver on the provided materials. The credit consequence of the political and economic factors is margin volatility, which the company manages mainly through supplier price pass-through.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 28–29, 32, 40; 10-Q_2026-10-10_aap-20261010.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 21

### Credit-translated SWOT

Strengths are liquidity of USD 3,979 million, a broad customer base, a recovery in earnings and a fixed-rate maturity profile starting in October 2027. Weaknesses are leverage of 6.67x, supplier-finance reliance of USD 2,600 million, fixed claims for leases and rent, and a shrinking sales base. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are further margin gains from the hub network and a rating that supports supplier programs, and the main threats are a withdrawal of supplier finance, a sustained fall in comparable sales and a use of cash for payouts.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C06; calculation C10; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Financial profile and credit quality

Supports are liquidity, a maturity profile without scheduled repayments before October 2027, positive free cash flow in 2026 and a payout policy that has kept cash in the business. Constraints are leverage of 6.67x, cover of 2.54x on cash interest, reliance on supplier finance, and structural priority held by the facility over receivables, inventory and cash. The trend is improving on earnings and cash flow and stable on liquidity, and the missing datapoints are the credit agreement terms, ratings and the proxy statement. The net result is a financial profile that is adequate for near-term obligations and weak for a stress case.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C06; calculation C07; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months, management guides to net sales of about USD 8,500 million, adjusted operating margin of 4.00% to 4.30%, capital expenditure of about USD 300 million and free cash flow of about USD 150 million for fiscal 2026, which implies flat sales and margin-led improvement. Tailwinds are lower restructuring cost, a store and hub programme, and tariff refunds; headwinds are cost inflation, softer do-it-yourself demand in summer, and the interest cost of the 2025 notes. Over three to five years the credit depends on whether the earnings recovery lets the company repay or refinance USD 1,475 million due in FY2030 and USD 1,325 million after it without using the cash cushion.
Basis: calculation C12; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_aap-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2; 10-Q_2026-10-10_aap-20261010.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 19

### Qualitative downside scenario

The initiating condition is a withdrawal by banks from the supplier finance programs, for example after a rating action, at a time when comparable sales are again negative. The first operational break is shorter payment terms on inventory, which in fiscal 2025 produced a USD 469 million payables outflow that contributed to operating cash flow of USD -46 million even without a bank withdrawal. The financial transmission is cash falling, free cash flow turning negative as capex of about USD 300 million continues, and EBITDA falling back to the fiscal 2025 level of USD 229 million, which would put total debt of USD 3,388 million at 14.79x and cover cash interest of USD 200 million only 1.15x. Cash and availability of USD 3,979 million would cover the 2027 and 2028 notes in that case, but they would not cover the 2030 wall, and restricted cash and availability floors would reduce usable liquidity. Management levers are cutting capex, slowing store openings and withholding dividends; the observable triggers are falling confirmed supplier finance, negative comparable sales and shrinking facility availability.
Basis: calculation C03; calculation C10; calculation C12; calculation C18; calculation C19; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 33, 49, 67–68

### Overall credit view

Overall, Advance Auto Parts presents an adequate near-term business and liquidity profile supported by USD 3,979 million of liquidity, a maturity profile with no repayment before October 2027 and a recovery in earnings, but credit quality is constrained by leverage of 6.67x, reliance on supplier finance, structural priority held by a secured facility and heavy fixed claims. Cash-flow durability appears adequate but still working-capital dependent. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on sustained earnings, the supplier-finance programs and market access that cannot be assessed without ratings. The primary downside path is a withdrawal of supplier finance combined with weaker sales. Further analysis would require the credit documents, ratings, the proxy statement and the guarantor list. Fundamental credit analysis completed. Top risk: loss of rating-linked supplier finance.
Basis: calculation C06; calculation C10; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)
