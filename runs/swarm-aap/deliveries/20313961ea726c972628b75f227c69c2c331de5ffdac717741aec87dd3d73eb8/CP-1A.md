## Findings

### Bottom line

Advance Auto Parts is a widely held retailer with no sponsor, so creditors depend on the company's own financial policy, which has cut shareholder payouts and pre-funded maturities but also placed a secured facility ahead of the unsecured notes.

The business is a single segment, about half professional trade customers and half do-it-yourself, concentrated in the United States, and in the second year of a restructuring that closed 10.90% of its stores and followed the sale of Worldpac for net proceeds of USD 1,440 million. Net sales fell 5.4% in fiscal 2025 on the closures. Governance evidence is thin: the proxy statement was not supplied, so board composition and large holders are not established, and a securities class action dismissed in 2025 is on appeal. The sponsor and governance risk level is Medium because the evidence is mixed rather than adverse.
Basis: calculation C04; 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 39, 100, 105; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

No sponsor stands behind this credit, and governance evidence is incomplete. The common stock is listed and widely held, the annual report names no controlling holder, and the items on directors, ownership and related-party transactions are incorporated by reference to a proxy statement that was not supplied, so board composition, large holders, related-party dealings and any fees are not established. The consequence is that there is no evidenced route for equity support or for sponsor extraction, and creditor protection rests on documents that were not supplied rather than on an owner's willingness.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 37, 61; Source readiness (CP-0)

Shareholder payouts are modest and have been cut, but they were paid while free cash flow was negative. Dividends declared per share fell from USD 2.25 in fiscal 2023 to USD 1.00 in fiscal 2024 and 2025, and no shares were bought back under the programme in either of those years. Even so, the company paid USD 60 million of dividends in fiscal 2025 when operating cash flow was USD -46 million and free cash flow was USD -298 million, so cash consumed after dividends was USD -358 million, which cash balances enlarged by the August 2025 note issue absorbed. The mechanism is a small distribution funded in effect by new debt; the size is minor against the cash balance, but the ABL restrictions on larger payouts are known only from the company's summary.
Basis: calculation C06; calculation C07; calculation C08; 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 37, 52, 67–68, 101; 10-Q_2026-10-10_aap-20261010.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6

The August 2025 refinancing pre-funded maturities but moved the revolver from unsecured to secured. The company issued USD 1,950 million of senior unsecured notes, redeemed USD 300 million of 2026 notes and replaced an unsecured USD 1,000 million revolver with a first-lien asset-based facility over receivables, inventory and cash. The facility is undrawn, but a drawing would rank ahead of the notes in those assets, and about three quarters of the cash sits in accounts under springing control that become restricted on a default or covenant breach. Noteholders therefore gained maturity cover and lost priority on the most liquid assets.
Basis: calculation C05; calculation C11; 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 87–92, 105; 10-Q_2026-10-10_aap-20261010.htm.jsonl, page 12

The operating model is mid-recovery and depends on supplier finance. Net sales were USD 8,601 million in fiscal 2025, down 5.4% on closures partly offset by the fifty-third week, with comparable store sales up 0.8%. Professional customers are about half of sales, nearly all sales are in the United States, and about 80% of stores are leased, so rent is a large fixed claim. Confirmed supplier finance obligations of USD 2,600 million fund most of accounts payable, and the annual report ties bank participation to the company's credit rating, so a downgrade could shorten payment terms and consume cash.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C10; calculation C12; 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 33, 39, 42, 49, 103, 105; 10-Q_2026-10-10_aap-20261010.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 14

Controls and litigation are mixed rather than adverse. Internal control over financial reporting was effective at the 2025 year end after material weaknesses disclosed earlier, and the company still booked a small remediation cost in fiscal 2025. A securities class action and related derivative suits remain open after the dismissal was appealed, and executive turnover costs were recorded in each of the last three years. These are monitoring points; none is quantified as a creditor claim in the filings.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 46–47, 60, 100

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better view is financial discipline and recovery: the dividend was cut by more than half in 2024, the company holds USD 3,085 million of cash against USD 3,388 million of debt, comparable store sales turned positive in the last four weeks of the third quarter, and the company has raised its fiscal 2026 free cash flow guidance to about USD 150 million. The view would improve if the proxy statement showed no large holder or related-party arrangement and the credit documents showed tight restricted-payment limits. It would worsen if the credit documents allowed large payouts, if confirmed supplier finance obligations fell sharply from USD 2,600 million, or if the appeal revived the securities class action with a quantified claim.
Basis: calculation C09; 10-Q_2026-10-10_aap-20261010.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 14; EX-99.1_2026-11-05_aap-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2; 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 100, 103

### Limitations

- The proxy statement was not supplied, so the finding that no controlling holder or sponsor shapes financial policy rests only on the annual report's silence; a large holder or related-party arrangement in the proxy would raise the governance risk level above Medium.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, page 2
- The executed asset-based facility agreement and indentures were not supplied, so the finding on shareholder-payout restrictions relies on the company's summary; wide payout capacity in those documents would move the risk level toward High, and tight limits toward Low.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 37, 91
- The subsidiary guarantor list was not supplied, so the statement that operating subsidiaries guarantee the notes cannot be tested for the finding on structural priority; a narrow guarantor group would weaken the notes' position against the secured facility.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, page 88
- Prior-year interim figures for comparable store sales and store counts were not read, so the finding that the footprint has stabilised rests on the 2026 forty-week figures alone; a renewed decline would reverse it.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-10_aap-20261010.htm.jsonl, pages 20–22

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The company has no pending acquisition, buyout or sponsor transaction in the supplied files; its transactions are a disposal, a restructuring and a refinancing. It sold the Worldpac business, which closed on 2024-11-01 for net proceeds of about USD 1,440 million after costs against a headline price of USD 1,500 million, and the final working capital adjustment led to a USD 55 million payment in 2026. The 2024 Restructuring Plan, approved on 2024-11-13, closed the intended stores and independent locations in the first quarter of 2025 at a cumulative restructuring cost of USD 1,100 million across active plans.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 14, 49, 105–106; 10-Q_2026-10-10_aap-20261010.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 19

New debt was issued on 2025-08-04: USD 975 million of notes due 2030 and USD 975 million of notes due 2033, both senior unsecured and guaranteed by subsidiary guarantors. The proceeds redeemed USD 300 million of 2026 notes, and on 2025-08-12 an unsecured USD 1,000 million revolver was replaced by a five-year first-lien asset-based facility of up to USD 1,000 million with an uncommitted accordion of USD 500 million, undrawn at 2026-10-10. In the second quarter of 2026 the company repurchased USD 29 million of the 2028 notes and a nominal amount of the 2027 notes under a repurchase plan that has ended, and in the third quarter it moved USD 349 million of the 2027 notes to current liabilities.
Basis: 10-K_2026-01-03_aap-20260103.htm.jsonl, pages 87–91; 10-Q_2026-10-10_aap-20261010.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12
