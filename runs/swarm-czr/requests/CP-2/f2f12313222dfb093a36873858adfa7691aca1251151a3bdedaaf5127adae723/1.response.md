### Company description

Caesars owns, leases, brands or manages 54 properties in 20 jurisdictions, runs retail and online sports betting in 35 jurisdictions and iGaming in six, and earns 57.61% of revenue from casino gaming, 16.93% from hotels and 14.92% from food and beverage. That mix is discretionary and cyclical, but it is spread across four segments, a loyalty program that links the properties and an owned digital platform, which supports cash-flow visibility better than a single-site operator. Of 47 casinos, 25 are leased, 24 of them from VICI and GLPI, so a large share of cash flow is committed to landlords before debt service, and gaming licences, tax rates and regulators set the operating terms.

### Ownership and group structure

Caesars is a listed company with a single class of common stock, no identified controlling holder, a founding family and an investor agreement whose terms are not supplied, and a pending sale to Fertitta Gaming Holdco, LLC. The parent borrows for the group and its material wholly owned domestic subsidiaries guarantee the secured and unsecured debt, with 79.94% of principal secured by substantially all assets. The Caesars Virginia facilities of USD 371 million, 3.18% of principal, are secured only by that venture's assets and are not guaranteed by the parent, and the venture's partner received USD 65 million of distributions in nine months 2026. Leased casinos and the lease obligations sit alongside the debt, and a lease termination could cross into the debt agreements.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is volume-and-hold driven rather than contracted. Group net revenues rose 2.7% in nine months 2026 on casino growth, but Las Vegas fell 2.0% as fewer city-wide conventions and weaker leisure visitation cut occupancy and rates, while Regional rose 6.1% with Caesars Windsor consolidated and Caesars Digital rose 6.3% on iGaming handle and hold. Hold is volatile and sits inside typical ranges the company states, so reported growth can reverse without a change in demand, and Las Vegas hotel rates compressed instead of rising. For creditors this means revenue is predictable only within a band and the Las Vegas component has the least visibility.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Costs are heavy in labor, gaming taxes and fixed rent, which makes margins hard to defend. Gaming taxes were 15.73% of net revenues against 15.03% a year earlier, about 21,000 of about 50,000 team members are covered by collective bargaining agreements, and corporate costs rose 24.8%. Las Vegas lost USD 102 million of Adjusted EBITDA on USD 59 million less revenue, and Regional kept 23.13% of added revenue against a 31.3% margin, so operating leverage is working against the group. Marketing and capital expenditure are the main discretionary levers, while rent is fixed.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense: the company describes new casinos, tribal expansion and large Las Vegas developments, and it took 2025 impairments in three Regional reporting units because localized competition lowered expected cash flows, so rivalry already reduces asset values. New entrants face licensing barriers, but new online jurisdictions and tribal supply still add competition. Substitutes are growing: prediction markets, sweepstakes products, daily fantasy sports and lotteries compete for the same spend. Buyer power is high because customers can switch properties and apps, and promotional spending in digital was USD 249 million in nine months. Supplier power sits with unions, performers and the landlords who set rent; the landlords are the most material for creditors.
