## Findings

### Bottom line
Edgewell's net leverage worsened from 3.82x to 6.40x in FY2025 on lower EBITDA and higher net debt, then net debt fell USD 305.0 million after the Feminine Care sale, but no comparable current leverage exists, so creditors must lean on liquidity.
Basis: calculation C16; calculation C19; calculation C21; 10-Q_2026-06-30_epc.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6

Liquidity is the credit's strongest support: cash of USD 397.1 million and an undrawn revolver of USD 418.8 million at June 30, 2026 total USD 815.9 million, against USD 34.2 million of short-term bank debt and no bond maturity before fiscal 2028. The company's own adjusted net leverage of 3.7x rests on a definition that changed each quarter, and last-twelve-month continuing EBITDA is missing, so the leverage direction since the sale is not established.
Basis: calculation C23; 10-Q_2026-06-30_epc.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 43; EX99.1_2026-06-30_epc_q3fy26-epcexhibit991-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 11–12; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Leverage and cover
Reported EBITDA fell from USD 285.4 million in FY2024 to USD 185.6 million in FY2025, a fall of USD 99.8 million, because operating income fell from USD 199.3 million to USD 96.6 million after a USD 51.1 million goodwill impairment on the Feminine Care unit and USD 47.9 million of restructuring; with cash interest of USD 73.3 million the cash interest cover dropped from 4.54x to 2.53x, so a fixed interest burden now sits on a thinner earnings base.
Basis: calculation C45; calculation C17; calculation C20; 10-K_2025-09-30_epc.htm.jsonl, pages 89–91

The impairment is non-cash, which is the strongest argument against the weak reading: before it, FY2025 EBITDA would be USD 236.7 million and net leverage 5.02x, an Analyst Judgement shown beside the reported figures and not an issuer adjustment. What would make the weak reading win is the continuing earnings base, where reported EBITDA for nine months 2026 was USD 97.4 million against USD 158.0 million a year earlier.
Basis: calculation C43; calculation C44; EX99.1_2026-06-30_epc_q3fy26-epcexhibit991-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17

### Cash generation and shareholder returns
Free cash flow, taken as operating cash flow less capital expenditures on the consolidated cash flow statement, was USD 41.4 million in FY2025 against USD 119.5 million of share repurchases and dividends, so the revolver draw rose to USD 140.0 million at the year end; in nine months 2026 free cash flow was only USD 5.9 million against USD 37.3 million of returns, so shareholder returns, not internally generated cash, absorbed the balance sheet in FY2025, and in nine months 2026 the sale proceeds, not operating cash, drove the fall in net debt.
Basis: calculation C38; calculation C41; calculation C40; calculation C42; 10-K_2025-09-30_epc.htm.jsonl, pages 90–91; 10-Q_2026-06-30_epc.htm.jsonl, page 6

### Liquidity and debt maturities
Debt is USD 1,250.0 million of fixed-rate notes due in fiscal 2028 and fiscal 2029 plus USD 34.2 million of short-term bank borrowings, with the revolver undrawn at June 30, 2026; the earlier maturity of the revolver is the pressure point, because it is brought forward to 2028-03-02 if more than USD 150.0 million of the 2028 notes remain outstanding on that date, so the 2028 notes must be refinanced or reduced before then to keep the facility to 2029.
Basis: calculation C22; 10-Q_2026-06-30_epc.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 46; 10-K_2025-09-30_epc.htm.jsonl, pages 116–117

### Definition and data-quality risks
Adjusted EBITDA of USD 177.7 million for nine months 2026 is built from reported EBITDA of USD 97.4 million by adding back USD 80.3 million, mostly restructuring and related costs of USD 68.4 million, and it includes transition services income from Essity that lasts twelve months from closing, which was USD 7.7 million of the third quarter's USD 78.9 million adjusted EBITDA, so run-rate earnings are weaker than the adjusted figure alone suggests; the add-back realisation status is not stated by the issuer.
Basis: calculation C31; calculation C32; calculation C46; EX99.1_2026-06-30_epc_q3fy26-epcexhibit991-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17; 10-Q_2026-06-30_epc.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13

Fiscal year columns are as first reported and include Feminine Care, interim columns are continuing operations, and the cash flow statements are consolidated in every column; ratios are therefore stated only at fiscal year ends, and the interim leverage and cover cells are marked not calculable rather than built across the two perimeters.
Basis: Source readiness (CP-0); 10-Q_2026-06-30_epc.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10

### Subsequent events and funding outside debt
The Feminine Care sale closed on 2026-02-02 with cash proceeds of USD 338.9 million and was used to repay the revolver; the company still expects about USD 55 million of cash taxes on the gain in fiscal 2026, the part unpaid at June 30 is not stated, and the third-quarter dividend of USD 0.15 per share was payable on 2026-10-08, with payment not confirmed in the sources. Receivables sold under the receivables facility and not on the balance sheet stood at USD 120.4 million at June 30, 2026, up USD 25.7 million from year end, and supplier finance obligations of USD 15.4 million sit inside accounts payable, so funding outside reported debt is rising and should be read beside the debt figures.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_epc.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 18, 20, 43; calculation C47; EX99.1_2025-12-31_epc_ex991femdivestpressrelease-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1
