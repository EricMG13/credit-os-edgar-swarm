## Findings

### Bottom line
No controlling sponsor is identified in Edgewell's filings, so creditor risk lies in payout policy: FY2025 buybacks and dividends outran free cash flow and were partly debt-funded, while the Feminine Care sale proceeds first repaid the revolver.
Basis: calculation C24; calculation C25; calculation C27; 10-Q_2026-06-30_epc.htm.jsonl, pages 43, 46

The retained business is a two-segment personal care group with one retailer at about 17.4% of FY2025 net sales, a restructuring programme of about USD 92 million in fiscal 2026, and a Wet Shave unit whose fair value exceeded its carrying value by only 7% at the year end. Ownership percentages, board committees and the credit documents are not established from the supplied filings.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_epc.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 78; EX99.1_2026-06-30_epc_q3fy26-epcexhibit991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 5; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

- Governance looks like that of a widely held listed company rather than a sponsor-controlled one, which removes sponsor extraction as an evidenced risk but does not say who the largest holders are; share buybacks cut shares outstanding by 7.29% in two years.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_epc.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 53; calculation C28
- Payout policy is the governance lever that matters: FY2025 buybacks and dividends of USD 119.5 million exceeded free cash flow of USD 41.4 million and revolver borrowings rose by USD 106.0 million, yet over three years payouts were 82.46% of free cash flow, so the pattern is pressure in one year rather than systematic leakage.
  Basis: calculation C24; calculation C25; calculation C27; calculation C23
- The Feminine Care sale was used in a creditor-aligned way, because all revolver borrowings were repaid from the proceeds, but a new USD 100.0 million buyback authorisation still has USD 84.9 million unused, so a later switch of liquidity into buybacks remains possible.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_epc.htm.jsonl, pages 43–44, 46; calculation C35
- The retained business is concentrated and under margin pressure: Wet Shave segment profit fell from USD 137.3 million to USD 106.0 million in nine months 2026 and the unit had only 7% valuation headroom at the year end, so a non-cash impairment is a live possibility.
  Basis: calculation C18; EX99.1_2026-06-30_epc_q3fy26-epcexhibit991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-K_2025-09-30_epc.htm.jsonl, page 78
- Seasonal working capital is the liquidity swing: first-quarter operating cash outflow was USD 125.9 million in fiscal 2026 and revolver borrowings reached USD 277.0 million before the sale proceeds repaid them, so the USD 425.0 million revolver is the seasonal funding source.
  Basis: EX99.1_2025-12-31_epc_epcexhibit99112312025-htm.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2025-12-31_epc.htm.jsonl, page 37; 10-Q_2026-06-30_epc.htm.jsonl, page 43
- Finance leadership changed in 2024 and the chief operating officer left in 2025, but the audit opinion is unqualified and internal control over reporting is effective, so continuity risk is a monitoring point and not an adverse finding.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_epc.htm.jsonl, pages 22–25, 83–89, 139

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a payout-pressure reading is that payouts were 82.46% of three-year free cash flow, dividends stayed at USD 0.15 per share each quarter, nine-month 2026 buybacks were only USD 15.8 million, and the sale proceeds went to repay the revolver first. The view would turn more adverse if buybacks again exceed free cash flow after the revolver is redrawn, if a holder above 5% with control rights is disclosed, or if the credit agreement shows wide restricted-payment capacity; it would turn more favourable if the unused authorisation lapses and the retained business returns to organic growth, as it did in the third quarter with growth of 1.1%.
Basis: calculation C23; 10-Q_2026-06-30_epc.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 44; EX99.1_2026-06-30_epc_q3fy26-epcexhibit991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 2

### Limitations

- The proxy statement is not supplied, so the conclusion that no controlling holder exists rests on the absence of a named sponsor and would change if a holder above 5% or any control right is disclosed.
- The credit agreement and indentures are not supplied, so payout risk is judged from behaviour and not from restricted-payment capacity, which could be wider than past payouts show.
- Customer concentration is stated only for one retailer at group level and one at segment level, so the true top-customer share of the retained business is not established and could be higher than 17.4%.
- The fiscal 2025 revenue mix includes Feminine Care and no continuing-operations mix is supplied for that year, so the mix shift since the sale is shown for nine months 2026 only.
- Nothing after the third-quarter report of 2026-08-05 is supplied, so the July 1, 2026 impairment test and any later buyback activity are not established.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The only transaction in the period is the sale of the Feminine Care business to Essity for USD 340.0 million, agreed on 2025-11-12 and closed on 2026-02-02; the company used the proceeds to repay all revolver borrowings, entered a twelve-month transition services agreement under which Edgewell provides services to Essity, and recorded gross proceeds of USD 338.9 million after a purchase price adjustment of USD 1.1 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_epc.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 12–13, 43; EX99.1_2025-12-31_epc_ex991femdivestpressrelease-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1

The company issued no new notes in the period read: the two fixed-rate note series of USD 750.0 million and USD 500.0 million are unchanged from 2024-09-30 to 2026-06-30, and the only debt event is the April 2, 2024 amendment and restatement that extended the USD 425.0 million revolver to April 2, 2029 with earlier springing dates tied to the two note series. Executed indentures and the credit agreement are listed in the exhibit index but are not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_epc.htm.jsonl, pages 114–116, 142; 10-Q_2026-06-30_epc.htm.jsonl, page 19
