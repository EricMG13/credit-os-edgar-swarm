## Findings

### Bottom line

Insulet's credit profile is supported by low leverage, liquidity of USD 1,334.4 million and no large maturity before 2031, but one product platform sold through three distributors makes a safety event the main route to credit deterioration.

Total debt was 1.38x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA at 2026-09-30 and net debt 0.54x, against 4.80x and 2.41x at the end of FY2023, and cash interest cover was 8.95x in FY2025. Free cash flow for the nine months of 2026 was USD 211.7 million against USD 329.5 million a year earlier, and the USD 300 million repurchase completed by March 2026 exceeded it, so net debt rose to USD 377.8 million from USD 233.1 million at FY2025. The pro forma net debt and net leverage after the repurchase programme authorised in October 2026 are not established. Covenant levels, ratings and executed debt documents were not supplied.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 17, 31, 32; calculation C1; calculation C5

### Key findings

Leverage and liquidity are the main support. Total debt of 1.38x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA, cash of USD 584.4 million and an undrawn USD 750 million revolver, with the term loan due in 2031 and the notes in 2033, mean that creditors do not depend on market access to be repaid in the medium term. The liquidity figure of USD 1,334.4 million is shown before any letters of credit, which are not stated after 2024.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 10, 32; calculation C1

Growth is real but cash conversion has fallen. Revenue grew 25.0% in the nine months of 2026 and operating income grew 34.81%, with a gross margin of 71.7%, yet free cash flow fell by USD 117.8 million to USD 211.7 million because receivables rose by USD 179.6 million, inventories by USD 103.0 million and capital expenditure doubled to USD 113.9 million. Growth is absorbing working capital and plant spend, so deleveraging from free cash flow has slowed even as earnings expand.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 5, 21, 24, 30, 31; calculation C3; calculation C9

The main downside path is a product-safety or regulatory event on a single platform sold through concentrated distributors. Omnipod products were 99.40% of nine-month 2026 revenue, three distributors supplied 66% of it and one held 38% of trade receivables, and the company issued two voluntary device corrections in 2026 and faces a securities class action over product quality. A further correction or regulatory action would first hit distributor orders and receivables, then gross margin through warranty cost, before it reached liquidity.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 10, 14

Financial policy has moved from debt reduction toward shareholder returns. Total debt fell by USD 430.7 million in 2025, but the USD 300 million repurchase completed by March 2026 exceeded nine-month free cash flow by USD 88.3 million and the board authorised USD 1 billion more in October 2026, with about USD 400 million expected in 2026. Cash paid for it, so creditors are not yet harmed, but the legal limit on distributions is not established and the effect of the new programme on net debt is not established.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 31, 33; calculation C4; calculation C5

Information gaps limit the view on structure rather than on fundamentals. No executed credit agreement or indenture, rating, market price or peer figure was supplied, so covenant protection, guarantor coverage and the market's view of the debt are not assessed here.
Basis: Source readiness (CP-0); 10-K_2025-12-31_podd-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 92, 108, 111

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a weaker view is that two corrections within two months and a class action may point to weaker manufacturing quality control than the reported cost of about USD 50 million suggests. With 66% of revenue through three distributors and almost all revenue from one platform, a regulatory action could move revenue far more than that cost, and reported leverage of 1.38x is helped by growth in last-twelve-months EBITDA from USD 454.6 million at FY2025 to USD 695.3 million. The case for a stronger view is that the company pays no dividend, retired USD 800.0 million of convertible notes in 2025, refinanced its term loan at a lower margin in September 2026 and holds USD 1,334.4 million of liquidity before letters of credit. The view would weaken if a third correction, a regulatory action or the loss of a large distributor appeared, if repurchases ran well above free cash flow again, or if revolver borrowings began. It would strengthen if executed documents showed restrictive limits on distributions and if free cash flow recovered toward the USD 329.5 million of the prior-year nine months.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 10, 11, 14, 20, 31

Strength one is low leverage. Total debt was 1.38x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA at 2026-09-30, down from 4.80x at the end of FY2023, so debt service and refinancing do not depend on a favourable market, which supports repayment ahead of the 2031 and 2033 maturities.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1)

Strength two is liquidity. Cash of USD 584.4 million plus an undrawn USD 750 million revolver gives USD 1,334.4 million before letters of credit against USD 19.1 million of debt due within a year, so scheduled debt payments are covered many times without new borrowing.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 32; calculation C1

Strength three is recurring, high-margin growth. Revenue grew 25.0% in the nine months of 2026 at a gross margin of 71.7%, because disposable Pods are bought repeatedly by a growing user base, which turns volume growth into earnings and cash.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 21, 24

Strength four is a long-dated debt profile. The revolver expires in 2030, the term loan in 2031 and the notes in April 2033, so the next large repayment is five years away and repayment risk is deferred well beyond the near-term business risks.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 11, 32

Weakness one is concentration in one platform and a few distributors. Omnipod products were 99.40% of nine-month 2026 revenue and three distributors supplied 66%, so a safety event or a distributor loss reaches most of revenue at once and would show first in orders and receivables.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 8; calculation C11

Weakness two is product-safety and litigation exposure. Two voluntary corrections in 2026 for a cannula defect and a securities class action over product quality add warranty cost, scrutiny and legal cost, and a regulatory action would be harder to absorb on a single platform.

Weakness three is shareholder returns running ahead of free cash flow. The USD 300 million repurchase exceeded nine-month free cash flow of USD 211.7 million by USD 88.3 million and more is authorised, so headroom is being spent while the legal limit on distributions is unknown.

Weakness four is falling cash conversion. Free cash flow fell by USD 117.8 million, or 35.75%, in the nine months of 2026, because receivables, inventories and plant spend absorbed cash, which slows debt reduction from internal cash.

### Company description

Insulet develops, makes and sells the Omnipod tubeless insulin delivery platform, whose disposable Pods are worn for up to three days and sold mainly through wholesalers and distributors, with a small Drug Delivery line that supplies modified Pods to Amgen. The recurring Pod purchases make revenue visible, because each user needs a continuous supply, and the pharmacy channel lowers the up-front cost to payors. The business therefore supports cash-flow visibility and debt service, but nearly all of it depends on one product family and on payor coverage, which constrains refinancing confidence if either is disrupted.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 22

### Ownership and group structure

Insulet is a listed company in which no sponsor or controlling shareholder is identified, so there is no sponsor-driven leverage, but the proxy statement that would show beneficial ownership was not supplied. Borrowings under the credit agreement are guaranteed by certain wholly owned domestic subsidiaries and secured by substantially all assets, and the unsecured notes sit behind them, so structural priority favours the secured lenders; the subsidiary list and the notes' guarantors are not established.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_podd-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 92, 115

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is recurring and growing: it rose 25.0% in the nine months of 2026, or 23.8% in constant currency, driven by customer-base growth and, internationally, by higher average selling prices from the move to Omnipod 5, with US Omnipod revenue up 20.6% and international up 40.8%. Concentration is the weak point: three distributors supplied 66% of revenue, and the U.S. pharmacy rebate provisions deducted from gross sales reached USD 654.7 million in FY2025, so pricing power sits partly with payors and intermediaries. The implication is that growth is credible, but a change in rebate terms or distributor behaviour would move revenue and cash conversion faster than volume alone.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 21, 22; 10-K_2025-12-31_podd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 63

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Gross margin was 71.7% for the nine months of 2026, and operating expenses of USD 1,282.2 million, mostly research and development and selling costs, took 53.31% of revenue, so most costs below gross profit do not fall automatically if volume falls. That operating leverage helped operating income grow 34.81% on revenue growth of 25.00%, and it would work in reverse in a downturn. Margin resilience therefore rests on warranty cost staying contained and on management cutting discretionary spend if revenue weakens; the report says the elimination of the current tariff exemption for certain medical devices would have a material effect on gross margin.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 21, 24; calculation C7; calculation C9

### Capital intensity and free cash flow conversion

Free cash flow was USD 377.7 million in FY2025 and USD 211.7 million in the nine months of 2026, which is 34.42% of nine-month adjusted EBITDA of USD 615.1 million, because working capital and capital expenditure rose with growth. Capital expenditure is expected to rise again in 2026 for the new plant, which is funded from existing cash and financing, and the maintenance and growth split is not stated. The effect is that free cash flow available for debt service is large against debt but is not growing as fast as earnings, and capital spending is the lever management can pull to protect it.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_podd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 60; 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 30, 31; calculation C8

### Competitive forces

Rivalry comes from tubed pump makers, emerging pump companies, developers of new patch pumps and multiple daily injections, and the report names the spin-out of a competitor's diabetes division as a development, so price and share pressure are possible. Entry barriers are regulatory clearance and more than 1,000 patents, which protects margin. Substitutes are injection therapy with smart pens and, for type 2 diabetes, GLP-1 products. Buyer power is high because three distributors supply 66% of revenue and payors negotiate rebates. Supplier power is meaningful because some components such as application-specific integrated circuit chips and Bluetooth chips are single-sourced or sole-sourced, although the company holds inventory and has a purchase commitment of USD 45.1 million with one chip supplier. The credit effect is that margin and supply are defensible but the buyer side could squeeze revenue if a safety event weakened the company's bargaining position.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_podd-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 11, 12, 26; 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 33

### Macro, regulatory and policy factors

Regulatory and reimbursement factors do alter credit quality here: every product needs FDA clearance, coverage depends on payors including Medicare Part D plan sponsors, the report flags tariffs on medical devices, and about 28% of FY2025 revenue was in foreign currencies. Currency and tariffs matter less to leverage than a safety action, because the notes carry a fixed rate and most of the term loan is swapped to a fixed rate, but a tariff change or a payor decision could cut margin or volume.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_podd-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 13, 65; 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 24

### Credit-translated SWOT

Strengths are recurring high-margin revenue, low leverage, USD 1,334.4 million of liquidity before letters of credit and a debt profile that does not mature before 2030. Weaknesses are concentration in one platform and three distributors, product-safety exposure and cash conversion that has fallen. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are the type 2 diabetes indication, international expansion, which grew 40.8% in the nine months of 2026, and the next-generation Omnipod 6 system. Threats that could weaken it are a further correction or regulatory action, a tariff change, competitor launches and a shift of cash to repurchases ahead of free cash flow.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 19, 20, 21, 32; calculation C1

### Financial profile and credit quality

The financial profile is supported by low leverage, adequate liquidity and a long-dated debt profile, constrained by falling cash conversion and a policy shift toward shareholder returns, and the trend in leverage is down over three years while net leverage has levelled off since FY2025. The main missing datapoints are cash interest paid for interim periods, letters of credit, the maturity schedule at 2026-09-30 and the executed documents, so liquidity and coverage are shown as reported and not netted for items the filings do not state.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 10, 32

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months the report expects net interest expense of about USD 40 million for 2026, capital expenditure above 2025, total product-correction costs of about USD 50 million mostly in 2026 and about USD 400 million of repurchases in 2026 from existing cash, with the type 2 closed-loop pivotal study intended to support a 510(k) submission in 2027. Tailwinds are the type 2 and international opportunities and the lower term loan margin; headwinds are the cash returned to shareholders, plant spend and safety scrutiny. No quantitative revenue or earnings guidance was found: the first- and second-quarter reports expect strong United States revenue growth and higher international revenue for full year 2026 without a figure, and the third-quarter report does not repeat that expectation, so the medium-term outlook of three to five years rests on trend, and the Amgen supply agreement that expires in December 2028 covers a line that was 0.61% of nine-month 2026 revenue.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-03-31_podd-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-06-30_podd-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 11, 13, 20, 26, 30, 33; 10-K_2025-12-31_podd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 25; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Downside scenario

The realistic downside is a third device correction or a regulatory action that makes distributors pause orders. The first break is distributor orders and receivables, since three distributors supply 66% of revenue; revenue and gross margin then fall together because warranty and replacement costs rise while research and selling costs stay. Free cash flow, already down by USD 117.8 million, would fall further as committed plant spend continues, and management has levers, namely suspending repurchases, deferring capital expenditure and releasing inventory. Cash of USD 584.4 million and an undrawn USD 750 million revolver, against only USD 19.1 million of debt due within a year, suggest resilience sufficient to avoid liquidity distress, unless the revolver had to be drawn to the level at which the leverage covenant applies, a level the filings do not give. Observable triggers are further correction announcements, order and receivable trends, and the pace of repurchases.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 8, 10, 30, 32; calculation C1; calculation C3

### Overall credit view

Overall, Insulet presents an adequate business profile supported by recurring high-margin revenue and a low-leverage balance sheet, but credit quality is constrained by concentration in one platform and three distributors and by product-safety exposure. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow remains large against debt, although conversion has fallen. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the company keeping its shareholder returns within free cash flow, since the nearest large maturities are in 2031 and 2033. The primary downside path is a safety or regulatory event on the Omnipod platform. Further analysis would require the executed debt documents, the proxy statement, letters of credit and ratings.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_podd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 10, 31
