## Findings

### Bottom line

Entegris presents a solid fundamental credit profile: net leverage of 2.96x and cash interest cover of 5.42x leave room for a downturn, but a cyclical, concentrated revenue base and USD 2,000.0 million due in 2029 mean creditors depend on market access.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C-12; calculation C-15; calculation C-23

Net debt is USD 2,904.1 million at 2026-09-26, or USD 2,919.38 million pro forma for the dividend payable on 2026-11-18. The primary downside path is a fall in capital-spending-linked demand that cuts EBITDA before the 2028 and 2029 refinancing windows; last-twelve-months free cash flow of USD 616.0 million and liquidity of USD 1,106.5 million would absorb a first-year shock, but not the 2029 principal.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C-07; calculation C-08; calculation C-18; calculation C-22; calculation C-33

### Key findings

Leverage and coverage are the strongest support. Net debt over reported EBITDA fell to 2.96x from 3.95x at FY2025 and total debt over reported EBITDA to 3.32x from 4.37x, because USD 450.0 million of term loan was repaid while last-twelve-months reported EBITDA rose to USD 982.1 million from USD 845.6 million; cash interest cover rose to 5.42x from 4.56x. Net leverage is 2.88x on the issuer's adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,008.2 million. All remaining debt is fixed-rate notes, so coverage now moves with earnings, not with rates.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C-12; calculation C-13; calculation C-10; calculation C-11; calculation C-21; calculation C-01; calculation C-02; calculation C-15; calculation C-16; calculation C-14; calculation C-04

Cash flow is good but should not be annualised. Free cash flow of USD 616.0 million over the last twelve months is 62.7% of reported EBITDA and 18.9% of total debt, and nine-month free cash flow was USD 482.0 million against USD 262.2 million a year earlier. Capital expenditure is only 49.6% of the 2026 plan and working capital absorbed USD 65.8 million net, with receivables and inventories building while payables and accruals rose on the timing of vendor payments and higher incentive compensation accruals, so part of the improvement may reverse as spending and payments normalise.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C-18; calculation C-31; calculation C-32; calculation C-19; calculation C-20; calculation C-25; calculation C-24; 10-Q_2026-09-26_entg-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 33

Liquidity is adequate and the 2029 maturity wall is the weakest dimension. Cash of USD 356.5 million and an undrawn USD 750.0 million revolver maturing in 2031 give USD 1,106.5 million, with letters of credit under the revolver not stated; nothing falls due before 2028, but 12.1% of principal is due in 2028, 60.7% in 2029 and 27.2% in 2030. The 2029 principal is 3.25 years of last-twelve-months free cash flow and liquidity covers 46.1% of the 2028 and 2029 principal, so repayment from cash alone is not the base case and refinancing access decides the outcome. The company states it has had no difficulty accessing capital markets, but no rating or market price was supplied to test that.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C-22; calculation C-35; calculation C-23; calculation C-36; calculation C-33; calculation C-43; 10-Q_2026-09-26_entg-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 32

Revenue durability is average rather than strong. Management describes revenue as predominantly unit-driven or recurring and says switching away from its products is costly, but the top ten customers were 50% of FY2025 sales, customers typically give no long-term purchase commitments, and Equipment and Engineering sales, 18.3% of nine-month 2026 sales, grew 42.0% against 13.8% for total net sales. Advanced Purity Solutions earns 67.6% of segment profit, so a fall in fab capital spending would reach EBITDA through both volume and margin.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C-29; calculation C-28; calculation C-30; calculation C-27; 10-K_2025-12-31_entg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; 10-K_2025-12-31_entg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-09-26_entg-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 10

Financial policy and governance support the view but are not locked in. No sponsor or controlling holder is named, USD 450.0 million of debt was repaid, there is no repurchase programme and the dividend took 8.8% of FY2025 operating cash flow. Legal capacity for debt, liens and distributions, the guarantor list and the proxy statement were not supplied, so a Low governance risk level is provisional and the policy rests on observed behaviour, not on documented restrictions.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C-21; calculation C-26; 10-K_2025-12-31_entg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 57; 10-K_2025-12-31_entg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 66

Structural priority and covenant questions stay open, with one refinement to Financial foundation. Financial foundation ranked the revolver and the USD 1,600.0 million of secured notes together on the filings' labels and said no rank among them was stated; the annual report's exhibit index lists an equal priority intercreditor agreement dated 2022-07-06 between the collateral agents of the senior credit facilities and of the notes, which supports equal rank but was not supplied, and names certain subsidiaries as guarantors without listing them. Unsecured notes total USD 1,695.0 million and secured notes are 48.6% of principal. The first lien net leverage covenant of 5.20x is tested only when revolver use passes an unstated threshold, and the annual report calls the test a secured net leverage ratio, so headroom is not worked out.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest challenge to a solid view is that leverage and coverage are measured near a cyclical high. Last-twelve-months reported EBITDA of USD 982.1 million is 16.1% above FY2025's USD 845.6 million and Q3 2026 net sales grew 24.6%, so the ratios flatter the through-the-cycle position. If reported EBITDA fell back to the FY2025 level, net debt at 2026-09-26 would be 3.43x EBITDA and cash interest cover 4.67x: still below the FY2025 net leverage of 3.95x, but with less room to refinance USD 2,400.0 million due in 2028 and 2029 on terms that are not yet known. The view would weaken if quarterly net sales fell below the low end of the fourth-quarter 2026 guidance of USD 1,025 million, if capital expenditure caught up with the plan and free cash flow conversion fell from 62.7%, or if the credit agreement showed a restrictive covenant trigger or broad distribution capacity; it would strengthen if the 2028 notes were refinanced early, more debt were repaid, or ratings and market prices showed open access.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C-01; calculation C-02; calculation C-49; calculation C-45; calculation C-39; calculation C-40; calculation C-13; calculation C-31; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_entgq32026ex991.htm.jsonl, page 2

### Limitations

- Market access for the 2028 and 2029 refinancing is judged from the revolver, the absence of reported difficulty and the company's own statement, because no agency rating, bond price or spread was supplied; a closed or expensive market would make the 2029 wall a larger weakness than shown.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-26_entg-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 32; Source readiness (CP-0)
- The Low governance conclusion and the structural priority comment rest on filings that summarise the debt documents; the executed credit agreement and indentures could show covenant limits, guarantor scope or distribution capacity that change both.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_entg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 96–97
- The downside illustration anchors on FY2025 reported EBITDA, which is derived from operating income plus depreciation and amortization, so it shows scale and not a forecast; a deeper trough than FY2025 would raise leverage further than shown.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C-39; calculation C-40

### Revenue drivers, margins and cash conversion

Revenue follows wafer volumes and fab capital spending. Volume added USD 341.2 million to nine-month 2026 net sales while currency subtracted USD 13.1 million, and the customer categories linked to equipment grew fastest. Because customers typically give no long-term purchase commitments and the top ten are half of sales, revenue is cyclical and concentrated even though products are specified into customer processes; revenue visibility rests on near-term orders, not on contracts.
Basis: calculation C-46; calculation C-28; 10-Q_2026-09-26_entg-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-K_2025-12-31_entg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 18

Gross margin was 48.2% in nine months 2026 against 44.6% a year earlier, helped by volume, lower depreciation after the January 2026 useful-life change and tariff refunds, and reported EBITDA margin is 27.9% over the last twelve months. Lower depreciation lifts gross margin and operating income but not EBITDA, so the EBITDA margin gain comes from volume and plant utilisation, which reverse with demand. Cost flexibility is moderate: engineering, research and development is 9.1% of net sales and management says it will keep investing in it, while capital expenditure and the dividend are the discretionary items.
Basis: calculation C-47; calculation C-42; calculation C-48; 10-Q_2026-09-26_entg-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-09-26_entg-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-09-26_entg-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-K_2025-12-31_entg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11

Cash conversion is high because capital expenditure is below plan and interest is falling: free cash flow is 62.7% of reported EBITDA after USD 181.1 million of cash interest, which is 5.14% of last-twelve-months net sales. Capital expenditure is the main lever in a downturn, and cutting it would protect free cash flow at the cost of slower capacity additions for new fabs.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C-31; calculation C-17; calculation C-41

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is real but the company states that no global competitor matches its full range: named competitors include Pall Corporation, part of Danaher, and the EMD Performance Materials division of Merck KGaA, so pricing power is limited to specified-in products. Buyer power is high because the top ten customers are half of sales and typically give no long-term purchase commitments, which is a revenue risk, not a pricing certainty. Supplier power is moderate: certain filtration membranes, resins, abrasive particles and chemicals come from a single source or limited suppliers, and multi-year supply agreements cover part of that exposure. Threats from new entrants and substitutes are not established by the sources beyond technology transitions that management presents as an opportunity.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_entg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20; 10-K_2025-12-31_entg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-K_2025-12-31_entg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 18

### Macro, policy and technology factors

Trade policy and technology cycles are material to credit quality. Tariffs and export controls are a stated cost and demand risk, China sales were flat in nine months 2026 and Taiwan is the largest region at 26% of sales; support under the CHIPS Act of up to USD 77.0 million is tied to milestones. Node transitions raise materials content per wafer, which management presents as a tailwind, but exposure to a small number of fabs means technology shifts can also move share.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-26_entg-20260926.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20, 24; 10-K_2025-12-31_entg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2025-12-31_entg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9

### SWOT in credit terms

- Strengths: lower leverage and higher coverage after the repayment of the term loan, liquidity with no maturity before 2028, and recurring, specified-in consumables.
- Weaknesses: the 2029 maturity wall, concentrated customers without long-term purchase commitments, and free cash flow that benefits from timing.
- Opportunities that would improve credit quality: early refinancing of the 2028 notes and further voluntary repayment from free cash flow.
- Threats that would weaken it: a fall in fab capital spending, new tariffs or export controls, and a covenant or documentation constraint not yet seen.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C-12; calculation C-23

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

The only guidance for the next 12 to 24 months covers the fourth quarter of 2026: net sales of USD 1,025 million to USD 1,055 million and an Adjusted EBITDA margin of 30.7% to 31.2%, which is management's view and supports near-term deleveraging if met. Tailwinds are node migration, new fab construction and a lower interest bill after the term loan repayment; headwinds are tariffs, a possible reversal in equipment-linked demand and the 2028 and 2029 maturities. No multi-year guidance is supplied, and a Capital Markets Day on 2026-11-09 falls after the as-of date and is not used.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-29_entgq32026ex991.htm.jsonl, page 2; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_entgq32026ex991.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-K_2025-12-31_entg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-09-26_entg-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 20

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a pause in fab capital spending or a customer inventory correction. The first operating break is orders cut or deferred, because customers typically give no long-term purchase commitments, which lowers volume and plant utilisation and reverses the margin gains. EBITDA would fall toward the FY2025 level; on that illustration net leverage would be 3.43x and cash interest cover 4.67x, with liquidity of USD 1,106.5 million, no maturity before 2028 and management levers in capital expenditure and the dividend, so a liquidity shortfall is not indicated. The refinancing consequence arrives later: the 2028 and 2029 maturities would be refinanced from a trough in earnings, so the observable triggers are net sales below guidance, free cash flow conversion falling, and any revolver draw.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C-39; calculation C-40; calculation C-22; calculation C-43

### Overall credit view

Overall, Entegris presents an adequate business profile supported by specified-in consumable products and a deleveraging financial policy, but credit quality is constrained by customer concentration, cyclical demand and a 2029 maturity wall. Cash-flow durability appears adequate: free cash flow conversion is high but flattered by timing. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on market access in 2028 and 2029 and on earnings holding near the current level. The primary downside path is a fall in capital-spending-linked demand that cuts EBITDA before the notes are refinanced. Further analysis would require the executed credit agreement and indentures, agency ratings and market prices. Top risk: refinancing the 2029 maturity from lower earnings.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths, ranked, each as evidence, mechanism and implication.

- Deleveraging: net debt over reported EBITDA fell to 2.96x from 3.95x as USD 450.0 million of term loan was repaid and earnings rose, which widens the cushion before leverage reaches earlier levels and removes floating-rate debt.
- Coverage: reported EBITDA covers cash interest 5.42x on fixed-rate notes, so coverage depends on earnings, and a downturn that cut EBITDA to the FY2025 level would still leave 4.67x.
- Liquidity: USD 1,106.5 million of cash and undrawn revolver, with nothing due before 2028, supports interest and working capital through a downturn of the size illustrated.
- Cash generation: last-twelve-months free cash flow of USD 616.0 million funds voluntary repayment, which is the route to a smaller 2029 refinancing.
- Financial policy: no sponsor, no repurchase programme and a dividend at 8.8% of FY2025 operating cash flow leave cash for creditors, with extraction risk low on observed behaviour.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C-12; calculation C-15; calculation C-40; calculation C-22; calculation C-18; calculation C-26

Weaknesses, ranked, each as evidence, mechanism and implication.

- The 2029 maturity wall: USD 2,000.0 million, 60.7% of principal, falls due in 2029 as bullet notes, so repayment needs refinancing or 3.25 years of free cash flow, and market access becomes the deciding factor.
- Cyclical, concentrated demand: the top ten customers are 50% of sales, there are typically no long-term purchase commitments and equipment-linked sales grew fastest at 42.0%, so a capital-spending pause would hit volume and margin together.
- Free cash flow timing: capital expenditure is 49.6% of plan and accruals rose, so conversion of 62.7% may fall as spending and payments normalise.
- Unassessed legal terms: the credit agreement and indentures were not supplied, so the covenant trigger, guarantor scope and distribution capacity are not established and structural priority and recovery cannot be concluded.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C-23; calculation C-33; calculation C-28; calculation C-25; calculation C-31
