## Findings

### Bottom line

Governance risk is medium: no sponsor is named and 2026 actions favored creditors, but the ARKO Petroleum listing and a pending USD 205 million acquisition put more of the cash flow in a partly owned subsidiary. Stock-offering proceeds repaid USD 206.7 million of bank debt and the company repurchased USD 100.0 million of Senior Notes at a discount.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_arko_arko-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_arko_arko-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation C19

Outside holders own 26.4% of ARKO Petroleum, which earned 48.75% of segment operating income in the nine months to 2026-09-30 and intends to pay USD 2.00 per share a year, about USD 25.14 million of which would go to outside holders. Control sits with a group of unnamed holders, so sponsor intent and exit pressure cannot be assessed.

### Key findings

Control is concentrated in holders the filings do not name, so there is no sponsor record to underwrite. A limited number of stockholders beneficially owned about 59% of the voting stock at the end of 2025, 8 percentage points less than the 67% a year earlier, but the filings give no names, fund life, board seats or fees because directors, ownership and related-party detail are incorporated by reference from a proxy statement that is not supplied. The Chairman, President and Chief Executive Officer holds the same offices at ARKO and its listed subsidiary, so decisions on dividends, related-party terms and acquisitions between the two sit with one management team.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_arko_arko-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 53; 10-K_2024-12-31_arko_arko-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 42; calculation C37; 10-K_2025-12-31_arko_arko-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 89; 10-K_2025-12-31_arko_arko-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 50

The 2026 listing of ARKO Petroleum moved close to half of segment profit into a company that outside holders own 26.4% of, which lowers the share of that cash flow that reaches the parent. ARKO keeps 93.3% of the vote through Class B shares carrying five votes each, so control is not at risk, but ARKO Petroleum intends to pay USD 0.50 per share each quarter, which at that rate sends USD 70.0 million a year to ARKO and USD 25.14 million to outside holders, and its Capital One borrower may pay dividends up the chain only to the extent of available cash. The agreements between ARKO and its subsidiary were prepared when it was wholly owned and were not approved by a separate or disinterested board, and the fixed margin the group sets for its GPMP fuel-supply segment rose from USD 0.05 to USD 0.06 per gallon at the start of 2026.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a medium reading is the repair already made. Total debt is 31.51% lower than at the end of 2025, the Senior Notes were repurchased below par, covenants were reported as met at the end of 2025, no default or waiver is described, the parent still receives 73.6% of ARKO Petroleum's dividends, and the share of voting stock held by the unnamed group fell by 8 percentage points in a year. A proxy statement showing independent directors, a conflicts committee that reviewed the post-listing fuel supply and cost-sharing terms, and no pledge of the large holders' shares would move the view toward low.
Basis: calculation C18; calculation C37; 10-K_2025-12-31_arko_arko-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 135; 10-Q_2026-09-30_arko_arko-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_arko_arko-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 65

Three developments would move the view toward high: the U.S. Petroleum Partners acquisition closing on Capital One borrowings that raise leverage materially, the Series A redemption right being met from revolver borrowing in 2027, or a release of ARKO Petroleum or its subsidiaries from the Senior Notes guarantee together with dividends above USD 2.00 per share a year. A covenant waiver or an amendment on weaker terms would do the same.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_arko_arko-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–19; 10-K_2025-12-31_arko_arko-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 147

### Limitations

- The holders of the roughly 59% voting block are not named because the proxy statement is not supplied, so the medium rating rests on structure and observed actions and not on any owner's record; a named sponsor with a fund life or pledged shares would move it either way.
- Whether ARKO Petroleum and its subsidiaries guarantee the Senior Notes is not stated after the listing, so the view that noteholders keep a claim on the subsidiary's cash flow is limited to the dividends and loans that reach the parent.
- The credit agreements, indenture and Series A terms are described but not supplied, so covenant levels, the definition of the 7.00x consent test and restricted-payment capacity are not established, and no conclusion on headroom after 2025-12-31 can be drawn.
- No amounts are printed for the management services, fuel supply or cost-sharing charges between ARKO and ARKO Petroleum after the listing, so any leakage from related-party pricing to outside holders cannot be sized and the related-party judgment rests on terms and not on amounts.
- The U.S. Petroleum Partners acquisition had not closed in the latest filing, and its funding mix, closing date, inventory payment and any price multiple beyond the earn-out target are not established, so its effect on leverage at ARKO Petroleum is unmeasured.
