## Findings

### Bottom line

ARKO's fundamental credit is adequate: debt to EBITDA fell to 2.75x after a 2026 subsidiary stock offering repaid bank debt, but free cash flow is close to zero, so creditors depend on liquidity and on refinancing the 2028 and 2029 maturities. Liquidity of about USD 1.0 billion covers the near term, but the only printed maturity schedule predates the 2026 repayments and the unsecured Senior Notes carry a market-based value of 89.23% of principal.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C02; calculation C21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_arko_arko-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_arko_arko-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 58

The strengths are scale, the equity-funded debt reduction and fixed-fee fuel supply; the weaknesses are a 2.74% EBITDA margin, cash interest cover of 3.01x, payouts to shareholders that exceeded free cash flow in 2026 and secured debt of 1.20x EBITDA ranking ahead of the unsecured notes. Financial policy and governance risk are medium.

### Key findings

Leverage is lower because debt fell, not because earnings rose, so the improvement is a one-time step. Total debt to reported EBITDA was 2.75x at 2026-09-30 against 3.85x at 2025-12-31, since total debt fell 31.51% while last-twelve-months reported EBITDA slipped 3.98%. Net debt to EBITDA is 1.94x, which is 1.95x pro forma for the common dividend payable 2026-12-03 and 1.97x pro forma for the subsidiary dividend payable 2026-11-30, each event taken alone, and the effect of the pending acquisition is not established. Counting finance leases lifts total debt to EBITDA to 3.63x.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious reading is the speed of the repair. Total debt is 31.51% lower than at the end of 2025, cash interest paid over the last twelve months is 13.38% lower than in 2025, reported cover improved to 3.01x from 2.71x, Adjusted EBITDA grew 8.54% in the nine months, scheduled principal due before 2028 was small on the last printed schedule, and the weak 2026 cash flow comes largely from working capital tied to high fuel prices, which can reverse. Equity investors funded the subsidiary at USD 18.00 per share in 2026, so access to capital is not in doubt today.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C04; calculation C11; calculation C10; calculation C09; calculation C19; calculation C31; 10-Q_2026-09-30_arko_arko-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 16

Two developments would move the view toward stronger: free cash flow after payouts turning positive for consecutive quarters, and a Capital One extension that also moves the PNC maturity beyond 2027-11-05, ideally with further Senior Notes repurchased below par without revolver borrowing. Four would move it toward weaker: a second period in which operating cash flow is below capital spending, borrowing under the Capital One line to fund the acquisition without matching EBITDA, a Series A redemption funded from the revolver, or a covenant amendment on terms that are not printed.

### Limitations

- The Capital One leverage and interest cover tests and the M&T tests are named but their levels are not printed, so the distance between today's ratios and any breach is not established and liquidity could be tighter than the headline figure suggests.
- The latest maturity schedule is dated 2025-12-31, so the 2028 and 2029 maturities after the 2026 repayments are taken from stated drawn amounts and not from a schedule, which makes the funding calendar approximate until a new schedule is printed.
- No rating, peer metric or bond price is supplied, so the comparison with typical leveraged-credit issuers in the sector is qualitative and the market value of the Senior Notes is only the company's own period-end estimate.
- Last-twelve-months figures to 2026-09-30 add unaudited nine-month periods to audited FY2025, so conclusions about the earnings and cash flow trend carry quarter-end timing noise, above all in working capital.
- No management guidance, forecast or standalone ARKO Petroleum statements are supplied, so the outlook is qualitative and the parent's own cash generation after the listing is not established.

### Credit-relevant company description

ARKO is a convenience store operator and fuel wholesaler whose profit comes from fuel margins and in-store merchandise margins on a very large, low-margin revenue base. At 2026-09-30 it operated 1,038 stores, supplied fuel to 2,124 dealer sites and ran 294 cardlock sites in more than 30 states; retail supplied 52.17% of nine-month external revenue, wholesale 39.94% and fleet fueling 7.59%, and fuel was 83.08% of revenues, so revenue size says little about profit. Wholesale sells on fixed fees per gallon and management describes fleet fueling as a durable cash flow business, so both give more predictable cash flow than retail, where margins move with wholesale fuel costs and local competition, and operating income is seasonal, with the second and third quarters well above the first and fourth.

The model is being reshaped by the Transformation Plan, which converts retail stores to dealer sites supplied by wholesale, and by 26 acquisitions since 2013, none of them in 2025 or the nine months of 2026. For creditors this lowers store labor and capital but also merchandise profit, adds fixed-fee supply and rent, and leaves cash-flow visibility better in the contracted channels than in retail; debt service capacity rests on reported EBITDA of USD 227.442 million over the last twelve months, and refinancing confidence rests on that earnings base and on liquidity rather than on any sponsor.

### Ownership and group structure

ARKO is a public company with no sponsor named in its filings: a limited group of unnamed stockholders held about 59% of the vote at 2025-12-31, down from about 67%, and the Chairman, President and Chief Executive Officer leads both ARKO and its listed subsidiary. Business & governance rates sponsor and governance risk as medium: the 2026 actions reduced debt and cost creditors only the 26.4% outside share of the subsidiary's dividends, but payouts exceeded free cash flow in 2026, agreements between ARKO and the subsidiary were set without a separate board, and a Series A preferred claim of USD 100.0 million can be called from 2027-08-31.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C45; calculation C40

The group has three debt levels: Senior Notes at the parent, guaranteed by certain domestic subsidiaries; the Capital One line at GPMP inside ARKO Petroleum, secured by pledges over the subsidiary's supply agreements and its equity in GPMP; and the PNC and M&T facilities at GPM and at ARKO Petroleum. ARKO holds 73.6% of the subsidiary's economics and 93.3% of its vote, the parent's own cash was USD 2.577 million at 2025-12-31, and parent-only statements show that note service depends on interest and repayments from subsidiaries, so the dividend limits in the Capital One and PNC documents are the link between operating cash flow and the notes. Covenant capacity is not assessed here.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is mostly pass-through fuel, so it is cyclical and price-driven: revenues grew 11.28% in the nine months to 2026-09-30 although fuel gallons fell 7.61%, because the average price of fuel rose, and merchandise revenue fell 12.28% as stores closed or converted and same-store sales declined. Retail fuel margin is the swing factor because retail prices lag wholesale costs; the company says margins are lower when costs rise gradually and higher when costs fall or are volatile. Wholesale and consignment contracts earn a fixed fee or share gross profit, which largely removes exposure to commodity price movements in the wholesale channel.
Basis: calculation C38; calculation C42; calculation C53; 10-Q_2026-09-30_arko_arko-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-Q_2026-09-30_arko_arko-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 41–44; 10-K_2025-12-31_arko_arko-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9

Demand is discretionary at the margin and shrinking in volume: same-store fuel gallons fell about 5% to 6% in every period, same-store merchandise sales fell 1.3% in the nine months with about 90% of the decline from cigarettes, and no customer exceeds 10% of revenue, but four suppliers each exceed 10% of purchases. Loyalty enrollment of about 2.7 million, up 11.2% from a year earlier, and fixed-fee dealer contracts give some stickiness, yet retail revenue is not contracted. Revenue is therefore large, volatile in price terms and declining in volume.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Costs are largely fixed once stores are open: site operating expenses were 6.83% lower in the nine months because stores were closed or converted, general and administrative expenses rose 1.72%, and rent is a large fixed element, with operating lease cost of USD 192.929 million inside site operating expenses in 2025. Fuel and merchandise costs pass through to prices with a lag, so margins are exposed to input-cost swings, and store wages are a continuing pressure with about 8,450 store employees at the end of 2025.
Basis: calculation C35; calculation C36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_arko_arko-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-K_2025-12-31_arko_arko-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 139; 10-K_2025-12-31_arko_arko-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 39

Margins can be defended under moderate volume pressure because conversions cut store costs and wholesale fees are fixed, but not against a sharp compression in fuel margin: one cent per gallon on 2025 volumes is USD 20.64 million, 8.71% of reported EBITDA, and the reported EBITDA margin is only 2.74%. Operating deleverage is the main risk, because rent, labor and depreciation stay while gallons fall.

Capital intensity is modest, with purchases of property and equipment at 1.62% of revenue over the last twelve months, but conversion of EBITDA to free cash flow has collapsed: 47.64% in FY2024, 27.57% in FY2025 and 0.33% over the last twelve months. Cash interest paid is USD 75.590 million, cash taxes were USD 6.188 million in the nine months to 2026-09-30, and 2025 cash taxes were lowered by a timing benefit of USD 26.9 million from the 2025 tax law change, so the 2025 free cash flow of USD 65.299 million flatters the underlying level.

### Competitive forces in credit terms

Rivalry is intense and limits margin durability: the annual report names 7-Eleven and Speedway, Circle K, Casey's, Murphy USA, QuikTrip, Royal Farms, Sheetz and Wawa as large competitors, many building new sites in ARKO's markets, and says competitors often sell unbranded fuel at lower prices, so retail fuel and merchandise margins have little protection.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_arko_arko-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15

The threat of new entrants is moderate: the barriers are the financial strength needed for fuel supply agreements and the capital for sites, but national chains are building new-to-industry sites and ARKO's own plan to open such sites needs the same capital.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_arko_arko-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 14–16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_arko_arko-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a sustained spike in wholesale fuel prices, with diesel at record highs as in the third quarter of 2026, together with weaker demand. The first operational break is working capital: receivables and inventory rise as they did in 2026, when three working-capital lines absorbed more cash than the whole fall in operating cash flow, and retail fuel margin is squeezed when costs rise gradually. Operating cash flow then stays below capital spending, so free cash flow turns negative and the payouts to shareholders and the dividends to outside holders of the subsidiary are met from cash balances and revolver borrowing.

Financially, borrowing on the Capital One and PNC lines raises debt while EBITDA falls; reported cash interest cover would be 2.0x if EBITDA fell 33.53% at current interest, an illustrative level because no covenant level is printed, and a PNC borrowing base shrinks if receivables and inventory fall. The leverage and refinancing consequence is that the 2027 calendar of the Series A redemption right and the PNC maturity of 2027-11-05 arrives with less free cash flow, and the USD 205 million acquisition, if borrowed, would add debt first and earnings later. Management levers are cutting growth capital, stopping buybacks and dividends, slowing conversions and drawing the Capital One line, whose unused amount was USD 621.4 million. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress within 12 to 24 months on stated liquidity, but refinancing risk in 2028 and 2029 would rise sharply.

### Overall credit view

Overall, ARKO presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, a fixed-fee wholesale channel, a fleet fueling base and a repaired balance sheet, but credit quality is constrained by thin margins, near-zero free cash flow, secured debt and a minority interest ahead of the unsecured notes, and a crowded funding calendar from late 2027. Cash-flow durability appears weak to adequate because free cash flow conversion fell to 0.33% of EBITDA while 81.21% of retail stores are leased. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on extending the Capital One line before 2027-11-05, on the fuel market, which drives working capital and margin, and on whether the subsidiary's dividends keep reaching the parent. The primary downside path is a fuel price spike that turns working capital into revolver borrowing ahead of the 2027 to 2029 maturities. Further analysis would require covenant levels, a current maturity schedule, the guarantor list and standalone statements for the subsidiary. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: refinancing the 2027 to 2029 obligations from thin free cash flow.
