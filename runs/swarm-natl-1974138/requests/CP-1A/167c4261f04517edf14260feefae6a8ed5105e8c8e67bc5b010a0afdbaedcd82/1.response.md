## Findings

### Bottom line

NCR Atleos is a publicly listed, mostly recurring-revenue ATM technology and network business with no sponsor or controlling shareholder disclosed, so creditors face event risk from the pending Brink's takeover more than sponsor-extraction risk. Recurring streams were 70.6% of FY2025 revenue and no customer exceeds 10% of revenue, but recurring revenue fell by USD 49 million in FY2025, rose only 0.7% in the first nine months of 2026 and contracted backlog has fallen to about USD 1.8 billion, so the earnings gain comes from margin and mix rather than growth in the recurring base.

### Key findings

Ownership carries no sponsor risk but is about to change hands. NCR Voyix distributed all Atleos shares to its stockholders in October 2023 and holds none, no sponsor, fund or controlling shareholder is disclosed, and holder percentages and director holdings are not supplied. Brink's will own Atleos outright once the two-step merger closes, expected in early January 2027, so the owner whose behavior matters to creditors will soon be an acquirer whose financing the documents do not describe.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against a low sponsor-risk, event-led view is that the debt was created by a debt-funded USD 2,996 million payment to the former owner, that the next owner is an acquirer whose leverage is unknown, and that the contracts that protect creditors are not in the sources: the credit facilities make a change of control an event of default, noteholders gave up their put for a fee that is not stated, and no document says how the facilities are treated at closing. The earnings improvement is also thinner than it looks, because recurring revenue is flat, contracted backlog has fallen for two quarters, hardware is falling, 2026 margins include tariff refunds of unstated size, and a legal recovery of USD 31.5 million sits in reported earnings.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_ncr-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 133; 10-K_2025-12-31_ncr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 168; 10-Q_2026-03-31_natl-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-Q_2026-09-30_natl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_natl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_natl-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 43; calculation B5; calculation B32

Disclosure of Brink's financing that repays the credit facilities at closing, or confirmation that the tax indemnity does not apply, would lower event risk, as would a proxy statement that shows no large holder with blocking rights. Brink's financing that leaves Atleos creditors behind new secured debt, a revolver draw to pay the consent fee or termination fee, a tax indemnity that the merger engages, or a further decline in hardware and Network volumes would raise it.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The pending merger with Brink's is the only transaction that changes who owns the debt. Under the agreement signed on 2026-02-26 Brink's acquires each Atleos share for USD 30.00 in cash plus a fixed fraction of a Brink's share, through a first merger in which Atleos survives as a Brink's subsidiary and an immediate second merger into a Brink's limited liability company; stockholders of both companies approved it on 2026-06-30, and closing is expected in early January 2027 subject to antitrust clearances, other regulatory approvals and money transmitter licenses. Atleos may owe Brink's a termination fee of USD 145 million in certain circumstances and has agreed to run its business in the ordinary course until closing.
