## Findings

### Bottom line

CACI's fundamental credit profile is solid but leveraged: federal revenue, rising margins and strong cash conversion support 3.69x net leverage that is falling, while a budget delay combined with a fixed-price cost overrun is the primary downside.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2–4

Liquidity of USD 1,674.39 million against USD 46.75 million of scheduled amortisation and no large maturity before fiscal 2031 gives time to absorb that downside, but the liquidity rests on one revolver, and the receivables programme that adds cash flow is at its cap and ends on 18 December 2026 unless renewed. Covenant headroom and ratings are not established, so the view is a fundamental one.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 8, 20–21; 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 85, 89; calculation C25

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a solid profile is that fiscal 2026 cash flow was helped by items that may not repeat: deferred income taxes swung by USD 172.45 million, income tax expense exceeded cash taxes paid by USD 75.38 million, and sales of receivables added USD 44.62 million to operating cash flow, so free cash flow could fall short of the issuer's guidance of at least USD 900 million for fiscal 2027 if those effects reverse; organic growth has slowed to 2.1% and the fixed-price share is rising. The view would weaken if the September 2026 collection days of 59 rise further, if the receivables programme lapses on 18 December 2026, or if a continuing resolution delays awards against the small funded backlog. It would strengthen if free cash flow reaches the guided floor while net leverage keeps falling from 3.69x and the executed debt documents show tight distribution limits.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 53, 67, 85; EX-99.1_2026-10-21_fy27-q1caci20261021ex991.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 4–5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18; calculation C5

### Strengths and weaknesses

Cash conversion is high: free cash flow was 62.65% of EBITDA and the issuer guides to at least USD 900 million in fiscal 2027, which funds deleveraging. Liquidity is large against near-term maturities: USD 1,674.39 million against USD 46.75 million of amortisation. Capital intensity is low, with capital expenditures of 1.11% of revenue, so cash flow is not absorbed by plant. Financial policy is conservative on distributions: no dividend has been paid and repurchases fell to USD 17.08 million in fiscal 2026.

### Credit-relevant company description

CACI is a holding company whose subsidiaries sell Technology, such as software-defined, cyber, electronic warfare and space solutions, and Expertise, meaning cleared technical staff, to national security customers. Technology was 58.4% of fiscal 2026 revenue and Domestic Operations 96.8%, with a small U.K. and European business. The model supports cash-flow visibility through multi-year contracts and a backlog of USD 34.0 billion, but renewals and funding are decided year by year by the customer, so visibility is longer than the funding behind it, and the business has grown through acquisitions that added debt.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 5–9; EX-99.1_2026-08-05_fy26-q4caci20260805ex991.htm.jsonl, page 10

### Ownership and group structure

CACI is a listed holding company with one class of common stock and no sponsor or controlling holder identified; the guarantor group, ownership percentages and board independence are in documents that are not supplied. With no sponsor there is no sponsor extraction or support to assess, and financial policy rests on the Board: no dividend has ever been paid, repurchases are small, and acquisitions are debt-funded. Creditor alignment is therefore judged from conduct, which is moderate, and not from structure, which is not established.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 46, 103–105

### Revenue durability

Revenue grew 12.64% in fiscal 2025 and 10.89% in fiscal 2026, with organic growth of 7.2% in fiscal 2026 and 2.1% in the September 2026 quarter. The Department of War supplied 53.6% of revenue, the Intelligence Community 24.6% and federal civilian agencies 17.4%, and the ten largest contracts were 22.5% of revenue. The revenue is predictable in aggregate but concentrated and budget-dependent: continuing resolutions and the shutdown from 1 October 2025 to 12 November 2025 delay awards, and only USD 6.2 billion of USD 34.0 billion of backlog is funded. The credit implication is that a funding delay moves cash flow before it moves covenants or ratings.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 12, 50; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 18, 20

### Margin resilience

The EBITDA margin was 11.2% in fiscal 2025, 12.3% in fiscal 2026 and 13.3% in the September 2026 quarter. Cost-plus-fee contracts, 52.8% of revenue in the quarter, pass cost inflation to the customer, while fixed-price work, 33.2%, leaves overruns with CACI; direct costs fell to 64.6% of revenue on mix, and indirect costs rose to 22.2% on a larger labor base. Margins can be defended against volume pressure because labor is variable with contract volume, but an adverse fixed-price outcome would not be passed on. A fall of 1.00 percentage points on trailing revenue removes USD 97.55 million of EBITDA.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-08-05_fy26-q4caci20260805ex991.htm.jsonl, page 12; EX-99.1_2026-10-21_fy27-q1caci20261021ex991.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; calculation C3

### Free cash flow conversion

Capital expenditures were 1.11% of revenue in fiscal 2026, so most EBITDA reaches cash flow before interest and tax. Issuer-defined free cash flow was USD 735.43 million, 62.65% of EBITDA, but operating cash flow of USD 886.71 million included a USD 172.45 million swing in deferred income taxes and USD 44.62 million from receivable sales, and cash taxes paid were USD 75.38 million below income tax expense. In the September 2026 quarter working capital absorbed cash, with days sales outstanding at 59. Cash flow is durable in direction but fiscal 2026 overstates the repeatable level by the tax and receivable-sale effects, whose size cannot be fixed from the filings alone.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is high: the company states that it competes against many firms, some larger, with no dominant competitor and a relatively small share of its addressable market, so re-competes can be lost, which would show first in backlog and awards. Non-traditional entrants in cloud, cyber and satellite operations raise entry pressure, and agencies that perform services for themselves are an indirect substitute. Buyer power is strong because the government sets contract terms and can terminate for convenience, although terminations have been rare. Supplier power sits mainly in cleared labor, where competition for skilled staff is intense and limits cost control. The credit effect is limited pricing power and a reliance on win rates and contract mix.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_caci-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8, 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18

### Macro and policy factors

Policy is the dominant external factor. Defense appropriations of USD 838.7 billion were enacted for the government's 2026 fiscal year and mandatory funding of about USD 156 billion for defense became available outside annual appropriations, which supports demand regardless of continuing resolutions. Economic exposure is mainly interest rates, since most debt is floating. The credit effect is demand support from defense funding against timing risk from shutdowns, which interrupted government operations from 1 October to 12 November 2025.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_caci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17, 22

Strengths: federal scale, cash conversion of 62.65% of EBITDA, low capital intensity, large liquidity and no dividend history. Weaknesses: concentration, fixed-price drift, acquisition leverage, goodwill-heavy assets and a capped receivables programme. Opportunities that could improve credit quality: defense funding and the guided free cash flow of at least USD 900 million, which could pay down debt. Threats that could weaken it: a prolonged shutdown or continuing resolution, a large fixed-price overrun, integration problems at ARKA and a rise in floating rates.

Competitive advantage and diversification are average: customers are many agencies but one government, and the company reports no dominant position. Cost and capex flexibility is a support, with capital expenditures of 1.11% of revenue and a labor-based cost base. Margin stability is average because the mix is shifting toward fixed-price work. Free cash flow stability is average because fiscal 2026 had tax and receivable-sale help. Liquidity is a support at USD 1,674.39 million. Ability to refinance is average because recent issuance is strong but ratings and covenants are not supplied. Financial policy and governance is average: distributions are minimal but acquisitions are debt-funded and structural governance is not established. The trend is improving leverage from a high of 4.63x at 31 March 2026 to 3.69x; the main constraints are concentration and fixed-price risk; the key missing datapoints are covenant levels, ratings, ownership detail and ARKA pro forma earnings.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over 12 to 24 months the issuer raised fiscal 2027 revenue guidance to USD 10,700 million to USD 10,900 million and guides to free cash flow of at least USD 900 million, supported by a 2.0x book-to-bill in the quarter and defense funding; that supports deleveraging if working capital cooperates. Headwinds are collection days at 59, a fixed-price share of 33.2%, slower organic growth of 2.1% and the end of the receivables term on 18 December 2026. Over three to five years the issuer states it is on track for its three-year targets, but the filings give no detail, so the medium-term view rests on the maturity ladder, with USD 1,706.75 million due in the year to 30 June 2031, and on continued defense funding.

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a continuing resolution or shutdown, as occurred from 1 October 2025, that delays task order awards while a fixed-price program overruns. The first operational break is slower revenue conversion from a backlog of which only USD 6.2 billion is funded, with the overrun cutting the margin. The financial transmission is lower EBITDA and weaker cash conversion; a fall of 1.00 percentage points in the margin on trailing revenue removes USD 97.55 million of EBITDA, lifts net leverage to 4.01x and cuts cover to 3.88x against the annualised interest charge, while collection days of 59 and a capped receivables programme absorb cash. The liquidity consequence is a draw on the revolver from USD 1,674.39 million of liquidity; the leverage consequence is a return to the level of 30 June 2026. Management levers are repurchases, which are small, acquisitions, which can be paused, and capital expenditures of only 1.11% of revenue. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress, because liquidity is 35.82 times scheduled amortisation, but covenant headroom is not established. Observable triggers are collection days above 59, a book-to-bill below 1.0x and free cash flow below USD 900 million.

### Overall credit view

Overall, CACI presents an adequate business profile supported by federal scale, broad agency demand, cash conversion of 62.65% of EBITDA and large liquidity, but credit quality is constrained by customer concentration, a rising fixed-price share and acquisition debt. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because fiscal 2026 benefited from tax timing and receivable sales. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on federal funding continuity, leverage continuing to fall from 3.69x and the November 2030 revolver and Term Loan maturities. The primary downside path is a budget delay combined with a fixed-price overrun. Further analysis would require the executed debt documents, ratings, the proxy statement and ARKA pro forma earnings. Top risk: federal funding delay.
