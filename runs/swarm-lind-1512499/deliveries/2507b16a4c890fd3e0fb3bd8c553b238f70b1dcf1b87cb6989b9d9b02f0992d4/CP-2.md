## Findings

### Bottom line
Lindblad's fundamental credit is adequate but thinly covered: net debt is 2.92x last-twelve-month reported EBITDA with no debt principal due before the third quarter of 2030, yet reported EBITDA covers cash interest only 2.32x and liquidity rests on customer deposits that exceed cash.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01

The trend is favourable: gross leverage on reported EBITDA fell from 7.68x at FY2025 to 4.97x and free cash flow was USD 95,985 thousand over the last twelve months. The primary downside path is a booking shortfall that lowers occupancy from the 92% of nine months 2026, reverses deposits and cuts cover, while minority puts, charter commitments and acquisitions keep drawing cash. Covenant levels, ratings and peers are not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C01; calculation C09

### Key findings

Deleveraging is earnings-led and has further to run only if earnings hold. Last-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA is 15.30% above FY2025 and reported EBITDA 54.85% above it, while carrying debt barely moved, so net leverage on reported EBITDA fell from 4.70x to 2.92x. The creditor consequence is that the improvement is exposed to any fall in occupancy or price, not locked in by repayment.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C02; calculation C01

Interest cover is the tightest metric. Reported EBITDA covers cash interest 2.32x and Adjusted EBITDA 2.52x, and on the 7.00% coupon alone reported EBITDA covers interest 2.83x. The mechanical break-even is large, a 34.51% fall in reported EBITDA before it equals cash interest plus capital spending, but the same earnings reverting to the FY2025 level would put cover at 1.50x and net leverage at 4.51x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01; calculation C15; calculation C04; calculation C03

Liquidity is adequate in size but deposit-funded. Cash and undrawn revolver of USD 330,249 thousand compare with unearned passenger revenues of USD 431,788 thousand, and unrestricted cash is 63.68% of them. Even if every implied cancellable deposit were refunded at once, cash would be USD 118,395 thousand and, with the revolver, 2.06x calendar 2027 coupon and charter payments, so the exposure is the speed of deposit reversal, not its size. Restricted cash of USD 33,223 thousand, held as escrow and card-processor reserves, is excluded from the cash used here.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_lindb20260930c_10q.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lindb20260930c_10q.htm.jsonl, page 9; calculation C01; calculation C05

Refinancing risk is remote before 2030 but unverified after it. The only principal is the 7.00% notes due September 2030, with the revolver ending in August 2030, and the issuer estimates the debt's fair value at 96.04% of principal. Both facilities limit added debt and restricted payments and the issuer reports compliance, but the indenture, credit agreement, guarantors and covenant levels are not supplied, so headroom to add debt or pay out cash is not established.

The business has two real concentrations: a brand license and permits. The National Geographic license runs to 2040 but National Geographic can end it early on a change of control, and Galápagos permits run on 20-year terms that can end early for non-compliance. A sale of the company would therefore trigger a credit question that the supplied debt documents cannot answer.

Financial policy is neutral to supportive but cash is going to growth. No dividends were paid, the preferred stock converted, and the 2025 refinancing extended maturities, yet cash purchases of businesses and minority interests used 56.31% of free cash flow from FY2024 to September 2026, and put options remain outstanding.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The main case for a better view is that earnings are rising, occupancy was 92% over nine months of 2026, free cash flow converted 71.82% of reported EBITDA over the last twelve months and nothing is due until 2030; even at the FY2025 earnings level, free cash flow was USD 63,838 thousand. The view would improve if reported EBITDA cover rose toward 3x on a stable coupon, if the debt documents showed tight restricted-payment limits and a change-of-control put, and if deposits kept growing alongside cash. It would worsen if occupancy fell back toward the 78% of FY2024, if National Geographic changed the license, if a proxy statement showed concentrated control, or if cash acquisitions kept outrunning free cash flow.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C09; calculation C03

### Company description and credit linkage

Lindblad sells premium expedition cruises on owned small ships and land-based adventure travel, with deposits paid at booking and the balance 60 to 120 days before departure. That gives revenue visibility about nine months ahead and a deposit float, which supports debt service when bookings hold but turns into a refund and cash-timing risk if they fall. The Lindblad segment was 64.28% of FY2025 tour revenues and Land Experiences 35.72%, so the ship business sets the credit and the land brands add diversification at a similar Adjusted EBITDA margin (16.10% against 16.87%). Recent history is acquisitive: the Thomson Group in 2024, Torcatt in 2025 and White Desert and Echo Charlie in 2026.

### Ownership and group structure

Lindblad is a listed Delaware company with a founder as board co-chair, no identified sponsor and no holder above a threshold is named; the proxy statement is not supplied. The group holds controlling stakes in Natural Habitat, Off the Beaten Path, DuVine and Classic Journeys, and since September 2026 in White Desert and Echo Charlie, with minority put and call options, and the notes and revolver are guaranteed by the Company and certain subsidiaries that are not listed. A board divided into three classes, a charter limit of 22% on non-U.S. citizen ownership that lets the company redeem excess shares, and a Coastwise Laws limit of 24.99% for the entities that own U.S.-flagged vessels all limit takeovers and equity sales. Financial policy is judged from conduct, which is neutral to supportive, because control cannot be read.

### Revenue durability

Revenue is premium-priced and discretionary. Net yield per available guest night rose 14.10% in FY2025 and occupancy from 78% to 88%, and 92% over nine months of 2026, but in past recessions occupancy and pricing fell, and about 90% of expedition guest ticket revenue comes from U.S. guests. Direct sales are 69.97% of Lindblad guest ticket revenue over nine months of 2026, which gives control of pricing and deposits but concentrates marketing cost. Revenue is therefore visible for the booked season and cyclical beyond it.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins are expanding but the cost base is exposed to royalties and fuel. Adjusted EBITDA margin rose from 16.37% in FY2025 to 16.93% over the last twelve months, helped because cost of tours fell from 52.50% of tour revenues over nine months of 2025 to 51.05% in 2026 and general and administrative expense grew 3.40% against tour revenue growth of 15.07%, which shows operating leverage; selling and marketing rose 24.80% on the final royalty step-up and marketing, and Lindblad fuel expense rose 26.52%. Fuel is a modest and generally unhedged cost, 3.1% of tour revenues in FY2025 against 4.9% in FY2023, while currency exposure is partly hedged. Fixed and variable cost are not split, so resilience under a volume fall is not established beyond the FY2025 and FY2024 comparison.

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is moderate and rising: the market is fragmented among private operators and the company expects large cruise operators to enter through acquisitions and new vessels. Entry barriers appear meaningful on analyst inference from permits, ship lead times and remote-operations experience, which helps pricing but does not stop well-funded entrants. Land-based and other premium travel are substitutes for discretionary spending. Buyer power is limited by direct sales, but travel agents and wholesalers supplied about 32% of expedition guest ticket revenue in FY2025. Supplier power sits with shipyards, fuel, charter owners and the National Geographic license, which carries a change-of-control termination right.

### SWOT

- Strengths: premium pricing and high occupancy; distant 2030 maturity with secured status; positive free cash flow; non-extractive conduct record.
- Weaknesses: thin interest cover; deposit-funded liquidity; dependence on one brand license and permits; negative book equity and a working-capital deficit.

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a shock to travel demand, such as a geopolitical event, a weather or ship incident or an economic downturn; past recessions lowered occupancy and pricing. The first operational break is bookings: fewer new deposits and some cancellations lower revenue visibility, and Lindblad segment Adjusted EBITDA rose by USD 20,375 thousand when occupancy rose 10 percentage points and price improved in FY2025, so a comparable reversal would remove a similar amount. On the financial side, earnings returning to the FY2025 level would leave cover at 1.50x and net leverage at 4.51x with the 2026-09-30 balance sheet, yet FY2025 itself produced positive free cash flow. On liquidity, refunding every implied cancellable deposit would still leave cash of USD 118,395 thousand and, with the revolver, 2.06x the calendar 2027 coupon and charter payments, so a deposit reversal alone would not cause liquidity distress within the 2027 fixed charges, and cash alone would cover them 1.40x. Management levers are lower capital spending, slower acquisitions and puts, and fewer discretionary costs; the observable triggers are occupancy, the deposit balance relative to cash, and cover.

### Primary downside path and overall credit view

Overall, Lindblad presents an adequate business profile supported by premium pricing, high occupancy and a secured 2030 maturity, but credit quality is constrained by thin cover, deposit-funded liquidity, brand and permit dependence and cash used on acquisitions. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on earnings staying above the FY2025 level, on revolver availability and on the change-of-control terms. Further analysis would require the debt documents, the proxy statement, the brand license terms, the put-option prices and a peer set, because the comparison with other leveraged issuers in the sector is not established.
