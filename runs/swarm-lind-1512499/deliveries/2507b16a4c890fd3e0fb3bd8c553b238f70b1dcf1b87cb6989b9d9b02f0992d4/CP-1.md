## Findings

### Bottom line
Lindblad's creditor risk is now earnings and cash timing rather than refinancing: net debt is 2.92x last-twelve-month reported EBITDA at September 30, 2026, and the only debt, USD 675.0 million of 7.00% secured notes, is not due until September 2030.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_lindb20260930c_10q.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lindb20260930c_10q.htm.jsonl, page 10; calculation C02; calculation C10; calculation C15

The cushion is real but deposit-funded. Unrestricted cash of USD 274,949 thousand and USD 55.3 million of undrawn revolver sit against unearned passenger revenues of USD 431,788 thousand, and reported EBITDA covers last-twelve-month cash interest only 2.32x. A booking slowdown that reverses customer deposits, or further cash acquisitions after USD 54,604 thousand paid this year, would change the view; covenant levels and the guarantor group are not established.

### Financial interpretation

Leverage has fallen because earnings grew, not because debt was repaid. Total debt moved only from USD 621,825 thousand at the end of 2023 to USD 664,516 thousand at 2026-09-30, while reported EBITDA rose from USD 53,995 thousand in FY2023 to USD 133,642 thousand over the last twelve months. Gross leverage on reported EBITDA therefore fell from 11.52x to 8.54x, 7.68x and 4.97x, and net leverage from 8.61x to 2.92x; on the issuer's Adjusted EBITDA the path is 8.73x to 4.57x gross and 6.53x to 2.68x net. Tour revenue grew 19.59% in FY2025 while cost of tours rose more slowly, and the Adjusted EBITDA margin rose from 12.50% in FY2023 to 16.93% over the last twelve months. The consequence for creditors is that deleveraging depends on occupancy and pricing holding, because the debt itself did not fall.
