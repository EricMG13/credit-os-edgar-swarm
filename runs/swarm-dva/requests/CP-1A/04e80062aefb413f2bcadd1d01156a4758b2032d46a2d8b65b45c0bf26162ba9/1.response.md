## Findings

### Bottom line

Creditors rely on a Medicare-anchored dialysis franchise whose profit rests mainly on commercial payors, and governance risk is Medium because buybacks exceed free cash flow and partner distributions come first, not because any sponsor extracts value.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 114; calculation A04

Non-hospital commercial payors are 26% of U.S. dialysis patient service revenue and 11% of patients yet generate nearly all profits. Repurchases of USD 1,788 million in 2025 against free cash flow of USD 1,024 million came while principal debt rose by USD 833 million, although the 2026 pace is 40.57% lower. Berkshire Hathaway received 27.10% of those repurchase dollars under a contract that holds it at 45.0%, and its actual holding is not stated.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 114; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 179; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 199; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 200; calculation A04; calculation A05; calculation A06

### Key findings

Profit is more concentrated than revenue. Non-hospital commercial payors are about 26% of U.S. dialysis patient service revenue and 11% of patients, yet the company says they generate nearly all profits, while Medicare and Medicare Advantage supply 57% of revenue and Medicare rates are set by regulation. The company reports that the end of enhanced premium tax credits hurt its commercial mix in 2026, so a small shift of patients out of commercial plans removes more profit than revenue. Profit by payor class is not disclosed, so the sensitivity cannot be sized.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37

Growth comes from price and acquisitions, and unit costs are catching up. Treatments fell 1.1% in 2025 and rose 0.5% in nine months 2026, while patient care cost per treatment rose 5.9% against revenue per treatment up 4.7% in 2025, lifting clinic cost from 65.96% to 66.74% of revenue per treatment. The nine-month 2026 share is 67.09% against 66.95%. Clinical wage rates rose about 3.8%, and Medicare rate updates of 2.2% for 2026 and an estimated 2.7% for 2027 sit below the 3.0% rise in cost per treatment in nine months 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 120; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 121; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37; calculation A07

International and ancillary growth adds scale at a lower margin and more variance. International revenue grew 37.8% to USD 1,346 million at a 6.61% operating margin against 17.67% for U.S. dialysis, and a goodwill and asset impairment of USD 62 million in China in Q3 2026 shows acquired value can be written down. Integrated kidney care takes medical cost risk through shared savings and shared losses arrangements.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 123; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10; calculation A02; calculation A03

Ownership is dispersed, but one holder shapes buyback cash. Berkshire Hathaway sells shares to the company each quarter so that it is held at 45.0%, and received 27.10% of 2025 repurchase dollars and 48.62% of nine-month 2026 repurchase dollars. Its standstill limits efforts to influence control, no dividend has been paid since 1994 and no sponsor fee appears in the sources, so no sponsor extraction is documented and no sponsor support can be assumed.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 108; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 199; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 200; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 202; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18; calculation A05

Shareholder returns have exceeded free cash flow and debt has risen, although the pace is slowing. Repurchases were 1.75x free cash flow in 2025 and 1.28x in nine months 2026, and principal debt rose USD 833 million in 2025 and USD 487.66 million in nine months 2026. Many credit agreement limits on repurchases fall away while the issuer-defined leverage ratio is below 4.00x, and that ratio is 3.38x, so management’s 3.0x to 3.5x target, not the credit agreement, is the more likely constraint on buybacks.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 114; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 179; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 183; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 11; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_dva93026ex991.htm.jsonl, page 13; calculation A04; calculation A06

Partner distributions are a second leak of cash. Payments to joint venture partners were 0.98x their income in 2025 and 1.21x in nine months 2026, and puts on partner interests are exercisable at the owners’ discretion, so a share of consolidated cash is committed to physician and hospital partners ahead of creditors.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 194; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation A08

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a Medium reading is that the company states a credit agreement leverage ratio of 3.38x inside its 3.0x to 3.5x target, has paid no dividend, discloses no sponsor fee or creditor-adverse transaction, cut repurchases by 40.57% in nine months 2026 and holds USD 1,420 million undrawn on its revolver. Medicare and Medicare Advantage also supply 57% of U.S. dialysis patient service revenue, and Medicare rate updates are 2.2% for 2026 and an estimated 2.7% for 2027.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 108; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 114; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_dva93026ex991.htm.jsonl, page 13; calculation A04

The view would move toward Low if repurchases fell to or below free cash flow with principal debt flat and the proxy statement showed no related-party leakage. It would move toward High if repurchases kept exceeding free cash flow while the issuer ratio approached 4.00x, if commercial mix fell further, if the qui tam case or the federal trade inquiry produced a material penalty or a change in physician non-compete practice, or if Berkshire’s holding or standstill terms changed.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 183; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 191; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 192; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 200; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 202; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; calculation A04

### Limitations

- The 2026 proxy statement is not supplied, so board composition, executive pay, director and officer holdings and related-party dealings are not established; the Medium governance risk level cannot be moved to Low on documented governance alone.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 145; 10-K_2025-12-31_dva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 146
- Berkshire’s actual holding is not stated, so the size of the contractual cash call can be read only from amounts already settled; the finding that the agreement ties buyback cash to one holder rests on the 45.0% cap and the settlements, not on a projected stake.

- Funding sources for repurchases and acquisitions are not disclosed, so the conclusion that buybacks were debt-funded rests on repurchases exceeding free cash flow while principal debt rose, not on a stated source.

### T2D.9 - Sponsor risk assessment

Sponsor / Governance Risk Level: Medium. The level is driven by repurchases that exceed free cash flow and by partner distributions that rank ahead of creditors, because they leave debt high relative to earnings, which implies thinner refinancing capacity for the 2030 and 2031 maturities. Countervailing evidence is the absence of dividends, sponsor fees or creditor-adverse transactions, a leverage ratio inside the company’s target range and slower buybacks in 2026.
