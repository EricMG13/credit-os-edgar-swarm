## Findings

### Bottom line

Churchill Downs has an adequate credit profile with leverage falling through 2026, but the improvement is reversible because shareholder returns are discretionary and a third of revenue depends on machine laws in two states.

Net debt was USD 4,555 million at 2026-09-30, or 3.94x last-twelve-months EBITDA; pro forma for the revolver-funded redemption of the 2027 notes on 2026-10-19 it is USD 4,555 million and 3.94x, and USD 4,631 million and 4.00x if about USD 76 million of implied October repurchases is added. Free cash flow after capital expenditure of USD 529.9 million is 11.22% of debt, interest cover is 4.22x and liquidity is USD 678 million pro forma, so near-term liquidity risk is low. The primary downside path is a ruling or tax change on machines, which supply 49.39% of segment earnings, landing on a balance sheet with committed projects and a USD 750 million buyback authority.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C6; calculation C9

### Key findings

Leverage has improved but not yet durably. Net debt over EBITDA fell from 4.64x at year-end 2025 to 3.94x at 2026-09-30 and from 4.09x to 3.66x on Adjusted EBITDA, with higher earnings and repayment contributing about equally, while the new buyback authority and the implied October repurchases show how the 3.94x can become 4.00x within weeks; the quarterly report's own net debt line is not net of cash, so this view uses net debt after cash.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C1; calculation C2

Cash generation is the core strength. Free cash flow after capital expenditure, cash interest and cash tax was USD 529.9 million over the last twelve months, 42.55% of Adjusted EBITDA, while capital expenditure took 18.14% of Adjusted EBITDA and EBITDA covered cash interest 4.22x, so the business can repay debt from internal cash if shareholder returns stay restrained.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an adequate-but-reversible reading is that the profile may be better than it looks: free cash flow covers 11.22% of debt, buybacks paused for nine months, the revolver fell by USD 578 million, maturities were pushed to 2031 and 2033, and no extraction or liability management is documented. The reversibility case rests on a USD 750 million authority that the board has not yet fully used and on a ruling risk that has not materialised in Kentucky or Virginia. A fourth quarter release showing no buyback resumption and sale proceeds applied to debt would move the view toward strong; a ruling or tax increase on machines, resumed revolver-funded buybacks, or a failure to refinance the 2028 notes ahead of the springing test would move it toward weak.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C3; calculation C5; calculation C8; calculation C13

### Company description

Churchill Downs operates the Kentucky Derby and Churchill Downs Racetrack, 17 historical racing machine venues in Kentucky, Virginia and New Hampshire, the TwinSpires online horse racing wagering business with United Tote and Exacta technology, and ten wholly owned regional casinos plus two equity-method casino investments, reported in three segments. The model supports cash-flow visibility through a recurring annual event, regulated gaming licences and several revenue lines, but earnings depend on state-granted permission for machines and on the Derby quarter, so debt service capacity is strong in normal conditions and exposed to legal change, and refinancing confidence rests on that legal stability.

### Ownership and group structure

The company is a listed corporation with no sponsor or controlling holder identified, so there is no sponsor equity backstop and no sponsor extraction pattern, and creditor alignment rests on board capital-allocation choices that have swung from heavy buybacks in FY2025 to repayment in 2026. Rivers Casino Des Plaines at 61.3% and Miami Valley Gaming and Racing at 50% are equity-method affiliates with shared consent rights and their own debt of USD 760 million, so their earnings reach the company only as distributions and the guarantor structure of the company's own debt is not named.

Machine revenue of 33.35% of revenue and the Virginia tax and competition effect in Q3 2026 show limited pricing power and legal exposure, while wagering services grew only slightly in 2026, so revenue is predictable at the portfolio level but cyclical, discretionary and regulated.

### Porter's five forces

Competitive rivalry is high: racetracks compete for horses and for simulcast wagering dollars, casinos compete with other gaming and sports betting for discretionary spend, and more states legalising gaming increases supply, which pressures margins and share; this links to lower EBITDA and higher leverage. The threat of new entrants comes from new state authorisations and licences rather than capital, which matters because new gaming near the company's venues could take revenue. Substitutes such as sports wagering and other entertainment compete for the same spend, buyers are individual customers whose discretionary spend can shift, and suppliers matter in two places: horsemen's groups negotiated annually and machine manufacturers that could limit popular games or require participation leases, which can raise costs or reduce machine yield.

### SWOT analysis

Strengths are the Derby asset, free cash flow of USD 529.9 million over the last twelve months, scale across three segments and extended maturities. Weaknesses are machine legal concentration, discretionary buybacks, the 2028 notes and springing maturity, and an Adjusted EBITDA that includes affiliate earnings. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are the use of any sale proceeds to repay debt, Rockingham Grand Casino opening in mid-2027, and further revolver repayment. Threats are an adverse ruling or tax change on machines, resumed buybacks funded by the revolver and a failed refinancing of the 2028 notes.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over 12 to 24 months the tailwinds are Rockingham Grand Casino opening in mid-2027, the Thoroughbred Championship Series from 2027, extended maturities and the ability to repay the revolver from free cash flow; the headwinds are Virginia tax and competition, the 2028 notes refinancing window by about 2027-10-16, a dividend of about USD 32.15 million payable 2027-01-05 and the repurchase authority. Over three to five years the sector headwind is the legal durability of machine revenue and the tailwind is the completion of the first-turn building by the 2028 Derby, so the outlook for leverage is stable to improving if buybacks stay restrained.

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a ruling or tax increase that stops or taxes machines in Kentucky or Virginia, as happened in Louisiana. The first operational break is loss or relocation of machines, which cuts Live and Historical Racing revenue and Adjusted EBITDA, and the financial transmission is lower free cash flow while project capital and the dividend continue; each USD 100 million of lost EBITDA lifts net leverage from 3.94x to 4.31x. Liquidity of USD 678 million pro forma and scheduled amortization of USD 60 million a year mean liquidity distress is unlikely within 12 months, but leverage of about 4.3x would complicate the 2027 refinancing of the 2028 notes ahead of the springing maturity, so management levers are the pause in buybacks, the cut in project capital and asset sales, and the observable triggers are the machine revenue share, the Adjusted EBITDA margin and the revolver draw.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C4; calculation C6; calculation C7; calculation C8

### Overall credit view

Overall, Churchill Downs presents an adequate business profile supported by the Derby asset, scale and free cash flow, but credit quality is constrained by machine legal concentration, discretionary shareholder returns and an unsettled perimeter. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow covers 11.22% of debt, refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the 2028 notes being refinanced before about 2027-10-16 and on the revolver absorbing the redemption, and the primary downside path is a machine ruling or tax change. Further analysis would require the credit agreement, the indentures, the proxy statement and the sale terms. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: legal or tax change on machine revenue.
