## Findings

### Bottom line

Churchill Downs is a cash-generative, seasonal racing and casino operator whose leverage has fallen to 4.08x reported EBITDA, which supports creditors, but the bank facilities' springing maturity makes the notes due in January 2028 the key refinancing test.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_chdn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chdn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 16; calculation C01; calculation C06

Free cash flow after capital expenditure was USD 484 million in nine months 2026, against shareholder payouts of USD 75 million, but payouts took 92.67% of free cash flow in FY2025, so the view depends on continued restraint. Covenant headroom, legal priority and the post-redemption revolver balance are not established, and quarter-end balances are not representative because the Kentucky Derby falls in the second quarter.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_chdn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chdn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 28; calculation C03; calculation C04

### Key findings

Business quality is adequate and event-concentrated: Live and Historical Racing earns the largest share of segment profit, and the second quarter produced 37.42% of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA, so one flagship event and historical racing machine rules in Kentucky and Virginia carry a large part of the debt service capacity. The 16 historical racing venues, ten owned casinos and online wagering give geographic spread that single-asset operators lack.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_chdn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 125–126; EX-99.1_2026-07-29_ex991pressrelease07-29x26.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation C04

Cash flow conversion is solid: last-twelve-months free cash flow of USD 529.9 million is 45.79% of reported EBITDA, with capital expenditure well below the FY2025 level, but conversion in 2026 benefits from lower project capital and the planned Rockingham and First Turn projects will absorb cash again.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C02

Leverage is improving but still material: total debt is 4.08x and net debt 3.94x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA at 2026-09-30, down from 4.83x at 2025-12-31, and cash interest cover is 4.22x against 3.61x. On the issuer's Adjusted EBITDA, which adds back its share of equity investees' EBITDA, total leverage is 3.79x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01

Liquidity is adequate: unrestricted cash of USD 167 million and about USD 1,111 million of undrawn revolver capacity give 1.83x cover of the USD 700 million notes due in January 2028, but the October 2026 redemption of the 2027 notes was funded from the revolver, so the post-redemption position is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_chdn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chdn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 28; calculation C05

Refinancing depends on the 2028 notes: the revolver and term loan A, USD 1,148 million at 2026-09-30, mature in September 2031 only if notes maturing earlier are refinanced at least 91 days before maturity, a test that falls on 2027-10-16 for the 2028 notes; the longest-dated debt is the USD 500 million term loan B due 2033. No large note maturity falls before January 2028 after the 2027 redemption.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_chdn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chdn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 16; calculation C06

Financial policy is the main judgement: dividends are small, but FY2025 buybacks and dividends took 92.67% of free cash flow with revolver borrowings rising, while payouts were 15.50% in nine months 2026. Sponsor and governance risk is medium, with no sponsor, no documented creditor-adverse action and legal capacity not testable.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C03

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against a constructive view is that leverage of 4.08x reported EBITDA is still material for a business whose earnings fall in one quarter, that Adjusted EBITDA flatters leverage by including the share of equity investees' EBITDA whose cash reaches the company only as distributions, and that the interest rate exposure rises after the revolver-funded redemption of the 2027 notes. A further weakness is that covenant ratios for 2026 and the definition of the quoted net bank leverage of 3.8x are not printed. The view would weaken if buybacks under the new USD 750 million authorization are funded with revolver draws, if the 2028 notes are not refinanced before the test date, if Virginia historical racing earnings keep falling after the new tax, or if the post-redemption revolver balance shows liquidity well below the USD 1,278 million seen at 2026-09-30. It would strengthen if the next quarterly report shows revolver repayment continuing and covenant ratios with wide headroom.

### Company description

Churchill Downs operates the Kentucky Derby venue, 16 historical racing venues in Kentucky and Virginia, online horse wagering and wagering technology, and ten wholly owned and two equity-investee casinos. Revenue is largely transactional and discretionary, tied to attendance, wagering and gaming volume, so cash flow visibility is lower than for contracted businesses, but it is regulated, licensed and spread across many venues, which supports debt service in ordinary conditions and refinancing confidence.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_chdn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 6–14

### Ownership and group structure

The company is publicly listed with about 1,940 holders of record and no sponsor or controlling owner identified, so there is no sponsor equity cure and no sponsor-driven recapitalisation in the documents, and creditors depend on board and management financial policy. Casino Salem is 90% owned, two casinos are equity-accounted investees, and the subsidiary guarantors and collateral terms are not printed, so structurally senior claims and restricted group boundaries are not established.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_chdn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-K_2025-12-31_chdn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 14

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue growth is slowing: net revenue rose 7.01% in FY2025 and 3.50% in nine months 2026, with volume from new venues in Kentucky and Virginia offset by competition and tax in Central Virginia. Pricing power is limited because wagering is a pass-through of pari-mutuel commissions and gaming taxes are set by states, so revenue is cyclical, regulated and exposed to substitution from other gaming and betting, and growth depends on new venues rather than price.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-10-28_ex991pressrelease10-28x26.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2025-12-31_chdn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 12–14; calculation C08

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins have been stable: Adjusted EBITDA margin was 42.39% in FY2024, 41.19% in FY2025 and 42.67% in nine months 2026 against 42.39% a year earlier. Pari-mutuel taxes and purses of USD 349.4 million against Live and Historical Racing segment revenue of USD 1,442.4 million and gaming taxes are variable with volume, which protects margin in a downturn, while salaries, maintenance and venue costs are fixed and create operating leverage when attendance falls.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_chdn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 125–126; calculation C08

### Capital intensity and cash flow conversion

EBITDA converts to free cash flow at 45.79% on a last-twelve-months basis, helped by distributions from investees and by project capital falling to USD 119 million in nine months 2026 from USD 172 million. Capital expenditure is largely discretionary until committed, but the Rockingham investment of USD 180 million to USD 200 million and the First Turn building of USD 280 million to USD 300 million are committed, and deferred revenue from advance Derby receipts unwinds through the year, so cash conversion is seasonal.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_chdn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2025-12-31_chdn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 7; EX-99.1_2026-04-22_ex991pressrelease04-22x26.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation C02

### Market forces

Competition from other casinos, sports betting and other states' historical racing venues is the main force, buyer power is low because customers are individual patrons, and licensing and tax barriers limit new entrants but are set by states and can change. Macro, regulatory and tax factors are credit drivers only through gaming and pari-mutuel regulation, which the monitoring triggers cover; other macro and technology factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver on the provided materials.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_chdn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15; 10-K_2025-12-31_chdn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 22–23; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_ex991pressrelease10-28x26.htm.jsonl, page 2

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months tailwinds are the full-year effect of new Kentucky and Virginia venues, lower interest cost from debt repayment and extended bank maturities; headwinds are the Virginia tax and competition effect, the revolver-funded note redemption, committed project capital and the January 2028 notes. Over three to five years the opening of Rockingham and the First Turn building add earnings capacity only if funded without lifting leverage, and the sector remains exposed to state policy.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.1_2026-10-28_ex991pressrelease10-28x26.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chdn-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14–16

### Downside scenario

A supported downside begins with a poor Kentucky Derby week or a regulatory or tax change in Kentucky or Virginia, which would first cut second-quarter Adjusted EBITDA, the quarter that supplies 37.42% of the year. Lower earnings would raise leverage from 4.08x and reduce free cash flow, and with the revolver already used for the 2027 redemption and a buyback authorization of USD 750 million, management levers are to cut buybacks and project capital, which are discretionary. Liquidity of USD 1,278 million at 2026-09-30 and no bank maturity before 2031 mean resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress unless the 2028 notes are not refinanced by the springing test date, when about USD 1,148 million of bank debt could be pulled forward.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_chdn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_chdn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 28; calculation C04; calculation C05; calculation C06

### Overall credit view

Overall, Churchill Downs presents an adequate business profile supported by regional diversification, regulated venues and a flagship event, but credit quality is constrained by event and regulatory concentration, committed project capital and shareholder-return capacity. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow is 45.79% of reported EBITDA, and refinancing capacity is likely to depend on cash flow, revolver availability and the refinancing of the USD 700 million notes due in January 2028. The primary downside path is a weak Kentucky Derby quarter or a Virginia or Kentucky rule change that lifts leverage while buybacks and projects continue. Further analysis would require the post-redemption revolver balance, 2026 covenant ratios and the executed financing documents. Top risk: refinancing the January 2028 notes before the bank facilities' springing maturity test.
