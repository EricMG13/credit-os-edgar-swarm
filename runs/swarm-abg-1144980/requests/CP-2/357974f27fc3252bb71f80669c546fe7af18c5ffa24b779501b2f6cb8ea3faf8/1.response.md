## Findings

### Bottom line

Asbury's franchise network and service-led profits support a fundamentally sound credit, but net debt of 3.92x EBITDA and falling interest cover leave noteholders with a thinner cushion until earnings stabilise.

The strongest support is a profit base in which parts and service earned 47.9% of FY2025 gross profit, owned real estate and company-defined liquidity of USD 1,031.9 million. The main constraints are twelve-month EBITDA of USD 838.6 million, interest cover of 2.93x, a USD 401.7 million real estate loan due in December 2026 whose refinancing is not yet documented, and covenant levels that no filing prints. The primary downside path runs from weaker new vehicle margins through a cost base that does not flex to higher leverage and thinner cover.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 55–56; calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C6

### Key findings

The business profile is a strength: scale, brand and regional breadth, and a profit mix tilted to service. Parts and service earned 47.9% of FY2025 gross profit and finance and insurance 23.4%, while new vehicles earned 20.2% on 52.8% of revenue, so earnings are steadier than vehicle revenue implies, and 83.0% of dealership locations were owned at the end of 2025.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C6

Earnings are the weak point. Twelve-month EBITDA of USD 838.6 million is USD 104.4 million below the FY2025 figure of USD 943.0 million, because selling, general and administrative expense rose to 68.6% of gross profit from 64.0% while new vehicle gross profit per unit fell. The cost base follows commissions down but not fast enough to protect the margin.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 45, 54; calculation C2; calculation C9

Leverage is elevated against the company's own target and its own reported ratio. Net debt to EBITDA was 3.92x and total debt to EBITDA 4.02x for the twelve months to September 2026, against a company target of 2.5x to 3.5x on its own definition and a reported 3.2x at the end of 2025; treating floor plan interest as a cost lifts net leverage to 4.38x. Debt itself fell, so the deterioration is an earnings problem, not a borrowing problem.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_abg_abg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 14; calculation C1; calculation C7

Coverage and cash generation are adequate but narrowing. Interest cover on total cash interest paid was 2.93x against 3.43x at the end of 2025, and nine-month free cash flow after capital expenditure of USD 248.0 million was USD 369.7 million against USD 518.9 million a year earlier, with share repurchases of USD 277.6 million and dealership sale proceeds of USD 397.2 million the largest other items.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C3; calculation C8

Liquidity is adequate and refinancing capacity depends on secured real estate lenders and bank facilities. The company reports total available liquidity of USD 1.03 billion, which its components add to USD 1,031.9 million, of which USD 160.6 million is floor plan offset cash; counted on a stricter basis, cash and committed facilities are USD 905.9 million. The December 2026 real estate facility of USD 401.7 million is the nearest large maturity, and the 2028 to 2030 maturities are USD 610.2 million, USD 837.7 million and USD 482.9 million.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 55–56; calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C13

Financial policy is mixed rather than conservative. There is no dividend, no sponsor and buybacks paused in the third quarter, but the authorization was raised to USD 500.0 million in February 2026 and the debt-funded Herb Chambers purchase added secured and floor plan borrowings; the reporting weakness at the end of 2024 was remediated while the acquired stores were excluded from the 2025 controls assessment.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9; calculation C8

Structure favors secured lenders on the properties they financed and leaves noteholders exposed to value outside them. The real estate term loans, USD 1,129.4 million in principal with the mortgage notes, carry first-priority liens, while the four senior notes are unsecured and guaranteed by every restricted subsidiary except the insurance and F&I entities.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C10

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a better view is that the company's own measure is within target, that parts and service gross profit is still rising, that no repurchases were made in the third quarter, that company-defined liquidity improved from USD 927.1 million at the end of 2025 to USD 1,031.9 million, and that net debt fell by USD 246.5 million over nine months. Twelve-month EBITDA before impairments of USD 957.8 million would put net leverage at 3.43x, inside the target range. The view would improve if selling, general and administrative expense moved back toward 64.7% of gross profit, new vehicle gross profit per unit stabilised, the December 2026 loan were refinanced on committed terms and the credit agreement showed ample headroom. It would worsen if a further EBITDA decline of the size already seen, USD 104.4 million, took net leverage to 4.47x and cover to 2.56x, if buybacks resumed with borrowings, or if a refinancing slipped past December.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 54, 56; calculation C2; calculation C7; calculation C11

### Strengths and weaknesses

The first strength is a service-led profit base that has held up while vehicle sales weakened: parts and service gross profit rose 2% in the nine months to September 2026 while new vehicle gross profit fell 15%, so the largest profit pool is the one that is growing, which supports cash flow visibility and debt service.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 45; calculation C6; calculation C9

The second strength is breadth: 34 brands in 14 states and no single manufacturer above 30% of new vehicle revenue spread franchise, regional and model-cycle risk, which limits the chance that one supplier decision removes a large share of earnings.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_abg_abg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 32

The third strength is asset backing: 83.0% of dealership locations are owned and the real estate term loans and mortgage notes total USD 1,129.4 million, so there is tangible collateral behind secured debt and a funding source for stores that are sold or relocated.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C6; calculation C10

The fourth strength is company-defined liquidity of USD 1,031.9 million with no revolver drawn, which covers the nearest large maturity of USD 401.7 million more than twice over on the company's own total and on a stricter basis.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 55; calculation C4; calculation C12

The fifth strength is a plain financial policy: no sponsor, no dividend since 2008 and a pause in buybacks in the third quarter, which keeps distributions within management's discretion and out of any lender-adverse structure.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9

The first weakness is falling earnings with a sticky cost base: selling, general and administrative expense rose to 68.6% of gross profit from 64.0% and income from operations fell 16% to USD 620.0 million, so each further point of gross profit lost reaches EBITDA almost in full.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 45, 54; calculation C9

The second weakness is leverage above where the company's own target sits: net debt was 3.92x twelve-month EBITDA, 4.38x if floor plan interest is a cost, against a target of 2.5x to 3.5x and a reported 3.2x at the end of 2025, which uses most of the room that the target leaves for a shock.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_abg_abg-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 14; calculation C1; calculation C7

The third weakness is refinancing concentration: USD 401.7 million falls due in December 2026 with refinancing expected but undocumented, and USD 610.2 million, USD 837.7 million and USD 482.9 million fall due in 2028 to 2030, so lender appetite for secured real estate and senior notes in those years determines whether debt can be rolled.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 56; calculation C5

The fourth weakness is opacity of lender protections: no covenant level, definition or compliance figure is printed and no executed agreement is supplied, so headroom against a minimum fixed charge coverage test and a maximum lease-adjusted leverage test cannot be measured.
Basis: Source readiness (CP-0); 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 56

The fifth weakness is distribution and acquisition appetite combined with integration risk: buybacks of USD 277.6 million in nine months 2026 followed a USD 1,761.8 million debt-funded acquisition whose stores were outside the 2025 controls assessment, so policy can move faster than controls and earnings.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C8

### Company and ownership

Asbury is a franchised retailer of new and used vehicles with parts and service and finance and insurance, operated through wholly owned subsidiaries under a public holding company with no sponsor or controlling holder identified in the supplied filings. Cash flow visibility comes from repeat service work and manufacturer-backed warranty and recall repairs, while the main structural dependence is on manufacturer relationships: Toyota and Lexus supplied 30% of FY2025 new vehicle revenue, and dealer agreements give manufacturers consent and termination rights on control changes, which limits recapitalisation risk but exposes the franchises to any large holder.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_abg_abg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18, 32

### Revenue, cost and cash conversion

Revenue depends on new vehicle volume, which is cyclical and discretionary, and on price and mix: Q3 2026 new vehicle units fell 9% and the seasonally adjusted annual sales rate eased to about 16.4 million from 16.6 million, with affordability and higher fuel prices cited. Parts and service revenue is recurring and supported by an average vehicle age of 13.0 years, and finance and insurance is tied to retail units while TCA defers commission revenue over contract terms, which depresses reported finance and insurance revenue during the rollout.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 36, 38, 41–43

Costs are mostly variable through commissions but not fully: personnel costs fell with gross profit while rent and system costs did not, so selling, general and administrative expense reached 68.6% of gross profit. Free cash flow after capital expenditure was USD 369.7 million in the nine months, against USD 518.9 million, and 2026 capital expenditure guidance of USD 250.0 million to USD 275.0 million leaves some flexibility if earnings fall further.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 54, 60; calculation C3; calculation C9

### Competitive forces and external factors

Rivalry among franchised dealers is intense but inventory comes on equal terms, so margin is set by volume and mix; direct sales by some electric vehicle makers circumvent state franchise laws and are a substitution threat to the new vehicle line only. Buyers can compare prices easily, which caps new vehicle margins, and manufacturers hold the strongest supplier power through allocation, incentives and floor plan assistance of USD 79.4 million for nine months 2026, which the company says it cannot assume will continue.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_abg_abg-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 62

Tariffs, higher fuel prices tied to 2026 conflicts, the expiry of federal electric vehicle incentives on September 30, 2025 and interest rates matter to credit: USD 1.94 billion of variable-rate debt including floor plan means a 100 basis point change moves annual interest by USD 19.4 million, partly hedged by swaps. Credit-supportive factors are scale, a service-led mix and owned property; credit-constraining factors are margin compression, leverage above target and refinancing concentration; improvement would come from stabilised new vehicle margins, and weakening from tariff-driven price increases that cut demand.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21–22, 33–34, 38, 62

### Outlook

Over twelve to 24 months new vehicle margins and volumes are the swing factor, with parts and service growth and the unwinding of TCA commission deferral as partial offsets: deferred revenue of USD 891.3 million will be recognised over contract lives, which supports later finance and insurance revenue while depressing it now. The medium-term outlook beyond three years is not established because the filings give no projections beyond capital expenditure and the tax rate, and the sector commentary is qualitative.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 42, 55, 60

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a further softening of new vehicle demand and margins from higher prices and weaker affordability, as in the third quarter of 2026 when new vehicle gross profit fell 20%. The first operational break is gross profit per new vehicle; because personnel, rent and system costs do not fall in step, income from operations and EBITDA drop faster than revenue. If EBITDA fell again by the USD 104.4 million already lost since year-end with net debt unchanged, net leverage would rise from 3.92x to 4.47x and interest cover would fall from 2.93x to 2.56x. Management levers are to keep buybacks paused against USD 322.4 million of remaining authorization, trim capital expenditure from the USD 250.0 million to USD 275.0 million plan, and sell further stores as it did for USD 397.2 million in nine months 2026. Company-defined liquidity of USD 1,031.9 million is 2.57 times the December 2026 maturity of USD 401.7 million, so a liquidity crisis does not appear likely within twelve months, but covenant headroom at the higher leverage cannot be established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_abg_abg-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9, 36, 55–56, 60; calculation C2; calculation C11; calculation C12

### Overall credit view

Overall, Asbury presents an adequate business profile supported by a service-led profit mix, brand and regional breadth and owned property, but credit quality is constrained by falling earnings, net leverage of 3.92x and a nearest maturity of USD 401.7 million that is not yet refinanced. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because parts and service and finance and insurance supply most gross profit, although free cash flow is lower than a year ago. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the willingness of real estate lenders and the bank group to renew secured and floor plan lines, and on noteholders in 2028 to 2030. The primary downside path is margin compression in new vehicles feeding through a fixed cost base into leverage and cover. Further analysis would require the credit agreement and covenant levels, the company's leverage computation for September 2026, ratings and market prices, and any financing completed after October 30, 2026. Completed. Top risk: earnings decline on a largely fixed cost base while refinancing is pending.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C1; calculation C5; calculation C6
