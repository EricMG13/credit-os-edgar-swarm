## Findings

### Bottom line

Unisys is a fragile credit that is liquid for now: nothing matures before January 2031 and liquidity is USD 353.3 million, but interest, pension funding and capital spending exceed earnings, so creditors rely on the ClearPath renewal season. Leverage is 4.03x total and 2.57x net on last-twelve-months EBITDA of USD 181.7 million (2.61x net pro forma for the Brazil payment in the fourth quarter of 2026), and cover is 2.19x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_uis-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_uis-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C4

The coupon on the 2031 notes and 2026 pension contributions absorb 94.23% of that EBITDA, or 74.31% before a non-cash impairment, ahead of capital spending. Profit is concentrated in the enterprise computing segment, which earned 61.17% of FY2025 segment gross profit, while digital workplace gross margin fell to 11.17%. A strong fourth-quarter renewal season would improve the view and a further ClearPath decline would weaken it.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a fragile view is that the cash gap rests partly on a non-cash impairment and on a pension schedule that eases: before the impairment, EBITDA is USD 230.4 million, leverage is 3.17x total and 2.03x net, and the gap narrows to USD 25.61 million, while contributions average about USD 80.33 million a year over 2028 to 2030 against about USD 97 million in 2026. Demand indicators are also improving, with backlog of USD 2.92 billion at 2026-09-30 above USD 2.83 billion a year earlier, nine-month total contract value up 21.93% and third-quarter revenue up 2.46%.

The view would improve if the fourth-quarter renewal season lifted last-twelve-months EBITDA toward the pre-impairment USD 230.4 million, which would bring total leverage to 3.17x and the cash gap to USD 25.61 million. It would weaken if the nine-month declines in ClearPath revenue and digital workplace gross profit were repeated, which would cut EBITDA by about USD 42.46 million to USD 139.24 million, lift total leverage to 5.25x and use up liquidity in about 3.03 years, before the 2031 maturity. A US pension contribution requirement above USD 100.0 million, or ABL availability falling toward the USD 12.5 million test level, would also change the view.

### Limitations

- No filing states an adjusted or covenant EBITDA, so every leverage and cover multiple here is built from operating income and depreciation and amortization; on the company's own definition each could be higher or lower, and the conclusion that cash cover is thin rests on that choice.
- The ABL borrowing base and fixed charge coverage level are not printed, and pension estimates for 2027 onward are global rather than the US-only measure that triggers the ABL springing maturity, so how close liquidity is to a facility trigger is not established and could shorten the runway.
- No discrete fourth-quarter statements, peer set, agency commentary or market prices are supplied, so the dependence on the renewal season rests on subtraction of nine-month figures and sector standing and market access rest on the filings alone.
- The downside scenario applies observed nine-month rates to a gross margin proxy, so its EBITDA, leverage and runway figures show direction and scale, not a forecast.

### Company and business model

Unisys is an IT services company whose volume comes from labour-based managed services and whose profit comes disproportionately from one proprietary software franchise. Services were 82.61% of FY2025 revenue of USD 1,950.1 million, spread across digital workplace (26.07%), cloud, applications and infrastructure (37.58%) and enterprise computing (32.25%), and the enterprise computing segment sells ClearPath licences and support to an installed base concentrated in financial services, travel and transportation and healthcare. Multi-year contracts and licence renewals give cash-flow visibility, with backlog of USD 3.16 billion at 2025-12-31, and no client exceeds 10% of revenue; the cost is that services are exposed to price competition, so debt-service capacity depends on holding the ClearPath base while services margins recover.

### Ownership and group structure

Unisys is publicly held with no controlling shareholder or financial sponsor identified, so no owner is positioned to extract cash or to supply equity if liquidity tightens, and the equity cushion is thin: the total deficit was USD 408.3 million at 2026-09-30 and the market value held by non-affiliates was USD 300.4 million at 2025-06-30. Debt sits at the parent with three wholly owned US guarantors; liabilities of other subsidiaries, including pension plans and trade payables, rank ahead of the notes in those subsidiaries, and the 51% owned United Kingdom joint venture shares its profit with three financial institutions. Federal loss carryforwards of USD 1.82 billion carry a full valuation allowance and an ownership change would limit them. Directors, independence and related-party dealings sit in a proxy statement that is not supplied, so governance beyond an independent audit committee is not established.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is predictable at contract level but structurally flat to declining, and pricing power is limited outside ClearPath. Nine-month total contract value rose 21.93% and new business rose 61%, but technology solutions and services renewals fell 5%, and the company states that competitors offer more aggressive pricing and that AI has reduced and replaced some historical services, leading clients to delay spending. The mechanism is that services renewals face price pressure from competitors and from automation savings that clients capture, while ClearPath licence revenue is recognised in full when a term begins, so quarterly revenue swings with renewal timing; the implication is that revenue visibility supports cash flow only if renewals are won at stable prices, and ClearPath renewal contract value, up 33% over nine months, is the offsetting support. Revenue fell 2.90% in FY2025 and rose 0.51% in the nine months of 2026, with currency adding 2 percentage points to third-quarter growth.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins are hard to defend under volume pressure because overhead absorbs most of gross profit and cost cuts need cash first. Gross margin was 28.17% in FY2025 and 25.73% in the nine months of 2026, and selling, general and administrative expense took 79.34% of nine-month gross profit, so a small revenue shortfall moves earnings sharply. Digital workplace cost of revenue rose 7.87% in the nine months against revenue growth of 2.17%, which shows adverse operating leverage in the weakest segment, and the workforce is labour-based with 28.00% of staff in India, which lowers cost but adds currency exposure. Net cost-reduction charges were USD 30.5 million in FY2025 and the remaining workforce liability was USD 14.1 million at 2026-09-30, so savings are a lever but they are paid for up front and are not quantified in the filings.

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

EBITDA does not convert into free cash flow available for debt service. Capital expenditure and capitalised software absorbed USD 84.8 million over the last twelve months, 4.33% of revenue, which leaves EBITDA less capital expenditure of USD 96.9 million against cash interest of USD 82.9 million; after pension contributions, cash taxes and working capital, free cash flow was an outflow of USD 46.4 million. A fall in deferred revenue of USD 75.6 million since 2025-12-31 removed a non-debt funding float that clients had prepaid, which adds to the drain, and finance leases of USD 35.6 million are inside debt while operating leases are outside it. Capital expenditure is partly discretionary and is the main internal lever, but cutting it would also cut the software that supports the ClearPath renewals.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is the force that matters most to credit: the company names systems integrators, consultants, outsourcers and hardware and software providers as competitors, some with greater resources and more aggressive pricing, and digital workplace gross margin fell from 15.86% to 11.17% between the first nine months of 2025 and 2026, which links rivalry directly to earnings. Substitutes are a second force, because AI and automation have reduced and replaced some historical services and an accelerated shift of ClearPath to subscription would delay cash receipts. Buyers are large enterprises and governments with budget power, softened by the absence of any client above 10% of revenue; new entrants are less of a threat to the ClearPath installed base because of switching costs, and suppliers matter through scarce skilled labour and a limited set of component suppliers.

### Macro, regulatory and technology factors

Three categories change credit outcomes. Public-sector budgets and political decisions can reduce, delay or terminate government contracts, and the company reports that this has hurt results before; currency matters because 59.34% of FY2025 revenue was earned outside the United States and about one quarter of the USD 187.1 million of foreign cash may not be readily transferable; and interest rates and discount rates drive the pension contributions that compete with debt service. Technology change, led by AI, is both an opportunity for new managed services and a substitute for existing ones. Brazil tax matters of about USD 27 million remain unreserved and are small against the other factors.

### Strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats in credit terms

Strengths are the absence of any maturity before 2031, a high-margin installed-base franchise, contract backlog and a diversified client list, and the absence of owner-driven leakage. Weaknesses are fixed claims that absorb 94.23% of EBITDA, a digital workplace business with falling profit, earnings that depend on one fourth-quarter renewal season, and a pension obligation that competes for cash. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are the completion of the pension settlement programme in the first half of 2027, which should reduce required contributions, growth in ClearPath renewals and managed services for AI workloads, and cost savings from restructuring. Threats that could weaken it are AI-driven substitution of services, competitor pricing, a shift of ClearPath to subscription, and a single refinancing window in 2030 and 2031 at a time when the pension schedule is still heavy.

### Financial profile and credit quality

Across nine dimensions the profile is mostly average with weakness where cash is concerned. Scale and market position are average: revenue of USD 1,957.1 million over the last twelve months is modest against competitors that the company says have greater resources. Competitive advantage is average, strong in the ClearPath installed base and weak in digital workplace. Business diversification is average, with three segments, no client above 10% and 47.59% of FY2025 revenue earned outside the United States and the United Kingdom, but profit is concentrated. Cost and capex flexibility is average, since capital expenditure is 4.33% of revenue but overhead absorbs 79.34% of gross profit. Margin stability is weak, with EBITDA margin of 8.91% in FY2025 and 9.28% on the last twelve months, down from 10.15% in FY2024. Free cash flow stability is weak, with an outflow of USD 46.4 million. Ability to refinance and access capital markets is average, shown by the June 2025 refinancing but at a higher coupon and with ratings of B and B2. Liquidity position is average, with USD 353.3 million and no maturity before 2031. Financial policy and governance are average, with no sponsor, unqualified audit opinions and a debt-funded pension contribution. The main supports are the maturity profile and the ClearPath franchise; the main constraints are cash conversion and the pension; the trend is stable revenue with weaker cash, and the missing datapoints are covenant levels, the borrowing base and an issuer-defined EBITDA.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months earnings depend on the fourth-quarter 2026 renewal season, while cash depends on interest and pensions: the coupon is fixed at about USD 74.21 million a year and pension contributions are about USD 97 million in 2026 and about USD 101 million in 2027, so the cash gap stays negative on last-twelve-months earnings and liquidity funds it. Tailwinds are rising contract value, backlog above the prior year, an improving cloud segment margin of 22.48%, a joint venture margin benefit of about USD 12 million in 2026 and cost actions; headwinds are the ClearPath nine-month decline, falling digital workplace profit, AI substitution, competitor pricing and the loss of the joint venture benefit if the transaction does not recur. Over three to five years the pension settlement programme should lower obligations, contributions of about USD 241 million over 2028 to 2030 are lighter than 2026, and the decisive event is refinancing the ABL in June 2030 and the notes in January 2031, which is easier if EBITDA has recovered. Management gives no numeric revenue or earnings guidance in the filings, so this outlook is the analyst's reading of the disclosed schedules.

### Downside scenario

The scenario that matters is a further decline in ClearPath renewals and digital workplace profit, not a generic recession. The first operational break would be a weak fourth-quarter renewal season, because enterprise computing supplied an implied 74.75% of fourth-quarter 2025 segment gross profit. If the observed nine-month declines were repeated, with ClearPath revenue down 9.14% at the enterprise computing gross margin and digital workplace gross profit down 28.05%, EBITDA would fall by about USD 42.46 million to USD 139.24 million, total leverage would rise to 5.25x, cover would fall to 1.68x and the fixed-claims cash gap would widen to USD 116.77 million a year. Liquidity of USD 353.3 million would then last about 3.03 years, shorter than the 4.16 years to the notes' maturity, and a smaller receivables base would also reduce ABL availability and move it toward the USD 12.5 million level at which the fixed charge test applies. Management levers are capital expenditure, restructuring and the pension settlement schedule, but none of them closes a gap of that size without damaging the ClearPath and services base; resilience appears sufficient to avoid a liquidity crisis for about three years but not to refinance the notes from internal cash, and the ratio of the stress to the cushion is an illustration, not a forecast.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_uis-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 72–73; calculation C8; calculation C10; calculation C13; calculation C17

### Overall credit view

Overall, Unisys presents an adequate business profile supported by an installed-base software franchise, contract backlog and the absence of any maturity before 2031, but credit quality is constrained by fixed claims that absorb 94.23% of EBITDA (74.31% before a non-cash impairment), falling digital workplace profit and a heavy pension schedule. Cash-flow durability appears weak because free cash flow was an outflow of USD 46.4 million. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on EBITDA recovering before the 2030 and 2031 window, and on the fourth-quarter renewal season in between. The primary downside path is a weak ClearPath renewal season combined with continued digital workplace erosion, which raises leverage to 5.25x and uses liquidity before the notes mature. Further analysis would require the issuer's own EBITDA definition, covenant levels, the borrowing base, the US share of future pension contributions and the proxy statement. Top risk: fixed interest and pension claims that exceed earnings in a business whose profit depends on a single renewal season.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Credit strengths are four. No debt matures before January 2031 except USD 13.3 million, and liquidity of USD 353.3 million would fund the current cash gap for about 4.75 years, which lowers near-term default risk. The ClearPath installed base earns 61.17% of segment gross profit and its renewal contract value rose 33% over nine months, which supports EBITDA and the case for refinancing.
