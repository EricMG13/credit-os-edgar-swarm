## Findings

### Bottom line

Sonic is an adequate but weakening credit: earnings are slipping on new vehicle margins and overhead while debt, shareholder payouts and 2027 repayments rise, and covenant headroom cannot be seen.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C1; calculation C4; calculation C7

Adjusted EBITDA fell to USD 589.5 million over the last twelve months from USD 614.6 million in FY2025 while total debt rose to USD 1,749.7 million, lifting adjusted leverage to 2.97x from 2.63x. Fixed operations and finance and insurance supply 78.00% of gross profit and cushion the decline, but USD 561.2 million of debt falls due by the end of 2027 against USD 395.2 million of cash and undrawn facilities.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C4; calculation C6; calculation C13

### Key findings

Earnings are falling where it matters most, and the cause is overhead as much as volume. The Franchised Dealerships Segment earned USD 189.7 million before tax and impairment in the first nine months of 2026 against USD 244.4 million, a fall of 22.38%, and total segment income fell 18.29% to USD 223.3 million. In that segment gross profit rose USD 22.7 million while selling, general and administrative expenses rose USD 76.8 million, and across the group those expenses took 73.04% of gross profit against 69.75%.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C3; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 26, 73–76

Leverage on adjusted earnings has risen even though reported leverage looks better. Total debt of USD 1,749.7 million is 2.97x last-twelve-month adjusted EBITDA against 2.63x at 2025-12-31, while on reported EBITDA it is 3.06x against 3.62x, a fall that comes only from the 2025 impairment leaving the base. Adjusted EBITDA covers cash interest 2.96x against 3.24x. These ratios use carrying-value debt and leave out floor plan notes of USD 2,288.0 million and leases.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C4; calculation C14

Cash flow does not cover growth and payouts. On the issuer's alternative basis, in which all floor plan changes are operating, operating cash flow of USD 424.2 million less investing outflows of USD 329.7 million left USD 94.5 million, against USD 180.2 million of dividends and repurchases, so the USD 85.7 million gap was met by net new borrowing of USD 90.1 million. As printed, operating cash flow was USD -14.5 million because inventories rose.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C7; calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 92

Liquidity is thinner and 2027 is the refinancing year. Issuer-defined liquidity fell USD 116.4 million to USD 585.2 million, USD 110.0 million of it from the floor plan deposit, and cash plus committed undrawn facilities of USD 395.2 million cover the USD 561.2 million of bridge and mortgage debt due by the end of 2027 only 0.70x. The USD 1,150.0 million of senior notes do not mature until 2029 and 2031, but the issuer's market quotations value the 2031 notes at 91.50% of par and the 2029 notes at 94.51%, down from 95.76% and 97.00% at 2025-12-31, a move the filings do not split between rates and credit.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C6; calculation C11; calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21, 24, 87

Interest cost depends on short-term rates with little protection. Variable-rate debt is USD 2.7 billion, a 1.0% rise in rates would add USD 20.4 million of interest over nine months, equal to 4.63% of nine-month adjusted EBITDA, and the interest rate caps on USD 1,000.0 million of notional pay only above 5.50% against one-month SOFR of 3.91%, with USD 400.0 million of them expiring on 2026-12-31. Floor plan rates have fallen, from a weighted average of 5.66% to 4.99% over nine months, which helps, although floor plan interest expense still rose USD 2.5 million to USD 64.7 million on larger balances.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C10; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 22, 88, 96

Inventory is building into a softer new vehicle market. New vehicle inventories rose USD 269.3 million, or 23.62%, days supply is 65 days against 51 days a year earlier, same-store retail new vehicle unit sales fell 6% and gross profit per new vehicle fell USD 226 to USD 2,957. Management estimates 2026 new vehicle industry volume at 1% to 3% below the 2025 level, so volume is not expected to recover this year.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C14; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 32–33

Two stabilisers keep the credit adequate. Fixed operations and finance and insurance produced 78.00% of gross profit in nine months 2026 from 18.98% of revenue, with a fixed operations gross margin of 51.2%; and management has levers, because 73.71% of capital expenditure was construction and real estate projects, no shares were repurchased in the third quarter and land and buildings at cost of USD 2,144.3 million can be mortgaged or sold.
Basis: calculation C9; calculation C13; calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 34, 87, 89–90

Financial policy is the weakest part of the profile. One family controls 86.00% of the votes, dividends plus repurchases were 113.05% of nine-month 2026 net income, and the issuer's measure of earnings free of distribution restrictions fell USD 109.1 million to USD 282.0 million in the first quarter and has not been printed since; covenant ratio values were last printed for 2024-12-31. Business & governance rates the sponsor and governance risk as medium.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C13; 10-Q_2026-04-30_sah.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-K_2024-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, page 107

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case that credit is firmer than it looks is that reported leverage fell to 3.06x from 3.62x, adjusted leverage of 2.97x remains under three times EBITDA, recurring service and finance income supplies most gross profit, and management can stop repurchases, defer construction and raise cash against owned real estate. Sonic has a record of extending and adding bank facilities, with the credit agreement extended to 2029-03-13 in March 2024 and the sidecar facility added in December 2024, and the senior notes do not fall due until 2029. The view would improve if adjusted EBITDA steadies above USD 614.6 million, if overhead falls back toward 69.75% of gross profit, or if the 2027 facilities are extended early.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C4; calculation C9; calculation C13; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 146, 148

The strongest case that credit is weaker than it looks is that the payout and acquisition programme is already financed by borrowing while adjusted EBITDA falls, that inventory and floor plan balances are rising into a market where industry volume is expected to fall, and that covenant values are not printed so creditors cannot confirm headroom. The view would weaken if the fourth quarter repeats the 10.04% fall in adjusted EBITDA seen in the third quarter, if days supply stays above 65 days into 2027, if the bridge facility is refinanced at higher cost, or if repurchases resume under the USD 527.9 million authorization.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C5; calculation C7; calculation C8; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 33, 90

### Limitations

- No rating, lender statement or price history is supplied beyond the issuer's quarter-end note quotations, so the 2027 refinancing conclusion judges market access from the issuer's own disclosures; wider spreads than those quotations imply would worsen it.
- The downside path is qualitative: no management projection, no covenant ratio value after 2024-12-31 and no segment-level debt is printed, so the scenario names causes and observable triggers but not the point at which a covenant would be breached or liquidity would run out.
- No peer figures are supplied, so adequate is judged against Sonic's own history and the industry data the issuer quotes; against other dealer groups the profile could rank higher or lower.
- Interim comparisons mix acquired and existing stores and same-store data are printed only for some lines, so how much of the 2026 earnings change is organic is partly unknown; the pressure on franchised earnings could be overstated if acquired stores are still ramping.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The strengths, in order of weight, are recurring fixed operations and finance and insurance income, which supply 78.00% of gross profit and so cushion a vehicle downturn; long-dated senior notes of USD 1,150.0 million that mature in 2029 and 2031; discretionary capital spending, with 73.71% of the total in construction and real estate projects, and no repurchases in the third quarter; and an owned real estate base of USD 2,144.3 million at cost.
Basis: calculation C6; calculation C9; calculation C13; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 146–147; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 14, 34, 89–90

The weaknesses, in order of weight, are falling franchised earnings as overhead outgrows gross profit; payouts and acquisitions funded by net new borrowing of USD 90.1 million; USD 561.2 million of bridge and mortgage debt due by the end of 2027 against USD 395.2 million of cash and undrawn facilities; USD 2.7 billion of variable-rate debt with caps that pay only above 5.50%; and covenant ratio values that were last printed for 2024-12-31 under the control of one family that holds 86.00% of the votes.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C6; calculation C7; calculation C10; calculation C13; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 22, 26, 73–74, 96; 10-K_2024-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, page 107

### Company description

Sonic is one of the largest US automotive retailers and earns from three sources: new, used and wholesale vehicle sales, parts, service and collision repair, and finance and insurance commissions. The Franchised Dealerships Segment produced 85.0% of FY2025 revenue across 24 brands, with luxury brands at 63% of franchised new vehicle revenue; EchoPark, a pre-owned retailer, produced 13.7% and Powersports 1.3%. Texas and California together are 50.8% of revenue, so regional demand and regulation matter more than the national spread of stores suggests.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C13; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6

The model supports debt service unevenly. Service, parts and finance income recur through the cycle, but vehicle sales are discretionary and cyclical, so cash-flow visibility is moderate and depends on how fast inventory turns. Franchise agreements give manufacturers approval rights over ownership changes, which protects the franchise base and limits any change of control; refinancing confidence rests on the franchise value and owned real estate rather than on contracted revenue.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11

### Ownership and group structure

Sonic is publicly listed but controlled: Class B holders led by Sonic Financial Corporation and the OBS Family, LLC carry 86.00% of the votes on 38.05% of the shares, and the Smith family also controls affiliates that do business with the company, with payments of USD 14.6 million in FY2025, or 2.38% of adjusted EBITDA. The operating subsidiaries are wholly owned, apart from one jointly owned dealership, and the domestic operating subsidiaries guarantee the senior notes; the guarantees of the EchoPark subsidiaries may be released in certain circumstances, and the legal rank of the secured facilities against one another is not stated.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C13; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 37–38, 128, 147, 152–153

For creditors the control structure means payout, leverage and acquisition policy follow the controlling holders, who also carry a 38.05% economic interest; protection comes from the covenants, not from governance, and the proxy statement that would show board independence is not supplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C13

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is mostly discretionary vehicle sales, driven by unit volume, price and mix, and it is cyclical rather than contracted. Nine-month 2026 revenue rose 2.92% to USD 11,611.3 million, but the growth came from EchoPark, up 10.59%, and Powersports, up 37.00%, while Franchised Dealerships revenue rose only 1.04% and its retail units fell 0.92%. Pricing power on new vehicles is weak: gross profit per new vehicle fell USD 226 to USD 2,957 as available inventory raised price competition, whereas finance and insurance gross profit per retail unit rose to USD 2,615.
Basis: calculation C12; calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 32–34, 74

Fixed operations are the most predictable line: service capacity added through technician hiring, higher parts and labor prices passed to customers and a gross margin of 51.2% support revenue that does not depend on new vehicle volume. Revenue is therefore recurring in part, cyclical in part and concentrated by region and by brand.
Basis: calculation C13; calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, page 34

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Overhead is the swing factor. Compensation is 62.75% of selling, general and administrative expenses and is partly commission-based, which flexes with gross profit, but advertising and other expenses follow business activity and the number of stores and other expenses include fixed items that may not change in proportion to gross profit: other expenses rose USD 48.3 million, or 14.65%, in nine months while gross profit rose 2.70%, and overhead reached 75.60% of gross profit in the third quarter against 73.37%. Operating leverage is therefore adverse when gross profit stalls, and the adjusted EBITDA margin has slipped to 3.81% from 4.06%.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C5; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 75–76

Margins can be defended against modest volume pressure through the commission element and the fixed operations base, but not against a larger fall in new vehicle gross profit combined with higher other expenses, and the first quarter, when overhead is highest against gross profit, is the weakest point in the year. The filings describe cost pass-through only in fixed operations, where higher parts and labor costs were passed on to customers.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C3; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 75–76, 95

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital spending was USD 223.7 million in nine months 2026, up 98.67% from USD 112.6 million, with USD 164.9 million on construction and real estate projects, and all of it was funded from existing cash; commitments of USD 81.3 million are nearly all due within twelve months. Cash interest paid was USD 198.9 million over the last twelve months and includes floor plan interest, and leases add finance lease liabilities of USD 407.1 million and operating lease liabilities of USD 278.8 million outside debt.
Basis: calculation C9; calculation C14; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 89–90

Conversion of EBITDA to free cash flow was weak in 2026: last-twelve-month free cash flow is USD -208.6 million as printed and USD 15.3 million on the alternative basis that treats all floor plan changes as operating, and after acquisitions of USD 169.3 million and payouts of USD 180.2 million the business borrowed. Capital spending is flexible because most of it is discretionary, but the construction commitments reduce that flexibility over the next year.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C7; calculation C9; calculation C14

### Industry forces

Rivalry is high: the industry is fragmented, competitors include larger multi-franchise groups, and internet sellers and brokers narrow price differences on new vehicles, which pressures margin per unit. New entrants are limited by state franchise laws, but direct-to-consumer sellers have succeeded in some states and manufacturers have introduced agency sales in European markets, a risk to new vehicle profit pools if extended. Buyers hold power over new vehicle price through internet price comparison; suppliers, meaning the manufacturers, hold power through production levels, incentives, facility image requirements and approval rights over ownership. The substitution risk is the move of sales from dealers to manufacturers, not another product.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 10–12, 17–18, 28; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 32, 89

### Macro and regulatory factors

Four outside factors matter for credit. Tariffs and trade policy, interest rates, consumer confidence and manufacturer production and incentive levels are among the factors the issuer cites for an industry volume it now puts 1% to 3% below 2025; interest rates also set floor plan and mortgage cost; the expiry of a federal electric vehicle tax credit in the third quarter of 2025 pulled demand forward into that year; and a 2024 systems outage at a third-party provider cut pre-tax income by about USD 47.2 million. State franchise laws and manufacturer policies shape ownership and sales model risk.
Basis: calculation C14; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 55–56; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, page 32

### Credit-translated SWOT

Strengths are recurring service and finance income, long-dated senior notes and an owned property base; weaknesses are falling franchised earnings, payouts funded by borrowing and unobservable covenant headroom. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are the growth of EchoPark and Powersports earnings, where segment income rose, and a normalisation of new vehicle margin per unit as inventories adjust. Threats are further margin compression on a high days supply, higher short-term rates against caps that pay only above 5.50%, direct-sales models that bypass dealers, and a refinancing of USD 561.2 million in 2027 at worse terms.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C6; calculation C10; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 73–74, 96; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, page 18

### Financial profile

The supports are a recurring profit base, adjusted leverage of 2.97x that remains under three times EBITDA, cover of 2.96x on adjusted EBITDA, long-dated senior notes and flexible capital spending; the constraints are falling franchised earnings, free cash flow that does not cover payouts, liquidity that fell USD 116.4 million and a concentrated 2027 maturity. The trend is down on earnings and up on debt. Information needed for a firmer opinion is the covenant ratio values after 2024-12-31, the agreement definitions, management projections, a rating and peer figures.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C4; calculation C6; calculation C14

### Outlook

In the next 12 months to 24 months the headwinds are industry volume 1% to 3% below 2025, days supply of 65 days, new vehicle margin per unit that has fallen two years running, overhead at 75.60% of gross profit and the 2027 refinancing; the tailwinds are lower floor plan rates, with the weighted average down to 4.99% from 5.66%, growth in EchoPark and Powersports, and a fixed operations margin of 51.2%. Over the next three years to five years the main risks are the extension of direct and agency sales and electric vehicle adoption, which could reduce new vehicle profit pools, while the company's own plan is further acquisitions, which raise debt need before earnings arrive.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C14; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 18, 28; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 32–35, 88

### Downside scenario

A realistic downside starts with new vehicle demand falling toward the low end of the industry range while inventory is already high. The first operational break is days supply rising above 65 days, which forces price concessions, so gross profit per new vehicle falls below USD 2,957 and same-store unit sales fall by more than 6%. Fixed operations and finance and insurance hold up, but commission pay flexes only in part and other expenses do not, so overhead rises above 75.60% of gross profit and franchised segment income falls by more than 22.38%.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C8; calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 32–33

The financial transmission is through cash and liquidity: operating cash flow on the alternative basis falls below USD 424.2 million, the USD 85.7 million gap between payouts and cash after investment widens unless payouts stop, and the floor plan deposit of USD 190.0 million and revolver availability of USD 289.5 million become the buffer. Cash plus undrawn facilities of USD 395.2 million cover the USD 561.2 million due by the end of 2027 only 0.70x, so the bridge facility, due 364 days after 2026-03-27, would have to be refinanced on lower earnings and with covenant values that creditors cannot see.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C6; calculation C7; calculation C14

Management has levers: stop repurchases, which were already absent in the third quarter, cut the dividend of USD 0.41 per share, defer the 73.71% of capital spending that is construction and real estate projects, sell or mortgage property carried at USD 2,144.3 million and reduce inventory. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress if these levers are used early, but that cannot be confirmed without covenant values, and the first observable triggers are days supply, overhead as a share of gross profit and the floor plan deposit.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C9; calculation C13; calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 87, 89–91

### Overall credit view

Overall, Sonic presents an adequate business profile supported by recurring service and finance income, a franchise base spread across 24 brands and long-dated senior notes, but credit quality is constrained by falling franchised earnings, payouts funded by borrowing and a concentrated 2027 refinancing. Cash-flow durability appears adequate on the fixed operations base and weak once payouts and acquisitions are included. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on lender appetite for USD 561.2 million of bridge and mortgage debt, the level of covenant headroom and the price of the senior notes, which sit at 91.50% and 94.51% of par. The primary downside path is new vehicle margin compression on high inventory feeding through sticky overhead into lower cover, with no visible covenant headroom. Further analysis would require covenant ratio values, the agreement definitions, a rating, peer figures and management projections.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C4; calculation C6; calculation C11

Top risk: a fall in franchised segment earnings that arrives before the 2027 refinancing and while payouts continue.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C6; calculation C7
