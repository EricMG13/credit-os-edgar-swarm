## Findings

### Bottom line

Control by one family and heavy shareholder payouts leave creditors reliant on covenants rather than on governance, while acquisitions and weaker new vehicle margins add pressure; governance risk is medium, not high.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C7; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 37–38, 154; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 90

Class B shares carry 86.00% of the votes on 38.05% of the shares, and dividends plus repurchases took 113.05% of nine-month 2026 net income while USD 609.6 million went on acquisitions across 2025 and 2026. Audited reporting, small affiliate payments of 2.38% of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA and no described waiver or exchange keep the risk below high.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C5; calculation C7; calculation C9; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 138, 152–153; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 12–13

### Key findings

One family group controls the vote, and no independent check on how it uses that control is evident. Class B shares carry 86.00% of the votes on 38.05% of shares outstanding, and six of the nine people who signed the 2025 annual report as Chairman or director hold executive roles at Sonic or officer or director roles at companies the controlling family owns; whether any director is independent is not stated in the filings read. Manufacturers hold approval rights over ownership and board changes, so control is hard to transfer, but creditors have no governance voice of their own.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C10; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11, 37–38, 118, 152–153; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, page 2

Shareholder payouts are the clearest creditor-adverse pattern: dividends plus repurchases were 113.05% of net income in the first nine months of 2026 and 110.53% in FY2025, against 34.81% in FY2024, and they ran alongside acquisition spending. Two facts soften the point: FY2025 net income carried a USD 173.8 million non-cash impairment, and there were no repurchases in the third quarter of 2026. Against that, the issuer's own measure of earnings free of distribution restrictions fell by USD 109.1 million in the first quarter alone, to USD 282.0 million at 2026-03-31, and it is not printed after that date.
Basis: calculation C7; calculation C13; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, page 102; 10-Q_2026-04-30_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 18, 61; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 90

Acquisitions have been funded ahead of earnings. USD 609.6 million was spent across 2025 and nine months 2026, including USD 440.3 million for five Jaguar Land Rover dealerships, while the 2026 acquisitions contributed only USD 2.2 million of pre-tax income so far and pro forma EBITDA is not printed. The history shows growth plans can reverse at cost: the 2023 suspension of EchoPark locations carried a charge of about USD 75.2 million, and the 2025 franchise asset test required a USD 172.4 million impairment, so spending and payouts sit alongside real write-downs.
Basis: calculation C9; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 138, 141; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13, 16, 84

Related-party leakage is small today but unchecked. Affiliates of the controlling holders received USD 14.6 million of 2025 expense, which is 2.38% of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA, mostly naming rights for the Atlanta speedway and aircraft arrangements, and the company states that the majority of affiliate arrangements have not been verified by third parties; 2026 amounts are not printed because the quarterly reports contain no related-party note.
Basis: calculation C5; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 38, 152–153

Creditor protections were loosened in 2024 and compliance cannot be tracked. The March 2024 amendment extended the credit agreement to 2029-03-13 but raised the unrestricted dividend basket from USD 0.12 to USD 0.18 per share and removed the consolidated liquidity ratio covenant, and the remaining tests of 1.20x minimum fixed charge coverage and 5.75x maximum lease-adjusted leverage have printed values only at 2024-12-31, when they were 2.15x and 3.07x. The guarantees of the EchoPark subsidiaries on the senior notes may be released in certain circumstances, which could move a segment that produced 8.01% of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA out of the noteholder guarantee group.
Basis: calculation C4; calculation C15; 10-K_2024-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 102–103, 107; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 146–147, 149; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, page 21

The business behind the credit is steadier than its vehicle sales suggest, which is the main reason the risk is medium. Fixed operations plus finance and insurance produced 78.00% of gross profit in the first nine months of 2026 from 18.98% of revenue, and the Franchised Dealerships Segment produced 90.12% of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA; against that, same-store new vehicle unit sales fell 6%, gross profit per new vehicle fell USD 226 to USD 2,957 and days supply rose to 65 days from 51 days, so the profit pool that funds payouts is under pressure.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C4; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, page 123; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 32–33

Disclosure and audit are supportive. Grant Thornton audited FY2024 and FY2025 and the controls over financial reporting, management concluded that disclosure controls were effective at 2026-09-30, and no waiver, exchange offer, priming transaction or equity cure is described in the filings read; that is an absence of disclosure, not proof that no such event occurred.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 129, 132; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 119–121; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, page 97

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

Several facts argue for lower risk than medium. The controlling holders own 38.05% of the shares, so they bear a large part of any loss in value; manufacturer approval rights and the noteholders' right to require repurchase on a change of control protect creditors if control changes; the statements are audited; the related-party total is only 2.38% of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA; and USD 282.0 million of earnings was still free of distribution restrictions at 2026-03-31. A proxy statement showing an independent board majority and independent review of affiliate dealings, together with payouts back below earnings, would support a low risk level.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C5; calculation C13; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 35, 37–38; 10-Q_2026-04-30_sah.htm.jsonl, page 18

Other facts argue for higher risk. Payouts above net income, a 2024 amendment that widened the dividend basket and removed the liquidity covenant, covenant values that are no longer printed and a controlling group that sets policy without an evident independent check could together justify a high rating if the remaining restricted-payments capacity proves small. The view would move to high if capacity at 2026-09-30 is shown to be nearly used, if a debt-funded distribution, an exchange offer or a release of the EchoPark guarantees is announced, or if affiliate payments rise well above USD 14.6 million; later compliance figures and the full credit agreements would show how close the tests are.
Basis: calculation C5; calculation C7; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 146–147; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 21, 91

### Limitations

- The proxy statement is not supplied, so board independence, committee membership, the affiliate review policy and holdings by named holder are not established; the control conclusion stands, but the medium risk level could move one step either way once the board and policy are known.
- The issuer's own measure of earnings free of distribution restrictions is printed only at 2025-12-31 and 2026-03-31, and its definition sits in agreements outside the filings, so the payout conclusion cannot be tested against the limit at 2026-09-30; if the remaining capacity proved small, the risk level would rise.
- Acquisition funding beyond inventory financing is not itemised and no pro forma EBITDA is printed, so the acquisition conclusion rests on spending and early earnings contribution rather than on returns.
- Related-party amounts are available for 2025 only, so the finding of low leakage applies to 2025 and affiliate payments in 2026 could differ.
- Support from the controlling holders and any waiver or amendment history are judged from the absence of disclosure in five filings, so the finding that no creditor-adverse liability management has occurred is a limit of the documents read, not proof.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Sonic has spent heavily on acquisitions across 2025 and 2026 and paid for them with floor plan inventory financing, cash and new term debt, so creditors are underwriting a growing business whose acquisitions are only beginning to show in earnings. The 2025 purchase of five Jaguar Land Rover dealerships cost USD 440.3 million, of which USD 166.1 million was inventory funded by floor plan notes, and the nine months of 2026 added a Powersports business of six locations and a Porsche store for USD 169.3 million, which contributed USD 51.8 million of revenue and USD 2.2 million of pre-tax income. The company prints pro forma revenue and pre-tax income for these deals but no pro forma EBITDA, and the Powersports business could not be included in the 2026 pro forma figures.
Basis: calculation C9; 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 100, 138; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13

New debt in the period is a USD 149.1 million sidecar term loan drawn on 2025-10-24 and a USD 150.0 million bridge facility borrowed on 2026-03-27, an unsecured term loan that matures on the earlier of 364 days after closing and a refinancing of the mortgage facility; proceeds from borrowings of USD 190.0 million in the nine-month 2026 cash flow statement include the bridge facility. The senior notes of USD 650.0 million due 2029 and USD 500.0 million due 2031 date from the 2021 financing of the RFJ Auto Partners acquisition and are unchanged, and the March 2024 amendment of the credit agreement extended its maturity to 2029-03-13 with commitments of USD 2.4 billion.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 146–148; 10-Q_2026-04-30_sah.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 16–17, 21

Disposals and shareholder returns run the other way: five locations were sold for USD 62.7 million, while repurchases of USD 142.0 million and dividends of USD 38.2 million were paid in the nine months, with no repurchases in the third quarter. The Board added USD 500.0 million to the repurchase authorization on 2026-04-30, leaving USD 527.9 million at 2026-09-30. No sponsor or equity contribution is described, and the split between cash, floor plan and borrowings for acquisitions beyond inventory financing is not itemised.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C9; 10-Q_2026-10-29_sah.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 14, 90; 10-Q_2026-06-30_sah.htm.jsonl, page 96
