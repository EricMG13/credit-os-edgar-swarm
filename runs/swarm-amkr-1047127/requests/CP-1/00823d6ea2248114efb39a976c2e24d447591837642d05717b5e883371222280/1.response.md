## Findings

### Bottom line
Amkor's leverage is low but rising fast: net debt reached USD 864.22 million, or 0.61x last-twelve-month EBITDA, after nine months of spending outran operating cash flow, so credit quality now depends on customer advances and delivery of the Arizona build rather than on coverage. Gross leverage rose from 1.30x at year-end 2025 to 1.75x and net leverage from 0.06x to 0.61x, because the USD 1,150 million convertible issue funded capital spending that exceeded operating cash flow by about USD 368.46 million. Cash interest cover was 15.07x in FY2025, but the latest cash cover is not calculable because nine-month cash interest is not stated. Pro forma for the land purchase expected in the fourth quarter of 2026, net debt is USD 897.22 million and net leverage 0.64x. The USD 1,000 million revolver is undrawn and USD 1.9 billion of customer advances are expected within twelve months.

### Key findings
Net leverage was 0.03x at the end of FY2024 and 0.06x at the end of FY2025 and is 0.61x on last-twelve-month EBITDA of about USD 1.41 billion at September 30, 2026, while gross leverage moved from 1.12x to 1.30x to 1.75x. Net debt rose by about USD 797.32 million in nine months because total debt rose by about USD 1.02 billion, mainly the USD 1,150 million convertible notes issued in May 2026, while cash rose by only about USD 224.67 million. The cause is capital spending: payments for property, plant and equipment were about USD 1.24 billion against operating cash flow of about USD 868.35 million, a shortfall of about USD 368.46 million before about USD 62.10 million of dividends and about USD 32.33 million of share repurchases. Amkor expects about USD 2.5 billion of capital spending in 2026 and has spent USD 553.3 million on the Arizona facility so far, which leaves about USD 1.26 billion to spend in the fourth quarter, so net debt is likely to rise further unless customer advances arrive on schedule.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5–8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37; calculation C01; calculation C03; calculation C04; calculation C05; calculation C06

Cash interest cover fell from 17.14x in FY2024 to 15.07x in FY2025 because cash interest paid grew 22.13% while EBITDA grew 7.38%. Nine-month cash interest is not stated, so cash cover at September 30, 2026 is not calculable; on accrued interest expense of about USD 73.19 million over the last twelve months, EBITDA cover is 19.30x, a proxy that is not a cash measure. Interest expense for the nine months was about USD 52.60 million against USD 54.85 million a year earlier despite more debt, because the convertible notes carry a 0.00% coupon and part of the interest on the Arizona build is capitalised. The implication is a light interest burden today; the USD 1,150 million principal falls due after 2030, so the cost shows up as refinancing or conversion risk, not as coverage.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_amkr_amkr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 90–93; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–20; calculation C05; calculation C07

Operating cash flow for nine months grew 92.48% to about USD 868.35 million, which Amkor attributes to higher operating profit, customer advance payments and working capital changes. Contract liabilities rose from USD 390.8 million at year-end 2025 to USD 547.6 million, so customer advances are now a material non-debt source of funds for the Arizona build: they must be earned by delivering product, are backed by USD 200.0 million of standby letters of credit and are not in the debt figures. Operating cash flow less capital spending was a shortfall of about USD 156.06 million over the last twelve months. The mechanism is that capital spending is paid first while advances arrive later, so a slowdown in advances while spending continues would fall on cash and the revolver.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7–10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 38; calculation C05; calculation C06

Cash and short-term investments were USD 2,376.2 million, of which USD 1,356.5 million is held by foreign subsidiaries and reachable mainly by repaying intercompany debt, and the USD 1,000 million revolver is undrawn, together USD 3,376.2 million. Stated obligations due within twelve months, namely debt, purchase obligations of USD 2,635.3 million, interest and leases, total USD 2,926.5 million, so liquidity covers them 1.15x before operating cash flow and the USD 1.9 billion of expected customer advances. The maturity ladder is back-ended: 66.11% of contractual principal falls due after 2030, entirely the 2031 convertible and 2033 senior notes, so refinancing risk is distant and the near-term test is funding capital spending, not repaying debt.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 34–36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 41–42; calculation C08; calculation C14

After the balance sheet date Amkor reports an October 2026 agreement with a customer under which it expects USD 1.3 billion in 2027, and an agreement to buy additional Arizona land for about USD 33 million expected to close in the fourth quarter of 2026. Both are kept out of the period figures: the land purchase is a pro forma adjustment that takes net debt to USD 897.22 million and net leverage to 0.64x, and the customer receipt has not occurred and is not adjusted.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; calculation C10

Amkor states no EBITDA, so EBITDA here is calculated as operating income plus depreciation and amortization; adding other income, mainly interest, would raise last-twelve-month EBITDA by 5.25% and lower gross leverage to 1.66x. Net debt deducts only cash and cash equivalents; also deducting short-term investments of about USD 773.18 million would lower it to about USD 91.04 million. Lease obligations of USD 185.6 million for finance leases and USD 98.5 million for operating leases, in undiscounted future payments, sit outside debt. Calculating EBITDA without other income and deducting cash only make leverage look higher than the issuer's own cash presentation would, while keeping leases outside debt makes it look lower.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest case that credit quality is stable is that net leverage is still 0.61x, cash and short-term investments of USD 2,376.2 million are close to total debt, the revolver is undrawn and Amkor expects USD 1.9 billion of customer advances within twelve months and USD 1.3 billion in 2027; if those advances arrive, net debt could stop rising. The case against is that about USD 2.5 billion of capital spending and USD 2,635.3 million of purchase obligations are committed while the advances are expected, not received, and cash interest cover cannot yet be measured on the latest twelve months. Quarterly capital spending, the contract liabilities balance, any revolver draw and the first cash interest figure for the nine months are the observable tests: a falling contract liabilities balance or a drawn revolver would worsen the view, while receipt of the 2027 advance with spending falling back would improve it.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7–10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 35–37; calculation C06; calculation C14

### Limitations
Amkor states no EBITDA, so every EBITDA-based ratio rests on a figure calculated here, and no issuer-adjusted measure exists to compare it with.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_amkr_amkr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 90–92; 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4–7

Cash interest for the nine-month periods is not stated, so cash interest cover for the latest twelve months is not calculable, and the accrued-basis proxy is not a substitute.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8

Security and ranking of the Korea and Japan term loans are not stated individually, so the debt stack is partial and no recovery inference should be drawn from it.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18–21

The pro forma adjustment is mechanical, applied to the September 30, 2026 position alone, and is not a forecast.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_amkr_amkr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17
