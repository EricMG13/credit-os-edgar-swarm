### Company description

Matador is a founder-led independent oil and natural gas producer that drills for oil in the Wolfcamp and Bone Spring formations of the Delaware Basin, supplemented by Haynesville and Cotton Valley gas, and owns 51% of the San Mateo midstream venture. The Delaware Basin supplied 100% of oil production and 97% of natural gas production in the third quarter of 2026, production was 230,470 BOE per day, and reserves were 667.0 million BOE at the end of 2025 with 61% proved developed. Because Matador sells at market prices, revenue is a function of price and volume: oil provided 76.84% of FY2025 revenue and third-party midstream fees only 4.46%, so cash-flow visibility depends on price and on the hedges that currently cap upside more than they protect downside. Debt service capacity is supported by LTM EBITDA of USD 2,474,028 thousand against cash interest of USD 219,121 thousand, and refinancing confidence by a 2026 market refinancing, but the 2026 acquisition programme has added debt faster than earnings.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is price-driven, cyclical and concentrated rather than contracted. Oil revenue rose 36% in the nine months to September 30, 2026 as the realized oil price reached $87.25 per barrel in the third quarter against $64.91 a year earlier, while natural gas revenue fell 56% because the second quarter realization was negative at USD 0.79 per Mcf before the Hugh Brinson pipeline began service on September 1, 2026. Three purchasers took 72% of 2025 oil, natural gas and NGL revenue, so a purchaser failure or a price shock reaches revenue directly, and the realized price after derivatives of $74.84 per barrel shows that collars with a ceiling of $66.36 transfer upside to counterparties. ### Porter's five forces

Rivalry is intense among Delaware Basin producers, and Matador is a price taker whose differentiation is cost: well cost per completed lateral foot of $804 and a core acreage position, which was extended by more than 25,000 net acres across more than 1,100 transactions in 2025 and 2026, support margins but do not set prices. New entrants face a high capital and acreage barrier in the core basin, and substitutes are not a near-term credit factor, so those two forces do not change the view. Buyer power matters because three purchasers took 72% of 2025 revenue, and supplier power matters through oilfield service inflation and through takeaway capacity: natural gas prices were negative in the second quarter of 2026 until the Hugh Brinson pipeline opened, which shows that midstream constraints can erase gas revenue.

### Qualitative downside scenario

The downside is a price reversion on a larger debt base. If realized oil prices fall from the $79.63 per barrel last twelve months average to the 2025 average of $64.99, revenue and EBITDA fall by about $667.7 million on last twelve months volumes before cost savings, while lower prices would stop the collar ceilings absorbing cash, which offsets at most the $239.08 million of realized derivative losses in the period, so pro forma net leverage would be between 3.01x and 3.40x against 2.48x. ### Overall credit view

Overall, Matador presents an adequate business profile supported by production scale, a reserve life of 8.8 years, interest cover of 11.29x and integrated midstream, but credit quality is constrained by acquisition-driven leverage, narrower revolver headroom, a hedge book that caps upside and non-recourse San Mateo debt with a 2027 maturity. Cash-flow durability appears adequate at current prices because LTM operating cash flow was USD 2,476,555 thousand, but it depends on oil prices holding well above the 2025 average. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on repaying the revolver as planned, on the borrowing base and on a long-term refinancing of the San Mateo term loan. The primary downside path is an oil price reversion on a larger debt base that draws the remaining revolver capacity. Further analysis would require covenant definitions, the redetermination result, acquired earnings and the proxy statement. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: acquisition-driven leverage meeting a fall in oil prices.
