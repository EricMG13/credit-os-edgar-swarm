Creditors have current liquidity, but the durability of acquisition returns and future deleveraging remains unproven.

### Company description and operating model

Deluxe Corporation is a publicly traded, U.S.-centred provider of merchant payment processing, B2B treasury and payment services, data and marketing solutions, and checks, forms and promotional products. Its consolidated results combine transaction-linked service activity with a physical-product business exposed to secular substitution. Merchant, B2B and Data Solutions revenue depends on merchant and bank relationships, transaction activity, customer marketing demand and continued access to networks and data; Print depends on order volumes, product mix, paper, fulfillment and delivery. This combination spreads revenue across different end markets, but the mature Print business remains large enough that its decline can offset service growth. No individual customer exceeded 10% of reported revenue in the latest nine-month period, though this does not establish broad customer retention or pricing power.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_dlx.htm.jsonl, pages 24, 26–27; 10-K_2025-12-31_dlx.htm.jsonl, pages 29–34; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Deluxe's payments and data services can generate repeat transaction activity and leverage existing bank and merchant channels, supporting cash-flow visibility when customer relationships persist. The sources do not quantify contract terms, churn or price-volume splits, so the service base should not be treated as contractually recurring. Print demand faces ongoing electronic substitution and business process digitization; pricing and cost actions may slow the earnings decline but do not remove volume risk. The company relies on payment networks, bank partners, data access, vendors, paper and delivery services; disruption or adverse fee and cost changes can affect margins and operations.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dlx.htm.jsonl, pages 29–34; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dlx.htm.jsonl, pages 24, 26–27, 43

### Revenue, pricing and competitive position

Payment and data services are exposed to the volume of merchant transactions, bank-client activity, payment acceptance and marketing demand. Their growth in the reported period supports diversification and can partially offset Print, but the available evidence does not separate organic pricing from acquired volume, transaction growth or customer retention. Celero expands the merchant channel but was consolidated only from its closing date, so initial contribution is not a full-cycle measure of its durability.

Competitive rivalry is relevant because payment processing, data and print services face alternatives and price competition; pressure on fees or retention can reduce contribution margins and cash available for debt service. Entry by technology-enabled providers could weaken merchant or bank relationships, although the filings do not quantify competitor shares or switching rates. Electronic payment methods are a direct substitute for checks and forms, creating structural Print volume pressure. The reported absence of a customer above 10% reduces single-name concentration, but bank partners and payment networks remain important intermediaries. Supplier and outsourced-service dependence, together with paper, postage, technology and delivery costs, can compress margins if pricing does not adjust.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Deluxe presents an adequate business profile supported by payment and data activities, a broad customer base and positive free cash flow, but credit quality is constrained by secular Print decline, higher acquisition-funded debt and refinancing concentration.
