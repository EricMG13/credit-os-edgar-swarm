## Findings
### Bottom line
Leverage has stopped improving: for the twelve months to 2026-09-30 total debt is 3.31x EBITDA, up from 3.27x at FY2025, and net debt is 2.48x, level with 2.49x; creditors should read the flat net figure as 2.74x once net special credits are removed. EBITDA is derived because the issuer states none, and it includes net special credits of USD 756 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2–4; 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 95–97; calculation C15; calculation C16; calculation C19

Free cash flow fell to USD 1,116 million over those twelve months from USD 2,557 million in FY2025 as capital expenditure rose, and debt grew to USD 26,398 million. Liquidity is large, with USD 14,653 million of cash and short-term investments and USD 3,000 million of undrawn revolver commitments, which the October 2026 amendment raises to USD 5,000 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4, 16; calculation C21; calculation C22

Cash interest paid is printed only for full years, so interest cover on a cash basis is not calculable for the latest twelve months; on interest expense, EBITDA covers interest 5.88 times.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 97; calculation C20

### Financial interpretation

Leverage fell sharply and has since stopped improving. Total debt at carrying value dropped from USD 31,660 million at FY2023 to USD 24,988 million at FY2025 while derived EBITDA rose from USD 6,882 million to USD 7,652 million, taking total debt to EBITDA from 4.60x to 3.27x. Since then debt has risen to USD 26,398 million as aircraft financings of 4.6 billion outran repayments, and total debt to EBITDA is 3.31x for the twelve months to 2026-09-30. Net debt to EBITDA of 2.48x looks level with FY2025 at 2.49x, but the last twelve months include net special credits of USD 756 million, which reflect aircraft sale-leaseback gains recorded in 2026, and without them net leverage is 2.74x against 2.41x. For creditors, the improvement came mainly from repaying debt, with payments of long-term debt, finance leases and other financial liabilities of USD 10,138 million in FY2024 and USD 4,771 million in FY2025 against issuance of USD 6,139 million and USD 578 million, and the next test is whether earnings carry the added fleet debt without special credits.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 95–96, 128; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2–4, 16–21; calculation C15; calculation C16; calculation C19

Cash interest cover strengthened as expensive debt was repaid. EBITDA covered cash interest paid 3.72 times in FY2023, 5.37 times in FY2024 and 5.75 times in FY2025, while interest expense fell from USD 1,956 million to USD 1,373 million. The last twelve months cash figure cannot be built because the quarterly reports omit cash paid for interest, so the accrued-expense cover of 5.88 times is shown as a proxy only. The strongest contrary reading is that new aircraft debt at floating rates, about 1.4% above the benchmark, adds interest that the earlier years did not carry; interest expense for the nine months already ran at USD 1,029 million against USD 1,048 million a year earlier, so the effect is not yet visible.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 95, 97; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3, 17–18; calculation C17; calculation C20

Cash conversion has weakened. Free cash flow, operating cash flow less capital expenditure, was USD 3,830 million in FY2024 and USD 2,557 million in FY2025, and USD 1,116 million for the twelve months to 2026-09-30, or 0.14 of EBITDA against 0.33 in FY2025. Capital expenditure rose to USD 7,044 million over the latest twelve months from USD 5,874 million as aircraft deliveries continued, and operating cash flow was held back by a one-off flight attendant ratification payment. The mechanism is that fleet investment is outrunning internally generated cash and being met with new secured debt, which raises leverage unless EBITDA grows with it.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 97; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 19–21; calculation C21; calculation C11; calculation C12

Liquidity is ample against near-term maturities but depends on customer-funded float. Cash and short-term investments were USD 14,653 million at 2026-09-30 and the undrawn revolver USD 3,000 million, against USD 1,848 million of current debt and USD 934 million of current operating leases. Advance ticket sales of USD 10,672 million and frequent flyer deferred revenue of USD 7,965 million are a non-debt funding source of USD 18,637 million that exceeds the liquidity pool; a fall in bookings would draw that float down before it shows in debt metrics. The ladder shows USD 5,276 million of principal due in 2029, the largest single year before 2030, which is the refinancing point to watch.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4, 16, 29; calculation C22; calculation C28; calculation C13

Three definitions of debt and two of cash are in play and each serves one purpose. The balance-sheet captions, USD 26,398 million, serve leverage because they carry finance leases and other financial liabilities; the debt note carrying value of USD 22,964 million and principal of USD 23,116 million serve the maturity ladder and the reconciliation; and the issuer total of USD 34.3 billion adds operating leases and serves its own fixed-obligation disclosure. Net debt deducts cash and cash equivalents only, USD 6,648 million; the issuer counts short-term investments as well, and doing so lowers net leverage to 1.47x.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4, 13, 16, 29; calculation C14; calculation C18; calculation C26

The revolving credit facility amendment of 2026-10-16 is a subsequent event and is excluded from every period figure: commitments rise from USD 3,000 million to USD 5,000 million and maturity extends to 2031-10-16, with no borrowing outstanding. The amended facility carries a minimum unrestricted liquidity covenant of USD 2,000 million and a minimum collateral coverage ratio of 1.6 to 1.0, but no reported coverage figure or collateral value is printed, so covenant headroom is not calculable and none is inferred.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 38–39; 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 129–130; calculation C15
