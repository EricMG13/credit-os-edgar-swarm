## Findings

### Bottom line

United's credit profile is sound but no longer improving: scale, liquidity and lower debt support creditors, while an unhedged fuel shock, higher labor cost and heavy aircraft spending have cut margin and free cash flow, so risk lies in cash conversion, not liquidity. Total debt is 3.31x EBITDA and net debt 2.48x, or 2.74x without special credits, and free cash flow fell to USD 1,116 million over twelve months. Cash and short-term investments of USD 14,653 million cover principal due through 2029 1.52 times with no free cash flow, so the main downside is fuel staying above fares until the 2029 maturities must be refinanced.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against caution is pricing power: revenue rose 14.84% on capacity growth of 2.7%, unit revenue rose 11.9%, management states that its long-term outlook is positive because it expects demand to stay strong, and three agencies upgraded the company in 2025. If fuel falls back toward the USD 2.43 a gallon of the prior-year nine months while fares hold, the underlying margin would recover quickly because fuel is the swing cost, and the view would move to stable or improving.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 31–32; calculation C16; calculation C17

The view would worsen if margin before special items stayed below the nine-month level of 5.56%, if twelve-month free cash flow turned negative as it did in FY2023, or if repurchases resumed while capital spending stayed high. A drawing on the revolver for operating needs, or a rating outlook change to negative, would confirm that the downside path described later had begun.

### Limitations

- No peer filings, rating agency reports or market prices are supplied, so the judgement that the business profile is stronger than the leverage profile is not benchmarked against comparable airlines, and the rating trajectory rests on the company's own statements.
- The downside arithmetic holds volume, prices and all other costs at latest levels and ignores fare, capacity and capital spending responses, so it measures exposure and is not a forecast; a faster response would shrink the cash burn shown.
- Maturity coverage uses the principal ladder for long-term debt agreements only, which excludes finance leases and other financial liabilities and any payments on them, so the coverage of principal due through 2029 is overstated by an amount that is not established.
- No forward model or management plan beyond the 2026 capital expenditure guidance is supplied, so the outlook and the timing of the 2029 refinancing are read from disclosed commitments and not from projected cash flow.

### Company description

United is a hub-based network airline whose cash flow depends on passenger revenue, which was 90.47% of FY2025 revenue, with 59.28% earned in the domestic market and 40.72% in Atlantic, Pacific and Latin American markets. Cargo, loyalty partner income and ancillary fees add diversification of source but not of cycle, so earnings follow U.S. air travel demand, fares and fuel; tickets are sold at most twelve months ahead, so revenue visibility is short and the credit rests on liquidity and network scale rather than on contracted revenue.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 4–8; 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 71; 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 110; calculation C16; calculation C18

The strategic shift that matters for creditors is the United Next fleet plan, which commits the company to several hundred new aircraft by 2034 and to the financing that goes with them, while the 2025 and 2026 history is one of debt repayment, a larger regional supplier merger and a labor reset. The model supports debt service through scale and customer prepayments, and it weakens refinancing confidence only if fuel and labor costs outrun fares for longer than liquidity can bridge.

### Ownership and group structure

United Airlines Holdings, Inc. is a holding company whose wholly owned subsidiary United Airlines, Inc. supplies nearly all revenue, expenses, assets and operating cash flow, so the holding company notes rely on the subsidiary guarantee and the subsidiary's assets are the real credit support. No sponsor or controlling holder is disclosed, no dividend is paid and repurchases were small, and Business & governance assesses sponsor and governance risk as low on documented behaviour alone, because the debt documents and the proxy statement are not supplied.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is cyclical and discretionary but currently price-led: nine-month capacity rose 2.7% while unit revenue rose 11.9%, and revenue grew 14.84%, so yield and fees, not volume, carried the increase. Management states that it has historically had limited ability to raise fares and fees enough to offset a large, fast and sustained fuel increase, and that air travel demand follows economic strength, so the 2026 pricing gain is evidence of present demand, not a contractual floor.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Costs are largely fixed in the short run: labor and fuel together are 53.43% of FY2025 operating expense, regional carriers are paid fixed-fee rates with USD 18.7 billion of estimated future payments, and firm aircraft orders carry material liabilities if modified. Margin can be defended only through fares and capacity: unit revenue less unit cost before special items fell 24.39% in the nine months, so cost inflation has so far outrun pricing at the margin even though revenue per seat mile rose.

Flexibility exists in capacity, which management used in 2026 by cutting lower-margin capacity, and delivery delays have held adjusted capital expenditure for 2026 below USD 8.0 billion, but labor agreements, regional contracts and the order book do not shrink quickly. The nine-month operating margin before special items fell to 5.56% from 8.23%, so margins cannot be defended against a further fuel or wage shock without a fare response.

### Capital intensity and FCF conversion

EBITDA converts weakly into free cash flow because capital intensity is high: capital expenditure over the twelve months was USD 7,044 million, 88.41% of EBITDA, nine-month spending rose 29.37%, and free cash flow was 14.01% of EBITDA against 33.42% in FY2025. Most of the spending is growth and renewal capital under firm orders that run to 2034, so it can be slowed by delivery delays but not cancelled without cost, and it is increasingly financed with new secured aircraft debt, which raises leverage unless EBITDA grows.

### Porter's Five Forces

Rivalry is intense and pressures margin: the industry competes on routes, fares, capacity and loyalty, consolidation and immunized joint businesses reshape competitors, and the company states that it often has to match discounted fares to keep traffic. That limits the pass-through of cost increases and is why a fuel shock lands on margin first.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 33

The threat of new entrants is moderate: any fit U.S. carrier may fly domestic routes, but slots, gates and airport infrastructure at the hubs are constrained, and the company's route authorities, slots and terminal rights are collateral for its secured debt, which supports their value to lenders.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 38–39

Substitutes matter for short-haul and business travel: surface travel replaces short flights and videoconferencing replaces trips when conditions weaken, so a fall in business and premium travel would hit revenue first, which raises the cyclicality of cash flow.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 36

Buyer power is limited for consumers individually but real in aggregate: tickets are sold through the company's own channels and through agencies and online agencies, a legacy distribution cost remains, and the largest partner income depends on one co-brand card agreement whose terms are not disclosed.

### Qualitative downside scenario

The initiating condition is fuel staying at the third-quarter price of USD 3.90 a gallon for a further twelve months while fare gains stall, which on twelve-month volume adds USD 2,531 million of fuel expense, 31.77% of EBITDA. The first operational break is margin: EBITDA would fall to about USD 5,436 million, free cash flow would turn to a burn of USD 1,415 million, and total debt to EBITDA would rise to 4.86x with debt unchanged.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25–26; calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C20

Liquidity absorbs it comfortably over a year, with cash and short-term investments covering the burn and the next 15 months of principal 3.93 times, and the amended revolver, a subsequent event, adds committed capacity of USD 5.0 billion that the ratio does not count. The pressure point is the refinancing: over three years the same burn plus principal due through 2029 would use almost all cash, 1.06 times covered, so resilience then depends on refinancing the 2029 principal, on market access at BB+ and Ba1, and on management levers such as capacity cuts, fare and fee increases, the timing of capital spending and no repurchases.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 38–39; calculation C4; calculation C21; calculation C18

The primary downside path is a prolonged fuel price above fares and rising labor cost that turns free cash flow negative and lifts leverage ahead of the 2029 maturities, with the shared collateral pool leaving unsecured creditors exposed if market access closes. The scenario is arithmetic exposure, not a forecast, and it does not assume default.
Basis: calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C20; calculation C21

### Overall credit view

Overall, United Airlines Holdings, Inc. presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, liquidity, lower debt and a loyalty income stream, but credit quality is constrained by unhedged fuel, high capital intensity and rising labor cost. Cash-flow durability appears adequate to weak because free cash flow fell to USD 1,116 million over twelve months and underlying margin has fallen. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on liquidity, the extended revolver, positive rating momentum and market access for the 2029 principal. The primary downside path is fuel staying above fares while labor and aircraft costs keep rising. Further analysis would require the debt documents, covenant coverage figures, peer comparisons and a forward model. Fundamental credit synthesis completed. Top risk: unhedged fuel cost against fares, feeding weak free cash flow ahead of the 2029 maturities.
