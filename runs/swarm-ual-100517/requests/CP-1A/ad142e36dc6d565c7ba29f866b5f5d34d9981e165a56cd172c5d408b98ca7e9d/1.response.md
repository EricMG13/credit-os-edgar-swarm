## Findings

### Bottom line

United's credit risk is operating, not owner-driven: no controlling holder is disclosed, no dividend is paid and the loyalty business carries no secured debt, so creditors bear airline-cycle and fuel risk rather than extraction risk. In the nine months to 2026-09-30 fuel expense rose USD 4,520 million (53.35%) and operating income before special items fell 22.37% to USD 2,790 million, while reported operating income rose only because of USD 749 million of special credits.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 66; 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 75; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation C6; calculation C7

The credit case therefore rests on pricing power against fuel and labor costs and on aircraft financing, because the revolver, the term loan and the 2029 senior secured notes share one collateral pool of route authorities, slots and terminal rights, and capital spending is rising.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 38–39; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25; calculation C8; calculation C15

### Key findings

Ownership creates no identified sponsor risk: the shares are listed on Nasdaq, no holder is identified as controlling, the Treasury warrants issued in the pandemic were all exercised by March 2025, and the only other equity security outstanding is two junior preferred shares whose rights the filings read do not describe. The tax benefits preservation plan that deters any buyer from crossing 4.9% of the shares expires on 2026-12-04, after the as-of date, and the filings do not say it will be renewed.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 57; 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 66; 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 115; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 2

The business is a scale network carrier with diversified geography but one core product: passenger revenue was 90.47% of FY2025 revenue and domestic revenue 59.28%, so earnings follow U.S. air travel demand and fares more than any second business. Cargo was 3.01% and other operating revenue 6.52%, most of it loyalty partner income.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 71; 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 110; 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 114; calculation C1; calculation C3; calculation C18

Loyalty is a real strength and prepayments are a real obligation: partner agreements, led by the co-brand card with JPMorgan Chase, produced about USD 3.2 billion of FY2025 revenue and grew 12.50% in the nine months to USD 2.7 billion, and no debt is now secured on the loyalty assets. Against that, customer prepayments of USD 18,637 million are 1.27x cash and short-term investments of USD 14,653 million, so a drop in bookings or a processor demand for reserves would reach liquidity quickly.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 75; 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 138; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10; calculation C12; calculation C13; calculation C18

Fuel is the largest swing cost and is not hedged: it took 27.87% of nine-month operating expense against 21.00% a year earlier, at an average USD 3.65 per gallon against USD 2.43, the company does not hedge fuel and the filings give no price sensitivity. Fares have recovered much of it, with total revenue per seat mile up 11.9% on capacity up only 2.7%, but operating margin before special items still fell from 8.23% to 5.56%.

The annual report also lists several other groups whose agreements were already open for amendment and states that negotiations are active with all of them.

The shared collateral pool is the main structural point for unsecured creditors: route authorities, slots and terminal rights secure the revolver, the term loan and the 2029 senior secured notes on a shared senior basis, a substantial part of the aircraft is also pledged, and the revolver was enlarged from USD 3.0 billion to USD 5.0 billion on 2026-10-16, after the latest balance sheet. Owned aircraft are 89.87% of the mainline fleet, but owned does not mean unencumbered.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a benign view is the cost trend: reported nine-month operating income rose 6.4% only because of USD 749 million of special credits, mostly gains on aircraft sale-leasebacks, while the underlying measure fell 22.37% and the third-quarter margin slipped to 8.09% from 9.16%. A further fuel rise that fares cannot recover, or a demand slowdown with the same cost base, would cut free cash flow that already fell in 2026, and the unhedged position gives creditors no buffer.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25; calculation C7; calculation C8; calculation C20

The view that owner behaviour is not a risk would change if a holder crossed 5% after the preservation plan lapses, if repurchases resumed at the FY2025 pace while capital spending rises, if secured debt were again raised on the loyalty assets, or if the revolver were drawn for distributions. The remaining USD 755 million repurchase authorisation and the loyalty assets that no longer secure debt show the capacity exists, though use of either is not yet documented.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 57; 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 66; 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 75; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 11

### Limitations

- The proxy statement is not supplied, so board composition, independence, committee structure and holders above 5% are not established; the conclusion that no holder controls the company rests on the filings naming none, and a large holder or voting arrangement would overturn it.
- The credit agreements and indentures are not supplied, so capacity to pay dividends, repurchase shares, add secured debt or move assets cannot be measured; the low governance risk rests on documented behaviour and would be reassessed once the covenant package is read.
- The filings report one segment and give no customer, route or hub profitability, so the claim that revenue is diversified rests on the split by region and by revenue type alone and hub concentration cannot be tested.
- The filings describe no fuel hedge and give no price sensitivity, so the estimate that fares have recovered much of the fuel increase rests on nine-month unit revenue changes and not on a stated pass-through, and it could reverse if demand softens.
- Loyalty partner income is disclosed only as rounded totals with no split of the co-brand card agreement, its term or minimum commitments, so the durability of the largest non-ticket income and the financing value of the loyalty assets that no longer secure debt are not established.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The 2026 financing was a refinancing plus aircraft borrowing, not a shareholder distribution: on 2026-02-02 and 2026-02-06 the holding company issued USD 1,000 million each of senior notes due 2031 and 2029, both at par and guaranteed by the operating subsidiary, and on 2026-02-24 the operating subsidiary redeemed all USD 2,000 million of its senior secured notes due 2026. The filings do not state whether the new notes are secured or how they rank against the term loan and the secured notes.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-03-31_ual-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-06-30_ual-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-K_2025-12-31_ual-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 76

The term loan was repriced on 2026-02-03 by Amendment No. 4, which cut the margin from 2.00% to 1.75% on Term SOFR loans and from 1.00% to 0.75% on other market-rate loans; the filings state no consideration given for the change. In the nine months to 2026-09-30 the operating subsidiary also borrowed USD 4.6 billion secured on aircraft, maturing between 2029 and 2038 at Term SOFR plus an average margin of 1.4%, and made USD 4.9 billion of debt payments, including a USD 0.6 billion partial prepayment on 2026-06-23 of the USD 1.5 billion Treasury payroll support note due 2030.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-03-31_ual-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–19

After the latest balance sheet, on 2026-10-16, the revolving credit facility was amended and restated: commitments rose from USD 3.0 billion to USD 5.0 billion, maturity moved from 2029-02-15 to 2031-10-16 and the margins were lowered, with no borrowing outstanding at the date of the report. The facility is secured by route authorities, slots and terminal rights that also secure the term loan and the 2029 senior secured notes, and its lenders may require prepayment if a change of control occurs and the lender accepts a change of control offer. Figures at 2026-09-30 in this review do not include it.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ual-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 38–39

No purchase of an operating business and no sponsor transaction is described in the filings read. The equity-type transactions were investments: financial contributions to the merger of Mesa and Republic completed on 2025-11-25, whose amount is not stated, in exchange for 2,744,348 additional Republic shares received on 2026-02-03, and a USD 100 million subscription for Azul depositary shares agreed on 2026-02-17 that left United with 8.6% of Azul at 2026-09-30.
