## Findings

### Bottom line

Arcosa's credit risk now turns on a pending cash takeover by CRH: a change of control could cause a default or prepayment event under the credit agreement and both note indentures, and how the debt will be treated is not established. The operations are diversified and visible: utility structures backlog of USD 648.1 million supports 2026, wind tower backlog of USD 537.4 million fades as tax credits end after 2027, and one customer was 12.2% of 2025 revenue. No sponsor or controlling holder is named, and barge sale cash prepaid only USD 83.0 million of debt. Nothing dated after 5 August 2026 was supplied, so the shareholder vote scheduled for 4 September 2026 and the July amendment to the credit agreement are unresolved.

### Key findings

The takeover terms favour completion: shareholders receive USD 150.00 in cash per share, about USD 7,366.0 million of equity value; the merger has no financing condition, although Arcosa lists CRH's ability to obtain financing among its risks; CRH would owe a termination fee of USD 372.0 million against USD 260.4 million payable by Arcosa; and closing is expected in the first quarter of 2027, but the vote result and the regulatory position after 5 August 2026 are not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_aca_aca-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 5–7; 10-Q_2026-06-30_aca_aca-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 37; 10-Q_2026-06-30_aca_aca-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 40; calculation C-A1; calculation C-A2

The change of control is the open creditor question: the annual report warns that a change of control, as each financing document defines it, could result in a default or prepayment event under the credit agreement and under both note indentures, and both series of senior notes carry a repurchase offer at 101% of principal on a Change of Control Triggering Event; the indentures, the credit agreement terms and CRH's plans for the debt were not supplied. At 30 June 2026 the 2029 notes were valued at 98.40% of principal and the 2032 notes at 104.45%, so a 101% offer would sit above the market for the first and below it for the second if the trigger were met.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_aca_aca-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-06-30_aca_aca-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-06-30_aca_aca-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 45; 10-K_2025-12-31_aca_aca-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 133–137; calculation C-F1

There is no sponsor to underwrite and shareholder returns are small: the stock is publicly traded, no controlling holder is named in the annual report, dividends are USD 0.20 per share a year, and share repurchases were USD 17.5 million in the first half of 2026, small against net barge sale proceeds of USD 429.9 million, of which USD 83.0 million prepaid the term loan.

The business is diversified but not evenly resilient: without the barge business, aggregates-led Construction Products were 52.41% of 2025 revenue, concentrated in Texas and New Jersey at about 35% and 20% of segment revenue, and Engineered Structures 47.59%, of which utility structures 33.39% and wind towers 14.21%; the largest customer, in wind towers, was about 14.07% of that revenue.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against treating the takeover as the dominant credit risk is that CRH is paying cash with no financing condition and has accepted a reverse termination fee of USD 372.0 million, larger than the USD 260.4 million Arcosa would owe, which points to completion by a strategic owner rather than a leveraged sponsor, and Arcosa holds USD 432.1 million of cash, enough for a 101% offer on the notes due 2029 of USD 404.0 million but not on both series, which would cost USD 1,010.0 million. The view would turn more negative if the debt documents show a Change of Control Triggering Event is likely or if the July 2026 amendment or the merger agreement added leverage or restricted creditors; it would turn positive if they show the notes and loans staying in place on unchanged terms or CRH repaying the credit agreement debt in full.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_aca_aca-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 5–7; 10-Q_2026-06-30_aca_aca-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-06-30_aca_aca-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-06-30_aca_aca-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 45; calculation C-F2

### Limitations

- The proxy statement and the merger agreement were not supplied, so the conclusion that no controlling holder exists rests on the annual report alone and would change if a beneficial owner holds a large block or if the board has special rights.
- Nothing dated after 5 August 2026 was supplied, so the conclusion that the takeover is still pending, and its expected closing in the first quarter of 2027, could be overtaken by the shareholder vote, regulatory decisions or a competing offer.
- The indentures and the credit agreement were not supplied, so the conclusions on the 101% repurchase offer and on what a change of control does to the term loan, revolver and notes are limited to what the filings summarise and would change once the defined terms are read.
- The governance reading covers structure, disclosure and documented behaviour only and does not assess individuals, so it changes no conclusion about management quality.
