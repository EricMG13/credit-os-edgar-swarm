## Findings

### Bottom line

Arcosa's fundamental credit profile is adequate, with net leverage of 1.97x and USD 432.1 million of cash, but the outcome for creditors depends on the pending CRH cash takeover, whose effect on the debt is not established. A change of control could cause a default or prepayment event under the credit agreement and both note indentures, and repaying every instrument at closing would cost USD 1,460.0 million against at most USD 1,132.1 million of cash and revolver commitment. The 1.97x corrects the 1.91x in Financial foundation, whose EBITDA was USD 14.7 million too high; pro forma net debt after the dividend paid on 31 July 2026 is USD 1,007.1 million, also 1.97x.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against a cautious reading is that creditors sit under a wide cushion at the price the buyer pays: net debt is 12.00% of the USD 8,370.6 million enterprise value implied by the offer, the merger has no financing condition, and CRH has agreed to pay a termination fee of USD 372.0 million in specified circumstances, 5.05% of the equity value, against USD 260.4 million that Arcosa may owe. Cash of USD 432.1 million exceeds the notes due in 2029, nothing else falls due before then beyond USD 17.5 million of amortization, and the filings' own estimates put the notes due 2029 at 98.40% and the notes due 2032 at 104.45% of principal, from quoted prices in a thinly traded market. The view would improve if the debt documents or CRH show the credit agreement debt repaid and the notes staying in place on unchanged terms, or if second-half cash flow after capital expenditures turns positive; it would worsen if a Change of Control Triggering Event is met while liquidity is below the call, if wind tower orders do not refill the 2028 backlog, or if first-half gains on asset sales prove to be the main source of earnings growth.

### Limitations

- The credit agreement and the indentures were not supplied, so the conclusion that leverage is moderate is stated on reported EBITDA, not on the covenant measure that nets at most USD 150.0 million of cash against a 4.00x maximum, and headroom against that covenant would change the view of liquidity risk once it can be calculated.
- Continuing EBITDA is rebuilt from the annual segment note and the quarterly statements, not recast by the company, so the leverage of 1.97x would move one for one with any recast of the barge business figures or of the 2025 corporate costs.
- No profit is reported separately for wind towers and utility structures, so the conclusion that profit growth now depends on utility structures is a revenue-based reading and would change if wind tower margins differ materially from the Engineered Structures average.
- Only the first half of 2026 is supplied for cash flow, so the conclusion that cash generation is weak rests on the seasonally weakest period and would soften if the second half repeats the 2025 pattern.
- No peer set, market price or credit rating was supplied, so the adequate assessment of the profile is a judgement against the issuer's own history and the sources, not against comparable issuers.

### Company description

Arcosa is a producer of infrastructure products whose continuing business has two segments after the barge sale: Construction Products at 51.47% of first-half revenue of USD 1,230.4 million, covering aggregates, specialty materials, asphalt and trench shoring, and Engineered Structures at 48.53%, covering utility, wind tower, traffic, lighting and telecom structures. Aggregates and asphalt are sold locally, with Texas about 35% and New Jersey about 20% of 2025 segment revenue, to contractors and road agencies, while the structures businesses sell to utilities, turbine producers and state transport agencies, so cash flow follows public infrastructure budgets, housing and the wind tax credit rather than one commodity.

Business & governance describes the conversion of an idled wind tower plant to utility structures as expected in the second half of 2026; the June 2026 report says it has been converted and began delivering utility poles in the second quarter, and that a further wind tower plant will be converted in 2027, which supersedes the earlier wording.

### Ownership and group structure

Arcosa is a listed company with no controlling holder or sponsor named, so its financial policy is set by management and a board that a creditor cannot assess from the supplied filings; the proxy statement that would show board, holdings and pay was not supplied. The credit agreement and both note series are guaranteed by domestic subsidiaries that the filings do not name, the revolver and term loan are secured on substantially all personal property while the notes are unsecured, so the priority between them is by security, not by legal entity, as far as the sources state.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is partly visible and partly cyclical: first-half revenue rose 2.95% to USD 1,230.4 million, with utility structures up 13.72% and wind towers down 20.19%, and Construction Products up only 2.59%, where higher trench shoring revenue was the main driver and single-family housing weakness held back volumes. Utility revenue is supported by alliance contracts and a backlog of USD 648.1 million, wind tower revenue depends on a tax credit that ends after 2027 and on turbine producers, one of which was 12.2% of 2025 revenue, and construction materials depend on public funding, with a new surface transport authorization or an extension expected by the end of 2026, and on mortgage rates.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Selling, general and administrative expense rose 16.95% and corporate costs 43.59% on acquisition and divestiture expenses, which hold reported EBITDA down and persist while the merger is pending, so the margin is held up by gains that may not repeat and held down by deal costs that may.

### Porter's Five Forces

Competitive rivalry is regional in aggregates and national in structures: the aggregates industry is highly fragmented and grows by acquisition, which supports local pricing but invites debt-funded roll-up, while structures producers compete on quality and delivery and, for utility work, in competitive bids; the risk to creditors is acquisition spending, not price war.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_aca_aca-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-K_2025-12-31_aca_aca-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11

The threat of new entrants is low in aggregates, where proximity to demand centres and permitted reserves act as barriers, and moderate in structures, where capacity can be converted, as Arcosa itself is converting wind tower plants; this protects aggregates margins and exposes structures margins to competitors that can also convert.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_aca_aca-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-06-30_aca_aca-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 23

The threat of substitutes is limited for aggregates and asphalt, and recycled aggregates, of which Arcosa is the largest US producer, are a complement; for wind towers the substitute is a change in policy that removes the tax credit, which the 2025 law does for sales after 2027.

Buyer power is concentrated in wind towers, where turbine producers are the main customers and one was 12.2% of 2025 revenue, and moderate in utility structures, where alliance contracts offset competitive bids; the effect is price and volume pressure on the wind tower line when orders slow.

### PEST analysis

Policy and economic factors are material to credit quality; social and technological factors are not primary drivers on the provided materials. The 2025 law ends the manufacturing credit for wind towers sold after 2027, which sets the date of the wind tower revenue gap, and a new federal surface transport authorization is expected by the end of 2026 or an extension of the current one; interest rates and housing affordability held back residential volumes, with Texas housing permits down about 8% in 2025, which pressures Construction Products volumes. Demand for electricity, including from artificial intelligence projects, supports utility structure orders, which partly offsets the policy loss.

Weaknesses are the negative first-half free cash flow, earnings growth that comes from gains, concentration in one wind customer and an opaque debt structure.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months utility structures, with 71% of the backlog due in 2026 and 20% in 2027, and public infrastructure budgets in Texas and New Jersey are tailwinds, while the shift from wind towers, housing weakness and the merger's conduct restrictions are headwinds; net leverage is likely to stay near its current level unless the proceeds are spent or cash flow improves. Wind tower backlog would sustain 2027 revenue at about the 2025 level, USD 354.7 million against USD 355.2 million, then falls away, which is the first break in the medium-term outlook.

### Qualitative downside scenario

The issuer-specific downside begins with the end of the wind tower tax credit after 2027: with only USD 32.2 million of backlog due in 2028 and no replacement orders, wind tower revenue of USD 355.2 million in 2025 falls away, and one customer that was 12.2% of 2025 revenue stops being a source of volume. The first operational break is lost absorption at the wind tower plants, which cuts Engineered Structures margin; the financial transmission is lower EBITDA, and at the Engineered Structures margin of 18.72% the loss is about USD 66.5 million, 13.02% of EBITDA, taking net leverage to 2.26x, total leverage to 3.24x and interest cover to 4.48x with net debt held constant, an illustration that ignores utility growth and is Analyst Judgement.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_aca_aca-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_aca_aca-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11; calculation C-15; calculation C-17; calculation C-30

Liquidity would not be the first casualty: cash of USD 432.1 million exceeds debt falling due to 2029 of USD 424.5 million, and management can cut capital expenditures, reduce buybacks and dividends of USD 0.20 per share, and redirect plants to utility structures, so resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress from operations alone. The sharper path is combined: a change of control that triggers prepayment or the 101% offer when the revolver's availability is uncertain, because the call of USD 1,460.0 million would exceed liquidity of at most USD 1,132.1 million. The primary downside path is therefore a change of control that forces refinancing of the credit agreement debt before the wind tower gap shows in earnings, and the observable triggers are the vote and closing dates, any repurchase offer and the 2028 wind backlog.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Arcosa presents an adequate business profile supported by a utility structures backlog of USD 648.1 million, local aggregates positions and net leverage of 1.97x, but credit quality is constrained by a change of control whose effect on the debt is not established, a wind tower revenue gap from 2028 and earnings growth that comes from gains. Cash-flow durability appears adequate for debt service on the evidence of 2025, weak in the first half of 2026 and not established for the second half. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the debt documents' treatment of the merger, the revolver's availability and its August 2028 maturity, and CRH's financial policy. Further analysis would require the credit agreement, the indentures and Amendment No. 3, the vote and closing outcome, any report after June 2026, ratings, and profit by product line. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: a change of control that forces repayment or refinancing of the debt.
