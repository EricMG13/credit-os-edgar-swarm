## Findings

### Bottom line

Diebold Nixdorf has an adequate but weakening credit profile: services-led revenue and a single 2030 maturity support creditors, but cash generation turned negative in 2026 and buybacks lifted net leverage, so credit quality now depends on a fourth-quarter cash rebuild.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C7; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

Net debt rose by USD 213.2 million in nine months to USD 764.4 million at 2026-09-30, or USD 783.7 million pro forma for repurchases through 2026-10-23, and net leverage rose to 2.33x from 1.69x, or 2.39x pro forma. Reaching the full-year outlook needs fourth-quarter free cash flow of USD 226.2 million to USD 246.2 million on the company's definition, against USD 195.7 million a year earlier.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C7; Financial foundation (CP-1); EX99.1_2026-09-30_dbd_ex-991q32026erlive-htm.htm.jsonl, page 2

Retail grew 21.88%, but its segment gross margin of 21.69% is below Banking's 27.45%, so Retail growth dilutes margin as Banking shrinks.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a weakening view is that the 2026 weakness is seasonal and discretionary. Fourth-quarter 2025 operating cash flow was 72.33% of the year, USD 32.3 million of the nine-month outflow was discrete German tax paid for earlier years, adjusted EBITDA for the third quarter rose 4.84% to USD 127.8 million, nothing matures before 2029-12-18, and the board can suspend buybacks at any time. The view would soften if fourth-quarter free cash flow reaches USD 226.2 million and repurchases pause, and it would harden if free cash flow falls short while repurchases continue, if product gross margin stays at or below the 22.2% of the third quarter, or if the next outlook cuts adjusted EBITDA again.

### Company description and ownership

Diebold Nixdorf makes and services ATMs, cash recyclers, point-of-sale and self-checkout systems with software for banks and retailers worldwide. Services were 56.99% of FY2025 revenue of USD 3,805.7 million, Banking was 73.50% and Retail 26.50%, EMEA was 53.91% by point of origin, and no customer exceeds 10%, so cash flow rests on a large installed base serviced under maintenance and managed-service contracts whose tenor is not stated. Hardware refresh cycles at banks drive the product half of the business, which is why backlog and Banking product sales matter more to the credit than the services share alone.
Basis: calculation C13; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 7–13

The company is listed and has no sponsor. Funds associated with two holders own 46.9% between them, the share count fell 10.66% in 20 months, and the filings document repurchases but no related-party dealings or control rights. The 2024 refinancing left one secured note and one revolver that rank equally under first-priority liens on substantially all assets, with guarantees from subsidiaries whose names were not supplied, so creditors share one collateral pool and are exposed to the same shareholder-return decisions.
Basis: calculation C13; Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Revenue drivers, cost structure and cash conversion

Revenue is part recurring and part cyclical. Services grew 3.69% in nine months of 2026 and give visibility, while product revenue depends on bank and retailer refresh cycles; product backlog was USD 733.1 million at 2025-12-31 against USD 790.1 million a year earlier, and tariff recoveries of USD 10.0 million and pricing actions show partial pass-through of cost increases. Revenue is therefore predictable in services, cyclical in Banking products and growing but lower-margin in Retail, with limited pricing power in hardware where large competitors are named.
Basis: calculation C13; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 6–7; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Margins are exposed to component prices and overhead growth. The principal raw materials are steel, plastics and electronic parts bought from major suppliers, memory costs were elevated in 2026, selling and administrative expense grew 7.6% against revenue growth of 4.27%, and the restructuring programme has recognised USD 105.1 million of an expected USD 125 million, mainly for headcount, with savings not quantified. Operating leverage is therefore negative today, and margins can be defended only if the programme's savings arrive while product margins stay low.
Basis: calculation C13; 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 20

Capital intensity is low and cash conversion is volatile. Capital expenditure and capitalised software were USD 37.4 million and USD 24.3 million in FY2025, and USD 19.0 million and USD 20.2 million in nine months of 2026; FY2025 free cash flow of USD 239.0 million converted 73.34% of EBITDA, but nine-month 2026 free cash flow was an outflow of USD 68.5 million as inventories absorbed USD 51.1 million, deferred revenue fell 22.01% and discrete German tax payments took USD 32.3 million. Capital spending is modest against EBITDA, so conversion depends mainly on working capital and taxes.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 12; EX99.1_2026-09-30_dbd_ex-991q32026erlive-htm.htm.jsonl, page 7; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Competitive forces, external factors and SWOT

Competitive rivalry is high in hardware, where the annual report names NCR Atleos, Hyosung TNS and Hitachi Channel Solutions among Banking competitors and NCR Voyix and Toshiba among Retail competitors, and it is fragmented in services, where local and regional providers compete. Buyer power is likely high because banks and large retailers buy in volume, although no customer above 10% limits single-customer loss; supplier power is material for memory and electronic parts, whose elevated cost cut product margin; the threat of substitutes comes from payment options other than cash, which the company itself cites as a risk to ATM usage; and the threat of new entrants is not established from the sources.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dbd.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 7

External factors are material in three places. Tariffs and trade policy are cited risks and USD 10.0 million of tariff recoveries and pricing actions only partly offset the margin decline, the euro and German exposure matter because EMEA is 53.91% of revenue and Germany is 19.03%, and the German cash taxpayer position adds a recurring tax outflow that free cash flow did not carry before. The social and technological factor that matters most is the shift away from cash, which could reduce ATM usage over the life of the 2030 notes.
Basis: calculation C13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dbd.htm.jsonl, page 7; EX99.1_2026-09-30_dbd_ex-991q32026erlive-htm.htm.jsonl, page 7; Business & governance (CP-1A)

In credit terms the strengths are the services base, the customer spread, the refinanced structure and Retail momentum; the weaknesses are product margin, volatile cash conversion, discretionary buybacks and overhead growth; the opportunities that could improve credit quality are restructuring savings and large cash-recycler and self-checkout contract wins reported for the third quarter; and the threats are lower cash use, memory and tariff costs, and German cash taxes.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next year or two the company's own outlook is the reference point, and its full-year range implies fourth-quarter revenue of USD 1,053.3 million to USD 1,093.3 million, adjusted EBITDA of USD 142.5 million to USD 162.5 million and free cash flow of USD 226.2 million to USD 246.2 million; that is a change of -4.61% to -0.99% in revenue from a year earlier but 11.50% to 27.15% above the third quarter in adjusted EBITDA, so the plan relies on cost savings and seasonality rather than growth. Tailwinds are Retail wins, cash-recycler and branch-automation contracts and restructuring savings that are not yet quantified; headwinds are memory and tariff costs, German cash taxes and the lower backlog. Over the longer term the 2029 revolver and 2030 notes will need refinancing from a business whose Banking hardware demand faces structurally lower cash use, so cash discipline now determines refinancing terms later.

### Downside scenario

The downside scenario is specific to this issuer. The initiating condition is weaker Banking hardware demand and product margins staying at the third-quarter level of 22.2%, so fourth-quarter revenue lands at the low end of the implied range of USD 1,053.3 million and adjusted EBITDA below the USD 490 million floor. The first operating break is cash conversion: free cash flow falls short of the USD 226.2 million that the outlook needs and net debt stays near USD 764.4 million, or USD 783.7 million pro forma, instead of reversing. The financial transmission is that repurchases under the new USD 250.0 million programme would be paid from a liquidity cushion that was USD 470.7 million at 2026-09-30, before the repurchases through 2026-10-23, and net leverage would move further above 2.33x, or 2.39x pro forma. Management can suspend repurchases, cut capital spending and draw on the revolver, so liquidity distress is unlikely before the 2029-12-18 revolver maturity; the realistic outcome is a weaker rating trajectory and a harder refinancing in 2029, not a default.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Diebold Nixdorf presents an adequate business profile supported by a large services base, a customer spread with no customer above 10% and a refinanced structure, but credit quality is constrained by negative 2026 cash generation, discretionary buybacks and thin product margins. Cash-flow durability appears adequate in services and weak in products. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the fourth-quarter cash rebuild, the pace of repurchases and a stable ratings trend ahead of the 2029-12-18 revolver and 2030-03-31 note maturities. The primary downside path is a fourth-quarter cash shortfall with continued repurchases. Further analysis would require the executed debt documents, the proxy statement, fourth-quarter results and peer data. Top risk: shareholder returns that exceed free cash flow.
