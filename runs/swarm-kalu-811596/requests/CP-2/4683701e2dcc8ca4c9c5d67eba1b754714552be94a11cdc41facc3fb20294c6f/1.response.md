## Findings

### Bottom line

Kaiser's fundamental credit profile is adequate: trailing net leverage of 1.98x (2.00x pro forma for the dividend declared on 2026-10-13) and no maturity before October 2030 limit near-term refinancing risk, but earnings have slowed since the first half.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_kalu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15, 31; calculation CALC-1

Third-quarter Adjusted EBITDA was USD 84.0 million against an average of USD 147.4 million a quarter in the first half, and an outlook at the low end of the issuer's range implies leverage near 2.97x on the second-half run rate. Customer concentration, metal price timing and covenant and guarantor terms that are not supplied keep the view at adequate.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_kalu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 29, 31; calculation CALC-3; calculation CALC-4; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Key findings

Deleveraging is real and mostly earnings-driven: net debt of USD 921.4 million (USD 934.2 million pro forma for the dividend) is 1.98x trailing EBITDA (2.00x pro forma) against 3.38x at 2025-12-31, a fall of 1.40x of which 1.12x came from higher EBITDA and only 0.28x from lower net debt.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation CALC-1

Underlying earnings improved by more than the metal price lag explains: Adjusted EBITDA excluding the favorable lag was USD 315.8 million in the nine months of 2026 against USD 158.4 million a year earlier, and the lag's share of Adjusted EBITDA fell from 28.78% to 16.63%.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_kalu-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 29; calculation CALC-2

Momentum is slowing and the trailing figures flatter the run rate: Adjusted EBITDA fell from an average of USD 147.4 million a quarter in the first half to USD 84.0 million in the third quarter, and the outlook, which the issuer now places toward the low end of 45% to 55% growth, implies about USD 70.99 million in the fourth quarter if it lands at 45%, which puts leverage at 2.97x on the second-half run rate before the dividend.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_kalu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 29, 31; calculation CALC-3; calculation CALC-4

Free cash flow is the least proven dimension: it was USD -13.7 million in FY2024 and USD -25.5 million in FY2025 and reached USD 124.8 million on a trailing basis only after capital spending fell and USD 157.2 million of payables built, so cash conversion has not been shown through a metal price cycle.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_kalu-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; calculation CALC-10; calculation CALC-20

Business risk is moderate and customer-led: packaging is 39.95% and aerospace plate 31.00% of nine-month 2026 conversion revenue, but the two largest customers take 31% of net sales and the five largest 56%, so one contract loss would move earnings more than any single end-market cycle.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_kalu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 30, 116; calculation CALC-14; calculation CALC-17

Margin resilience rests on pass-through pricing, not cost flexibility: metal is 65.51% of 2025 cost of products sold and is passed to customers, yet Adjusted EBITDA over conversion revenue moved from 16.55% in FY2024 to 21.35% in FY2025, 29.58% in nine months 2026 and 19.13% in the third quarter, because metal lag, scrap spreads and plant outages drive much of the swing.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_kalu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 56, 58–59; 10-Q_2026-09-30_kalu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 29–30; calculation CALC-6; calculation CALC-9

Liquidity and the maturity profile are the strongest supports: liquidity of USD 687.2 million is 12.87x trailing cash interest, the revolver is undrawn, and no principal falls due before the revolver matures in October 2030 and the 2031 notes follow, although availability depends on receivable and inventory values that fall with metal prices.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_kalu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15, 31; 10-K_2025-12-31_kalu-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 35; calculation CALC-12

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an adequate profile is the earnings run rate: the first half of 2026 carried most of the improvement, third-quarter Adjusted EBITDA grew only 3.32% on net sales growth of 43.98%, and an annualised second half at the low end of the issuer's outlook leaves net debt of USD 921.4 million (USD 934.2 million pro forma for the dividend) at 2.97x, close to 2.96x if EBITDA simply returned to its 2025 level. The nine-month cash flow is also flattered by USD 157.2 million of payables, and a fall in aluminum prices would reverse that build and lower the borrowing base at the same moment, taking liquidity to USD 530.0 million before any base reduction.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_kalu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 29, 32; 10-K_2025-12-31_kalu-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 35; calculation CALC-4; calculation CALC-5; calculation CALC-8; calculation CALC-12

The strongest counter-argument is that the earnings improvement is not merely metal: excluding the lag, third-quarter Adjusted EBITDA was 59.47% higher and its margin on conversion revenue rose to 19.36% from 15.20%, packaging conversion revenue per pound rose 17.89%, and capital spending is falling. The view would improve with full-year 2026 free cash flow that stays positive after the payables build unwinds, a fourth quarter near the high end of the outlook, and disclosure that the covenant test is far from its trigger and that guarantors cover the operating subsidiaries. It would weaken with a fourth quarter at or below the low end, loss of one of the two largest customers, a metal price fall that cuts the borrowing base, or a stoppage at the Richmond plant.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_kalu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 29–31; calculation CALC-2; calculation CALC-7; calculation CALC-15; calculation CALC-17

### Strengths and weaknesses

The first strength is liquidity and a clear maturity runway: liquidity of USD 687.2 million, an undrawn revolver and no principal due before October 2030 mean a metal price shock or a customer stoppage would be absorbed before it became a refinancing problem, which supports a low near-term default pathway and gives management room to wait out the cycle.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_kalu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15, 31; calculation CALC-12

The second strength is the earnings recovery beyond metal lag: Adjusted EBITDA excluding lag nearly doubled in the nine months of 2026 and net leverage fell by 1.40x, so debt service capacity is stronger than a year ago and trailing cover of 8.73x on cash interest gives wide room, although the run rate is below the trailing figure.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_kalu-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 29; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation CALC-1; calculation CALC-2; calculation CALC-5

The third strength is pass-through pricing: index and spot pricing pass aluminum and alloy cost to customers, firm-price sales are only 11.43% of shipments, and packaging conversion revenue per pound rose 17.89%, so the business earns on conversion rather than on metal and most price swings do not reach EBITDA, except through lag and scrap spreads.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_kalu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_kalu-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 30; calculation CALC-15; calculation CALC-16

The fourth strength is a qualified position that is hard to enter: suppliers must pass rigorous qualification, some packaging approvals take multiple years, and the company has added capacity at Trentwood and a coating line at Warrick, so the recovery in volume after the 2025 outage has a barrier against new entrants and supports revenue durability.

### Company description

Kaiser is a single-segment producer of semi-fabricated specialty aluminum mill products, and its revenue splits over four cyclical end markets in which packaging is the largest by sales and aerospace plate the second-largest contributor to conversion revenue. About 70% of shipments go directly to manufacturers and tier one suppliers, mostly under long-term agreements with metal pass-through pricing, so volume is reasonably visible while margin visibility depends on a few large contracts and on metal timing; debt service capacity therefore rests on conversion revenue, not on net sales.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_kalu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 16; calculation CALC-14

### Ownership and group structure

Kaiser is a Nasdaq-listed holding company with no sponsor or controlling holder stated, operating through wholly owned subsidiaries of which four borrow under the revolver; holder-level ownership and the guarantor list are not supplied. The board is classified and chaired by the chief executive, and the incentive plan rewards total shareholder return and Adjusted EBITDA margin, so financial discipline rests on policy choices already visible: repurchases suspended since March 2020 and dividends at 20.60% of nine-month net income.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_kalu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 35; calculation CALC-19

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Volume and mix, not price, drive earned revenue: shipments rose 7.94% in the nine months of 2026 and conversion revenue per pound rose to USD 1.42 from USD 1.30, while net sales grew 46.40% on metal pass-through and conversion revenue only 17.68%. Firm-price sales are 11.43% of 2025 shipments, so pricing power shows in conversion margin rather than in net sales, and packaging conversion revenue per pound rose 17.89% as coated products ramped. Demand is cyclical in aerospace and automotive, concentrated in a few customers and exposed to substitution by composites, steel and plastics, according to the risk factors.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_kalu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26, 30; 10-K_2025-12-31_kalu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 26–27; calculation CALC-14; calculation CALC-15; calculation CALC-16

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Metal is 65.51% of 2025 cost of products sold and passes through to customers, leaving non-metal costs of USD 1,010.8 million (34.49%) made up of manufacturing 23.70%, plant overhead 6.19%, freight 2.87% and other 1.73%, with a high share of fixed cost in packaging that makes margin depend on plant utilization. Operating leverage cut both ways: in 2025 lower volume and start-up costs were outweighed by favorable metal consumption cost and mix, and in the third quarter of 2026 net sales grew 43.98% but Adjusted EBITDA only 3.32% because of lower scrap utilization, tighter scrap spreads, planned outages and freight, so margins can be defended against metal but not against operating deleverage.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_kalu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 56, 59; 10-Q_2026-09-30_kalu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 29–30; calculation CALC-8; calculation CALC-9

### Capital intensity and cash flow conversion

EBITDA has converted weakly into free cash flow: capital spending was 9.42% of conversion revenue and 1.12x depreciation in FY2025, free cash flow was -5.99% and -8.19% of reported EBITDA in FY2024 and FY2025, and the trailing 26.78% follows lower capital spending and a net working capital outflow of USD 74.6 million that is helped by the payables build. Capital spending is discretionary and the plan of USD 130.0 million implies USD 57.8 million in the fourth quarter, so flexibility exists if volumes weaken; leases, pension and retiree obligations and the environmental accrual sit outside debt but are small beside it, according to Financial foundation.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_kalu-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 32, 34; calculation CALC-10; calculation CALC-11; calculation CALC-20

### Porter's Five Forces

Rivalry is moderate and tied to scale: some competitors for aerospace plate and packaging are substantially larger, and imports and trade orders can alter pricing, so margin cannot be assumed to hold in a downturn. New entrants face multi-year customer qualification and heavy equipment investment, which protects volume, although the filing warns that new parties may qualify. Substitutes matter by end market, with composites and titanium in aircraft, steel in vehicles and glass or plastic in packaging, and a high aluminum price can push customers to switch. Buyer power is the largest force because five customers take 56% of net sales, though long-term agreements and index pricing soften it. Supplier power is moderate: five suppliers provide 82% of primary aluminum and Warrick depends on one party for essential resources, so a supply failure would hit output more than margin.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_kalu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 25–27, 30, 33–34; 10-K_2025-12-31_kalu-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 116; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### PEST factors

Trade policy is material to credit because the filing lists duties and tariffs among the drivers of primary aluminum pricing, and the Midwest premium rose from USD 0.19 to USD 0.59 per pound, which with the LME price lifted the average to USD 1.78 in 2025 from USD 1.29 in 2024, up 37.98%; that inflates receivables, inventory and the borrowing base on the way up and reverses on the way down. Fuel-efficiency standards and defense budgets move aerospace and automotive demand, and sustainability preferences support aluminum packaging, but the filings give no quantified effect, so these are monitored rather than weighted.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_kalu-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 28–29, 34; calculation CALC-18

### Credit-translated SWOT

The credit strengths are liquidity with a long maturity runway, earnings recovery beyond metal lag, pass-through pricing, qualification barriers and moderate financial policy; the weaknesses are customer concentration, metal and working capital timing, unproven free cash flow, margin volatility and an opaque creditor position. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are the coated packaging ramp, aerospace plate volume on the added Trentwood capacity and lower capital spending; threats are a metal price fall that shrinks the borrowing base, a customer loss, a plant stoppage and refinancing at higher yields after 2030.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

For the next 12 to 24 months the issuer expects conversion revenue growth toward the high end of 10% to 15% and Adjusted EBITDA growth toward the low end of 45% to 55%, which implies a fourth quarter below the third because of planned outages, execution, customer year-end inventory alignment and metal headwinds, while the coated packaging ramp, aerospace plate volume and lower capital spending are tailwinds and weaker automotive extrusion volume is a headwind. Over three to five years the maturity profile is benign until the revolver in October 2030 and the notes in 2031 and 2034, and refinancing cost will depend on yields that currently value the notes below principal; management's longer-term demand view is stated in its own words and is not tested here.

### Qualitative downside scenario

The initiating condition is a fall in aluminum prices together with customer destocking in aerospace and automotive. The first operational break is metal price lag turning unfavorable and scrap spreads staying tight, which cuts Adjusted EBITDA toward the 2025 level and puts net debt of USD 921.4 million (USD 934.2 million pro forma for the dividend) at 2.96x, or 4.03x at the 2024 level. The financial transmission is a reversal of the USD 157.2 million payables build and falling receivable and inventory values, which lowers the borrowing base and takes liquidity from USD 687.2 million to USD 530.0 million before any base reduction; trailing cover would still be 5.83x at the 2025 EBITDA level. Management can cut capital spending below the USD 130.0 million plan, suspend the dividend of about USD 12.8 million a quarter and draw the undrawn revolver, and no principal is due before October 2030, so resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress. The first observable signals would be fourth-quarter Adjusted EBITDA below USD 70.99 million, a fall in the Midwest Transaction Price and renewed revolver borrowing. Primary downside path: a metal price fall that reverses working capital and shrinks the borrowing base while earnings return to the 2025 level.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Kaiser presents an adequate business profile supported by liquidity, a long maturity runway, pass-through pricing and a recovery in underlying earnings, but credit quality is constrained by customer concentration, metal and working capital timing, unproven free cash flow and slowing quarterly earnings. Cash-flow durability appears adequate rather than strong because trailing free cash flow of USD 124.8 million rests on lower capital spending and a payables build. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on aluminum prices through the borrowing base, on the cost of refinancing the 2031 and 2034 notes and on covenant terms not supplied. The primary downside path is a metal price fall with customer destocking that reverses working capital and returns earnings to the 2025 level, which would raise net leverage to 2.96x while liquidity stays ample. Further analysis would require the credit agreement, the indentures, the proxy statement, peer data and the fourth-quarter result. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: metal price timing and customer concentration.
