## Findings

### Bottom line

Karman's leverage rose to 4.79x reported EBITDA at 30 June 2026 from 4.17x at year-end 2025 because acquisition debt arrived before the acquired earnings and operating cash flow was negative in 2025 and in the first half of 2026. Deleveraging therefore rests on earnings growth and backlog conversion, not on cash generation.
Basis: calculation C3; calculation C6; 10-Q_2026-06-30_krmn-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-06-30_krmn-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2025-12-31_krmn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 112–115

Net debt was USD 705,196 thousand at 30 June 2026 against USD 465,189 thousand at year-end 2025. With the Seemann acquisition paid for after year-end, pro forma year-end net debt was USD 675,189 thousand, or 5.63x year-end reported EBITDA that holds none of the acquired earnings. Finance leases of USD 102,139 thousand are outside these figures.

### Key findings

Leverage has risen on every measure. Total debt over reported EBITDA was 3.66x at year-end 2024, 4.17x at year-end 2025 and 4.79x on the twelve months to 30 June 2026, and net of cash it was 3.55x, 3.88x and 4.47x. On the issuer's adjusted EBITDA, net leverage was 3.27x, 3.20x and 3.94x. Pro forma for the Seemann cash paid on 3 February 2026, year-end 2025 net leverage would have been 5.63x on reported EBITDA. Total debt grew by USD 257,789 thousand in the first half of 2026, driven by a USD 265.0 million term loan increase that funded the Seemann acquisition, while the earnings base holds the acquired business only from 3 February 2026. The company states no pro forma EBITDA, so the 30 June 2026 ratio overstates run-rate leverage by an amount that is not established, and the only financial covenant, a springing test that applies when revolver loans exceed a threshold the filings do not state, was not required because the revolver was undrawn.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C6; calculation C10; calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-06-30_krmn-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16; 10-Q_2026-06-30_krmn-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19; 10-Q_2026-06-30_krmn-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-K_2025-12-31_krmn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 101; 10-K_2025-12-31_krmn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 107–108

Interest cover is modest but improving. Reported EBITDA covered cash interest 2.05x in 2024, 2.67x in 2025 and 2.97x on the twelve months to 30 June 2026, with cash interest paid of USD 53,144 thousand over that period. The August 2026 amendment cut the term loan margin by 50 bp, which lowers the interest burden but is in no period figure yet.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C5; calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-06-30_krmn-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-06-30_krmn-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-K_2025-12-31_krmn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 115

Cash generation does not support deleveraging yet. Operating cash flow was negative at USD 22,119 thousand in 2025 and negative at USD 2,975 thousand in the first half of 2026, although adjusted EBITDA was USD 145,302 thousand in 2025, mainly because contract assets absorbed cash: they rose USD 49,076 thousand in 2025 and stood at USD 187,120 thousand at 30 June 2026. After capital expenditure, free cash flow was negative at USD 42,455 thousand in 2025 and negative at USD 24,840 thousand in the first half of 2026, so the cash shortfall and the acquisitions were funded by borrowing and, in 2025, by equity. Contract liabilities, the customer advances that fund part of working capital, were USD 22,814 thousand at year-end 2025 against USD 29,868 thousand a year earlier, a smaller float from customers.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C13; calculation C14; 10-K_2025-12-31_krmn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 113–115; 10-Q_2026-06-30_krmn-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 2–4

Liquidity is adequate near term and only scheduled amortization falls due before 2030. Cash was USD 51,741 thousand and the undrawn revolver USD 150.0 million, giving liquidity of USD 201,741 thousand at 30 June 2026 against USD 83,959 thousand at year-end 2025. Scheduled principal repayments were USD 5,050 thousand a year to 2030 at year-end 2025, a schedule that predates the February 2026 term loan increase and has not been restated, and the revolver matures in 2030 and the term loan on 1 April 2032. No letters of credit were found, and the conditions for drawing the revolver are not supplied.
Basis: calculation C7; calculation C11; calculation C14; 10-K_2025-12-31_krmn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 140–141; 10-Q_2026-06-30_krmn-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-06-30_krmn-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 18

Definitions move the leverage figure materially. Finance leases of USD 102,139 thousand at 30 June 2026 sit on their own balance-sheet lines and are outside total debt; adding them lifts net leverage on reported EBITDA from 4.47x to 5.11x. Adjusted EBITDA exceeds reported EBITDA by USD 25,476 thousand in 2025, made up of acquisition costs, restructuring, lender fees, share-based compensation and legal settlements, none with a stated realisation status.

The pro forma view is limited. Counting the USD 210,000 thousand of net cash paid for Seemann after year-end, net debt would have been USD 675,189 thousand, which is 5.63x year-end reported EBITDA that holds none of the acquired earnings. The pending Walker acquisition for about USD 94.0 million is not reflected, and its closing and funding are not established.
Basis: calculation C10; calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-06-30_krmn-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-06-30_krmn-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 29

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest contrary case is that the rise in leverage is timing: the Seemann debt was raised in February 2026 while its earnings are in the base for only part of the period, the adjusted EBITDA margin has stayed near 30%, backlog reached USD 1.3 billion at 30 June 2026 from USD 801,056 thousand at year-end 2025, and net leverage on adjusted EBITDA is 3.94x. That case wins if pro forma earnings show net leverage falling toward the year-end level and if contract assets stop growing so that operating cash flow turns positive after capital expenditure. It loses if working capital keeps absorbing cash, if the Walker acquisition adds debt before earnings, or if the credit agreement's covenant definition proves tighter than the filings' summary.
Basis: calculation C4; calculation C13; 10-K_2025-12-31_krmn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 99; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_krmn-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2; 10-Q_2026-06-30_krmn-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 2
