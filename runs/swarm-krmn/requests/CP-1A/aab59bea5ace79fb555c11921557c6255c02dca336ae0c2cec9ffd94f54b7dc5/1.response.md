## Findings

### Bottom line

Karman is a sole- and single-source supplier to missile and space programs with a growing backlog, but three customers supply about half of revenue, acquisitions are debt-funded and internal control weaknesses remain, so governance risk is medium and provisional.

### Key findings

Demand visibility is strong but concentrated in a few prime contractors. The company holds sole- or single-source positions on many programs and estimates that no single program exceeded 12% of revenue in 2025, yet three customers supplied 51.5% of revenue in 2025 and two held 40.7% of receivables at year-end 2025. In the first half of 2026 two customers were above 10% of revenue and held 29.7% of receivables at 30 June 2026. A delay or loss at one prime would hit earnings and cash collection together. Backlog, the value of existing contracts less amounts already invoiced, rose to USD 1.3 billion at 30 June 2026 from USD 801,056 thousand at year-end 2025, which supports 2026 revenue, although the U.S. government may terminate contracts at its convenience.

The acquisition program is the main driver of governance risk. Four acquisitions since April 2025 carried a combined fair value of consideration of USD 470,428 thousand and were funded by term loan increases, with some shares; a fifth, Walker, was agreed in July 2026 for about USD 94.0 million. The term loan started at USD 300.0 million and was raised by USD 75.0 million, USD 130.0 million and USD 265.0 million, and USD 764.0 million was outstanding at 30 June 2026. Debt arrives before acquired earnings, and integration costs are added back to adjusted EBITDA, so leverage tolerance is high. Seemann contributed USD 63.0 million of revenue in the first half of 2026, 18.90% of the total, but no pro forma earnings are given for any acquisition, so payback cannot be tested.

The secondary offerings in July 2025 and May 2026, priced at USD 49.00 and USD 61.00 a share, were sales by existing stockholders with no proceeds to the company, so they changed holder liquidity but not the capital structure.

Reporting controls are a clear governance weakness. Management reports that internal control over financial reporting was not effective at year-end 2025 and that disclosure controls were not effective at 30 June 2026 because of the same material weaknesses, which led to material adjustments to share-based compensation in 2023 and immaterial ones in 2025 and in the first half of 2026. The company says its information technology controls work is complete and the other remediation is still being implemented and tested, and the Seemann purchase price allocation was revised in the second quarter. The audit opinion on the statements is unqualified and gives no view on internal control, so lenders should treat interim figures as provisional until remediation is tested.

Who controls Karman is not established. The documents describe a classified board whose directors can be removed only for cause by a supermajority, a stockholder rights agreement and a registration rights agreement with certain investors, and unnamed selling stockholders, but they give no holdings, board seats or consent rights. The chief executive changed on 23 March 2026. Without the stockholder agreement and the proxy statement, sponsor willingness and capacity to extract value cannot be assessed, so the risk level rests on issuer behavior alone and is provisional.

Operating pressure points are fixed-price exposure and working capital. Multi-year fixed-price contracts leave cost overruns with the company, contract assets rose USD 49,076 thousand in 2025 and absorbed operating cash, and capital expenditure of USD 21,865 thousand in the first half of 2026 was 6.56% of revenue against 4.31% in 2025. Growth is therefore consuming cash ahead of billing while debt has funded the acquisitions.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for lower risk is the behavior record: no distributions or fees to owners are documented, lenders accepted two margin cuts and a larger revolver, equity was raised at the initial public offering, none of the documents describes an amendment or exchange that disadvantages lenders, and backlog reached USD 1.3 billion. That case wins if the proxy statement and stockholder agreements show no controlling holder with extraction incentives and if pro forma earnings show the acquired businesses covering the new debt. The case for higher risk wins if the stockholder agreements give a holder control over financing, if the Walker acquisition is funded with further term loan before earnings, or if the control weaknesses lead to restated figures.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_krmn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 87; 10-K_2025-12-31_krmn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 157; 10-Q_2026-06-30_krmn-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 18–21; 10-Q_2026-06-30_krmn-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-Q_2026-06-30_krmn-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 41–43; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_krmn-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2

### Limitations

- The proxy statement and the stockholder agreements are not supplied, so the ownership, board and fee findings are provisional and could move the governance risk level up or down.
- The credit agreement is not supplied, so which distributions, investments and new debt the company may make is not established, and the conclusion that no creditor-adverse behavior is documented covers only the behavior actually described.
- Customer and program names and contract terms are not disclosed, so the concentration conclusion rests on the company's own percentages and program estimate and cannot say which primes carry the revenue.
- Management descriptions such as market-leading position and difficult requalification are the company's own language and are not independently verified, so the revenue visibility conclusion is bounded by them.
- Developments after the second-quarter 2026 report, including whether Walker closed and how it was funded, are not covered, so the acquisition-appetite conclusion may be out of date.
