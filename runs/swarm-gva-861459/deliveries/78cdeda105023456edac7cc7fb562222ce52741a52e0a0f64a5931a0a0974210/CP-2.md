## Findings

### Bottom line

Granite's credit profile is adequate: contracted public backlog, moderate leverage and USD 1,069.09 million of liquidity cover near-term claims, but five acquisitions and a rising interest burden leave limited room for a margin or funding shock.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation calc-01

Public customers are 83.2% of the backlog and fixed-price contracts were 91.5% of unearned revenue at the end of 2025, so a funding pause or cost overrun reaches EBITDA directly. Net debt is 1.84x last-twelve-month EBITDA, but cash interest cover of 8.57x does not yet include coupons on the 2034 senior notes, and USD 373.75 million of 3.25% convertible principal is cash-settled if holders convert.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a better view is that earnings and cash flow have grown faster than debt since the end of 2025: last-twelve-month EBITDA is 31.18% above FY2025 while total debt is 16.20% higher, EBITDA grew 43.20% in nine months 2026, last-twelve-month free cash flow was 0.28x total debt, backlog grew 6.97% since year-end, and net debt to EBITDA of 1.84x is below the 2.10x of 2026-03-31. The adequate view would strengthen with two quarters of free cash flow above 50% of EBITDA, a stable Materials margin, no further debt-funded acquisition and a federal reauthorization before 2026-12-11. It would weaken with a revolver draw to fund operations, cash conversion below 50% of EBITDA, total debt to EBITDA above the 3.11x of 2026-06-30, or conversion of the 3.25% convertible notes before the 2030 refinancing is arranged.

### Company description and credit linkage

Granite is a vertically integrated US civil contractor and construction materials producer, listed on the New York Stock Exchange and headquartered in Watsonville, California. The Construction segment, 83.4% of nine-month 2026 revenue, builds highways, bridges, rail, water, power and defence-related projects, predominantly for federal, state and local agencies. The Materials segment mines aggregates and makes asphalt, partly for Granite's own projects and partly for third parties. Revenue was USD 4,424.379 million in FY2025 and USD 5,300.038 million over the last twelve months to 2026-09-30, a rise of 19.79% that includes five acquisitions since August 2025 for USD 970.9 million in cash.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 5–8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 35–36, 38; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation calc-15

The credit link is visibility with an execution tail. Public customers are 83.2% of committed and awarded projects, the largest customer, the California Department of Transportation, was 10.1% of FY2025 revenue after 14.2% in FY2024, and unearned revenue on executed contracts is USD 4,852.921 million, of which about USD 3.5 billion is expected within twelve months, equal to 66.04% of last-twelve-month revenue, so creditors can see about two-thirds of a year's revenue and the rest must come from awards not yet in unearned revenue and from short-cycle work. Pricing is mostly fixed, however, so cash-generation durability depends on estimating and cost control rather than on contractual pass-through, and the debt-funded acquisitions have added integration risk to a business whose reported EBITDA has to be calculated because Granite states none.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 40; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation calc-06; calculation calc-07; calculation calc-16

### Ownership and group structure

Granite is a listed Delaware corporation with 44,130,293 shares outstanding at 2026-10-23. No controlling holder, sponsor or parent is identified in the filings, and board composition, large-holder percentages and executive pay sit in a proxy statement that was not supplied, so the absence of a controlling owner is an observation of what the filings do not say and not a tested finding. The chief executive has served since June 2021, the chief financial officer since September 2024, and the chief executive became sole chief operating decision maker when the chief operating officer retired on 2025-07-04, which concentrates top-level decisions at a time of integration.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 5, 21–22; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The group structure matters for creditors. The borrowers under the credit agreement are Granite Construction Incorporated, Granite Construction Company and GILC Incorporated, the senior notes due 2034 are unsecured and guaranteed by each domestic subsidiary that is a borrower or guarantor under that agreement, and the convertible notes are issued by the parent with no stated guarantee or collateral. Consolidated construction joint ventures, ten active projects with Granite's share between 50.0% and 70.0%, hold USD 149.707 million of cash, of which Granite's own portion is USD 93.3 million, that is generally not available until distributed and carry joint and several performance obligations, so part of the reported cash and backlog belongs to partners. The collateral package, guarantor list and restricted payment capacity are not itemised, so the ranking of unsecured creditors behind the USD 600.000 million secured term loan is a description of the filings and not a legal conclusion.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21–22, 25, 30–31, 46; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

Dividends of USD 0.13 per share a quarter together with repurchases of USD 55.772 million in nine months 2026 took 18.01% of operating cash flow, USD 121.0 million of the 2022 repurchase authorisation remains, and Granite settled the 3.75% convertible notes largely in cash, funded by new senior notes and cash on hand. Creditors are not worse off for that choice in the filings read, since no priming, uptier or drop-down is described, but the choice increased cash claims ahead of equity and shows that management will use debt capacity when it sees value.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is project-based, backlog-supported and public-funding-driven. The five economic drivers Granite names are the health of the US economy and access to labor and supplies, federal, state and local funding, population growth, the need to replace aging infrastructure and commodity prices. The five-year federal surface transportation authorization, which the company says increased highway, bridge and transit funding to its highest level in more than six decades, expired on 2026-09-30 and a short-term extension runs through 2026-12-11, while California's SB-1 funding has no sunset provision. Public work was 78.3% of nine-month 2026 Construction revenue against 71.0% a year earlier, and private Construction revenue fell to USD 749.710 million from USD 786.333 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 38–40

The mechanism is that funding lets agencies issue lettings, lettings become awards, and awards become unearned revenue that Granite converts into revenue over about a year; federal lettings are the swing input and state and local measures offset them. The evidence on conversion is mixed: committed and awarded projects grew 6.97% from 2025-12-31 to USD 7,455.470 million, but the third-quarter rise was only USD 36.6 million, and the unearned revenue portion fell from USD 5,056.462 million at 2026-06-30 to USD 4,852.921 million while other awards, which include unexercised options and construction manager general contractor work, rose to USD 2,602.549 million. The credit implication is that near-term revenue is well supported but the 2027 revenue base depends on awards that are not yet contracts. The filing describes public work as approximately 85% of backlog while its own table shows 83.2% at 2026-09-30 and 86.9% at 2025-12-31; this analysis uses the table.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 38, 40–41; calculation calc-06

Pricing power is limited because contracts are fixed unit price or fixed price. Fixed unit price contracts were 56.9% and fixed price 34.6% of unearned revenue at 2025-12-31, so 91.5% of unearned revenue puts cost increases from inflation, tariffs or inefficiency on Granite unless the contract allows recovery, against 92.3% a year earlier. Customer concentration is low except for the California Department of Transportation at 10.1% of FY2025 revenue, no other customer exceeded 10% in any of the last three years, and the customers are public agencies that award competitively, which gives stability of payment but little room to raise prices. Revenue is therefore predictable and regulated, moderately cyclical through the 16.8% private share of backlog, and exposed to budget decisions rather than to substitution.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40; calculation calc-06; calculation calc-07

### Cost structure and margin resilience

The filings do not split fixed from variable costs, but the workforce flexes with the season. Hourly employees ranged from about 2,500 to 5,200 during 2025 against about 2,500 salaried employees, four subsidiaries are party to collective bargaining agreements, and selling, general and administrative expenses were 8.5% of nine-month 2026 revenue, down from 9.3%, so operating leverage worked in Granite's favour as revenue grew and EBITDA margin rose from 8.33% in FY2024 to 11.01% over the last twelve months. The same leverage works in reverse if revenue stalls, because the salaried base and the fixed overhead do not flex at the speed of hourly labor.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9, 34; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 41; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation calc-04

Input costs are the main margin risk. Granite buys aggregates, cement, diesel and gasoline, liquid asphalt, natural gas, propane, resin and steel, and states that fuel and liquid asphalt prices fluctuate with oil, and the filings name tariffs and the conflict in Iran's effect on oil prices as sources of uncertainty. Granite's mitigants are fixed-price forward purchases of oil-related inputs, energy surcharges, schedule adjustments and heating oil derivatives to December 2027, which produced a gain of USD 5.3 million in nine months 2026. The evidence that mitigation is partial is the Materials segment, where energy surcharges offset diesel but cut gross margin from 25.2% to 21.0% in the third quarter; Construction margin rose to 18.6% over the same period, so the cost shock has not shown up in Construction results to date, although how much of the fixed-price backlog is protected by forward purchases is not stated.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 30; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 39, 41; calculation calc-08

Margins can be defended under moderate volume pressure but not under a combined cost and volume shock, which is an analyst judgement from the cost structure above. Estimate revisions on larger projects added USD 58.3 million against USD 18.5 million of reductions in nine months 2026, which supports estimating quality, and Papich Construction's gross loss of USD 6.3 million shows that acquired businesses can still reverse that. Integration costs are small to date, with other costs, net of USD 15.441 million against USD 38.698 million a year earlier.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 13–17, 39; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Capital intensity is moderate. Purchases of property and equipment were USD 138.270 million in FY2025, 3.13% of revenue, and USD 89.879 million in nine months 2026, 2.17% of revenue, and 2026 guidance is USD 140.0 million to USD 160.0 million including about USD 50.0 million of strategic materials investment. The company says the timing and amount of capital expenditure can vary with project progress and business outlook, so there is some flexibility over the maturity wall, but the maintenance and growth split is not stated.

Lease liabilities of USD 191.510 million are outside the debt figure, distributions to joint venture partners were USD 13.248 million in nine months 2026, and the first and fourth quarters are seasonally weakest, so cash flow is uneven within the year even when the full year converts well.

### Competitive forces

Competitive rivalry is high and local. Granite competes with firms from small local contractors to national and international groups, some larger than itself, and few compete in all of its markets. Competitors move between the public and private sectors when one is reduced, which the company says can reduce revenue growth or pressure margins; with 83.2% of backlog public, a slowdown in private work would push private competitors into public lettings and could compress bid margins on fixed-price work.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40

The threat of new entrants is low in materials and moderate in construction. Aggregate mining and asphalt production need significant capital and face zoning and permitting barriers, so Granite's owned reserves and plants protect its materials margin and supply security, while smaller construction projects need little capital and entry is relatively easy. Larger projects need more capital and bonding, and about USD 4.2 billion of the USD 7.5 billion backlog is bonded through three co-sureties, so surety capacity, which depends on capitalisation and working capital, is a quiet barrier and a dependency.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 40, 48

Substitution is not a credit issue because aggregates and asphalt are used in nearly all construction and public infrastructure has no practical substitute. Buyer power is high because public agencies award competitively under fixed-price and fixed unit price terms and may modify or cancel contracts, but no customer other than the California Department of Transportation exceeds 10% of revenue, which limits dependence. Supplier power is moderate: the prices of diesel, liquid asphalt, steel and cement and subcontractor capacity are outside Granite's control, vertical integration reduces exposure on aggregates, and subcontractors that cannot obtain surety bonds add performance risk.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 11–12, 14, 18, 20, 30; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 39–41

### Macro and policy factors

Political and regulatory factors are a primary credit driver. Roughly 85% of committed and awarded projects are public work funded from federal, state, regional and local revenues, the federal authorization expired on 2026-09-30 and only a short extension through 2026-12-11 is in place, and Congress is still working on fiscal year 2027 appropriations. The mechanism is that a gap in federal obligations delays lettings, the first effect would be on new awards with timing the filings do not state, and the implication for creditors is a slower revenue base after the current backlog burns off. State and local measures, including California SB-1, offset part of that risk.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 38–39

Economic factors are also material. Inflation, tariffs and oil prices drive input costs on fixed-price work, the term loan floats over SOFR with USD 350 million of it swapped to a fixed rate to January 2029, and USD 250 million is unhedged, so interest cost rises with rates on that portion. Social and technological factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver in the provided materials beyond labor availability, which is covered with cost structure.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 38–39; 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 79

### Financial profile and credit quality

The main credit supports are scale, visibility and cash conversion. Revenue of USD 5,300.038 million over the last twelve months, an EBITDA margin of 11.01%, free cash flow of USD 443.155 million and net debt of 1.84x EBITDA place Granite at a moderate leverage level, with EBITDA covering cash interest 8.57x and free cash flow earned after that interest, and the replacement of the 3.75% convertible notes with 2034 senior notes showed that the market was open to Granite in June 2026. The constraints are fixed-price risk, integration, a rising interest burden and a structure whose covenants and collateral cannot be assessed from the filings.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation calc-03; calculation calc-04; calculation calc-05

The trend is toward higher earnings and higher debt. Total debt carrying value rose from USD 739.048 million at the end of 2024 to USD 1,339.129 million at the end of 2025 and USD 1,556.025 million at 2026-09-30, while EBITDA rose from USD 333.694 million to USD 444.879 million and USD 583.582 million, so leverage peaked at 3.11x on 2026-06-30 and is 2.67x now. ### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

The short-term outlook for the next 12 to 24 months is supported by backlog and pressured by timing. Tailwinds are the USD 4,852.921 million of unearned revenue, state and local funding including SB-1, higher Construction margins in the third quarter and a USD 140.0 million to USD 160.0 million capital program that is covered by operating cash flow. Headwinds are the end of the federal extension on 2026-12-11, the first coupon on the 2034 senior notes on 2026-12-15, the term loan amortisation from the quarter ending 2026-12-31, the convertible window through 2026-12-31, the weak first and fourth quarters and the diesel and tariff exposure on fixed-price work.

The medium-term outlook for three to five years turns on reauthorization of federal surface transportation funding, integration of the five acquisitions and the 2029 to 2030 refinancing. ### Downside scenario

The scenario is a lapse or long delay in federal reauthorization after 2026-12-11 combined with a diesel or tariff cost shock during the 2027 construction season. The first operating break would be in federal lettings, which would slow new awards while the existing backlog is worked off, and on fixed-price work the cost shock would turn estimate revisions from positive to negative. Illustratively, if EBITDA margin returned to its FY2024 level of 8.33% on last-twelve-month revenue of USD 5,300.038 million, EBITDA would be USD 441.493 million, 24.35% below the current USD 583.582 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17, 38–39; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation calc-13

The financial transmission is manageable but not trivial. On that EBITDA, total debt to EBITDA would be 3.52x and net debt to EBITDA 2.44x, cover would be 6.48x on last-twelve-month cash interest and 4.15x if a full year of the senior notes coupon of USD 38.250 million is added as a rough allowance (Financial foundation states no pro forma interest, and this is analyst arithmetic on the stated rate and principal), and free cash flow would fall to about USD 301.066 million before any tax offset, to USD 163.442 million if the USD 137.624 million contract liability float reversed, and to USD 125.192 million after the senior notes coupon. Management levers are repurchases of USD 55.772 million in nine months 2026, the timing of the USD 50.0 million strategic materials program, acquisition spending and seasonal labor. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress in this scenario because free cash flow stays positive and liquidity exceeds the convertible principal, but headroom against covenants is not established, and the secured revolver is the instrument that would absorb any shortfall ahead of the unsecured notes. ### Overall credit view

Overall, Granite presents an adequate business profile supported by a USD 7.5 billion public-weighted backlog, vertical integration into materials and moderate leverage, but credit quality is constrained by fixed-price estimating risk, acquisition integration, a rising cash interest burden and dependence on federal reauthorization. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow has been 68.38% to 75.94% of EBITDA in the periods measured, although part of that rests on billings ahead of revenue. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on market access in 2029 for the USD 891.25 million falling due in 2030 and on covenant headroom that the filings do not disclose. The primary downside path is a lapse in federal funding combined with a cost shock on fixed-price work, which would cut EBITDA margin while the convertible principal and new coupons call on cash. Further analysis would require the credit agreement and indentures, the compliance certificate, the proxy statement, pro forma EBITDA for the acquired businesses and a peer set, so no comparison with typical leveraged issuers is made.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation calc-05; calculation calc-09; calculation calc-13

Top risk: a federal funding lapse combined with cost overruns on fixed-price contracts, which would reduce EBITDA while debt service rises.
