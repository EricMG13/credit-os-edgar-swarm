## Findings

### Bottom line

Granite is a vertically integrated heavy-civil contractor whose public-sector backlog gives creditors good earnings visibility, but five acquisitions since August 2025 and a debt-funded refinancing have made integration and financial policy the credit question. Public customers account for 83.2% of the USD 7.5 billion backlog, and the largest customer was 10.1% of 2025 revenue.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 38, 40; 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 6

Shareholder cash returns are modest against cash generation, no controlling holder or sponsor is identified, and board and ownership detail sits in a proxy statement that was not supplied, so the view of financial policy rests on observed behaviour rather than on documented limits.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6; 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 3; calculation calc-05

### Key findings

Revenue visibility is strong but depends on continued public funding. Public customers are 83.2% of committed and awarded projects of USD 7.5 billion, the largest customer was 10.1% of 2025 revenue, and about USD 3.5 billion of unearned revenue is expected to be recognised within twelve months, so near-term cash flow is largely contracted. The federal highway authorization expired on 2026-09-30 and a short-term extension runs only to 2026-12-11, so the pace of new federal awards is the first thing to watch.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 38, 40; 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 6

Vertical integration supports cost control and supply but does not insulate every line. Granite owns and leases aggregate reserves and owns processing plants, and Construction gross margin rose to 18.6% in the third quarter from 16.5% a year earlier, but Materials gross margin fell to 21.0% from 25.2% because energy surcharges only offset higher diesel costs, so the materials cushion is thinner than the annual figures suggest.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 12

Fixed-price exposure is large but recent estimate revisions have been favourable. Fixed unit price and fixed price contracts were 91.5% of unearned revenue at the end of 2025, so cost inflation and tariffs fall on Granite unless contracts allow recovery. Nine-month 2026 revisions on projects that each moved profit by USD 5.0 million or more added USD 58.3 million against USD 18.5 million of reductions, which supports estimating quality for now.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17; calculation calc-02

Acquisitions are the main change to the credit and integration is unproven. Five deals since August 2025 cost USD 970.9 million in cash; Warren Paving added USD 29.0 million of gross profit on USD 215.1 million of nine-month 2026 revenue, but Papich Construction posted a gross loss of USD 6.3 million, and Kenny Seng Construction earned USD 6.7 million of gross profit on USD 74.8 million of revenue after completion in April 2026. Purchase accounting for the 2026 deals is still preliminary, and the two largest 2025 deals were excluded from the 2025 assessment of internal controls.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–15; 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 82, 89; calculation calc-01

Financial policy looks measured, but the 2026 convertible settlement shows a willingness to add debt to avoid dilution. Cash dividends and share repurchases in nine months 2026 equal 18.01% of operating cash flow, with USD 121.0 million of repurchase authorisation left. Granite also paid USD 289.7 million in March to exchange USD 100.0 million of convertible principal and USD 714.9 million in August to settle the remaining USD 273.7 million, paying cash plus a small share issuance, funded by USD 600.0 million of senior notes and cash on hand.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6, 28–30, 49; calculation calc-05

Governance disclosure is thin but shows no creditor-adverse conduct. No controlling holder or sponsor is identified, the chief executive has served since 2021 and the chief financial officer since 2024, and a civil case between the Securities and Exchange Commission and a former officer was resolved in January 2026 with legal costs falling as a result. The auditor has served since 1982 and internal controls were assessed as effective, although the acquired businesses excluded from that assessment were 17.5% of total tangible assets and 4.8% of revenue.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 21–22, 70, 81–82, 87–89; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 42

Structure matters for recovery: the term loan and revolver are senior secured, while the senior notes due 2034 are unsecured and guaranteed by domestic subsidiaries, so unsecured creditors rank behind secured lenders on any collateral. The collateral package and the guarantor list are not itemised in the filings.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25, 30–31; 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 140

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against concern is that the acquisition programme has so far coincided with higher margins and a larger backlog: Construction gross margin rose to 18.6% in the third quarter, committed and awarded projects grew 6.97% from the end of 2025 to USD 7.5 billion at 2026-09-30, and shareholder cash returns were only 18.01% of operating cash flow. The view that integration and financial policy are the credit question would be overturned by two further quarters in which Papich Construction returns to a gross profit and Materials margin stabilises. It would be reinforced by another debt-funded acquisition before the term loan begins amortising in the quarter ending 2026-12-31, by repurchases that rise toward free cash flow, or by a lapse in federal highway funding after 2026-12-11.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 13–14, 31, 38, 40–41; calculation calc-05; calculation calc-06

  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 3

  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25, 31
- Purchase accounting for the 2026 acquisitions is preliminary and their contributions cover only part-periods, so the conclusion that integration is unproven could move either way as the measurement periods close.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–10
- Segment EBITDA is not stated, so margin conclusions use gross profit and operating income, and a shift of corporate costs between segments would change the comparison of Construction and Materials.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 35–36
- The backlog includes unexercised options and awards that customers may cancel or modify, so the revenue-visibility conclusion is weaker for the portion that is not unearned revenue on executed contracts.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40; 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Granite completed five acquisitions for USD 970.9 million in cash between August 2025 and September 2026: Warren Paving for USD 540.0 million and Papich Construction for USD 170.0 million on 2025-08-05, Cinderlite for USD 58.5 million on 2025-10-03, Kenny Seng Construction for USD 164.1 million on 2026-04-23, and the remaining half of an asphalt terminal entity for USD 38.3 million on 2026-09-09. The deals extend vertically integrated home markets in the Southeast, California's Central Coast and Central Valley, Nevada and Utah, and add property and equipment, mineral reserves and goodwill to the balance sheet.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 64–65, 118–121; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–15; calculation calc-01

Financing changed in two steps. On 2025-08-05 Granite entered a senior secured credit agreement with a USD 600.0 million revolver, a USD 600.0 million term loan and a USD 75.0 million delayed draw term loan, which, with a USD 10.0 million revolver draw and cash on hand, funded the 2025 acquisitions. In 2026 it issued USD 600.0 million of senior unsecured notes due 2034 on 2026-06-02 and used the proceeds, cash on hand and capped call unwind proceeds to exchange and then settle all of the 3.75% convertible notes, leaving the 3.25% convertible notes of USD 373.75 million due 2030 and the term loan as the other debt.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gva-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 140; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gva-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 24–31

New debt is therefore secured bank debt plus unsecured notes, and part of the 2026 issue replaced equity-linked instruments rather than adding to total debt at the same pace.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gva-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 24, 28–30
