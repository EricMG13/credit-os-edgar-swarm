## Findings

### Bottom line

Axon is an adequate fundamental credit: liquidity, a multi-year contracted software base and no debt due before 2030 support creditors, but thin free cash flow, investment-dependent earnings and a Skydio cash call mean leverage looks safer than it is.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a stronger credit is that Axon holds more liquidity than its leverage ratios suggest, pays no distributions, and is covered 8.13x on issuer-adjusted cash interest with net leverage of 1.59x on that measure (the pro forma figure is not established). On that reading the thin free cash flow is a choice to fund growth and equity pay, and the gains in other income are a bonus and not a support. That view would win if the investment gains proved recurring or realised in cash, if free cash flow after equity-pay taxes turned positive without new equity, and if the executed debt documents showed tight limits on payments and investments. The view would weaken if the Skydio cash settlement approached the USD 720.0 million ceiling while operating cash flow stayed weak, if the investment gains reversed, if the equity offering program were no longer available, or if the share price reached the level at which holders may convert the 2031 notes and Axon chose cash settlement.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_axon_axon-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_axon_axon-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33; calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation I6; calculation I8

### Company description

Axon sells integrated public-safety hardware and software, and its credit case rests on how much of that revenue recurs. Software and Services, which is subscription cloud evidence, records and real-time operations software with related services, made up 45.39% of net sales in the nine months of 2026, and Connected Devices (TASER devices, cameras, drones and counter-drone equipment) made up the rest. Customers are public agencies, enterprises and consumers, 82.47% of nine-month sales were in the United States, and no customer exceeded 10% of net sales, so concentration is by budget cycle and not by account.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_axon_axon-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12; 10-K_2025-12-31_axon_axon-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 5–8; calculation C8

The model supports cash-flow visibility, because multi-year contracts produced USD 10.2 billion of remaining performance obligations, and it supports refinancing confidence through scale, but it weakens debt service capacity in the near term, because hardware sales and large counter-drone orders arrive in lumps and subscription billing spreads cash over time. Axon has bought out four companies in which it already held minority stakes since January 2024, so growth has been partly acquired, with goodwill and intangible assets at 24.39% of total assets at 2026-09-30.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_axon_axon-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-12-31_axon_axon-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 137–139; 10-Q_2026-09-30_axon_axon-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4

### Ownership and group structure

Axon is a listed company with no disclosed sponsor or controlling holder, so creditors cannot assume sponsor support or sponsor extraction. Business & governance found no dividends or program repurchases, eight directors who signed the 2025 annual report against ten a year earlier, a chief executive who is also a director and the officer who reviews segment results, and no holder percentages, because the proxy statement is not supplied. Group structure is simple for creditors: the notes and revolver are the parent's obligations, no subsidiary guarantees the notes, and non-guarantor subsidiaries produced 19% of nine-month net sales and held 17% of consolidated assets, so the notes have no direct claim on those entities.

Financial policy so far is creditor-friendly on payouts and moderately aggressive on investment: Axon raised senior and convertible debt, bought capped calls for USD 114,885 thousand to limit dilution, and committed to Skydio, while equity programs funded employee taxes. Governance and sponsor risk is rated Medium by Business & governance, because the control weakness record and the missing proxy statement offset the absence of any extraction.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is mostly recurring or contracted, public-sector and not cyclical in the usual sense, but it is exposed to budgets and to lumpy hardware orders. Software and Services grew 38.28% in the nine months of 2026, driven by more users and adoption of premium add-ons, TASER grew 11.93% on TASER 10 volume, and Platform Solutions grew 108.57% on counter-drone equipment, whose timing depends on large customer contracts. Pricing power is not established: the filings state premium adoption but no price or renewal data.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_axon_axon-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 40–41; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C13; calculation C16

The risk mechanism is that agencies can fail to appropriate funds or terminate for convenience, so booked backlog converts to cash only as budgets allow; a government funding disruption, such as the 2025 shutdown the filing cites, delays billing and collection. The credit implication is that revenue timing, and not revenue loss, is the main risk, and subscription billing makes receivables and contract assets absorb cash first.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_axon_axon-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 46; 10-K_2025-12-31_axon_axon-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 45

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Costs are largely fixed in the short run, which makes margins sensitive to growth. Operating expenses were 54.22% of net sales in the nine months of 2026 against 60.96% a year earlier, selling, general and administrative costs rose with headcount of more than 5,100 full-time employees at 2025-12-31, and stock-based compensation was 16.60% of net sales. Gross margin fell as hardware mix, memory costs and professional services rose, partly offset by USD 47.4 million of tariff refunds.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_axon_axon-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 34–35; 10-Q_2026-09-30_axon_axon-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-K_2025-12-31_axon_axon-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 19; calculation C6; calculation C18

Margins can be defended against a modest volume shock because research and capital spending are discretionary, but not against an adverse mix or a memory-cost shock, because the annual report warns that raw-material price changes for the TASER 10 device may not be passed through to customers. The credit implication is that EBITDA before other income, of USD 188,261 thousand, is the right margin base for stress, and not reported EBITDA.

### Porter's Five Forces

Competitive rivalry is high and fragmented: the annual report lists dozens of competitors across body cameras, evidence software, records, real-time operations and counter-drone systems, including Motorola Solutions, so price and feature competition can compress margins. The threat of new entrants is moderate, because agencies require integration with evidence workflows and the TASER 10 device needs a federal firearms license, but the records software area is fragmented with more than 50 providers. The threat of substitutes is moderate, because less-lethal alternatives such as pepper spray and rubber rounds compete with conducted energy devices. Buyer power is meaningful, because buyers are public agencies with open bidding and appropriation cycles, though none exceeds 10% of sales. Supplier power is moderate to high for the TASER 10 device, because a limited number of suppliers provide its raw materials and Axon states it cannot change suppliers easily, and memory costs rose.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_axon_axon-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9–15; 10-K_2025-12-31_axon_axon-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_axon_axon-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-K_2025-12-31_axon_axon-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 30; 10-Q_2026-09-30_axon_axon-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34

The credit translation is that rivalry and buyer power cap pricing, supplier power exposes hardware margins, and entry barriers protect the software base, which is why Connected Devices carries the margin risk and Software and Services the stability.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_axon_axon-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 41; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Social and technological factors matter to demand and competition but not directly to debt service.

### SWOT

Strengths: the contracted software base, liquidity, no sponsor extraction and market access support credit. Weaknesses: thin free cash flow after equity-pay taxes, reliance on investment gains in reported EBITDA, and a record of control weaknesses constrain it. Opportunities: faster software growth, which carries a 75.3% adjusted gross margin against 49.6% in Connected Devices, could lift margins and cash conversion. Threats: public budget shocks, counter-drone volatility and a reversal of investment gains could weaken credit quality.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months, tailwinds are backlog conversion (the 20% to 25% expected within 12 months), software adoption and positive operating leverage, and headwinds are the Skydio cash settlement, memory costs, counter-drone timing and tariffs. The effect is to support revenue and interest cover while pressuring liquidity and free cash flow after equity-award taxes. ### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a public-budget or appropriation delay that coincides with a fall in large counter-drone orders and further memory-cost pressure. The first operational break is slower Connected Devices shipments, which carry the lower margin (49.6% adjusted gross margin) and which already fell from 52.0%. Financial transmission follows through gross margin and operating income, which was only 4.51% of net sales, and through working capital, because receivables and inventory absorb cash before it returns. If investment gains reverse, reported EBITDA falls toward EBITDA before other income of USD 188,261 thousand and net debt reaches 7.49x that figure, and the pro forma figure on that measure is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_axon_axon-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 34–35; 10-Q_2026-09-30_axon_axon-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 41; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C14; calculation I5

Liquidity and refinancing consequences are bounded by USD 2,146,055 thousand of liquidity before Skydio, the up to USD 720.0 million Skydio cash ceiling and the absence of maturities before 2030-03-15. Management levers are cutting capital spending and research spending and choosing share settlement of the 2031 notes, and, because equity awards vest on share-price goals, a fall in the share price would also reduce the cash taxes paid on net settlement. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress for several years, but not sufficient to keep reported net leverage near 3.21x (3.50x pro forma for the payment of 2026-11-05) if the investment gains reverse. The observable triggers are listed in the monitoring register, and the primary downside path is a reversal of investment gains and a stall in free cash flow while Skydio cash leaves and the 2030 to 2031 maturities approach.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_axon_axon-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33; calculation C17; calculation C4; calculation C7

### Overall credit view

Overall, Axon presents an adequate business profile supported by recurring software contracts, large liquidity and payout discipline, but credit quality is constrained by thin free cash flow after equity-award taxes, investment-dependent earnings and growing acquisition and investment cash calls. Cash-flow durability appears weak to adequate, because EBITDA converts poorly to free cash flow and the equity programs fill the gap. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on market access and liquidity in 2029 and 2030, because USD 2,150,000 thousand falls due or can be put within 12 months. The primary downside path is a reversal of investment gains with stalled free cash flow, and further analysis would require ratings, covenant levels after the amendment, guidance, the proxy statement and the executed debt documents.
