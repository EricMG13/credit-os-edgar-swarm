## Findings

### Bottom line
Brightstar's credit is solid on business quality but stretched on cash after the Italian Lotto payment: reported net leverage of 4.72x is well above management's 3.24x, and with distributions continuing, creditors depend on free cash flow recovering as license payments end.
At June 2026 reported net leverage was 4.72x, or 4.73x pro forma for the July 31, 2026 repurchases and 4.77x pro forma for the dividend payable September 1, 2026; the effect of the July 10, 2026 tax settlement payment is not established. Liquidity and maturities leave room to wait for that recovery, but management's own outlook implies thin second-half free cash flow.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C07

### Key findings
- Reported leverage is the figure to anchor on, and it has risen. Net debt of USD 3,796 million was 4.72x reported EBITDA at June 2026 against 3.94x at year-end 2025 (4.73x and 4.77x pro forma for the July 31 repurchases and September 1 dividend, with the July 10 tax payment effect not established), because the final Italian Lotto installment was paid in cash. Management's 3.24x adds back USD 201 million of license fee amortization, 35% of first-half adjusted EBITDA, for cash that was paid up front, so creditors should read the higher figure as the working measure.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 6-K_2026-06-30_brightstar-6302026xform6k.htm.jsonl, pages 3–5, 12; calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C05
- Business quality is strong and gives visibility. About 92% of revenue comes from long-term contracts with government lotteries, the main contracts have more than five years left on average, and the Italian Lotto license now runs to 2034. That durability helps support leverage above 4x on a reported basis; it weakens only where one regulator controls the license.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 40, 41
- Liquidity and maturities are not the pressure point. Liquidity of USD 1,742 million at June 2026 is 1.70 times the USD 1,026 million due in 2027 and 2028, and nothing is due in the rest of 2026, so a refinancing could wait for the outlook to be proved. The drawn revolver of USD 604 million and 40% floating-rate debt do raise interest sensitivity.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 6-K_2026-06-30_brightstar-6302026xform6k.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 27; calculation C03; calculation C04
- Cash generation after minority distributions and payouts is the weakest link. In the first half free cash flow excluding the Lotto payment was USD 269 million, distributions to minority partners took USD 194 million, leaving USD 75 million against USD 130 million of dividends and buybacks. Management's outlook implies second-half free cash flow before any distributions of only USD 6 million to USD 31 million, so net debt is unlikely to fall in 2026 without lower payouts or partner funding.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 6-K-EX-99.2_2026-06-30_q226earningsslidedeck-fi.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 7; calculation C06; calculation C08
- License concentration is the main business risk. The two Italian licenses supply 37% of revenue; the Scratch & Win license, 19% of revenue, expires in September 2028 and is stated to be non-renewable, so its replacement lands in the same year as the first large note maturity. The filings give no renewal timetable or terms.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 40; calculation C05
- Control and financial policy are a medium risk. One shareholder holds 63.28% of votes, there is no formal dividend policy and payouts continued during the license payment, but eight of thirteen directors are independent and earlier financing agreements capped payouts by rating level; whether the March 2026 revolving agreement does the same is not established.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 79, 171, 175
- Currency mismatch is a secondary risk. Euro debt is 66% of principal while Italy supplies about 40% of revenue, so a stronger euro raises the dollar value of debt faster than earnings, although half of cash is also in euros.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 6-K_2026-06-30_brightstar-6302026xform6k.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 27; calculation C09

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest evidence against concern is that the leverage jump reflects a one-off license payment that partners funded in part, that management reaffirms an adjusted EBITDA outlook of USD 1.16 billion to USD 1.19 billion with operating cash flow before license payments near USD 750 million, and that it expects free cash flow above USD 400 million a year after the 2025 to 2028 spending cycle. The view would soften if second-half cash flow beats the implied USD 6 million to USD 31 million, if the Scratch & Win renewal is secured early, or if payouts are cut. It would harden if reported net leverage stays above 4.77x at the next report, if the revolver is drawn to fund distributions, or if the Italian regulator signals a less favorable replacement license.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.2_2026-06-30_q226earningsslidedeck-fi.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 7; calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C07

### Limitations
- The outlook figures are management's and the second-half arithmetic subtracts first-half actuals from full-year ranges, so the conclusion that second-half cash flow will not cover payouts could reverse if working capital unwinds after the first-half timing drag the company describes.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.2_2026-06-30_q226earningsslidedeck-fi.htm.jsonl, page 7; calculation C07
- This is a fundamental view that assigns no rating and compares the issuer only with its own history, because no peer or market data was supplied; the strong assessment of business quality is therefore relative to the issuer's contracts, not to other lottery credits.
- The downside path is qualitative and no stress figures are produced, so how far earnings could fall before liquidity tightens is not sized, and the monitoring thresholds are anchored to current levels, not to covenant tests.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C04
- The currency conclusion uses principal by currency from the maturity table and revenue by customer location from the annual report, which are different bases, so the mismatch is indicative.
Basis: 6-K_2026-06-30_brightstar-6302026xform6k.htm.jsonl, page 14; 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 189

### Strengths and weaknesses

The main strength is contract visibility. About 92% of revenue comes from long-term operating contracts with government lotteries, fees follow wagers and the Italian Lotto license now runs to 2034, so revenue is more predictable than in most consumer businesses and supports tolerance for leverage above 4x on a reported basis. Liquidity of USD 1,742 million against USD 1,026 million due in 2027 and 2028 adds a second strength, and reported EBITDA covers cash interest 4.19x.
The main weaknesses are leverage and cash after distributions. Reported net leverage rose to 4.72x after the final Lotto payment, management's lower figure rests on adding back license amortization, and after USD 194 million paid to minority partners the first-half cash left was USD 75 million against USD 130 million of dividends and buybacks. License concentration, a 63.28% controlling holder and 40% floating-rate debt add to the risk; each is explained in the key findings.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 40; calculation C01; calculation C03; calculation C04; calculation C06

### Downside scenario

A credible downside begins with the Italian regulator's replacement award for the Scratch & Win license in 2028, or an earlier United States jackpot drought. The first operational break would be lower service revenue at fixed amortization and equipment costs, which cuts reported EBITDA faster than revenue because fees are percentages of wagers and cost is largely fixed. Higher reported leverage and lower cover follow, and distributions to minority partners and shareholders would continue unless cut, so cash after payouts would turn negative and the revolver would fund the gap. Liquidity of USD 1,742 million would absorb a year of that, but the 2028 maturities of USD 798 million then fall due with weaker earnings, which is when refinancing cost or access becomes the issue. Observable triggers are in the monitoring register: reported leverage above its pro forma level, second-half free cash flow below the implied range, and any renewal news.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); 20-F_2025-12-31_igt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 40; 6-K_2026-06-30_brightstar-6302026xform6k.htm.jsonl, page 14; calculation C02; calculation C04; calculation C07

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Management reaffirmed its full-year outlook on August 4, 2026: revenue of USD 2.50 billion to USD 2.55 billion, adjusted EBITDA of USD 1.16 billion to USD 1.19 billion, capital expenditure of USD 450 million to USD 475 million and operating cash flow of about USD 750 million before license payments. The adjusted EBITDA range implies USD 587 million to USD 617 million in the second half against USD 597 million a year earlier, so the outlook is roughly flat after the first half. Tailwinds are digital growth, cost savings of about USD 100 million by 2028 and the end of license payments; headwinds are higher license amortization, a growth investment of about USD 50 million in 2026 and jackpot volatility.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.2_2026-06-30_q226earningsslidedeck-fi.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 7; calculation C07
