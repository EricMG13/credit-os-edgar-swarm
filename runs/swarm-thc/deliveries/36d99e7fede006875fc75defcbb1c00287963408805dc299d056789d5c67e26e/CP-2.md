## Findings

### Bottom line

Tenet's fundamental credit profile is strong and improving on earnings and liquidity, but creditors rely on EBITDA growth for deleveraging because shareholder returns, minority distributions and acquisitions absorb all underlying free cash flow.

Net debt to adjusted EBITDA is 2.11x at 2026-09-30 against 2.25x at 2025-12-31, liquidity of USD 4,574 million covers the nearest notes maturity of USD 1,850 million in 2028 by 2.47x, and the main downside is policy-driven Hospital earnings pressure from 2027 on debt that is fixed in rate and size.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; calculation CALC-F7; calculation CALC-F9

### Key findings

Earnings momentum is the main support. Adjusted EBITDA rose 23.3% in the third quarter of 2026 and the issuer raised its FY2026 outlook to USD 5,050 million to USD 5,200 million, but the quarter includes a net favorable USD 147 million of timing items from the CommonSpirit transition; on an analyst adjustment that removes it, leverage would be 2.18x rather than 2.11x, still moderate, so the credit does not depend on that benefit.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-29_thc-20260930ex991earningsr.htm.jsonl, page 1; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_thc-20260930ex991earningsr.htm.jsonl, pages 2–4; calculation CALC-F8; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Liquidity and maturities are comfortable. Cash of USD 2,674 million and undrawn revolver availability of USD 1,900 million total USD 4,574 million against debt due within a year of USD 164 million, notes of USD 1,850 million due in 2028, USD 1,400 million due in 2029 and USD 3,450 million due in 2030, so a market closure would not force a near-term default.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; calculation CALC-F7; calculation CALC-F9; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Deleveraging depends on EBITDA growth, not cash retention. Underlying last-twelve-month free cash flow of USD 2,788 million, after excluding the USD 540 million CommonSpirit set-off, is outweighed by distributions of USD 838 million to minority partners, repurchases of USD 1,813 million and acquisitions of USD 243 million, which total USD 2,894 million, so net debt rose to USD 10,569 million from USD 10,288 million while leverage fell.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation CALC-F1; calculation CALC-F2; calculation CALC-F3; calculation CALC-F4; calculation CALC-F5; calculation CALC-F6

Policy risk is concentrated from 2027 and is the primary downside. The 2025 federal budget law can cap state-directed Medicaid payments and limit provider taxes from 2027, expired exchange subsidies are already lowering exchange admissions, and government programs and managed care together supply most hospital revenue, so a payment cut would hit Hospital margin first.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 16; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_thc-20260930ex991earningsr.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Earnings quality and minority leakage mean consolidated leverage understates pressure on creditors. Reported EBITDA includes USD 1,472 million of contract termination revenue in nine months 2026 that the issuer excludes from adjusted measures, and 39.35% of Ambulatory Adjusted EBITDA belongs to minority partners, so cash available to Tenet's creditors is lower than the consolidated ratio implies.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.1_2026-10-29_thc-20260930ex991earningsr.htm.jsonl, page 3

Financial policy is shareholder-oriented but funded from cash flow. With no sponsor, repurchases of USD 1,615 million in nine months 2026, USD 1,875 million of remaining authorization and no limit on distributions in the secured note indentures leave policy to management, and the record so far shows secured debt cut rather than increased.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 50; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 148

Refinancing has reduced secured debt but raised the share of unsecured claims. Secured notes fell from USD 9,800 million to USD 8,300 million while unsecured notes rose from USD 2,862 million to USD 4,362 million, which helps secured holders and the 2027 maturity wall but puts more claims at the unsecured rank that sits behind the revolver and secured notes.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 144; calculation CALC-F10

The CommonSpirit note is a modest net positive with concentrated counterparty risk. Payments of about USD 453 million a year from January 2027 would offset part of the leakage to partners if applied to debt, but they depend on one counterparty and replace a contract that ran to 2032.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a favorable view is that retained cash flow is already negative on an underlying basis (a shortfall of USD 106 million over the last twelve months) while policy risk starts in 2027, so a Hospital earnings setback would leave leverage to rise one for one with debt fixed. The view would turn more cautious if guidance were cut, if net debt to adjusted EBITDA rose above the 2.25x of 2025-12-31, or if repurchases were funded from the revolver or new debt; it would turn more favorable if the board held shareholder returns within free cash flow and applied the CommonSpirit receipts to debt.
Basis: calculation CALC-F6; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18

### Limitations

- The conclusion on deleveraging rests on last-twelve-month cash flow built from the annual and nine-month statements and treats the CommonSpirit set-off as non-cash, so it could change if later disclosure shows how the set-off was settled.
- The liquidity conclusion assumes the revolver can be drawn in full, which depends on a borrowing base that the executed credit agreement was not supplied to test.
- The profile is judged on absolute metrics because no ratings, market prices or peer data were supplied, so it does not say how Tenet ranks against peers or how the market prices its debt.
- The policy-risk conclusion rests on the issuer's risk-factor language, which does not quantify the effect of the 2027 provisions on Medicaid supplemental payments.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths: scale and diversification across a hospital network in eight states and a surgery-center platform in 37 states; improving margins in both segments; adjusted net leverage of 2.11x; ample liquidity with an undrawn revolver; fixed-rate notes with no maturity before 2028; and a reduction in secured debt in September 2026.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_thc-20260930ex991earningsr.htm.jsonl, page 3

Weaknesses: reliance on reimbursement set by government programs and a few large managed care payers; consolidated earnings that include large minority interests; reported earnings inflated by contract termination revenue; a shareholder-return policy that leaves almost no retained cash flow; a combined chairman and chief executive role; and exposure to one counterparty for the CommonSpirit receivable.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10

### Business and ownership

Tenet is a publicly held operator of 50 hospitals and a surgery-center platform, so there is no sponsor whose exit or extraction behavior could harm creditors, and the issue for creditors is board policy. Hospital Operations provides about three quarters of revenue and a lower margin, while Ambulatory Care provides a quarter of revenue and a larger share of Adjusted EBITDA but shares profit with physician and health-system partners, which makes consolidated EBITDA an upper bound on cash reaching Tenet.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 3–8

### Revenue durability and pricing power

Revenue is recurring and non-discretionary in nature, but price is set by third parties: managed care contracts renewed on negotiated rates, and Medicare and Medicaid rates set by government. Pricing power is therefore moderate, volume growth is supported by acuity and admissions, and the exposure to supplemental Medicaid payments and exchange coverage creates policy-driven revenue risk rather than cyclical demand risk.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 161; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 13–15; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_thc-20260930ex991earningsr.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Margin resilience

Labor is the largest cost and is exposed to nurse shortages, California staffing ratios and wage inflation, so margin depends on productivity and mix. The Hospital margin recovered to 19.2% in the third quarter from 15.1% a year earlier despite payer mix headwinds, but the quarter includes timing benefits, so margin resilience under a payment cut is not yet tested.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 11–13; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_thc-20260930ex991earningsr.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Cash conversion and capital intensity

Capital intensity is moderate and flexible, with capital expenditure of USD 910 million over the last twelve months, and conversion of adjusted EBITDA into reported free cash flow of 66.51% falls once the CommonSpirit set-off and distributions to partners are removed. Cash interest paid of USD 830 million is covered 6.03 times by adjusted EBITDA, so debt service is not the constraint; capital allocation is.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation CALC-F1; calculation CALC-F2

### Market frameworks

Buyer power sits with managed care payers and government programs, which set price. Competition comes from not-for-profit systems with tax advantages, physician-owned specialty hospitals and outpatient centers, and insurers and private equity buyers of providers, while certificate-of-need rules in some states limit new entry. The shift of surgery to outpatient settings favors the surgery-center platform. Policy is the dominant external factor, labor scarcity is the dominant input factor, and cyber risk is real given the 2022 incident.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 13–15; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 54

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Tailwinds are rising acuity and admissions, the outpatient shift, cost discipline and the removal of near-term secured maturities; headwinds are the 2027 Medicaid provisions, expired exchange subsidies, payer mix, labor costs and the loss of the contracted CommonSpirit revenue stream after 2026. Management expects a higher outlook for FY2026, which supports near-term credit metrics.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-29_thc-20260930ex991earningsr.htm.jsonl, pages 1–4; 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 16–18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17

### Downside pathway

The initiating condition is a cap on state-directed Medicaid payments or a deeper loss of exchange coverage from 2027. The first operational break would be lower supplemental revenue and a worse payer mix in Hospital Operations, already visible in lower exchange admissions. The financial transmission is lower Hospital Adjusted EBITDA on a largely fixed cost base, with consolidated leverage rising one for one because debt is fixed in rate and size. Liquidity would hold, since cash and the revolver exceed the nearest maturities, so the consequence is higher leverage and a slower return to shareholders rather than a liquidity crisis. Observable triggers are a guidance cut, falling Hospital margin, state approvals of directed payments and a pause in repurchases.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_thc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_thc-20260930ex991earningsr.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_thc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; calculation CALC-F7

### Overall credit view

The fundamental credit view is favorable with a negative bias on policy and capital allocation: earnings, leverage and liquidity are strong, but the profile depends on EBITDA growth and on management's restraint, because underlying free cash flow is fully distributed. Ratings, peer and market information were not supplied, so no relative judgement is made.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation CALC-F6
