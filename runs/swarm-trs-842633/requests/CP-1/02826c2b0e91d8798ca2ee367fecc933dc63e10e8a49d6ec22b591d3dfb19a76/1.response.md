## Findings

### Bottom line
Total debt of USD 397.14 million is now covered by USD 1,158.54 million of cash after the March 2026 Aerospace sale, so Trimas has net cash and creditors depend on a smaller earnings base rather than on deleveraging. Derived last-twelve-month EBITDA is USD 94.74 million, which puts total debt at 4.19x and EBITDA to cash interest at 4.98x.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_trs.htm.jsonl, pages 4–6, 15–17; 10-K_2025-12-31_trs.htm.jsonl, pages 89–90, 136; calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C03

The cushion is thinner than net cash suggests: last-twelve-month EBITDA includes a USD 35.80 million asbestos benefit booked in the fourth quarter of 2025, and without it total debt would be 6.74x EBITDA. USD 67.6 million of estimated sale tax is also still unpaid, and covenant EBITDA of USD 90.98 million is a different measure.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trs.htm.jsonl, page 89; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trs.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 10, 50, 53; calculation C05

### Financial interpretation

Leverage fell from 6.43x total debt to EBITDA at the end of 2024 to 5.90x at the end of 2025 and 4.19x on the last twelve months to 2026-09-30, while net leverage moved from 6.06x to 5.52x, which becomes -10.10x pro forma for the Aerospace sale of 2026-03-16, and then to a net cash position. The first step came from EBITDA rising from USD 61.91 million to USD 79.51 million faster than debt rose from USD 398.12 million to USD 469.17 million. The second came from the Aerospace sale, which lifted cash to USD 1,158.54 million while the revolver was repaid and debt fell to USD 397.14 million. The year-end ratios set debt that funded the whole group against continuing EBITDA only, so they overstate the leverage of the business that remains, and the last-twelve-month ratio is flattered by the asbestos benefit.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_trs.htm.jsonl, pages 4–6, 15–17; 10-K_2025-12-31_trs.htm.jsonl, pages 88–90, 136; calculation C03; calculation C04; calculation C05; calculation C08

EBITDA to cash interest paid improved from 3.31x in 2024 to 4.24x in 2025 and 4.98x on the last twelve months. Interest paid is gross: interest income of USD 23.9 million on the sale proceeds in the nine months of 2026 exceeds interest expense of USD 13.50 million, which is why the covenant interest-cover test prints as not applicable. Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_trs.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 27, 53; 10-K_2025-12-31_trs.htm.jsonl, page 90; calculation C04

Consolidated operating cash flow was USD 117.45 million in 2025 against capital expenditure of USD 48.35 million, but both include Aerospace until the sale. Cash flow for continuing operations alone is not stated, so cash conversion of the remaining business is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trs.htm.jsonl, page 90; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trs.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 10–11, 50

Liquidity is ample: cash of USD 1,158.54 million plus revolver availability of USD 243.80 million, against a maturity ladder whose only principal is the senior notes of USD 400.00 million due in 2029. How much of the cash is retained for acquisitions, buybacks or note repayment is a management choice the filings do not fix.
