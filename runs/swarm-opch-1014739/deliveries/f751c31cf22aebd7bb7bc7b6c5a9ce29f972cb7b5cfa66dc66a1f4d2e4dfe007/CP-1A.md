## Findings

### Bottom line

Option Care Health is a scaled, commercially funded infusion provider whose creditor risk now turns on its pending take-private, because control passes to a new owner group while its financing and the treatment of the existing term loan and notes are not stated.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21–23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37

Before the agreement of 2026-10-05 the shares were publicly traded with no controlling holder named in the filings supplied, no dividend had ever been paid, and cash went to buybacks of USD 307.5 million in 2025 and USD 167.5 million in the first nine months of 2026. Commercial payers supplied 86.78% of 2025 revenue and the largest payer about 14%, which spreads payer risk, but an operating margin of 5.76% leaves little cushion if reimbursement tightens. The merger agreement now bars dividends, buybacks, new debt and large acquisitions without the buyer's consent.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C7; 10-K_2025-12-31_bios-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 37; 10-K_2025-12-31_bios-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 86; 10-K_2025-12-31_bios-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 38

### Key findings

- The take-private moves control to a private investor group on terms creditors cannot yet size. Affiliates of Clayton, Dubilier & Rice and McKesson would pay USD 32.05 per share in cash, about USD 4,801.08 million on the basic share count, which is 4.10x the existing debt principal of USD 1,171.22 million. Closing is expected in the first half of 2027 and either side may terminate if the deal is not complete by 2027-10-05. The agreement contemplates equity and debt financing, but no amount, lender or treatment of the existing term loan, notes and revolver is given, so the effect on creditors is open in both directions.
  Basis: calculation C13; calculation C15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21–23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37
- The buyers' willingness to support or extract from the company cannot be judged from the filings, and it is not inferred from who they are. No fund terms, equity commitment, fees or leverage plan are supplied. The termination fees are a partial signal: the buyer would owe about USD 291.93 million, 6.08% of the equity value, in specified cases including a failure to fund or close the financing, against about USD 145.96 million, 3.04%, owed by the company.
  Basis: calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37

Buybacks and the USD 117.2 million Intramed Plus purchase together were 1.97x free cash flow of USD 217.14 million in 2025, so cash fell and term debt rose on a net basis to fund the difference. In the first nine months of 2026 the USD 167.5 million buyback was covered by free cash flow, and none was made in the third quarter.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is the size of the equity cushion and the record of the capital structure. The equity price of about USD 4,801.08 million is 4.10x the existing debt principal, the reverse termination fee may point to committed financing, the 2025 amendment cut the term loan margin from 2.25% to 1.75% and extended its maturity to 2032-09-22, and the 2026 amendment enlarged an undrawn revolver to USD 850.0 million, which are not the actions of a stressed borrower. If the financing leaves the existing facilities in place with modest added debt, creditors could end up better protected by a better-capitalised owner.
Basis: calculation C13; calculation C14; calculation C15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-K_2025-12-31_bios-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 75

Three outcomes would change the view. A financing that raises debt by a multiple of the existing USD 1,171.22 million, or repays the facilities without a change-of-control offer, would raise creditor risk; closing papers that leave the term loan and notes in place on unchanged terms would lower it; and a failed stockholder vote or regulatory outcome would return the company to a stand-alone issuer with USD 525.0 million of buyback authority still available and the 2025 pattern of shareholder returns ahead of free cash flow.
Basis: calculation C10; calculation C15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 39

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

On 2026-10-05 the company agreed to be acquired by an investor group of affiliates of Clayton, Dubilier & Rice and McKesson, through Onyx Bidco LLC, for USD 32.05 per share in cash. On the basic share count that is about USD 4,801.08 million of equity, 4.10x the existing debt principal of USD 1,171.22 million. The company would become a privately held, wholly owned subsidiary of the buyer and its shares would be delisted. Closing needs adoption by a majority of the outstanding shares, expiry of the Hart-Scott-Rodino waiting period and certain state healthcare approvals; it is expected in the first half of 2027, and either side may terminate if the deal is not complete by 2027-10-05.
Basis: calculation C13; calculation C15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21–23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 35

The company owes the buyer a termination fee of about USD 145.96 million in specified cases, including accepting a superior proposal, and the buyer owes the company about USD 291.93 million in specified cases, including a failure to fund or close the financing. Until closing the company may not pay dividends, repurchase shares, incur certain debt, make certain acquisitions or spend more than agreed amounts on capital projects without the buyer's consent.
Basis: calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 38

The documents supplied say that equity and debt financing are contemplated but give no amount, lender, pricing or sources and uses, and they do not say whether the term loan, the 2029 notes and the revolver would be repaid, assumed or left in place. These are gaps, not conclusions, and no pro forma leverage is worked out.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21–23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37

On 2026-03-30 the revolver was enlarged by USD 450.0 million to USD 850.0 million, leaving USD 846.0 million available after USD 4.0 million of letters of credit. The senior unsecured notes due 2029 date from 2021.
