## Findings

### Bottom line

Reported leverage is moderate and eased in 2026, with net debt at 2.04x last-twelve-months EBITDA against 2.27x at year-end 2025, but the pending take-private makes that a historical reading, because the post-closing financing is not stated.
Basis: calculation C06; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21–23

Cash interest cover is 6.74x on the same basis, nothing beyond small term loan amortization falls due before 2029, and the revolver was enlarged in March 2026 to USD 850.0 million, leaving USD 846.0 million available after letters of credit. The 2026 improvement came from an inventory release and lower buybacks rather than debt repayment, since total debt barely moved, so it can reverse. Fiscal-year EBITDA is derived on the issuer's own definition, and no adjusted figure is stated for any full year, so adjusted leverage is not established.
Basis: calculation C03; calculation C05; calculation C06; calculation C10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-03-31_bios-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 14; EX-99.1_2026-11-04_exhibit991-q32026.htm.jsonl, page 6

### Financial interpretation

Reported net debt to EBITDA rose from 1.52x at year-end 2023 to 2.27x at year-end 2025 and eased to 2.04x on a last-twelve-months basis at 2026-09-30; total debt to EBITDA moved from 2.25x to 2.84x and then 2.75x. The 2024 and 2025 increases came from capital allocation, not from falling earnings: buybacks of USD 252.726 million and USD 309.951 million, the USD 117.247 million Intramed Plus purchase in 2025 and incremental term loan borrowings of USD 49.959 million in 2024 and USD 229.472 million of new term debt in the 2025 refinancing (USD 180.239 million of old term debt retired) took cash from USD 343.849 million at year-end 2023 to USD 232.624 million at year-end 2025 while carrying debt rose from USD 1062.650 million to USD 1160.832 million. FY2023 is flattered: its reported EBITDA of USD 472.190 million includes the USD 106.0 million Amedisys termination fee, net of USD 21.1 million of merger expenses, inside Other, net of USD 89.865 million, and its operating cash flow includes the fee too, so the FY2023 ratios are not comparable with later years.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C03; calculation C06; calculation C17; 10-K_2025-12-31_bios-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 53; 10-K_2025-12-31_bios-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 66; 10-K_2025-12-31_bios-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 75–76

The 2026 improvement is a cash effect rather than deleveraging. Carrying debt fell only USD 2.998 million from year-end 2025 to 2026-09-30, while cash rose by USD 66.469 million in nine months: inventories released USD 63.104 million against a USD 24.899 million build in the prior-year nine months, buybacks fell to USD 170.545 million from USD 214.887 million and no acquisition was made against USD 117.247 million in 2025. A rebuild of inventory or a resumption of distributions would reverse it, and the merger agreement already bars buybacks without Parent's consent, which removes one use of cash while the transaction is pending.
Basis: calculation C02; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 38

Coverage and cash generation are comfortable. Reported EBITDA covered cash interest 6.31x in FY2025 and 6.74x on the last-twelve-months basis; free cash flow after capital expenditure was USD 217.140 million in FY2025 (conversion of reported EBITDA 0.5320) and USD 259.407 million in the nine months of 2026 against USD 194.895 million a year earlier. The strongest contrary evidence to the view that leverage is a concern is therefore that cash interest is a small claim on earnings; what would make it win is a merger financing that adds debt, because none of these ratios describe the capital structure after closing.
Basis: calculation C06; calculation C14; 10-K_2025-12-31_bios-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 53; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6

Liquidity is ample and refinancing risk is distant. Cash of USD 299.093 million plus USD 846.000 million of revolver availability gives USD 1145.093 million at 2026-09-30, after the revolver was enlarged from USD 400.0 million to USD 850.0 million on 2026-03-30. Apart from term loan amortization of USD 6.780 million a year, the next principal due is the USD 500.000 million of Senior Notes on 2029-10-31, and the revolver springs to 2029-08-01 if the notes are still outstanding 91 days before they mature. Interest rate risk rises after the cap of USD 300 million notional expires on 2026-11-30: the whole USD 671.220 million floating-rate term loan is then unhedged, so 100 basis points adds USD 6.712 million a year, against USD 3.763 million with the cap in force at year-end 2025.
Basis: calculation C13; calculation C15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 30; 10-K_2025-12-31_bios-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 49

The merger is a subsequent event dated 2026-10-05 and is excluded from every period figure. The sources state a cash price of USD 32.05 per share and say Parent's debt financing is contemplated, but give no amount, lender or treatment of the existing term loan, revolver and notes, so pro forma net debt and leverage are not established and none is worked out here. Until the financing is known the observable test is the closing documents: whether the existing facilities are repaid or stay in place determines whether today's ratios carry over.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21–23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37

Trade payables are the main non-debt funding source: they rose from USD 426.513 million at year-end 2023 to USD 670.676 million at 2026-09-30 against inventories of USD 274.004 million and USD 408.045 million, and purchases are concentrated in three or four vendors. No supplier financing programme is disclosed in the sources read. If suppliers shortened terms the cash call would fall on working capital, so payables against inventory and vendor terms should be watched alongside cash conversion.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_bios-20241231.htm.jsonl, pages 55–56; 10-K_2025-12-31_bios-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 51–52; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bios-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10

Data quality limits adjusted analysis more than reported analysis: statements reconcile in every identity, but the issuer states Adjusted EBITDA only for interim periods, so adjusted leverage cannot be built for a full year or for the last twelve months. FY2023 and FY2024 EBITDA growth is distorted by the FY2023 termination payment. A downside would run from slower acquisition of new patients or payer pressure to lower EBITDA, then to a cash cost if inventory has to be rebuilt, and finally to higher net leverage; the observable triggers are quarterly cash from operations, inventory days and the closing capital structure. No numerical stress is estimated here.
Basis: calculation C12; EX-99.1_2026-11-04_exhibit991-q32026.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-12-31_bios-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 71
