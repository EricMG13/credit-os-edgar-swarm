Accendra's fundamental credit profile is weak: net debt is 8.05x calculated EBITDA, cash interest cover is 1.67x and the third-quarter earnings run-rate is below the last twelve months, so creditors depend on the 2026 refinancing, the revolver and asset sales, not on cash generation.

### Company description

Accendra is a single-segment home healthcare provider that rents equipment and sells supplies to patients and home health agencies under the Apria and Byram Healthcare brands, and it sold Products & Healthcare Services on 2025-12-31 for USD 375 million in cash. Revenue comes from fee-for-service and capitation arrangements with government and commercial payors, and the company says it supports patients over the life of their therapy, which gives some visibility, but the terminated commercial contracts removed nearly all capitation revenue and showed that visibility depends on a few payors. The sale reduced diversification, as the 2025 annual report itself says, so debt service now rests on one business whose reimbursement is set by payors and regulators, which weakens cash-flow visibility and refinancing confidence.

### Ownership and group structure

Accendra is a listed Virginia corporation with no sponsor, parent or controlling holder identified in the supplied filings; the proxy statement that would show holders above 5% was not supplied. The notes issued in June 2026 are guaranteed on a senior secured basis by existing and future wholly owned domestic subsidiaries, a receivables subsidiary sells receivables to the programme, and non-guarantor subsidiaries and any restricted or unrestricted group designation are not identified, so structural seniority cannot be assessed here. Leverage tolerance is shown by the abandoned Rotech acquisition for USD 1.36 billion and by the use of an exchange to extend maturities; the filings show no dividend and stopped repurchases, so the creditor risk lies in strategy reversals, listing pressure and the new chief executive rather than in distributions.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is partly recurring because patients stay on therapy, but it is concentrated, regulated and exposed to substitution by a payor's choice of provider. In 2025 two commercial payors were 23% and 14% of revenue, Medicare and Medicaid 19%, and the largest payor ended contracts worth 12% of revenue, with a successor provider taking over the patients; the Medicare competitive bidding programme re-bids contracts at least every three years, and a June 2025 coverage determination can make qualifying for home ventilation harder. Price and volume are not separated in the filings and no pass-through mechanism is stated, so pricing power is not established; the evidence of buyer power is the contract loss itself, which lowers revenue visibility and the quality of cash flow available for debt service.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Costs fall more slowly than revenue. In nine months 2026 revenue fell 10.24% while cost of net revenue fell 4.3%, patient service equipment depreciation fell 3.3% and selling, general and administrative cost fell 6.0%, so margin cannot be defended under volume loss without further action; the issuer also notes that volume-based supplier discounts depend on order volumes, so falling volume can raise unit cost. Stranded costs from the sold business remain in continuing operations and in Adjusted EBITDA, and net fees under the transition services agreement were USD 26 million in nine months 2026, which will fall as services are replaced; this supports partial margin recovery but is not quantified by the issuer.

Buyer power is high because payors are concentrated and set reimbursement, supplier power is meaningful because the three largest suppliers were about 40% of purchases and a supplier recall affected 2024 results, and the filings state no evidence on new entrants or substitutes beyond pharmacy benefit managers, so those forces are not established. Economic factors matter through floating-rate debt, since the Term Loan B and the revolver carry a spread over a benchmark rate and the margin is not stated; social and technology factors do not appear to be primary credit drivers in the provided materials.

### Credit-translated SWOT

Strengths are the 2029 to 2033 maturity profile, liquidity of 15.08% of total debt, a base of stable categories and clean reporting. Weaknesses are payor concentration, falling margins, 8.05x leverage and an equity deficit. ## Gaps

Six gaps are open, two of them material. None blocks the credit view, but the first two limit how far the liquidity and revenue findings can be pushed, and three statements of earlier analyses are corrected at the end of this section.

Covenant levels, step-downs and definitions for the leverage and interest coverage tests are not in the filings, so headroom is not calculable; this limits the liquidity and leverage findings, is Material, and closing it needs the executed credit agreement and amendments.

The 2026 shares of the two largest commercial payors and the revenue still earned from the terminating payor are not stated, although revenue by payor type is, so the durability finding and the second payor sensitivity rest on 2025 shares; this limits the revenue and downside findings, is Material, and closing it needs payor concentration by quarter.

Margins and floors on the Term Loan B and the revolver are not stated, so total interest after 2026-12-15 is not calculable; this limits the cash flow finding to the two secured note series, is Minor, and closing it needs the credit agreements.

Management gives no quantified targets for its value creation plan and no forecast, so the outlook is analyst inference; this limits the outlook and margin recovery view, is Minor, and closing it needs guidance or earnings materials.

No agency ratings, dated market quotes or peers were supplied beyond the issuer's own fair value estimates, so relative standing is not assessed; this limits the business profile comparison, is Minor, and closing it needs those inputs.

The actual application of the sale proceeds is not itemised, although the first-quarter 2026 report states that the company anticipated repaying the revolver with them; this limits the view that debt reduction came from the sale, is Minor, and closing it needs a cash flow bridge.

Three statements in the earlier analyses are corrected here. Business & governance says the use of the sale proceeds is not stated, but the first-quarter 2026 report states the anticipated repayment of the revolver; it says the 2026 payor mix is not stated, but the third quarter report gives revenue by payor type, with Medicare plus Medicaid at 22.33% of nine-month revenue; and it reads the June 2026 exchange as lowering refinancing risk for participants, but the debt note shows the second lien notes issued in it at 35.81% of principal, so the company's maturity risk fell while investors in that tranche hold paper priced at a deep discount.
