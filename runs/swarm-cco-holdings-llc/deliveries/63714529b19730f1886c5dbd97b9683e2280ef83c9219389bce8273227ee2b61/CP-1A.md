## Findings

### Bottom line

Sponsor and governance risk for CCO Holdings creditors is Medium: the issuer is a wholly owned, centrally managed Charter subsidiary whose cash is passed to the parent, and two unclosed transactions would add debt and change Charter's owners. Distributions to the parent were USD 2,460 million in the first half of 2026 against free cash flow of USD 2,492 million, and USD 6,794 million in FY2025 against USD 5,827 million.

The Cox Transactions require about USD 4.2 billion of cash at closing, which the issuer's reports say will be funded with debt that may rank as guaranteed and secured, and neither they nor the Liberty Broadband Combination had closed at the latest filing. The filings state the cash consideration differently, so the size of the new debt is not established.

### Key findings

Creditors have no independent owner or management to rely on, because CCO Holdings is a holding company wholly owned by CCH I Holdings and Charter entities run its financing, cash management and treasury on a centralized basis. Protection therefore rests on the indenture tests: the indentures permit debt, dividends and investments in certain circumstances only while pro forma leverage stays below a specified level, the maximum total leverage ratio is 6.0 times, and the ratio was 4.0 times at December 31, 2025. A breach of the Charter Operating credit facility covenants, whose total leverage ratio stood at 2.8 times against a 5.0 times maximum at year-end, would also be an event of default on the issuer's debt.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ccoh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2025-12-31_ccoh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-06-30_ccoh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-31_ccoh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 156; 10-K_2025-12-31_ccoh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 158

Cash generated by the business is passed to the parent: distributions were 98.72% of first-half 2026 free cash flow and 116.60% in FY2025, when they exceeded free cash flow by USD 967 million while principal debt rose by USD 838 million. The filings state that CCO Holdings and its subsidiaries are the primary source of funding for Charter's market share repurchases through distributions to their parent companies, so deleveraging of the issuer depends on the parent choosing to distribute less.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ccoh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 97; 10-Q_2026-06-30_ccoh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-K_2025-12-31_ccoh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 154; calculation C9; calculation C10; calculation C11

Standing repurchase arrangements add parent-level cash demands whose funding entity the filings do not state: each month Charter must buy Liberty Broadband shares worth the greater of USD 100 million and a minimum liquidity amount, or lend that amount where a purchase is not possible, and it bought USD 595 million of shares in the first half of 2026 and advanced a term loan of about USD 359 million in May 2026. A/N has suspended its own sales to Charter until the Cox Transactions close or end, which removes a second claim for now but can be reversed by A/N.

The issuer's unsecured notes, 28.49% of principal debt at June 30, 2026, are not covered by those guarantees and are structurally subordinated, so a larger pool of guaranteed and secured debt would sit ahead of them. The annual report describes a split credit rating in which the secured notes are investment grade and the unsecured notes, USD 27.3 billion at year-end, are high yield.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a lower risk level is that parent behaviour has stayed within the indenture tests and no creditor-adverse action is documented: distributions were 91.96% of free cash flow across FY2023 to FY2025 and only 53.44% in FY2024, the indenture leverage ratio was 4.0 times against a 6.0 times maximum, the Charter Operating total leverage ratio was 2.8 times against a 5.0 times maximum, maturities were extended without an exchange offer, the issuer's debt was repurchased below par, and Charter's long-term leverage target has fallen to 3.5 times. The view would move toward Low if the Cox Transactions close with the new debt sized to Charter's stated target while distributions fall below free cash flow, and toward High if distributions continue at or above free cash flow after closing while guaranteed and secured debt grows ahead of the issuer's unsecured notes.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ccoh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 156; 10-K_2025-12-31_ccoh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 158; 10-Q_2026-06-30_ccoh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-Q_2026-06-30_ccoh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 27; EX-99.1_2026-07-24_chtrex991earningsrelease63.htm.jsonl, page 7; calculation C9

### Limitations

- The entity that funds the Liberty Broadband repurchases, the May 2026 loan and the Cox cash payment is not stated, so the finding that these claims fall on cash distributed by the issuer rests on Charter's general statement about repurchase funding and could be overstated.
- Ownership percentages inside the Charter group appear only in a chart that the supplied text does not carry, so the ownership map shows those links without percentages and the extent of Charter's economic interest in the issuer's parent chain is not established.
- Shareholder, antitrust and regulatory approval status and the closing date of the Cox Transactions are not stated in any supplied filing, so the Medium rating assumes both transactions remain open and would change on closing.
- Executed indentures and credit agreements are not supplied, so capacity to pay distributions, add secured debt or release guarantees is judged only from the leverage test the filings state, and capacity cannot be separated from willingness beyond that.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Charter, Charter Holdings and Cox Enterprises signed the Transaction Agreement on May 16, 2025. At closing Charter pays USD 3.5 billion in cash for Cox's commercial fiber and managed IT and cloud businesses, and Charter Holdings pays Cox Enterprises USD 650 million in cash, issues USD 6.0 billion of convertible preferred units and common units, and receives Cox's residential cable business; Charter also issues one share of a new Class C common stock to Cox Enterprises whose votes reflect its as-converted holdings of Charter Holdings units. The preferred units carry a fixed annual dividend worth USD 412.5 million, and the combined entity assumes about USD 12.4 billion of Cox net debt and finance leases. The annual report instead states the Charter Holdings cash as USD 500 million and the assumed net debt as USD 12.6 billion, so the terms are recorded with all figures in the conflict log.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_ccoh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_ccoh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 28; calculation C13; calculation C14

The Liberty Broadband Combination, agreed on November 12, 2024, would make Liberty Broadband an indirect wholly owned Charter subsidiary and is expected to close with the Cox Transactions. Liberty Broadband's preferred stock would convert into Charter preferred stock with a mandatory redemption date in 2039, and Charter bears the corporate-level tax on the divestiture of GCI, completed on July 14, 2025, with tax benefits realized by GCI shared back to Charter for any liability above USD 420 million.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ccoh-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 175–176; 10-Q_2026-06-30_ccoh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 17

New debt has been raised and replaced at the Charter Operating and issuer levels. In September 2025 Charter Operating issued USD 1,250 million of 2035 senior secured notes and USD 750 million of 2055 senior secured notes. In January 2026 the issuer and its co-issuer sold USD 1,750 million of 2033 senior notes and USD 1,250 million of 2036 senior notes, and in February 2026 redeemed USD 750 million of 2026 notes and USD 2,250 million of 2027 notes. The Cox cash payment is the next financing: about USD 4.2 billion of new debt is described as guaranteed and secured only when issued, so its issue date, issuer and terms are not established. The equipment installment plan financing facility was amended in November 2025 to allow borrowing of up to USD 2.0 billion and carried USD 1.6 billion at June 30, 2026.
