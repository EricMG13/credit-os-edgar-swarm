## Findings

### Bottom line

Fundamental credit quality is adequate but not improving: Adjusted EBITDA covers cash interest 4.43 times, yet leverage of 4.22 times is not falling because earnings are drifting lower and the parent takes all or nearly all free cash flow. Creditors therefore depend on lower parent distributions, not earnings growth, to reduce leverage. The primary downside path is continued broadband customer losses and cost growth, which at the first-half rate of earnings decline would lift leverage to 4.36 times.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F2; calculation F15

Liquidity of USD 3,985 million covers the 2026 and 2027 maturities that remain 2.48 times, so near-term distress is unlikely; the larger risk is the 2028 to 2030 maturities, which are 28.05% of principal and, for the unsecured notes, would cost USD 238.78 million more a year at the January 2026 coupons.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-03-31_ccoh-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-Q_2026-06-30_ccoh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 27; calculation F12; calculation F13; calculation F14

Cash generation of this kind keeps debt service secure while earnings stay near current levels.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a better view is that leverage has held in a narrow range, cash interest is covered 4.43 times, mobile growth is replacing declining lines, distributions were 91.96% of free cash flow across FY2023 to FY2025, and the capital programme that depresses free cash flow ends around 2027, so conversion could improve without any earnings growth. The view would move toward strong if capital expenditure fell after 2027 and the parent let free cash flow reduce debt, and toward weak if Adjusted EBITDA kept falling at the first-half rate while distributions stayed near free cash flow, which would take leverage to 4.36 times at constant debt and to 4.51 times at twice that rate.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_ccoh-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 7; calculation F2; calculation F15; calculation F16

### Limitations

- The downside and refinancing figures hold debt, capital expenditure and cash interest constant and take rates from the issuer's own January 2026 issue, so they show direction and scale rather than a forecast, and a change in capital spending after the network upgrade would move the conclusions on the primary downside path.
- The seamless entertainment allocation is reported only as a change in costs netted within video revenue, so the revenue and cost split excluding it is calculated here and the finding that earnings pressure is a cost and mix problem rests on that calculation.
- Customer churn and retention are not stated and competitor overlap is given for only two footprint shares, so the view that revenue durability is the weakest dimension rests on net customer trends and could be too mild or too severe.
- Earnings, customers and savings of Cox Communications are not in the supplied filings, so the credit effect of closing cannot be sized and the adequate assessment applies to the issuer on a stand-alone basis.
- No peer set or market pricing is supplied, so the profile cannot be compared with other leveraged cable issuers and the adequate assessment is absolute rather than relative.

### Company description

CCO Holdings is a holding company whose operating subsidiaries sell subscription Internet, mobile, video and voice services under the Spectrum brand to residential and business customers across 41 states, and its revenue is overwhelmingly recurring, with residential services at 76.85% of first-half 2026 revenue. Recurring revenue gives visible cash flow for debt service, but the business needs heavy network investment and faces intense competition, and a single reportable segment means no part of it can be isolated for credit purposes.

### Ownership and group structure

The issuer is wholly owned through CCH I Holdings by Charter, so financial policy is set at the parent: distributions were 98.72% of first-half 2026 free cash flow and 116.60% in FY2025, and Business & governance rates sponsor and governance risk Medium because two pending transactions would add debt and change Charter's owners. ### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is recurring and subscription based but no longer growing: reported revenue fell 0.56% in FY2025 and 1.37% in the first half of 2026, and excluding the seamless entertainment allocation the first-half decline was -0.08%. Beneath that, Internet revenue fell 2.28%, video revenue fell 9.44% and voice revenue fell 4.70% while mobile service revenue grew 17.00% and advertising revenue grew 8.86% in an election year, so mobile and advertising are offsetting broadband and video. Pricing power is limited: reported revenue per residential customer fell 1.82% while the company pursues lower promotional and persistent bundled prices, and fiber to the home competitors overlap about 27% and 16% of the footprint. Revenue is therefore predictable near term but structurally declining in video and broadband customers, which caps earnings growth.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Programming is the largest cost line at 26.87% of FY2025 operating costs and expenses, followed by other costs of revenue at 20.42%, so nearly half of costs move with video and mobile volumes: programming fees are generally paid per customer and fall as video customers leave, while other costs rise with mobile growth. In the first half of 2026 programming fell USD 432 million, of which USD 355 million was the seamless entertainment allocation that also lowered revenue, while other costs of revenue rose USD 367 million and transition costs added USD 89 million; cost growth excluding the allocation was USD 337 million against flat underlying revenue. Margins can be defended against volume loss through programming savings, but not against mobile cost growth and integration spend, which is why Adjusted EBITDA fell 3.16% on flat underlying revenue.

### Downside scenario

The downside path starts with continued Internet customer and video losses and mobile and integration cost growth, so that Adjusted EBITDA keeps falling at the first-half rate of 3.16%. At that rate last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA would be USD 21,548 million, leverage 4.36 times at constant debt, cash interest cover 4.29 times and free cash flow USD 4,367 million, and at twice that rate USD 20,845 million, 4.51 times, 4.15 times and USD 3,664 million. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress over 12 to 24 months because liquidity covers the remaining 2026 and 2027 maturities 2.48 times, but leverage would drift toward 4.5 times, which leaves the unsecured notes, ranking behind the secured debt, most exposed.

### Overall credit view

Overall, CCO Holdings presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, recurring revenue and a 41.28% Adjusted EBITDA margin, but credit quality is constrained by leverage of 4.22 times that is not falling, a parent that takes all or nearly all free cash flow and unsecured notes that sit behind guaranteed and secured debt. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because interest is covered 4.43 times, although conversion fell to 22.79% of Adjusted EBITDA. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on market access for the 2028 to 2030 maturities, which are 28.05% of principal and would cost more at today's coupons, and on whether the Cox cash payment adds secured debt. The primary downside path is falling broadband customers and rising costs compressing earnings while distributions continue. Further analysis would require the executed debt documents, Cox earnings and savings, and a peer set for relative assessment. Fundamental credit analysis is complete with limitations, and the top risk is the parent's cash sweep alongside shrinking earnings.
