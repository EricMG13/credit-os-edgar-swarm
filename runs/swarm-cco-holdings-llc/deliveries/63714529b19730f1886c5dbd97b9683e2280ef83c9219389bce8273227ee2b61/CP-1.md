## Findings

### Bottom line

Leverage is stable but not improving: debt of USD 93,959 million was 4.22 times last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA at June 30, 2026, against 4.19 times at December 31, 2025, because a USD 797 million reduction in debt only partly offset falling earnings.

Liquidity rests on about USD 3,700 million of undrawn facilities and USD 285 million of cash.

### Key findings

Debt at carrying value fell from USD 94,756 million at December 31, 2025 to USD 93,959 million at June 30, 2026, a fall of USD 797 million or 0.84%, while last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA fell from USD 22,611 million to USD 22,251 million, a fall of 1.59%. The debt reduction was smaller than the earnings decline, so Adjusted EBITDA leverage rose from 4.19 times to 4.22 times and net leverage from 4.18 times to 4.21 times. Second-quarter Adjusted EBITDA was 4.23% lower than a year earlier, so holding leverage near 4.2 times depends on continued debt reduction rather than on earnings recovery.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_ccoh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-06-30_ccoh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-06-30_ccoh-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 26; calculation C48; calculation C63; calculation C50; calculation C51; calculation C59

Free cash flow on the issuer's definition was USD 2,492 million in the first half of 2026 against USD 3,249 million a year earlier, a fall of 23.30%, because Adjusted EBITDA fell by USD 360 million and capital expenditure rose by USD 440 million. Distributions to the parent were USD 2,460 million in the same half-year, 98.72% of free cash flow, so the cash the business generates is passed up to the parent. The USD 498 million by which repayments of long-term debt exceeded borrowings therefore had to come from other sources, and the quarterly report attributes the lower financing outflow partly to higher short-term borrowings under the deferred payment program. Creditors depend on lower distributions or higher earnings, not on retained cash, for deleveraging.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest counter-argument is that leverage has held between 4.18 times and 4.22 times on Adjusted EBITDA across five balance-sheet dates and that the indenture test was met with room, at 4.0 times against a 6.0 times maximum at year-end. Free cash flow was USD 5,827 million in FY2025. The view would improve if distributions to the parent fell below free cash flow, and weaken if last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA fell to USD 22,000 million without a matching fall in debt, which would take leverage to 4.27 times.
