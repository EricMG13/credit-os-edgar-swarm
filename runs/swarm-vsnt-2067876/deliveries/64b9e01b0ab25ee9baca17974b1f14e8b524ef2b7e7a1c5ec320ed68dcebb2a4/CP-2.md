### Company and ownership in credit terms

Versant is a United States cable-network group built around news and sports: MS NOW, CNBC, USA Network and Golf Channel, with E!, SYFY and Oxygen True Crime, and the platforms GolfNow, Fandango, Rotten Tomatoes and, from 2026-08-03, Full Swing. ### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is largely recurring but structurally declining. Linear distribution is a per-subscriber fee, so volume follows distributor subscribers, which fell 8% in each of 2024 and 2025, while price rises under contracts only partly offset the loss; the ten largest distributors were 52% of receivables at 2026-09-30, so a few renewals decide pricing power, and the separation ended joint negotiation with NBCUniversal's broadcast networks. Advertising is priced on ratings, which declined 17% in 2025, and platforms revenue follows box office and golf rounds, so revenue is predictable in its core, discretionary and cyclical at the edges, and declining in the two largest lines.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins can be defended only partly. Programming and production costs of USD 1,777 million for nine months, mainly licensed content and sports rights, are contractual and fall with revenue only as rights end, other costs rise with platform volumes, and standalone costs replace the allocations Comcast charged before 2026, with fees to NBCUniversal for advertising sales added to selling costs. Adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 34.13% from 37.18% and operating income margin to 18.66% from 21.13% as revenue fell 3.01% and total costs stayed flat, so volume pressure and operating deleverage are the exposures and cost cuts depend on content decisions at renewal.

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a faster loss of cable subscribers and lower ratings, for example at a major distributor renewal made without NBCUniversal's joint negotiating weight. The first operational break is linear distribution and advertising revenue falling faster than the 6.49% and 3.56% seen in nine months 2026, while content obligations of USD 5,726 million do not fall. In the first scenario, with the nine-month declines repeated for twelve months and platforms growing at 6.43%, revenue and EBITDA fall USD 251.46 million, pro forma net leverage rises to 1.05x and interest cover on accrued interest stays at 8.91x. In the second scenario, with the declines doubled, EBITDA falls USD 558.60 million, pro forma net leverage rises to 1.25x, and discretionary uses at the nine-month pace exceed free cash flow by USD 534.60 million a year, leaving cash of USD 516.40 million after one year, from a base of USD 1,051 million before the announced repurchase, before any revolver draw.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Versant presents an adequate business profile supported by national news and sports brands reaching 49 million to 60 million households and a growing platforms line, but credit quality is constrained by structural decline in linear distribution and advertising, fixed content commitments of USD 5,726 million and a cash-heavy financial policy. Cash-flow durability appears adequate for debt service and weak against discretionary uses, because free cash flow of USD 1,640 million covers amortization many times while distributions and acquisitions took 100.17% of free cash flow in nine months. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on 2030 earnings, the outcome of sports rights renewals through 2033 and capital market access, as all debt matures in January 2031. The primary downside path is a faster fall in linear distribution and advertising against fixed content costs while buybacks and acquisitions continue. Further analysis would require the executed credit agreements and covenant definition, subscriber and renewal data, the 2026 proxy statement, and ratings and market data. Top risk: erosion of linear distribution and advertising cash flow against fixed content commitments.
