## Findings

### Bottom line

Governance is a moderate credit risk: one Class B holder holds a third of the votes, and cash is going to buybacks and acquisitions while earnings fall, so creditors should rely on covenants and liquidity, not on owner support.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_vsnt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 53; 10-K_2025-12-31_vsnt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 57; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vsnt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vsnt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23; calculation CALC-10; calculation CALC-11

The business is a United States cable-network group whose largest revenue line, linear distribution at 58.76% of nine-month 2026 revenue, fell 6.49% in the period. Versant borrowed USD 3,000 million for the separation and paid USD 2,250 million of it to Comcast, then spent USD 406 million on dividends and buybacks and USD 806 million on acquisitions and investments in nine months. The executed debt documents are not supplied, so capacity to distribute more is not established.

### Key findings

- Control sits with one Class B holder, not with a financial sponsor, which adds approval-right and transfer-of-control risk without any support commitment. The Class B shares carry a non-dilutable third of the votes and separate approval rights over mergers, equity issuances and changes to the articles even if the board and other shareholders approve, yet they are 0.28% of the shares outstanding. The annual report says the holder could transfer effective control by selling them, and no equity cure or continuing support commitment from this holder or from Comcast appears in the filings; the only payment from Comcast's side was USD 70 million at the separation, for a purpose the filings do not state.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_vsnt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 53; 10-K_2025-12-31_vsnt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 57; calculation CALC-08
- The separation was financed with debt that mostly left the company, so the borrowing funded a transfer to the former parent rather than the issuer's own liquidity. Of USD 3,000 million of principal raised across the notes and two term loans, USD 2,250 million, or 75.00%, was paid in cash to Comcast for the assets contributed, and the remainder of USD 750 million before costs went to general corporate purposes. All three debt instruments and the USD 750 million revolving facility are secured on substantially all assets of the company and its guarantor subsidiaries and fall due in January 2031; the first lien net leverage covenant of 3.50x applies from the third quarter of 2026 on a definition that is not supplied.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious reading is liquidity and conduct to date. Cash of USD 1,051 million sits beside an undrawn USD 750 million revolving facility, scheduled repayments are only USD 30 million for the rest of 2026, the first lien covenant was met at 2026-09-30, free cash flow covered distributions 2.98x, and the filings show no amendment, waiver or priming step. The Class B holder owns 0.28% of the shares and the Class A holders, former Comcast shareholders, hold two thirds of the votes.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_vsnt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vsnt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vsnt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vsnt-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 37–38; calculation CALC-09; calculation CALC-10

The view would move to high risk if buybacks or acquisitions were funded with revolver borrowings or new secured debt, if the first lien covenant were amended or waived, or if the Class B holder transferred effective control; it would move to low risk if the executed credit agreements showed binding limits on restricted payments and acquisitions and the covenant headroom proved wide.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Versant separated from Comcast on 2026-01-02 when Comcast distributed 100% of the Class A and Class B shares to its shareholders at one Versant share for every 25 Comcast shares, and the Class A shares began trading on 2026-01-05. Before that date Versant was a wholly owned Comcast subsidiary and its statements are combined carve-out figures. Agreements with Comcast cover separation and distribution, tax matters, transition services, employee matters, intellectual property licences and commercial arrangements.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_vsnt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_vsnt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 108

The separation was financed with USD 3,000 million of secured debt: USD 1,000 million of senior secured notes due January 2031, issued in October 2025 with proceeds held in escrow until the separation; a USD 1,000 million Term Loan A and a USD 750 million revolving facility under a credit agreement dated 2025-10-03; and a USD 1,000 million Term Loan B under a credit agreement dated 2026-01-02. Term Loan A was drawn on 2026-01-02 and the revolving facility is undrawn. USD 2,250 million of the proceeds was paid in cash to Comcast as consideration for assets contributed, which is 75.00% of the principal raised.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_vsnt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 46; 10-K_2025-12-31_vsnt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 99–101; 10-K_2025-12-31_vsnt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 108; calculation CALC-13

Term Loan A amortizes at 5.00% a year and Term Loan B at 7.00% a year in quarterly instalments from 2026-09-30; Term Loan A bears SOFR plus 1.75%, Term Loan B SOFR plus 3.50%, and the revolving facility carries a 0.25% commitment fee. The notes may be redeemed at par plus a make-whole premium before 2028-01-30 and carry customary limits on additional debt, liens, restricted payments and affiliate transactions. Term Loan A and the revolving facility carry a maximum consolidated first lien net leverage ratio of 3.50x from the third quarter of 2026, defined in an agreement that is not supplied, and the company reported compliance at 2026-09-30. Interest rate swaps of USD 975 million notional entered in March 2026 leave about 66% of debt principal at fixed rates.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_vsnt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 99–102; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vsnt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vsnt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_vsnt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34; calculation CALC-20

After the separation the company bought Full Swing for USD 536 million in cash on 2026-08-03, acquired other businesses for aggregate consideration of USD 215 million in the first half, sold SportsEngine in the second quarter for an aggregate USD 143 million, and returned USD 406 million to shareholders in nine months. The board declared a fourth-quarter dividend payable on 2027-01-25, and an accelerated repurchase of USD 100 million was announced to start on 2026-11-04; the filings do not confirm that it has started.
