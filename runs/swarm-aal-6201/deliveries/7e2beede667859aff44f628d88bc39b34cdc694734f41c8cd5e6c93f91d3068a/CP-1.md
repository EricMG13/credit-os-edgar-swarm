## Findings

### Bottom line

Leverage is high and rising: total debt is 10.25x derived EBITDA for the twelve months to September 2026, so creditors rely on USD 11,797 million of liquidity and refinancing rather than earnings.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C6; calculation C13

The deterioration comes from earnings, not borrowing: derived EBITDA fell from USD 4,970 million in FY2023 to USD 3,357 million in FY2025 while total debt fell from USD 32,902 million to USD 29,007 million, and in the nine months to September 2026 fuel cost rose by USD 4,427 million against revenue growth of USD 6,348 million. EBITDA covered cash interest only 1.81x on the last twelve months, down from 2.28x in FY2023.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C15; calculation C8

The figures rest on EBITDA calculated from statement lines because the issuer states none, on cash that includes short-term investments, and on quarterly cash flow statements that print operating cash flow as one total. Principal of USD 29,321 million reconciles to carrying value of USD 29,635 million through finance leases of USD 660 million and issuance costs of USD 346 million.
Basis: calculation C10; calculation C3; calculation C1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8, 15

### Leverage and coverage

Total debt to derived EBITDA rose from 6.62x in FY2023 to 6.71x in FY2024, 8.64x in FY2025 and 10.25x for the twelve months to September 2026, and net debt to EBITDA rose from 5.10x to 7.38x. Debt fell by USD 3,895 million between FY2023 and FY2025, so the deterioration comes from earnings: EBITDA fell from USD 4,970 million to USD 3,357 million as salaries, wages and benefits rose by USD 2,986 million while revenue rose by USD 1,845 million. For creditors, debt repayment has been outweighed by weaker operating earnings, which leaves refinancing capacity rather than debt reduction as the main source of comfort.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C17; calculation C1; calculation C2; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 142

In 2026 the shock is fuel. Aircraft fuel and related taxes rose by USD 4,427 million to USD 12,444 million in the first nine months as the average price per gallon rose from USD 2.38 to USD 3.58, and the issuer states it held no fuel hedging contracts at 30 September 2026, so the increase flowed straight through: operating income fell by USD 494 million to USD 521 million although revenue rose by USD 6,348 million. A further rise in fuel or a slowdown in revenue therefore moves EBITDA, and leverage, directly.
Basis: calculation C15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 44, 68–69

EBITDA covered cash interest paid 2.28x in FY2023, 2.35x in FY2024, 1.98x in FY2025 and 1.81x for the last twelve months. Cash interest paid fell from USD 2,180 million in FY2023 to USD 1,594 million over the last twelve months, but EBITDA fell faster, so coverage has weakened even as debt service cost fell.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C6; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 194; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 8

The strongest contrary evidence is liquidity and net debt. Cash and short-term investments of USD 8,287 million, against USD 5,836 million at the FY2025 year end, took net debt down from USD 23,171 million to USD 21,348 million even though gross debt rose, so near-term refinancing risk is lower than the leverage multiple implies. The view would improve if EBITDA recovers toward the FY2024 level of USD 4,540 million, and weaken if fuel costs stay at the 2026 level while revenue growth slows.
Basis: calculation C4; calculation C3; calculation C2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8

### Cash-flow quality

Cash generation does not cover investment on a last-twelve-months basis: free cash flow after working capital was a deficit of USD 680 million in FY2025 (operating cash flow of USD 3,099 million less capital expenditures of USD 3,779 million) and a deficit of USD 91 million for the last twelve months. Operating cash flow of USD 4,541 million in the nine months to September 2026 is helped by working capital: air traffic liability rose by USD 2,256 million and loyalty program liability by USD 950 million since the year end, and the issuer attributes the rise in operating cash flow mainly to those increases. This is customer-funded float that unwinds if bookings soften, so operating cash flow overstates the cash the business generates from earnings.
Basis: calculation C14; calculation C16; calculation C6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 61

Capital expenditures and aircraft purchase deposits were USD 2,728 million in the nine months to September 2026 against USD 2,149 million a year earlier, and the issuer does not split them into maintenance and growth. With free cash flow near zero on a last-twelve-months basis, net debt reduction depends on float growth and on access to financing rather than on internally generated cash.
Basis: calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 8

### Liquidity and maturities

Total available liquidity was USD 11,797 million at 30 September 2026, made up of cash and short-term investments of USD 8,287 million and undrawn facilities of USD 3,510 million, up from USD 9,233 million at 31 December 2025. Restricted cash and short-term investments of USD 555 million are excluded.
Basis: calculation C13; calculation C3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 59–60

Principal of USD 29,321 million matures as USD 631 million in the remainder of 2026, USD 3,550 million in 2027, USD 7,861 million in 2028, USD 3,884 million in 2029, USD 2,729 million in 2030 and USD 10,666 million from 2031. 2028 is the largest single year and a further USD 10,666 million falls due from 2031, so access to refinancing matters more than near-term liquidity. A cargo receivables facility of USD 50 million inside the undrawn total expires in December 2026, letters of credit outstanding against the facilities are not stated for 30 September 2026, and the issuer states compliance with its loan-to-value, collateral coverage, minimum liquidity and debt service coverage covenants without giving a level or headroom for each instrument, so covenant capacity is not established here.
Basis: calculation C9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16, 61, 65–66

### Funding float, subsequent events and multi-figure items

Customer and supplier funding is large and sits outside debt: air traffic liability of USD 9,414 million, loyalty program liability of USD 11,514 million and fuel financing of USD 933 million at 30 September 2026. Fuel financing, USD 74 million at the 2024 year end, is classified as financing in the cash flow statement yet is not in Total Debt, so it is treated as working capital float and flagged. These balances support liquidity and operating cash flow while bookings and supplier terms hold, and they reverse if demand falls or if card processors impose holdback requirements, which the issuer says would reduce its liquidity.
Basis: calculation C16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 194

Events after the FY2025 balance-sheet date are kept out of period figures: the first-quarter 2026 notice to redeem the 8.50% senior secured notes in full, which no longer appear in the 2026 debt table, and the 12 February 2026 amendment that replaced the 2025 AAdvantage term loan with new loans of the same principal at a lower margin. Both are already reflected in the 2026 balance sheets, so no pro forma adjustment is needed. After the 30 September 2026 balance-sheet date, the quarterly report states October 2026 exercises of purchase options on five Airbus A320 Family aircraft and a commitment to buy two Boeing 777-200ER aircraft in the fourth quarter, without amounts, so no net debt adjustment is stated.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 198; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 17, 65

Three multi-figure items change how the numbers are used. Debt is USD 29,635 million at carrying value and USD 29,321 million at principal, the difference being finance leases and issuance costs; cash is USD 8,287 million including short-term investments against USD 1,124 million for the balance sheet cash line, which moves net debt to EBITDA from 7.38x to 9.86x; and depreciation and amortization is USD 1,890 million on the income statement but USD 2,219 million on the cash flow statement for FY2025, which would lift FY2025 EBITDA by USD 329 million.
Basis: calculation C10; calculation C18

### Data quality and definitions

The issuer states no EBITDA, so every leverage and coverage figure is built from statement lines and is only as reliable as that derivation; no adjusted EBITDA, add-back or EBITDA bridge is stated. Quarterly cash flow statements are cumulative from 1 January and print operating cash flow as one total, so cash flow quality in 2026 can be read only at the level of totals, and the fourth quarter of 2025 is constructed as FY2025 less nine months 2025 and labelled calculated.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C7; 10-Q_2026-03-31_aal_aal-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-06-30_aal_aal-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 8

Unit cost excluding special items and fuel is stated on an annual definition (14.12 cents for FY2025) and unit cost excluding special items, fuel and profit sharing on a quarterly one (14.34 cents for Q3 2026), so the two series are kept apart. The 2024 year-end other accrued liabilities line is taken from the later annual report, which splits out fuel financing, and the earlier annual report's presentation is kept for FY2023.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_aal_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 111, 143; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 46–47; 10-K_2024-12-31_aal_aal-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 148; calculation C18
