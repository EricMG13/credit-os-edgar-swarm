## Findings

### Bottom line

ESAB's fundamental credit profile is adequate but stretched by acquisition debt, with total debt at 5.00x reported and 3.95x issuer-adjusted EBITDA, so creditors rely on integration and cash flow, not balance-sheet cushion, until leverage falls. Cash of USD 188,648 thousand (pro forma cash is not established), USD 760 million undrawn on the revolver, no large maturity before April 2029 and consumables at 60.96% of nine-month sales are the main supports; an estimated interest cover of 3.69x, down from 7.46x in FY2025, a raised dividend and finance leadership turnover are the main constraints.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-10-02_esab-20261002.htm.jsonl, page 17; calculation B1; calculation B8; calculation B9

The primary downside path is an earnings or working-capital shortfall during integration that leaves cash flow after capital spending too thin to cut debt, while the revolver covenants, which are not disclosed, govern the one liquidity backstop. This is not a rating and not a view on any security.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-10-02_esab-20261002.htm.jsonl, page 16

### Key findings

- **The business profile is the strongest dimension.** Consumables were 60.96% of nine-month net sales, about 80% of FY2025 sales came from outside the United States, and sales split 39.60% Americas and 60.40% EMEA & APAC, so no region or product group dominates; issuer-adjusted EBITDA was 19.03% of last-twelve-months net sales. The consequence is revenue that is recurring and spread, which supports cash flow through a downturn better than a project-driven equipment business would.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_esab-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 94; 10-K_2025-12-31_esab-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 15; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B4; calculation B10
- **Leverage is the weakest dimension and is driven by the acquisition.** Total debt is 5.00x reported and 3.95x issuer-adjusted EBITDA at October 2, 2026, against 2.49x and 2.21x at year-end 2025, which is before Eddyfi, so a pro forma year-end 2025 figure is not established. Net leverage is 4.59x reported and 3.63x adjusted; pro forma for the USD 7.5 million common dividend paid on 2026-10-16 it is 4.61x, and for the USD 2.8 million preferred dividend paid on 2026-10-15 it is 4.60x. Last-twelve-months EBITDA holds only about 4 months of Eddyfi, so the run-rate ratio is lower than these figures, but the filings do not say by how much.
  Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B1; calculation B2

Pro forma cash for the October 2026 dividends is not established. The consequence is no refinancing pressure for more than two years, but a concentrated wall from 2029 to 2031 that needs market access.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case that leverage is less of a concern than it looks is that the debt is fully loaded but the earnings are not: last-twelve-months issuer-adjusted EBITDA of USD 589.7 million holds only about 4 months of Eddyfi, whose 2025 revenue of about USD 240 million was 8.44% of ESAB's net sales, so a full year would lift EBITDA and lower leverage, and liquidity of USD 760 million undrawn plus cash gives time. The case that it is worse than it looks is that reported EBITDA is USD 466,094 thousand, well below the adjusted figure, because restructuring and acquisition costs are real cash, and that the estimate of cover at 3.69x leaves little room if earnings slip. The view would improve if a pro forma EBITDA showed total leverage below 4x, if cash flow after capital spending rose above 10% of debt, or if the credit agreement showed ample covenant headroom. It would worsen if reported EBITDA fell again, if the dividend rose or another large acquisition were debt-funded before leverage falls, or if the unexplained finance leadership change proved a sign of control problems.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-10-02_esab-20261002.htm.jsonl, page 35; calculation B4; calculation B9

### Limitations

- Covenant levels and definitions are not supplied, so the conclusion that liquidity is adequate holds only while the revolver stays available; if a covenant binds at current leverage, the USD 760 million of availability could shrink and the view on liquidity would weaken.
- The cover of 3.69x is an estimate from the weighted-average rate and principal, not interest paid, so the conclusion that fixed charges absorb much more of EBITDA could be too severe or too mild by the effect of swaps, timing and the new debt's actual cost.
- No management guidance, forecast or pro forma EBITDA was supplied, so the outlook and the downside scenario are qualitative and the size of the leverage overstatement cannot be put into the view.
- No agency rating, bond price or peer data was supplied, so the statement that refinancing access is adequate rests on the maturity profile and liquidity alone and not on market signals.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. A global, diversified, consumables-led business with about 80% of sales outside the United States and no customer above 10% of trade receivables turns volume into recurring cash flow, which supports debt service through a cycle. Liquidity of cash plus an undrawn USD 1,050 million revolver covers near-term needs, and nothing large matures before April 2029. Insiders and institutions put in USD 318 million of equity, which limited the debt added. The cash flow chain is evidence from the revenue and debt notes, the mechanism of recurring revenue and a back-ended maturity profile, and the implication of lower refinancing and default risk in the next two years.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_esab-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 113; 10-K_2025-12-31_esab-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-10-02_esab-20261002.htm.jsonl, pages 15–17; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Weaknesses. Debt has roughly doubled to 5.00x reported EBITDA against 2.49x at year-end 2025, interest cost will take a much larger share of EBITDA, cash flow after capital spending is only 7.76% of debt, and a dividend raise, option awards tied to share prices and unexplained finance leadership changes point to a policy that favours growth over repair. The mechanism is higher fixed charges and slower deleveraging, and the implication is greater sensitivity of default and refinancing risk to an earnings miss during the integration year.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B1; calculation B5; calculation B9

### Company description and ownership

ESAB makes welding and cutting systems, consumables, gas control equipment, automation and robotics, software and, since June 2026, advanced inspection technology, selling through distributors and direct sales in about 150 countries. Consumables are the larger and steadier part of sales, equipment the cyclical part, and acquisitions are a core strategy: four in 2025 and Eddyfi in 2026. The credit linkage is that recurring consumables and global spread support cash flow and refinancing confidence, while a strategy built on acquisitions means leverage will recur as a risk unless funding stays equity-light. ESAB is a listed company without a sponsor; Mitchell P. Rales is Executive Chair and the Rales Holders hold registration rights, but their percentage is not stated, so ownership concentration and distribution behaviour are rated on actions, not structure.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_esab-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-10-02_esab-20261002.htm.jsonl, page 23

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Consumables, which are replaced as they are used, were 65.81% of FY2025 sales and 60.96% of nine-month 2026 sales, so volume follows industrial activity but with a floor from maintenance and replacement demand. Pricing has helped: Americas net sales from existing businesses rose on USD 25.2 million of customer pricing in the nine months while volumes fell in South America and Mexico, and EMEA & APAC organic growth was supported by pricing and Asia volumes against Middle East weakness. Organic growth was 0.9% in the nine months after minus 1.1% in FY2025, so revenue is stable rather than growing and acquisitions supply most of the growth; the credit implication is steady but not expanding cash flow that cannot by itself deleverage quickly.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-02_esab-20261002.htm.jsonl, page 40; 10-Q_2026-10-02_esab-20261002.htm.jsonl, page 47; 10-K_2025-12-31_esab-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 56; 10-K_2025-12-31_esab-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 94

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Steel, iron, copper and aluminum are the main inputs, tariffs added cost from April 2025, and gross margin fell from 37.9% in FY2024 to 36.9% in FY2025 before recovering to 38.1% in the nine months of 2026 as pricing, favourable tariff effects and acquisitions helped. Operating income margin fell as selling and administrative costs and restructuring rose with the larger group, so the issuer-adjusted EBITDA margin of 18.8% for nine months is below 19.7% for FY2025. Margins can be defended through pricing, as 2026 shows, but integration and restructuring costs are cash, so reported EBITDA is the better measure of what services debt.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_esab-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_esab-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 57; 10-Q_2026-10-02_esab-20261002.htm.jsonl, pages 42–43; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Capital intensity and FCF conversion

Capital spending is modest at USD 49,700 thousand in nine months of 2026, though it rose from USD 27,654 thousand, and the issuer separates asbestos and restructuring cash from capital spending. Conversion of reported EBITDA to cash flow after capital spending was 38.78% over the last twelve months, held down by restructuring, integration, working capital and interest, and cash taxes and interest were substantial. The implication is that EBITDA overstates what is available for debt service, so deleveraging depends on EBITDA rising and cash costs falling, not on capital spending cuts.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B5

### Market frameworks, credit-translated

Rivalry is the force that matters: the filing names Lincoln Electric and the welding business of Illinois Tool Works as competitors and describes a fragmented market where price, quality and delivery decide, which caps pricing power and implies margins are defended by brands and aftermarket support rather than by dominance. Buyer power is limited by a diversified customer base with no customer above 10% of receivables, supplier power by multiple sources for steel, copper and aluminum, and entry and substitution threats are low in consumables and rising in automation, where the company itself invests in software and robotics. Macro, tariff and war factors are material enough for a PEST view: US tariffs raised costs and prices, IEEPA tariff refunds are not yet recorded as receivables, the Middle East conflict cut sales, and sanctions leave about USD 51 million of Russian cash hard to withdraw; the credit consequence is cost and cash volatility that the company has so far passed through to prices.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_esab-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-K_2025-12-31_esab-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 113; 10-K_2025-12-31_esab-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-10-02_esab-20261002.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2026-10-02_esab-20261002.htm.jsonl, page 36

In credit terms the strengths are scale and spread, recurring consumables, pricing power in 2026, a back-ended maturity profile and insider equity support; the weaknesses are acquisition leverage, a rising interest burden, thin cash flow against debt, and unexplained leadership changes; the opportunities are Eddyfi earnings and synergies, tariff refunds and margin programmes; and the threats are an earnings miss during integration, trade and geopolitical shocks, further debt-funded deals and covenant constraints on the revolver.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Financial profile and credit quality

On a ratings-style lens the assessments are as follows. Scale and market position are strong, and competitive advantage and business diversification average to strong given brands, aftermarket and geographic spread. Cost and capex flexibility and margin stability are average: pricing has defended margins but restructuring and integration costs are high. Free cash flow stability is average to weak because conversion is below 40% of reported EBITDA and falling interest cover will bite. Ability to refinance is average, helped by a back-ended wall and equity support but limited by leverage near 5x reported and unknown covenants. Liquidity is average to strong on cash and an undrawn revolver subject to covenants, and financial policy and governance are average because supportive equity funding is offset by a raised dividend, option incentives and turnover. The trend is deterioration in 2026 from the acquisition, with the main missing datapoints being covenant levels, pro forma EBITDA and interest paid.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B1; calculation B5

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over twelve to twenty-four months the tailwinds are a full year of Eddyfi, pricing, tariff refunds and margin programmes; the headwinds are a high interest bill, continuing restructuring and integration cash costs, weak South American, Mexican and Middle East volumes, and the refinancing step at the 2029 notes. Over three to five years the maturity wall of 2029 to 2031 is the dominant issue and depends on leverage having fallen by then; the structural tailwind is automation, inspection and high-growth markets, which the issuer puts at 52% of FY2025 revenue by its own definition. No management guidance was supplied, so the outlook is qualitative.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_esab-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-10-02_esab-20261002.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-10-02_esab-20261002.htm.jsonl, page 47; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Qualitative downside scenario

A realistic downside starts with softer industrial demand and tariff-driven pricing fatigue during the first full year after the deal, the first operational break being lower consumables volumes and integration costs that run longer than planned. Reported EBITDA, already USD 466,094 thousand over twelve months, would fall, working capital would absorb cash again as in the nine months of 2026, and cash flow after capital spending, now USD 180,737 thousand, would approach the cash dividends and fixed charges. Liquidity of cash and USD 760 million undrawn would still cover near-term needs, but leverage would rise further and covenant headroom, which is not disclosed, becomes the observable trigger: a tightening revolver would leave the 2029 notes to be refinanced from a weaker position. Management levers are the dividend, capital spending, restructuring and asset sales; the evidence supports resilience sufficient to avoid distress in the next two years but not a comfortable deleveraging path.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B5; calculation B7

### Overall credit view

Overall, ESAB presents a solid business profile supported by scale, a consumables-led and geographically spread revenue base and insider equity support, but credit quality is constrained by acquisition leverage, a much higher interest burden and thin cash flow against debt. Cash-flow durability appears adequate due to recurring consumables and pricing, but conversion is held down by integration and restructuring cash. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on deleveraging before the 2029 notes, on covenant headroom in the revolver and on market access. The primary downside path is an earnings or working-capital shortfall during integration. Further analysis would require pro forma EBITDA, covenant terms, interest paid and management guidance. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: acquisition leverage with an undisclosed covenant backstop.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)
