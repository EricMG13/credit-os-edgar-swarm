## Findings

### Bottom line

Trinity is a listed North American railcar lessor and manufacturer with no controlling owner disclosed, so creditors rely on lease-fleet cash flow that mostly services non-recourse debt, while a shrinking manufacturing arm and two partnership restructurings make the reporting perimeter unstable.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 7, 93–94; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 17, 20; calculation C1

The lease fleet was 97.0% utilized at 2026-09-30 with an average remaining lease term of 2.7 years, and non-recourse debt is 88.75% of consolidated debt, but manufacturing fell from 62.95% of revenue in FY2024 to 44.14% in FY2025 and one customer supplied 19% of FY2025 revenue. Sponsor and governance risk is Medium on documented behaviour, because shareholder returns continued while cash flow after fleet investment was negative, and the proxy statement and executed financing documents are not supplied.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 43, 46; 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 66, 93; calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C19; calculation C21; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

- No controlling owner is identified, so governance risk cannot be read from sponsor identity and none is inferred. The filings name no holder or sponsor with control, and the proxy statement that would list principal holders, board rights and related-party dealings is not supplied, so ownership conclusions stop at the listed-company facts.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 37, 127–128; Source readiness (CP-0)
- The Leasing Group is the earnings core and its revenue base is short-dated. Rentals are fixed monthly for one to ten years, utilization is 97.0%, and the average remaining term of 2.7 years means renewals reprice the fleet within a few years; management attributes offsetting support to higher lease rates and net fleet additions.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 42–43
- Perimeter changes, not operations alone, explain the fall in lease revenue. Leasing and management revenue fell 8.96% in nine months 2026, and the issuer attributes USD 85.1 million of the reduction to the two divested partnerships; on that reading revenue excluding them rose about USD 23.8 million, an Analyst Interpretation that the filings do not state.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 40, 42; calculation C11; calculation C12
- Manufacturing is cyclical, concentrated and exposed to Mexico trade action. Deliveries fell 45.93% in FY2025 and 15.03% in nine months 2026, one customer supplied 19% of FY2025 revenue, more than half of employees work in Mexico, and a petition filed on 2026-09-30 seeks antidumping and countervailing duties on tank cars imported from Mexico; the backlog of USD 1,646.7 million is 6.56% lower than a year earlier.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10, 48, 93; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 28, 45–46; calculation C14; calculation C13; calculation C17; calculation C15
- Financial policy is mixed rather than adverse. Dividends and buybacks totalled USD 170.0 million in FY2025 and USD 117.4 million in nine months 2026 while cash flow after fleet investment was negative USD 28.7 million and negative USD 32.8 million, yet no exchange, waiver, priming or related-party extraction was found and the non-recourse notes are ring-fenced from Trinity.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 53, 68–69; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4, 22, 49; calculation C19; calculation C20; calculation C21; calculation C22

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious reading is the stability of the leased fleet: utilization has stayed at or near 97.0%, rentals are fixed under multi-year contracts, the lessor debt is amortizing, secured on the railcars and non-recourse to Trinity, and the Leasing Group backlog of USD 333.7 million is described as fully supported by lease commitments with external customers. The annual statements carry an unqualified audit opinion, and the board is described by the company as having an independent chairman, which is management language, not a tested fact.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 22, 43, 45–46; 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 64

The view would soften if the proxy statement showed no dominant holder and no related-party fees, if the credit agreement and indenture showed tight restricted-payment limits, if the Mexico duty petition were dismissed, and if interim reports began to print cash interest and a current maturity ladder. It would harden if shareholder returns continued to exceed cash flow after fleet investment, if the single large manufacturing customer reduced orders, or if further partnership exchanges moved more debt and earnings off the consolidated statements.

### Limitations

- The proxy statement is not supplied, so the ownership register and governance register stop at the listed-company facts and the Medium governance level could move either way once principal holders, board rights and related-party fees are known.
  Basis: Source readiness (CP-0); 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 127–128
- The executed credit agreement and indenture are not supplied, so legal capacity for distributions, investments and asset transfers is not established and the conclusion on financial policy rests on documented behaviour alone, which cannot separate willingness from capacity.
  Basis: Source readiness (CP-0); 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 130–134
- Lessee concentration in the lease book is not disclosed, only one manufacturing customer, so the durability of lease revenue cannot be tested by counterparty and the view that leasing is stable rests on utilization and lease terms.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 93
- The earnings effect of the two partnership exits is quantified only for revenue, so comparisons of operating profit and mix across FY2024, FY2025 and nine months 2026 are not like-for-like and the shrinking-manufacturing finding is stated on reported figures.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, page 42
- No earnings release, lender presentation or current report is supplied, so events between 2026-10-29 and the 2026-11-18 cutoff are not established and the transaction list may be incomplete.
  Basis: Source readiness (CP-0); 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, page 55

### Governance view

Overall, Trinity presents Medium sponsor and governance risk on the evidence supplied. The supported behaviour is continued dividends and buybacks while cash flow after fleet investment was negative, which matters because net borrowing absorbs the shortfall and keeps debt from falling; no priming, exchange, waiver or related-party extraction was found, and the lessor debt is non-recourse. Further analysis requires the proxy statement, the credit agreement and the indenture. The governance review is complete with limitations.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 53, 68–69, 94; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 22, 49; calculation C19; calculation C21; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

In April 2026 a limited-purpose leasing subsidiary issued USD 480.8 million of Series 2026-1 secured railcar notes, USD 447.4 million at a fixed 5.35% and USD 33.4 million at a fixed 5.56%, with a stated final maturity of April 19, 2056; the notes are non-recourse to Trinity and the proceeds redeemed the Series 2019-1 notes, of which USD 377.1 million was outstanding at a 3.82% rate.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-03-31_trn.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 21–22

On April 9, 2026 the leasing subsidiary TILC agreed to contribute its 42.56% interest in TRIP Holdings and a 0.2% interest in Triumph Rail Holdings to NP SPE Holdings LP, a Napier Park vehicle, for an 11.2% limited partnership interest, valued at USD 112.9 million. The exchange was completed in April 2026, deconsolidated TRIP Holdings and its Tribute Rail debt of USD 270.6 million, and produced a non-cash pre-tax gain of USD 131.6 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-03-31_trn.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18, 36

In June 2026 Trinity amended and restated its USD 600.0 million unsecured revolving credit facility, extending maturity to the earlier of June 12, 2031 or April 15, 2028 if the senior notes due 2028 have not been repaid, adding an option to increase commitments by up to USD 300.0 million subject to customary conditions and modifying the leverage ratio calculation for certain cash balances. In the same month it acquired a 32.0% interest in an Indian railcar lessor for USD 37.6 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19, 21

On October 19, 2026, after the September 30 balance sheet, a limited-purpose subsidiary entered into a USD 349.8 million non-recourse term loan at daily simple SOFR plus 1.30% maturing October 19, 2031, and used the proceeds to redeem USD 280.9 million of Series 2019-2 notes at a 3.10% rate and to repay warehouse borrowings; an interest rate swap was added in October 2026.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 22–23

Earlier, in December 2025, TILC exchanged its interest in Triumph for Napier Park's interest in RIV 2013, giving it full ownership of RIV 2013 and a non-cash pre-tax gain of USD 194.2 million, and in October 2025 a subsidiary issued USD 535.2 million of Series 2025-1 notes that redeemed older notes and repaid warehouse borrowings. In April 2025 the TRL-2023 term loan was increased to USD 1.05 billion and extended to April 30, 2030.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 43, 94, 104–105
