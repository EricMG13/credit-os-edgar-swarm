No rating, relative-value, covenant-capacity or recovery conclusion is drawn.

## Findings

### Bottom line

Trinity is an adequate credit: fixed-rent railcar leases service mostly non-recourse debt, but leverage above seven times underlying EBITDA, a manufacturing arm that has halved and distributions that absorb most spare cash leave corporate creditors dependent on residual cash flow.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C2; calculation C19; calculation C12

Net debt is 5.04x reported EBITDA at 2026-09-30 and 5.04x pro forma, with net debt of USD 5,168.0 million, after share repurchases settled in October 2026; without two non-cash divestiture gains it is 7.39x. The effect of the October 2026 term loan on net debt is not established. Non-recourse debt is 88.75% of the total, and pledged railcars at book value are 1.21x that debt. Over twelve months, cash flow after fleet investment of USD 165.5 million exceeded distributions of USD 153.9 million by USD 11.6 million.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 20, 49; calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C4; calculation C6; calculation C10; calculation C11; calculation C12

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against caution is asset and contract quality. Pledged railcars are carried at 1.21x the non-recourse debt they secure, utilization is 97.0%, Leasing Group orders in backlog of USD 333.7 million are fully supported by external lease commitments, orders recovered to 0.97x deliveries, the issuer reports covenant compliance with large cushions on its own definitions, and it placed USD 480.8 million of notes with a final maturity in 2056 in April 2026 and a USD 349.8 million term loan in October 2026, so market access is shown by transactions and not assumed. Cash flow after fleet investment also exceeded distributions over twelve months.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 20–23, 35, 43, 45–46, 50–51; calculation C6; calculation C8; calculation C21; calculation C22; calculation C30; calculation C12

The view would improve if the filings print cash interest paid and a current maturity ladder that confirm the 2027 and 2028 refinancing, if the Mexico petition fails at the preliminary stage, if EBITDA without gains grows while debt falls, and if the proxy statement shows no dominant holder. It would worsen if the warehouse revolving period ends on 2027-03-15 without extension, if distributions again exceed cash flow after fleet investment, if duties are imposed, or if utilization falls from 97.0% while the remaining lease term shortens further.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Contracted, highly utilized fleet cash flow is the first strength. Utilization of 97.0% and fixed rents of one to ten years give rent visibility, and the cash goes first to secured notes with no recourse to Trinity, so a fleet shortfall is contained and the senior notes are not asked to fund it. This lowers the probability that fleet stress reaches the corporate creditor.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 43; calculation C8; calculation C4

Collateral and ring-fencing are the second strength. Pledged railcars at book value cover non-recourse debt 1.21x, and USD 798.8 million of equipment is unpledged and supports warehouse availability, so fleet lenders are well protected on book values and the unpledged pool is a source of liquidity. Market values and recovery are outside this assessment.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21; calculation C6

Demonstrated market access is the third. The issuer placed USD 480.8 million of notes in April 2026 at 5.36%, arranged a USD 349.8 million term loan in October 2026 and amended its USD 600.0 million revolver to mature on 2031-06-12, or on 2028-04-15 if the senior notes due 2028 are not repaid, so the issuer reports completing the refinancing of its 2026 maturities, which supports the near-term refinancing view at a higher cost.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 21–23, 35, 47; calculation C26; calculation C25

Vertical integration and order recovery are the fourth. Substantially all leased railcars are built by the Rail Products Group, orders were 0.97x deliveries in nine months 2026, and about 25% of backlog value is due in the fourth quarter of 2026 and 50% in 2027, which gives the manufacturing arm visible volume for the next year.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 45–46; calculation C21; calculation C22

Reported leverage that depends on gains is the first weakness. Two non-cash gains of USD 325.8 million are 31.78% of twelve-month EBITDA, so the reported improvement to 5.04x is partly accounting for exits, and leverage without them is 7.39x; creditors should size debt service capacity on the lower earnings base.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C2

A short remaining lease term with a falling margin is the second. The 2.7-year average remaining term means rents reprice within a few years while maintenance and compliance costs are elevated, so a softer rental market would reach cash flow quickly and the 5.0 percentage point fall in the Leasing Group margin may continue.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 34, 40, 43; calculation C8; calculation C9

Distributions and fleet growth funded by borrowing are the third. Cash flow after fleet investment was negative in FY2025 and in nine months 2026 while distributions of USD 170.0 million and USD 117.4 million were paid, and wholly-owned non-recourse debt rose USD 152.7 million, so debt does not fall with fleet amortization and the corporate buffer stays thin.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4, 49; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C10; calculation C11; calculation C13

Cyclical, concentrated manufacturing with Mexico duty exposure is the fourth. Manufacturing is 44.14% of revenue, deliveries fell 45.93% in FY2025, one customer is roughly 43% of manufacturing revenue and most railcars are built in Mexico, so an order pause or duty would cut volume or margin in the segment that supports corporate cash flow; Mexican long-lived assets are only USD 94.3 million, so the exposure is cost and volume and not asset impairment.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 93; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, page 28; calculation C19; calculation C20; calculation C23; calculation C24

Refinancing step-up and shrinking warehouse headroom are the fifth. Notes at 3.82% and 3.10% have been replaced at 5.36% and SOFR plus 1.30%, USD 2,360.2 million of year-end principal is due in 2027 and 2028, and committed liquidity fell 19.57%, mostly because warehouse availability depends on eligible unpledged railcars and fell, so interest cost rises and execution risk sits in 2027.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 101, 107; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 21–23, 47; calculation C25; calculation C27; calculation C29

### Business and ownership

Trinity is a North American railcar lessor and manufacturer, and the leasing business is what supports debt service. The Leasing Group earns full-service operating lease rents from railroads and industrial shippers across refined products, chemicals, energy, agriculture, construction, metals and consumer products, and leasing and services provided 55.86% of FY2025 revenue; the Rail Products Group builds substantially all the leased railcars and manufacturing provided 44.14%, after manufacturing revenue fell 50.88% in FY2025. Steady rents support cash-flow visibility and secured refinancing, while manufacturing swings volume and margin and adds a concentration in one customer, so the two halves of the business carry very different credit weight.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8, 66; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C19; calculation C36; calculation C23

No controlling holder or sponsor is identified, and ownership conclusions stop at listed-company facts because the proxy statement is not supplied. Group structure matters more than ownership: TILC holds the fleet through limited-purpose subsidiaries whose notes are non-recourse to Trinity, two partnerships were exchanged out of the consolidated group in December 2025 and April 2026, and investor-owned railcars are 34.09% of railcars under management, so servicing and fee income continue while debt left the balance sheet. The earlier governance review rated sponsor and financial-policy risk Medium, which this assessment keeps, because distributions continued while cash flow after fleet investment was negative.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18, 43; calculation C36; calculation C11

### Revenue, cost and cash conversion

Leasing revenue is predictable and contract-based, while manufacturing revenue is cyclical, discretionary and concentrated. Leasing and management revenue fell 8.96% in nine months 2026, but excluding the divested partnerships it rose USD 23.8 million, or 3.97%, so the base fleet grew with higher lease rates and net additions. Deliveries fell 45.93% in FY2025 and 15.03% in nine months 2026, orders recovered to 0.97x deliveries, and one customer is roughly 43% of manufacturing revenue, so manufacturing revenue visibility rests on a backlog of USD 1,646.7 million that is stable since year-end.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 48, 93; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 38, 40, 45–46; calculation C33; calculation C20; calculation C21; calculation C22; calculation C23

Margins are not secure in either segment. The Leasing Group margin before lease portfolio sales and the divestiture gain was 30.5% in nine months 2026 against 35.5% a year earlier, with higher fleet operating costs and administrative expense up 8.5%, while the Rail Products Group margin was 7.79% in FY2024, 5.23% in FY2025 and 6.26% in nine months 2026 and its operating profit fell 60.77% in FY2025, which shows heavy operating leverage to volume. Steel and component cost is managed through contract-specific purchasing and price escalation clauses, but some materials come from a limited number of suppliers.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18, 48; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 34, 38, 40, 44; calculation C9; calculation C18; calculation C34

Earnings convert to cash well below the reported EBITDA. Operating cash flow from continuing operations was 60.39% of EBITDA without gains over twelve months, after interest, and fleet investment then absorbs more: lease fleet investment is mostly new railcar additions, conversions and modifications and is partly funded by lease portfolio sales and secured debt, so cash flow after net fleet investment was USD -19.3 million in FY2023, USD 353.1 million in FY2024, USD -28.7 million in FY2025 and USD -32.8 million in nine months 2026. Fleet investment is the main discretionary lever, but maintenance and compliance spending is not.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 34, 49; calculation C35; calculation C10

### Industry forces

Rivalry, buyers and suppliers matter most. Trinity competes with five major railcar lessors and four major railcar manufacturers in a market the filing calls intensely competitive, so pricing power is limited and competition rests on quality, price and service; buyers are railroads, lessors and shippers, and one manufacturing customer is roughly 43% of manufacturing revenue, so buyer power is real in manufacturing; certain materials come from a limited number of suppliers, which exposes delivery schedules and margins. New entrants and substitutes are not established from the filings as credit drivers.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8, 17–19, 93; calculation C23

Political and economic factors are material and social and technological factors are not primary drivers. The petition seeking antidumping and countervailing duties on tank cars imported from Mexico, the Section 232 tariffs and the administration of trade policy in the United States and Mexico bear directly on a manufacturing base where most railcars are built in Mexico, and the filing says uncertainty rose in 2026; demand follows industrial production, interest rates and customer credit, and skilled-labor shortages raise cost, but social and technological factors are smaller than the trade risk.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 17–19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 28, 33; calculation C24

Opportunities that could improve credit quality are the recovery in orders, higher lease rates on a utilized fleet and fee income from investor-owned railcars, which are 34.09% of railcars under management. Threats that could weaken it are the duty proceedings, cost reset on maturing low-coupon notes, softer rental markets as leases roll over within 2.7 years, and a repeat of the FY2025 delivery collapse of 45.93%.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 28, 43, 45–46; calculation C36; calculation C8; calculation C21; calculation C20

### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale and market position is Strong, competitive advantage is Average and business diversification is Average. Trinity manages a large railcar fleet, leases it on fixed rents and builds most of its own cars, which gives scale and an integrated platform, but it names no market share, competition is intense and the business is North American with one concentrated manufacturing customer, so these dimensions support the credit without being a moat.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8, 19, 93; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, page 43; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Cost and capex flexibility is Average, margin stability is Average and free cash flow stability is Weak. Growth fleet investment and distributions can be cut, but maintenance and compliance costs cannot, margins fell in leasing and moved widely in manufacturing, and cash flow after net fleet investment swung from USD 353.1 million in FY2024 to USD -28.7 million in FY2025, which makes cash available for debt reduction unreliable.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 34, 40; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C9; calculation C18; calculation C10

Ability to refinance is Average, liquidity position is Average and financial policy and governance is Average. Market access was shown in 2026 and the revolver runs to 2031-06-12 or 2028-04-15 if the senior notes are not repaid, but coupons rose, committed liquidity of USD 897.2 million fell 19.57% and relies in part on eligible unpledged railcars, and distributions continue while cash flow after fleet investment is thin.

### Corrections to earlier analyses

Two earlier statements are corrected here. Financial foundation said the filings do not quantify the perimeter mismatch from the exited partnerships, and Business & governance said the earnings effect of the exits is quantified for revenue only. The quarterly report in fact states that the two divested leasing subsidiaries contributed USD 13.9 million of operating profit in the third quarter of 2025 and USD 35.9 million in nine months 2025, besides USD 85.1 million lower revenue in nine months 2026. The annual report also shows year-end debt for them, USD 469.4 million of Triumph notes at 2024-12-31 and USD 270.6 million of Tribute Rail debt at 2025-12-31. It states no interest, EBITDA or cash flow for them, and their earnings inside the twelve months to 2026-09-30 are not established, so the leverage figures stand, but the size of the earnings side is now bounded: 57.62% of the USD 62.3 million underlying fall in operating profit is the exited partnerships.

### Outlook

Over the next 12 months to 24 months the order book and the refinancing calendar decide the credit. Management expects about 25% of backlog value to be delivered in the fourth quarter of 2026 and 50% in 2027, net fleet investment of USD 300 million to USD 350 million in 2026, and operating capital expenditure of USD 55 million to USD 65 million; tailwinds are higher lease rates, net fleet additions and recovered orders, while headwinds are the pending Commerce decision and preliminary ITC investigation on Mexican tank cars, elevated maintenance cost, the warehouse revolving end on 2027-03-15, and replacement of low-coupon notes at higher cost. These support revenue visibility in 2027 but pressure margin and refinancing confidence.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, page 101; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 28, 38, 45–46, 51; calculation C22; calculation C14; calculation C28; calculation C25

Over three to five years, lease repricing and the ladder dominate. The 2.7-year average remaining term means much of the book reprices within the period, a fleet average age of 14.8 years, up from 14.3 years, may add to maintenance cost and obsolescence exposure, and the year-end ladder shows USD 259.0 million due in 2029, USD 938.0 million in 2030 and USD 813.2 million thereafter beyond the 2027 and 2028 amounts, with the revolver at 2031-06-12, so deleveraging depends on rents holding up and on distributions staying below cash flow after fleet investment. The railcar industry has had sharp cyclical downturns with minimal backlog, and the filing states that industry order volumes and backlog are currently reduced.

### Downside scenario

The issuer-specific downside begins with a duty on Mexican tank cars or an order pause in a weak freight cycle, which breaks first in the Rail Products Group because most railcars are built in Mexico and one customer is roughly 43% of manufacturing revenue. A repeat of the FY2025 pattern, when deliveries fell 45.93% and segment operating profit fell 60.77%, would cut operating cash flow and reduce railcar sales to the Leasing Group; in a weak freight cycle the secondary market behind lease portfolio sale proceeds of USD 168.7 million in nine months 2026 would also soften, so cash flow after net fleet investment, already thin after distributions, would turn negative, net borrowing would rise and leverage without gains would stay above seven times.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 17–19, 48; 10-Q_2026-09-30_trn.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 28, 46; calculation C20; calculation C18; calculation C23; calculation C12; calculation C14

Liquidity and refinancing would absorb the shock if management acts. The levers are suspending buybacks and dividends, which were USD 153.9 million over twelve months, cutting growth fleet investment guided at USD 300 million to USD 350 million, and drawing the revolver of USD 592.4 million; the exposure is the warehouse, whose availability depends on eligible unpledged railcars and whose revolving period ends on 2027-03-15, and the senior notes due 2028 with the revolver maturity tied to them. On stated liquidity of USD 897.2 million resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress over the next 12 months, because fleet debt is non-recourse and the levers are large relative to cash flow; if the warehouse is not extended, its undrawn availability ends on 2027-03-15 and the drawn balance falls due by 2028-03-15, which would test corporate liquidity if capital markets were closed at the same time. No stress amount is stated because the filings give no basis for one.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Trinity presents an adequate business profile supported by a 97.0% utilized, fixed-rent lease fleet, ring-fenced non-recourse funding with 1.21x book collateral cover and demonstrated market access, but credit quality is constrained by leverage of 7.39x without gains, a 2.7-year average remaining lease term and distributions that absorb 92.99% of cash flow after fleet investment. Cash-flow durability appears adequate at fleet level and weak after growth investment and distributions. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on extending the warehouse before 2027-03-15, addressing the senior notes due 2028 and holding market access at current coupons, and on the net debt effect of the October 2026 term loan, which is not established. The primary downside path is a duty or order pause in manufacturing that reduces cash flow while distributions continue and net borrowing rises. Further analysis would require parent-only cash flow, current cash interest, a current maturity ladder, lease expiries by year, and the executed credit agreement and proxy statement. Top risk: rising secured debt funding distributions and growth against a gain-inflated earnings base.
