### Company description

The issuer sells funeral and cemetery goods, services and property across North America, at need and on a preneed basis, and describes itself as the largest provider in the region. Funeral operations were 55.82% of 2025 revenue and cemeteries 44.18%, with 95.03% earned in the United States, and the footprint grew by six funeral locations and six cemeteries between 2025-12-31 and 2026-09-30. The credit linkage is revenue visibility: preneed sales are contracted years before delivery, price-guaranteed and funded through trusts or insurance, so cash flow depends less on the number of deaths in any one year than an at-need business would, while the funeral side stays tied to volume and a largely fixed cost base. Preneed funds are held in trusts or insurance policies and cannot be used freely for debt service.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_sci-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-31_sci-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 47; calculation C08

### Ownership and group structure

The issuer is a listed public company with no sponsor or controlling holder named in the supplied filings; ten directors, including a lead independent director, signed the annual report and the chief executive is also chairman. Operating subsidiaries are owned directly or indirectly, a majority of the domestic subsidiaries guarantee the bank credit agreement, the guarantors of the senior notes are not stated, and the preneed and perpetual care trusts are consolidated but hold assets for customers and cemetery upkeep. The financial policy is explicit: a payout target of 30% to 40% of adjusted earnings plus buybacks, a leverage target of 3.5x to 4.0x, and bank covenants limiting dividends and repurchases. Creditor alignment is moderate because distributions exceeded free cash flow in 2025 while the issuer's reported covenant ratio stays inside its own target range, and the governance risk level from the business and governance analysis is Medium.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is largely recurring and regulated and less cyclical than discretionary consumer spending, with demand that the issuer describes as deferred rather than lost, and it is predictable at the contract level but variable with deaths and flu seasons. Volume is the weak driver: comparable funeral services fell 0.83% in 2025 and 2.66% in the first nine months of 2026, while the first quarter alone fell 6% on a strong prior-year flu season. Price and mix partly offset it, with revenue per service up 2.92% in 2025 and 3.17% in the nine months, even as cremation, which earns less per case, reached 64.9% of comparable cases. Preneed contracts are price-guaranteed, so inflation in goods and labour is the issuer's risk until trust earnings and pricing catch up. No customer concentration is disclosed, switching costs are local reputation and convenience, and the evidence supports a revenue base that is predictable in total and exposed in funeral volume.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

The issuer calls both businesses high fixed-cost, and the observed response confirms it: a 2.66% fall in comparable funeral services came with a 6.95% fall in comparable funeral gross profit, and the comparable funeral margin dropped to 19.2% from 20.8%. Salaries and fringe benefits are 27.89% of funeral revenue and facilities 11.32%, while cemetery selling compensation is 18.11% of cemetery revenue and rose with preneed production and higher guaranteed hourly wages. Healthcare costs also rose. Cost cuts have limits because licensed staff and facilities do not scale with volume, and the margin defence is price and cemetery mix. Corporate expenses fell in 2026 on lower share-return-linked incentive pay, a favorable legal settlement and favorable self-insurance reserve adjustments, which are not a recurring source of margin. Margins can be defended under mild volume pressure but not under a second year of declines without cost action.

### Porter's five forces

Rivalry is moderate because most competitors are small local operators and the issuer estimates about 18% share, so price competition comes from independents, low-cost providers and casket retailers rather than from a few large peers, which caps pricing in the funeral business. New entrants face low to moderate barriers in funeral service but high barriers in cemeteries because of land, permitting and capital, which protects cemetery earnings. Cremation is the main substitute, at 64.4% of comparable cases in 2025, and it earns less per case, which drags average revenue unless memorialization is sold. Buyers are individual families with itemized price disclosure under the Funeral Rule, which limits price discretion at need but is offset by preneed contracts that fix price. Suppliers of caskets and merchandise and the licensed funeral workforce matter more than any single vendor, and tariffs and wages are the credit-relevant risks.

### Political, economic, social and technology factors

Regulatory and economic factors are material to credit and the social and technology factors are secondary. The Funeral Rule has been under federal review since 2020, state laws govern preneed trusts and surety use, about forty states are auditing unused preneed trust funds, and the annual report lists proposals that could prohibit common ownership of funeral homes and cemeteries in the same market, which matters because the issuer has the largest number of combination locations. Economic factors act through inflation and trust returns, which fed 2025 trust income and could reverse. Social factors are an ageing population, which the issuer expects to lift atneed results as preneed contracts mature, and a rising cremation share. Technology is mainly digital lead generation and an AI governance committee, neither of which moves credit quality.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months the issuer's own outlook points to lower earnings growth than first expected: full-year adjusted earnings per share of USD 3.94 to USD 4.04 against a range of USD 4.10 to USD 4.30 set in July, with the midpoint of adjusted operating cash flow guidance, about USD 1,085 million, maintained and maintenance capital expenditure of USD 335 million. ### Qualitative downside scenario

The initiating condition is another year of falling funeral volume, as in the first nine months of 2026, while market returns weaken. The first operational break is funeral gross margin: at the observed ratio of 2.61x, a repeat of the 2.66% volume fall would cut funeral gross profit by about 6.95%, or USD 34.46 million on the 2025 base, before cost action and tax. Financial transmission is small on its own: derived EBITDA would fall to USD 1,212.485 million and net leverage would rise to 4.12x with cover of 4.73x. The larger threat is compounding: lower trust returns reduce recognized trust income, a loss of surety capacity could require about USD 258.7 million of trust funding, and payouts continuing at the 2026 pace would hold derived leverage near the top of the target range and push it above if earnings fell. Management levers are large relative to the shock: buybacks of USD 318.942 million in nine months, growth capital expenditure, acquisitions and headquarters spending can all be slowed, and USD 1,513.7 million of revolver capacity is undrawn. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress; the exposure is leverage drifting above the top of the issuer's target range and a tighter refinancing market in 2030, not default.

### Overall credit view

Overall, SERVICE CORPORATION INTERNATIONAL presents a defensible business profile supported by a preneed backlog, scale and a cemetery segment, but credit quality is constrained by derived leverage at the top of its target range, a fixed funeral cost base and payouts above free cash flow. Cash-flow durability appears adequate, because conversion improved to 54.65% of derived EBITDA but with timing help. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on holding derived net leverage near 4.01x (4.02x pro forma for repurchases made after 2026-09-30), on the 2027 notes being met from liquidity, and on market access in 2030. The primary downside path is another year of falling funeral volume with weaker trust returns while payouts continue. Further analysis would require the covenant definition, the proxy statement and any rating or market data. Fundamental credit review completed.
