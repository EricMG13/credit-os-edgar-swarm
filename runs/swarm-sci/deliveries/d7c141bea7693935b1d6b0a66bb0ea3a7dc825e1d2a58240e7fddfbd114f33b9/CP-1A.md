## Findings

### Bottom line
SERVICE CORPORATION INTERNATIONAL is a listed deathcare consolidator with no sponsor or controlling holder named in the supplied filings; its USD 17.72 billion preneed backlog supports creditors, but payouts above 2025 free cash flow and the unsupplied proxy statement cap the governance view.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sci-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 40; 10-K_2025-12-31_sci-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 122; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 31; calculation C09

Funeral volume is the weak point: comparable services fell 2.66% in the first nine months of 2026 and management cut its outlook in October, while cemetery growth and pricing offset it at group level. Dividends and buybacks took 116.30% of free cash flow in 2025 and 78.14% in the first nine months of 2026. The issuer's covenant ratio is 3.69x against a 5.00x maximum and its stated leverage target is 3.5x to 4.0x, but whether covenant limits bind payouts cannot be tested because the baskets are not supplied.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 41–44; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_exhibit991q326earningsrele.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-K_2025-12-31_sci-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 42; calculation C06; calculation C09

### Key findings

- **The business is a scaled, backlog-supported consolidator, not a transaction story.** Funeral operations produced 55.82% of 2025 revenue and cemeteries 44.18%, with 95.03% earned in the United States. About 48.43% of 2025 revenue was recognized from preneed contracts, and the preneed backlog of USD 17.01 billion at the end of 2025 was 3.95x annual revenue; it reached USD 17.72 billion by September 2026, up 4.17%. That gives creditors revenue visibility most consumer-service issuers lack, but USD 9.15 billion of the backlog is insurance-funded, sits off the balance sheet and depends on third-party insurers.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C03; calculation C04; calculation C05; 10-K_2025-12-31_sci-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 49; 10-K_2025-12-31_sci-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 115–116; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 31–32
- **Funeral volume is the pressure point and it hits margin before revenue.** Comparable funeral services fell 2.66% in the first nine months of 2026 while average revenue per service rose 3.17%, and the comparable funeral gross margin dropped to 19.2% from 20.8% because staff, facility and vehicle costs largely do not fall with volume. Management cut its full-year adjusted earnings outlook to USD 3.94 to USD 4.04 per share in October 2026, citing lower funeral volumes than expected and higher healthcare, selling and marketing costs. Cemetery revenue rose 5.5% in the same nine months, and cemetery gross profit growth more than offset the funeral decline at group level.
Basis: calculation C06; calculation C07; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sci-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 43–45; 10-K_2025-12-31_sci-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 33; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_exhibit991q326earningsrele.htm.jsonl, page 4
- **Cemetery earnings lean on trust income and on the timing of property recognition.** Trust fund income was USD 313.3 million in 2025, the trusts hold about 60% in equities, and they returned 15.1% in 2025 and 6.2% in the first nine months of 2026, so a market fall would cut recognized revenue and could force replenishment in states that require it; unrealized losses in those states were USD 0.6 million at the end of 2025 and no top-up is expected. Preneed property sales production grew 7.1% in the nine months against 4.4% for recognized property revenue, which builds backlog but presses near-term cemetery margin.
Basis: calculation C08; 10-K_2025-12-31_sci-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 23–24; 10-K_2025-12-31_sci-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 54; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sci-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 45
- **Capital allocation favours shareholders over deleveraging.** Dividends and buybacks took 116.30% of free cash flow in 2025, buybacks alone costing USD 464.2 million, and carrying debt rose 6.30% over the year. In the first nine months of 2026 payouts were 78.14% of free cash flow, and in June 2026 the board added about USD 472.0 million to the repurchase authorization. The policy is deliberate and, on the issuer's own ratio, inside its limits; but buybacks are discretionary, so creditor protection depends on management slowing them if earnings weaken, since the restricted-payment baskets are not supplied.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest evidence against caution is the covenant ratio of 3.69x against a 5.00x maximum, a preneed backlog that grew 4.17% in nine months, steady pricing, and 2025 operating cash flow of USD 942.8 million against payouts equal to 68.37% of it; buybacks are also discretionary and management can slow them at any time. A proxy statement showing no controlling holder and independent oversight of capital returns, restricted-payment baskets that bind well before the leverage target, and funeral volumes stabilising so that margin recovers would each strengthen the credit view. Further falls in comparable funeral services after the October outlook cut, trust returns turning negative enough to require replenishment, loss of surety capacity, or payouts continuing above free cash flow while leverage moves above the 4.0x target would weaken it.

Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sci-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2025-12-31_sci-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 122
