## Findings

### Bottom line

Valvoline's operating model is resilient, but creditors now hold claims on an acquisitive, more leveraged issuer: nineteen years of same-store sales growth support cash flow, while a debt-funded acquisition, reliance on one lubricant supplier and a continuing control weakness add event and reporting risk.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_vvv-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 37; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 31–34

Nearly half of third-quarter revenue growth came from the acquisition: Breeze Autocare added USD 50.8 million of the USD 105.6 million increase. No sponsor or controlling shareholder is named in the supplied filings and the company pays no dividend and does not expect to, which limits extraction risk, but board and ownership detail is not established.
Basis: calculation M7; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 22; 10-K_2025-09-30_vvv-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 42, 70, 114–115

### Key findings

The business is a durable, low-ticket maintenance service. Oil changes and related fees were 73.60% of nine-month revenue, other maintenance services 21.26% and franchise fees 5.14%, and system-wide same-store sales grew 8.0% in the third quarter of fiscal 2026 after 19 consecutive years of growth through fiscal 2025, so demand follows vehicle maintenance needs rather than discretionary spending. Management characterises the network's record as operational excellence; the figures show growth, not the reason for it.
Basis: calculation M1; 10-K_2025-09-30_vvv-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16, 22

Growth now leans heavily on a debt-funded acquisition. Breeze Autocare closed on December 1, 2025 for USD 637.4 million of cash, funded by a USD 740.0 million Term Loan B, and contributed 7.75% of nine-month revenue; the required sale of 45 acquired stores cost a USD 57.9 million pre-tax loss. The mechanism is higher debt service against earnings that include the acquired stores for only part of the period, which is why creditors should read current leverage together with the Financial foundation.
Basis: calculation M5; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 8–11

Margins are under cost pressure that is not yet labour or materials. Gross margin fell by 1.00 percentage points to 39.46% in the third quarter, and most of the decline sits in other service delivery costs, which rose from 19.68% to 20.62% of revenue while labour and materials ratios were flat; management adds that lubricant costs rose through the quarter and are expected to stay elevated. A pricing lag against a single supplier's costs would hit margin next.
Basis: calculation M3; calculation M4; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 19, 22–23

Supply is concentrated in one counterparty. The stores buy substantially all lubricants from the owner of the former Global Products business under a long-term supply agreement, and the brand is shared with that owner; any interruption, quality failure or dispute would reach every company-operated store and the franchisees' product supply at once.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_vvv-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 19, 37

Governance evidence is thin and its main risk is control reliability. Management reports a material weakness in internal control that began with the 2024 system implementation and persisted at June 30, 2026, the auditor's opinion on internal control was adverse at both prior year-ends, and the acquired business is excluded from the fiscal 2026 assessment, so the reliability of reported figures depends on remediation that management expects during fiscal 2026. The chief financial officer changed in May 2025.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 31–34; 10-K_2025-09-30_vvv-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 66, 110

Capital allocation was shareholder-led before the acquisition and is creditor-friendly after it. Share repurchases and dividends totalled USD 1,850.2 million across fiscal 2023 to 2025, after the Global Products sale brought in USD 2,383 million, while management has since paused repurchases, prepaid USD 50.0 million of the Term Loan A and lowered its leverage target. The shift is documented but untested by a fiscal 2026 year-end.
Basis: calculation M8; 10-K_2024-09-30_vvv-20240930.htm.jsonl, pages 36, 53; 10-K_2025-09-30_vvv-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 57, 70; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 29–30

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is momentum and deleveraging: same-store sales growth of 8.0% in the third quarter, free cash flow of USD 112.3 million in the nine months, a USD 50.0 million prepayment of the Term Loan A, and a franchised half of the network (49.84% of stores) that adds fee income without store capital. The view would improve if the fiscal 2026 year-end shows net leverage inside management's target range, remediation of the control weakness is declared complete, and no further debt-funded acquisition is announced; it would worsen if a gap opens between lubricant costs and pricing or the supply relationship is disrupted.
Basis: calculation M9; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 22, 29, 34

### Limitations

- Directors, compensation and shareholder detail sit in a proxy statement that is not supplied, so the governance view rests on officers and filings and could move to lower risk if the proxy shows independent oversight of the control remediation.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_vvv-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 114–115
- The supply agreement's term, pricing and minimum purchase terms are not supplied, so the supplier concentration cannot be sized; terms that favour the supplier would raise the risk.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_vvv-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 37
- The earnings contribution of Breeze Autocare is not stated, only its revenue and net income, so the acquisition's effect on cash flow and on leverage is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 9
- No customer or franchisee concentration data is supplied, so the durability finding rests on the breadth of a consumer and commercial customer base that the filings describe but do not measure.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_vvv-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 5

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The transaction that defines the credit today is the acquisition of Breeze Autocare, an independent operator of quick lube stores in California, Texas and the Midwest, signed in February 2025 and closed on December 1, 2025 for USD 637.4 million of cash, with the Federal Trade Commission requiring the immediate sale of 45 acquired stores. The seller named in the annual report is Greenbriar; the buyer of the 45 stores named there is Mainstreet Auto, LLC.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_vvv-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 108–109; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 8–11

The acquisition was funded by a new seven-year Term Loan B of USD 740.0 million added to the Senior Credit Agreement in December 2025, with the excess used to repay the revolver. On June 30, 2026 the Term Loan B was repriced to the adjusted term Secured Overnight Financing Rate plus 1.750% from 2.000%, and Valvoline prepaid USD 50.0 million of the Term Loan A in the third quarter. The 2031 notes of USD 535.0 million were not changed.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_vvv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8, 12; 10-Q_2025-12-31_vvv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10

Earlier history shapes the structure: the former Global Products business was sold to Aramco Overseas Company B.V. on March 1, 2023 for net proceeds of USD 2,383 million, leaving a supply agreement, a shared brand agreement and indemnities with the buyer's business and with Ashland.
Basis: 10-K_2024-09-30_vvv-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-K_2025-09-30_vvv-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 19, 37–38
