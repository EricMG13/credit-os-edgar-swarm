## Findings

### Bottom line
Fundamental credit quality is sound but weakening: low leverage and wide liquidity absorb the falling margins of a housing-dependent installer, and the main creditor exposure is financial policy, because shareholder returns and acquisitions have taken almost all free cash flow.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C12; calculation C26; calculation C44

Net debt was USD 637.5 million, 1.37x EBITDA for the 12 months to 2026-09-30 against 1.14x at FY2025, and liquidity was USD 788.0 million; pro forma net debt and net leverage after the USD 250.0 million term loan add-on of 2026-10-27 are not established. Nine-month EBITDA fell 7.78% while revenue grew 1.05%, yet only USD 96.7 million of principal falls due before 2031, so the risk is gradual leverage drift, not a liquidity event.

### Key findings

Business risk is moderate: the company is one of the largest US insulation installers, spread across many customers and several building products, but it remains tied to a single housing cycle. The top ten customers are 14% of FY2025 net revenue and the largest about 4%, insulation is 58% of revenue and the commercial end market 17%, so no customer or product can by itself break cash flow, while single-family housing, the largest end market, sets the volume trend for most of the business.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 20; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C49; calculation C50; calculation C51; calculation C52

Demand is weakening and pricing power is limited, which is the first link in the credit story. In the nine months to 2026-09-30 same-branch installation job volume excluding heavy commercial fell 5.6% while price and mix added only 0.3%, single-family same-branch sales fell 7.7% and single-family starts fell 4.0%, and Fannie Mae forecasts a 3.7% fall in housing starts for 2026. Commercial same-branch sales grew 10.4% and multi-family fell only 1.8%, which limits the decline in installation same-branch sales to 2.9%, but backlog of USD 162.9 million is only 5.48% of FY2025 revenue, so the order book gives little notice of a turn.

The variable cost structure protects direct labor and material cost when volume falls, but it does not protect administrative cost, so the cushion is thinner than the cost description suggests.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a weakening view is the balance sheet: net leverage of 1.37x, liquidity of USD 788.0 million, almost no principal due before 2031 and light capital needs mean a sustained earnings decline would take years to threaten debt service, and commercial same-branch sales growth of 10.4% is cushioning the single-family decline. The opposite risk is that the view is too benign: nine-month free cash flow fell 22.11% while distributions exceeded it, the gross profit percentage fell in both the nine months and the third quarter, and the USD 250.0 million add-on raises floating-rate debt with its use unstated. The view would improve if the gross profit percentage recovered toward 33.6%, if the add-on proceeds were held as cash with repurchases below free cash flow, and if net debt fell in the next filings; it would worsen if LTM EBITDA fell below the USD 419.3 million illustration while distributions continued, if net debt rose without an earnings recovery, or if the revolver were drawn. Pro forma net debt and net leverage after the 2026-10-27 add-on are not established.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strength 1: low net leverage and a wide liquidity buffer. Net debt was 1.37x EBITDA and liquidity USD 788.0 million at 2026-09-30, against USD 96.7 million of principal due from the rest of 2026 to 2030. The mechanism is time: a decline in earnings has to be deep and long before debt service is threatened, and the revolver is undrawn. The implication is that near-term default risk is low even though the trend is negative, subject to the add-on's effect on net debt, which is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C12; calculation C26; calculation C29; calculation C87

Strength 2: a long maturity profile after the January 2026 refinancing. Of principal, 90.90% falls due after 2030 and the 2034 notes are fixed-rate, so refinancing and rate risk on most of the debt is deferred. The implication is that refinancing capacity is tested from 2030, when the revolver can spring forward to 2030-12-27, not in the current trading downturn.

### Company description

Installed Building Products is one of the largest US installers of insulation for new homes. It also installs waterproofing, fire-stopping, garage doors, gutters, blinds, shower doors, closet shelving and mirrors from a national branch network that serves all 48 continental states and the District of Columbia, and it runs regional distribution businesses and cellulose insulation manufacturing, 5% and 2% of FY2025 net revenue. Installation is the only reportable segment and was 92% of third-quarter 2026 net revenue; the company has completed more than 200 acquisitions since 1999.

The model supports cash-flow visibility only in part. Installation is a gating phase, since interior work cannot proceed until insulation passes inspection, and most of the ten largest customers have been served for at least two decades, which supports retention but does not protect volume. The implication for creditors is that debt service rests on the balance sheet and on cost flexibility rather than on contracted revenue.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is cyclical, transactional and driven by volume. In the nine months to 2026-09-30 same-branch installation job volume excluding heavy commercial fell 5.6% and price and mix added 0.3%, so revenue growth of 1.05% came from commercial work and the Other category, partly offset by the residential decline, not from the core single-family job. The implication is that single-family revenue is unlikely to recover before affordability does and that margin, not revenue, absorbs the shortfall.
