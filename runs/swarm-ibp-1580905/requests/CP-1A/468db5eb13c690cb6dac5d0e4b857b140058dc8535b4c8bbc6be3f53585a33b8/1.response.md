## Findings

### Bottom line
Governance is acceptable for creditors but not conservative: the chief executive, who is also chairman, beneficially owns about 14.5% of the stock, and dividends, buybacks and acquisitions have absorbed almost all free cash flow since FY2023.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 62; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 162; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 103–104; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ibp-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; calculation C30

Business risk is moderated by breadth: the top ten customers are only 14% of revenue, insulation is 58% of revenue and commercial work is 17%. The business is cyclical, though, and 2026 volumes and margins are falling. The filings document no sponsor, no creditor-adverse transaction and no liability management, but they omit the proxy statement and the executed debt agreements, so board independence and restricted-payment capacity are not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ibp-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 157

### Key findings

Control is influential but not absolute, and the roles are concentrated. Jeffrey W. Edwards is president, chief executive and chairman and beneficially owned about 14.5% of the stock at the end of 2025, enough to influence elections and major transactions without controlling them, and the certificate lets him pursue corporate opportunities himself. For creditors the mechanism is conflict between one holder's interests and lenders', not a sponsor exit, because the filings name no sponsor, no fund life and no monitoring fee. The chief financial officer also sits on the board, and the number of independent directors is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 62; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 162; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 157

Related-party dealing is small in revenue but carries the main governance flag, private buybacks from a holder linked to the chief executive. In 2025 the company bought shares in three private deals for USD 106.0 million, 61.41% of its FY2025 repurchases by value and 52.94% by share count, each at a 3.0% discount to the prior day's price, and one deal of USD 20.7 million in 2024. The discount favours the company, but the cash goes to an insider and rises with cash capacity. Related-party sales were USD 25.0 million, 0.84% of revenue, and no related-party repurchase appears in the 2026 quarterly reports.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_ibp-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 140; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 142–143; 10-Q_2026-03-31_ibp-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ibp-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26–27; calculation C31; calculation C32; calculation C34; calculation C36

Capital allocation is shareholder-heavy and leaves little room for debt reduction from cash flow. From FY2023 to the nine months to 2026-09-30 free cash flow was USD 1,026.7 million, dividends and repurchases took USD 741.4 million and cash acquisitions USD 262.9 million, together 97.82% of free cash flow. The 2026 position resembles FY2024 more than FY2025: in the nine months to 2026-09-30 dividends, repurchases and acquisitions took 125.06% of free cash flow, against 126.73% in FY2024 and 103.62% in FY2025. Repurchases are the flexible lever, USD 6.3 million in FY2023 against USD 145.3 million in FY2024, and none is shown in the third quarter of 2026, so creditors depend on that restraint continuing.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 103–104; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ibp-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ibp-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; calculation C25; calculation C26; calculation C27; calculation C30; calculation C12; calculation C20; calculation C18; calculation C19

Acquisitions are a repeated, cash-funded programme, small beside free cash flow in most years but not trivial. The company has completed more than two hundred acquisitions since 1999 and aims to acquire at least USD 100.0 million of revenue each year; cash paid was USD 59.6 million, USD 88.6 million and USD 51.5 million in FY2023 to FY2025 and USD 63.2 million in the nine months to 2026-09-30. No acquisition-specific borrowing is documented, but acquired earnings are not stated, and the audit report flags goodwill of the Distribution unit, so integration value is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 85; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 146–147; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ibp-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 99–101; calculation C27; calculation C24

The operating model is diversified by customer and product but tied to one housing cycle. Insulation is 58% of revenue, commercial 17%, the top ten customers 14% and the largest customer about 4%, while installers are paid by completed job and the issuer calls its cost structure highly variable. Single-family, the largest end market, is weakening, with same-branch volume growth of -5.6% in the nine months to 2026-09-30, and the lower-margin Other category grew 33.2%, so gross margin is falling.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ibp-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ibp-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18

Debt-market conduct is creditor-neutral to supportive on the evidence read. The 2028 notes were redeemed in full and replaced with 2034 notes, the revolver was enlarged and extended to 2031, and no waiver, exchange offer, priming or restructuring is described; the added USD 250.0 million of term loans after the quarter end has no stated use, so willingness cannot be separated from capacity, which cannot be tested without the executed agreements.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 150–151; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ibp-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ibp-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 33–34; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 124

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an acceptable view is the 2026 pattern: in the nine months to 2026-09-30 dividends, repurchases and acquisitions of USD 245.0 million exceeded free cash flow of USD 195.9 million while operating cash flow fell, gross margin slipped and USD 250.0 million of term loans was added after the quarter end. If the add-on proceeds or further cash fund buybacks, particularly private ones from the chief executive-linked holder, governance would be better described as shareholder-favouring and the risk level would move to high. A persisting buyback pause while EBITDA is below its FY2025 level, add-on proceeds held as cash and disclosure of covenant headroom would support a lower level. The insider's prepaid forward contracts, which cover 4.26% of shares outstanding, settle in November 2026, May 2027 and June 2027 and could change the holder's exposure.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ibp-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ibp-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 33–34; 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 62; calculation C12; calculation C04; calculation C37

### Limitations

- The proxy statement for the 2026 annual meeting was not supplied, so board independence, committee structure, director holdings and pay are not established; the finding that governance is acceptable therefore rests on audited reporting and the absence of creditor-adverse transactions, not on tested board checks.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 157
- No executed credit agreement or indenture was supplied, so restricted-payment baskets and their usage are not established; the finding that buybacks and dividends are discretionary cannot be tied to a legal limit, and the risk level could move either way once the baskets are read.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ibp-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 124
- Free cash flow in the allocation comparison is calculated from operating cash flow and purchases of property and equipment, and acquired earnings are not stated, so the acquisition finding rests on cash spent rather than value received.

After the third quarter, on 2026-10-27, it added USD 250.0 million of incremental term loans on the existing terms, maturing 2031-03-28, again for general corporate purposes and fees; the effect on net debt is not established because the use of proceeds is not stated.
