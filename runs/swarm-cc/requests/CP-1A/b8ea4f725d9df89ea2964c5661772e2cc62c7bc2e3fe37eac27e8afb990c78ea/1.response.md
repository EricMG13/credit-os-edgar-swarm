## Findings

### Bottom line

Chemours has no controlling shareholder or sponsor, so creditors depend on the company's own financial policy and on legacy-cost sharing with DuPont and Corteva, and governance risk is medium rather than low because of open regulatory inquiries and unquantified PFAS claims.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-K_2025-12-31_cc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 145; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 34–35; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 93–95

Financial policy has favoured debt reduction in 2026: the company said land-sale proceeds would reduce debt and repaid euro term debt of a similar size, share repurchases remain dormant and the dividend rate is 65.00% below 2024. Business risk matters as much as governance risk, because Thermal & Specialized Solutions supplied 72.25% of segment Adjusted EBITDA in the first nine months of 2026 while its third-quarter Adjusted EBITDA fell 30%.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-K_2025-12-31_cc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 162; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 133; EX-99.1_2026-11-02_cc-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 3; calculation C7; calculation C4; calculation C10

### Key findings

- Ownership gives creditors neither a sponsor to fear nor a sponsor to rely on. The filings name no controlling holder, the table of large holders sits in a proxy statement that was not supplied, and no equity injection or cure right is described, so support in stress would have to come from the public market.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-K_2025-12-31_cc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 145; Source readiness (CP-0)

- Earnings depend on refrigerants and on one titanium dioxide price cycle. Thermal & Specialized Solutions produced 72.25% of nine-month segment Adjusted EBITDA (USD 539 million of USD 746 million), Titanium Technologies 20.38% and Advanced Performance Materials 7.37%. In the third quarter refrigerant earnings fell 30% to USD 136 million as the aftermarket fill faded, while titanium dioxide earnings rose to USD 85 million from USD 25 million on price.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 133; EX-99.1_2026-11-02_cc-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4; calculation C4; calculation C14

- Customer concentration is a Titanium Technologies issue rather than a group issue. No customer exceeds 10% of group net sales, but one titanium dioxide customer takes more than 20% of that segment's sales, which is more than 8.36% of group net sales, and the ten largest take more than 44%, so a loss or repricing at one account would move segment earnings.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-12-31_cc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 20; calculation C5

- Legacy PFAS and environmental liabilities are the main source of event risk and are only partly quantified. Accrued litigation rose to USD 553 million from USD 484 million at year-end 2025; New Jersey will draw USD 875 million over 25 years and North Carolina USD 455 million over 15 years across all settling parties; the 2026 Consent Decree adds a USD 23 million penalty, USD 90 million of project funding and USD 136 million of environmental reserves. The company states that losses on the large water, personal-injury and state natural-resource matters cannot be estimated, so accruals may understate the cash that will be needed.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 91; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 111–112; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 116; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 47–48; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 92; calculation C12

- Cost sharing with DuPont and Corteva is a real offset, but it is capped, mostly used or committed, and depends on two counterparties whose finances were not reviewed. Chemours bears half of qualified spend up to USD 4,000 million; USD 1,130 million remained after insurance recoveries and settlement present values, so 71.75% of the cap is used or committed, and the same parties funded USD 150 million into escrow for New Jersey payments in return for Chemours' insurance rights on the first USD 300 million of recoveries. After the cap or 2040, Chemours' indemnities continue unchanged.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 34–39; calculation C16

Internal control was effective at year-end 2025 after the 2023 weaknesses were remediated in 2024, but the amended annual report filed on November 3, 2026 was not supplied.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for lower risk is that 2026 behaviour has favoured creditors and that two large counterparties share the legacy burden. Chemours has no sponsor to extract cash, bought no shares in 2024, 2025 or the first nine months of 2026, cut the annualised dividend rate by 65.00% from 2024 and repaid euro term debt of a size close to its asset-sale proceeds, while DuPont and Corteva funded USD 150 million into escrow and carry half of qualified PFAS costs; internal control over financial reporting was effective at year-end 2025. The view would move towards low if the proxy statement shows an independent board, the amended annual report shows no new deficiency, the securities claims are dismissed and the AFFF personal-injury claims settle within the remaining USD 1,130 million of cost-sharing capacity. It would move towards high if the credit agreement shows wide dividend or investment capacity that is then used, if a regulator or court action follows the 2024 review, if PFAS losses exceed the cost-sharing cap, or if the cost-sharing parties cannot meet their half.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 162; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 34–39; 10-K_2025-12-31_cc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 155; calculation C7

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Chemours has no acquisition, sponsor buyout or recapitalisation in fiscal 2025 or 2026; the transactions that matter to creditors are a refinancing, asset sales and settlements. On March 12, 2026 it issued USD 700 million of senior notes due 2034, guaranteed by a guarantor that the filings do not name, and used the proceeds with cash to redeem all USD 495 million of the 2027 notes and USD 188 million of the 2028 notes. That extended maturities but added USD 17.71 million a year of coupon on the replaced debt.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 187; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 24; calculation C8

Asset sales then reduced floating-rate secured debt. Sales of Kuan Yin land parcels brought USD 356 million of consideration against about USD 360 million agreed, with nine parcels sold in April 2026 and the last in September 2026, and the company repaid EUR 280 million, about USD 324 million, of its euro term loan in nine months, leaving EUR 135 million. Total debt principal fell by USD 310 million to USD 3,872 million between year-end 2025 and September 30, 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 24; calculation C9; calculation C10; calculation C17

Lender-facing terms changed in 2025. The May amendment raised the leverage test through the quarter ended September 30, 2026 and extended USD 780 million of revolving commitments to May 2030, and the October amendment moved the dollar term loan maturity to October 2032 at a margin 50 bp higher. USD 220 million of revolving commitments matured on October 7, 2026, after the quarter end. The New Jersey and North Carolina settlements and the 2026 Consent Decree are legal claims on cash rather than financing choices, and their amounts are set out under key findings.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25–26; calculation C11
