## Findings

### Bottom line

Comstock is a gas-only Haynesville producer whose spending runs well ahead of operating cash flow, so its credit profile is average at best and rests on a pending asset sale; creditors should size leverage at the normalised 3.49x, not the reported 2.37x.

Net debt rose from USD 2,785.14 million at 2025-12-31 to USD 3,338.34 million at 2026-09-30, and the pro forma figure after the sale agreed on 2026-11-04 is not established. The sale price of USD 1.65 billion and a bank maturity now in 2028 limit near-term refinancing risk, but nothing yet shows spending falling to the level of cash flow, which is why the profile stays average.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation C15

### Key findings

Internal cash generation does not fund the investment plan, and that is the central weakness. Capital expenditures were 1.97x operating cash flow in the twelve months to 2026-09-30 and 1.50x in FY2025, and the 2026 plan of USD 1.4 billion to USD 1.5 billion for development and exploration is 1.65x to 1.77x that twelve-month operating cash flow, before USD 100 million to USD 150 million planned for the midstream partnership. Bank borrowings, asset sales and partner capital have met the gap, so debt rises whenever prices do not.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 63; calculation C11

Reported leverage flatters credit quality. Net debt to twelve-month EBITDA is 2.37x as reported but 3.49x after removing sale gains and unrealised hedge gains, which sits just below the bank facility's leverage covenant of 3.5x; the covenant definition is not supplied, so no headroom is inferred. Net debt rose from USD 2,785.14 million at 2025-12-31 to USD 3,338.34 million at 2026-09-30, and pro forma net debt after the sale agreed on 2026-11-04 is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 19; calculation C15

Interest cover is adequate and rate exposure is small. EBITDA covers cash interest 6.71x as reported and 4.55x on the normalised basis, and only the USD 800.0 million of bank borrowings carries a floating rate, 23.61% of debt principal, while the 2029 and 2030 senior notes carry fixed coupons.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 32; calculation C5

Liquidity and refinancing risk are manageable until 2028 but rest on one bank facility. At 2026-09-30 cash was USD 18.63 million and undrawn commitments were USD 700.0 million of USD 1,500.0 million, and pro forma figures after the sale of 2026-11-04 are not established. The amendment of that date moved the bank maturity from 2027-11-15 to 2028-11-15, and no other debt falls due before 2029, when USD 1,623.88 million of notes mature, followed by USD 965.0 million in 2030. The issuer's own fair values put the 2029 notes at 99.5% of face and the 2030 notes at 95%.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 32; calculation C15

Business risk is concentrated in one commodity and one basin, so margins follow the gas price. A 60.10% rise in the realised gas price between FY2024 and FY2025 lifted normalised EBITDA by 21.97% even though output fell 14.67%, and normalised net leverage moved from 3.53x to 2.73x. Cash costs of USD 0.83 per Mcfe are 26.95% of the nine-month 2026 realised price, which leaves room at current prices, but a 10% lower price on twelve-month sales would cut normalised EBITDA by 14.59% and lift normalised net leverage to 4.09x before hedge gains and any cost response.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 58; EX99.1_2026-09-30_crk_crk-ex99-1-htm.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C3

Governance risk is medium and does not change the credit view by itself. The majority stockholder has supplied equity and paid no dividend since 2023, but the August 2026 farmout with a stockholder partnership and a minority investor in the midstream subsidiary route part of future cash flow to outside holders, and the terms of both are not supplied.

The USD 1.65 billion price is 2.06x the USD 800.0 million drawn on the bank facility and equals 49.43% of net debt at 2026-09-30, and the issuer plans to repay the bank facility and retire part of the 2029 notes, but closing is expected only by the end of 2026 and no source states net debt or the earnings of the interests sold. Until it closes, creditors should rely on the normalised 3.49x.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a better credit is asset quality and flexibility. Production costs were 36.87% of the realised price even in FY2024 when the price fell to USD 1.98 per Mcf, the issuer describes most capital spending as discretionary and cut it by 22.99% in FY2024, swaps and collars for 2027 equal about 41.88% of annualised 2026 volume with floors near USD 3.50 per MMBtu, and outside buyers have priced the assets: SOCAR agreed USD 1.65 billion and a fund paid USD 600 million for 27% of the midstream subsidiary. Against that, the cut in FY2024 was followed by a 14.67% fall in FY2025 output, and proved undeveloped reserves rose about fourfold in 2025 largely because the higher price in 2025 allowed locations to be booked, so asset coverage moves with the same gas price.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 58; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 28; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation C2; calculation C4; calculation C9

The view would improve if the sale closes, its proceeds repay the bank facility and part of the 2029 notes, and capital spending is held near operating cash flow, which would move normalised leverage back toward the 2.73x of FY2025. It would worsen if the gas price falls back toward the FY2024 level with spending unchanged, if the sale fails or its proceeds are redirected, which would leave the bank maturity in 2028 and the 2029 notes 106 days apart, or if the covenant definition proves tighter than the normalised measure used here.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Unit costs are low against the realised price, which keeps margins positive in weak price years. Cash costs were USD 0.83 per Mcfe in the nine months to 2026-09-30, 26.95% of the realised price, and the issuer states an operating margin of 73%; in FY2024 production costs of USD 0.73 per Mcfe were 36.87% of a USD 1.98 price. Margin therefore moves with price rather than cost, which supports cash generation through a price trough.
Basis: EX99.1_2026-09-30_crk_crk-ex99-1-htm.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 58; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 59; calculation C2

Capital spending is largely discretionary, which gives management a lever to protect liquidity. The issuer states that the timing of most future capital expenditure is discretionary because it has few long-term commitments, remaining drilling rig commitments are USD 12.7 million, and capital expenditures were cut 22.99% in FY2024. The lever protects liquidity before it protects earnings, because output was 14.67% lower in FY2025.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 28; calculation C1; calculation C4

The reserve base and operatorship give a long runway and control over timing. About 41% of proved reserves are developed, the reserve life is about 16 years and the issuer operates 99% of proved reserves, so it sets drilling pace and cost; the proved undeveloped volume is price-sensitive, however, because it rose about fourfold in 2025 on the higher gas price used to book it.

### Company description and ownership

Comstock produces natural gas almost entirely from the Haynesville and Bossier shales of North Louisiana and East Texas and operates 99% of its proved reserves, so it controls drilling pace and cost. Revenue is gas sales plus a gas services line that resells purchased gas and earns almost nothing, at 22.53% of FY2025 revenue and a margin of −3.20% of that revenue. The business therefore carries one commodity, one basin and one regulatory setting, which concentrates cash-flow risk in the gas price.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 59; calculation C8

A majority stockholder, not named in the statements, held 71% when last stated in 2024, and no dividend has been paid since 2023. The midstream subsidiary is consolidated but a fund holds a 27% interest, and the August 2026 farmout puts a stockholder partnership beside Comstock in 27 wells. These structures move part of the cash flow to outside holders and reduce the weight creditors can place on the consolidated figures.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Revenue, costs and cash conversion

Revenue is set by price and only partly predictable. Output was 14.67% lower in FY2025 and 1.68% lower in the nine months to 2026-09-30, with the third quarter of 2026 9.47% higher than the third quarter of 2025, while the realised price moved from USD 1.98 per Mcf in FY2024 to USD 3.17 in FY2025 and USD 3.08 in the nine months of 2026 against USD 3.13 a year earlier. Between 70% and 75% of gas is sold at the first-of-month index and the rest at daily prices, three customers took 39% of 2025 sales and the issuer states that other purchasers could replace them, and there is no long-dated fixed-price sales book beyond swaps and collars. Revenue is cyclical, price-set and moderately concentrated, not contracted.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 58; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 22; calculation C7

Margins are defended by unit costs but not by contracts. Cash costs of USD 0.83 per Mcfe leave a wide gap to the realised price, and the issuer's operating margin of 73% reflects it, but gathering and transportation commitments of USD 85.4 million in 2026 and firm transportation of about 1.7 Bcf per day do not fall with output. Cutting drilling reduces capital spending rather than operating cost, so volume pressure would raise unit costs before it reduced them.
Basis: EX99.1_2026-09-30_crk_crk-ex99-1-htm.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 101

Capital intensity is high and free cash flow is negative. Capital expenditures were 1.50x operating cash flow in FY2025 and 1.97x in the twelve months to 2026-09-30, no dividend is paid, and the tax provision in the nine months to 2026-09-30 was almost entirely deferred, which keeps cash taxes close to zero; federal tax losses of USD 1.5 billion, of which USD 740.6 million are expected to expire unused, shelter taxable income. EBITDA therefore does not turn into free cash flow at present: interest is paid from operating cash flow, but the capital programme beyond it is funded from borrowings and partner capital rather than from retained cash.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 30; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4; calculation C11

### Market forces and framework view

Competitive rivalry limits pricing power: the issuer competes for properties and leases with larger companies and sells at index prices, so it is a price taker and its margin depends on its cost position. Barriers to entry are real but are about acreage and capital: years of leasing built the Western Haynesville position, and a new entrant would have to buy it. Substitutes matter on the demand side: the issuer names competing fuels as a market factor and cites LNG exports, exports to Mexico, petrochemicals and power generation as demand support, none of which is contracted in the supplied documents. Buyers are few at the top but replaceable at an index price, and suppliers hold some power through rig and midstream commitments that run for years, which reduces cost flexibility if volumes fall.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 101; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 20

PEST factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver based on the provided materials.

Opportunities that could improve credit quality are the SOCAR proceeds if applied to debt, continued well results in the Western Haynesville area, which the issuer reports as successful in its 30 wells to date, and growth in Gulf Coast gas demand. Threats that could weaken it are a gas price near the FY2024 level, floating-rate exposure on the bank debt, claims of outside holders on subsidiary and well cash flow, and the 2028 bank maturity if the sale does not close.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 30

### Financial profile and credit quality

The supports are a low unit cost position, discretionary capital spending, long-dated mostly fixed-rate debt and a long reserve life. The constraints are negative free cash flow, normalised net leverage of 3.49x against a 3.5x covenant whose definition is not supplied, one bank facility behind liquidity and outside claims on cash. The trend is mixed: normalised net leverage fell 0.80x between FY2024 and FY2025 and has since risen 0.76x, so the 2025 improvement has reversed, while reported leverage of 2.37x hides that. The missing datapoints are covenant definitions, figures for the group that borrows under the bank facility, maintenance spending, ratings and pro forma figures after the sale of 2026-11-04.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 19; calculation C10

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months the plan is to grow output with capital spending of USD 135 million to USD 155 million still to come in the fourth quarter of 2026, to close the SOCAR sale by the end of 2026 and to use the proceeds on debt. Output was 9.47% higher in the third quarter than a year earlier and the issuer expects production to increase in 2026. The bank maturity of 2028-11-15 is 728 days from the as-of date unless the extension to 2031 takes effect on closing and repayment, and the 2029 notes mature 834 days from the as-of date, so the refinancing question arises inside the horizon only if the sale does not close.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 58; EX99.1_2026-09-30_crk_crk-ex99-1-htm.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C7; calculation C13

Over three to five years the maturity wall of the bank facility, the 2029 notes and the 2030 notes is the main test, supported by a reserve life of about 16 years and the issuer's plan to fund activity mainly from operating cash flow. Tailwinds are Gulf Coast demand from LNG exports, exports to Mexico and petrochemicals and the data-centre power partnership, which the issuer describes in its own words without terms. Headwinds are price volatility, a minority holder's minimum return in the midstream subsidiary, floating-rate exposure on the bank debt and spending above cash flow.

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a gas price that falls back toward the USD 1.98 per Mcf of FY2024 while the sale does not close and the 2026 plan continues. The first operational break is operating cash flow, which already covered only 47.88% of capital expenditures in the nine months to 2026-09-30. A 10% lower price on twelve-month sales of natural gas and oil removes about USD 139.37 million, cutting normalised EBITDA by 14.59% and lifting normalised net leverage from 3.49x to 4.09x, above the 3.5x covenant level if its definition resembled the normalised measure, which is not known. Undrawn commitments of USD 700.0 million would cover about 10.26 months of the twelve-month funding gap of USD 818.81 million if no partner capital or sale proceeds arrived, and the 2028 bank maturity would then fall 106 days before the 2029 notes. Management would cut drilling, as in FY2024, which protects liquidity at the cost of output. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid a liquidity crisis only if the sale closes or spending is cut toward cash flow; no default is assumed. The observable triggers are undrawn commitments, quarterly capital expenditures against operating cash flow and the closing of the sale.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 58; calculation C3; calculation C12; calculation C13; calculation C15

### Overall credit view

Overall, Comstock presents an average business profile supported by low unit costs, discretionary capital spending and long-dated debt, but credit quality is constrained by spending that runs ahead of operating cash flow and normalised leverage near the covenant level. Cash-flow durability appears weak at present because free cash flow is negative and follows the gas price. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the closing of the SOCAR sale and the use of its proceeds, with the notes valued at 95% to 99.5% of face on the issuer's own fair values. The primary downside path is a lower gas price with spending unchanged and the sale not closed. Further analysis would require the covenant definitions, figures for the bank borrowing group and the sale's closing statement. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: spending above operating cash flow while the asset sale is unclosed.
