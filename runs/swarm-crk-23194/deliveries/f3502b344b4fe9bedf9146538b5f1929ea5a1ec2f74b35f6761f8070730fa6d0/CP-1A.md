## Findings

### Bottom line

Governance risk to creditors is medium: a majority stockholder controls Comstock and has put capital in rather than taken it out since 2024, but the 2026 plan leans on partner capital and a pending asset sale whose terms are only partly disclosed.

Comstock is a single-basin natural gas producer, so the creditor case rests on gas prices and on how much cash stays inside the borrowing group. Outside capital now stands between creditors and part of that cash in two places: the midstream subsidiary, where a fund holds a 27% interest with a minimum return, and the wells covered by the August 2026 farmout with a stockholder partnership. The USD 1.65 billion sale to SOCAR, signed on 2026-11-04, is planned to repay the bank facility but has not closed.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 101; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21

### Key findings

Control is concentrated and its current size is not established, which keeps governance risk from being rated low. The majority stockholder held 71% after market purchases in the third quarter of 2024, the latest figure stated, and the 2026 proxy statement that would update it, confirm formal board independence and describe how related-party deals are approved is not supplied. Three of the five directors are listed as outside directors, but creditors cannot check whether the farmout described below was reviewed by directors without an interest.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 101; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 74–75; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 39–40

Owner behaviour since 2024 has supported creditors, with one open test. Entities controlled by the majority stockholder bought newly issued shares for USD 100.5 million in March 2024, no common dividend has been paid since 2023 and the bank facility restricts dividends and share repurchases. The test is the August 2026 farmout: a partnership owned by the stockholder pays 85% of the cost of 18 Western Haynesville wells and 80% of the cost of nine Legacy Haynesville wells, expected to cost about USD 450 million, and takes the net proceeds after Comstock recovers its costs until it earns a 15% return. That eases borrowing needs now and sends later cash flow from those wells to a related party, on terms not tested against an independent price in the documents supplied; the receivable from the stockholder partnerships has already risen from USD 3.6 million at 2025-12-31 to USD 51.5 million at 2026-09-30.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 101; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 56; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation C9

A second layer of outside capital sits inside the midstream subsidiary and takes a share of its distributable cash, so not all of that cash is available to Comstock's creditors. In June 2026 a fund managed by Sixth Street Partners paid USD 600 million for a 27% interest in Pinnacle Gas Services, with a minimum annualised return, exit provisions and a step down to 19.5% after return hurdles; the cash redeemed the earlier partner for USD 445 million. Comstock keeps control through a management agreement and a majority of the board and consolidates the subsidiary, but USD 471.5 million of net property and USD 16.4 million of cash cannot be used for general corporate purposes, and the bank facility's collateral excludes the subsidiary.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 19

The business is a single-commodity, single-basin gas producer with moderate customer concentration, so governance quality cannot offset price risk. Substantially all proved reserves are in the Haynesville and Bossier shales and 41% of them are developed, leaving an undeveloped balance that needs capital; three customers took 39% of 2025 sales; 70% to 75% of gas is sold on the first-of-month index and the rest at daily spot prices. The gas services line resells purchased gas and earns almost no margin: a margin of −3.20% of its revenue in FY2025 and 3.11% in the nine months to 2026-09-30.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation C3; calculation C4

Spending runs ahead of operating cash flow, so the plan depends on partner capital, asset sales and bank borrowings rather than internal cash alone. Operating cash flow covered 47.88% of capital expenditures in the nine months to 2026-09-30, against 68.65% a year earlier; in the same nine months the issuer borrowed USD 1,047 million on its bank facility and repaid USD 507 million, and took USD 600 million of minority capital into Pinnacle Gas Services, of which USD 445 million repaid the earlier partner.
Basis: calculation C5; calculation C8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 5

The SOCAR sale and the bank amendment of 2026-11-04 could change the credit picture materially, but no supplied document measures the effect. The agreed price of USD 1.65 billion is subject to adjustments, has an effective date of 2026-07-01 and is expected to close by the end of 2026 subject to approvals; the issuer plans to use it to repay the bank facility, on which USD 800.0 million was drawn at 2026-09-30, and to retire part of the 2029 senior notes. The price is 2.06x the bank borrowings and would leave USD 850 million for the notes, which have principal of USD 1,623.88 million. The bank maturity moves from 2027-11-15 to 2028-11-15, and to 2031-11-15 after closing and debt repayment. A pro forma figure is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 30; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 100; calculation C7

Reporting quality supports monitoring but does not cover governance. The auditor gave unqualified opinions on the 2025 statements and on internal control over financial reporting and the issuer reports every quarter with a results release, but the chairman and chief executive roles are combined, and formal director independence, executive pay and related-party approvals sit in a document not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 72; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 79; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 37; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 70; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 75

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for lower risk is conduct. The majority stockholder added USD 100.5 million of equity in 2024 and bought shares in the market, the company has paid no dividend since 2023 and its bank facility restricts distributions. Third-party buyers have also paid for minority interests: a fund managed by Sixth Street Partners paid USD 600 million for 27% of the midstream subsidiary and SOCAR agreed to pay USD 1.65 billion for minority interests in upstream assets and Pinnacle Gas Services. If the sale closes and its proceeds repay the USD 800.0 million drawn on the bank facility and part of the 2029 notes, the debt reduction would offset the cash flow ceded to outside holders and the rating could move toward low, although the farmout and the minority capital in the subsidiary would remain.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 101; 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 56; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 30

The view would worsen if a dividend resumed while the bank facility is drawn, if more wells were farmed out to stockholder partnerships, if the proxy statement showed that directors without an interest did not approve the farmout, if the sale failed to close or its proceeds went elsewhere, or if the unseen Pinnacle terms let the minority investor force an exit or take cash ahead of the parent. It would improve on evidence of independent approval of related-party agreements, Pinnacle terms that leave Comstock's share of cash unconditional, and a closing that is followed by repayment of the bank facility.

### Limitations

- The majority stockholder is not named in the statements and its latest stake is from 2024; if the current holding is lower, or the person named in the exhibit index and the release is not the stockholder, the control assessment and the related-party reading of the farmout would change.

- The farmout, Pinnacle Gas Services, indenture and SOCAR terms are described only in summaries, so the reading that the minority investor's return reduces cash available to creditors, and the reading that the farmout supports creditors, could each reverse once the agreements are seen.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Three transactions define the period. On 2026-11-04 Comstock agreed to sell SOCAR non-operated working interests in 15% of its Western Haynesville upstream interest, 20% of its other upstream interests and 15% of its interest in Pinnacle Gas Services for USD 1.65 billion, subject to customary adjustments, with an effective date of 2026-07-01 and closing expected by the end of 2026. SOCAR's Western Haynesville interest falls to 7.5% after five years and a 15% return, and the issuer expects a gain of USD 150 million to USD 250 million on closing. The issuer plans to repay the bank facility and retire part of its 2029 senior notes with the proceeds.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 30

On 2026-06-15 Pinnacle Gas Services issued Class A-2 Units equal to a 27% interest to a fund managed by Sixth Street Partners for USD 600 million and used the cash to redeem the earlier partner's interest for USD 445 million; no gain or loss was recognised because Comstock kept control. On 2026-08-31 Comstock agreed a farmout with a partnership owned by its majority stockholder, under which the partnership funds 85% of 18 Western Haynesville wells and 80% of nine Legacy Haynesville wells expected to cost about USD 450 million, and Comstock recovers its costs from the wells' net revenues before the partnership takes the net proceeds until it earns a 15% return, after which 50% of the interest in the subject wells reverts to Comstock.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21

Comstock has issued no notes since the USD 400.0 million of 2029 senior notes sold in April 2024. In the nine months to 2026-09-30 it borrowed USD 1,047 million on its bank facility and repaid USD 507 million, leaving USD 800.0 million drawn. The amendment of 2026-11-04 extends the bank maturity to 2028-11-15, with a further extension to 2031-11-15 after the SOCAR closing and debt repayment, and commitments stay at USD 1.5 billion. The subsidiary's own facility of USD 150 million was undrawn at 2026-09-30.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 100; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation C8
