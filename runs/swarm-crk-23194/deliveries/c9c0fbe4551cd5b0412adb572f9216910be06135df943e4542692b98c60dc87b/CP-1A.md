## Findings

### Bottom line

Comstock’s governance and transaction profile is mixed, so creditors should treat prospective deleveraging as execution-dependent and related-party capital allocation as a continuing monitoring risk.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 101; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21

The last disclosed beneficial ownership for the majority stockholder was 71% in Q3 2024; the current holder identity and percentage are not updated in the supplied filings. The pending USD 1.65 billion SOCAR sale may support debt repayment and a later maturity extension, but neither proceeds nor the extension condition are yet realized. A separate farmout with a partnership owned by the majority stockholder funds development while granting that partnership a return hurdle and contingent well interests.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 101; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21

### Key findings

- Comstock is a concentrated natural-gas producer whose production and reserves are principally in the Haynesville and Bossier shales. This ties cash generation to gas prices, drilling execution and access to gathering and transport capacity; the company’s marketing mix remains substantially index-linked.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 21

- Majority-stockholder influence is material, but the available evidence does not establish current personal or vehicle identity, current ownership percentage, or issuer-level board and consent rights. The last stated 71% beneficial ownership followed open-market purchases in 2024-Q3.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 101

- Related-party capital has both supportive and adverse mechanics. A stock placement delivered USD 100.5 million to Comstock in 2024, and the common stock has paid no dividend since 2023; the 2026 farmout shifts near-term well costs to a partnership owned by the majority stockholder but gives it proceeds and contingent well interests after its stated return hurdle.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 56, 101; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21

- PGS supports gathering and treating in the Western Haynesville while remaining a separately financed entity with a noncontrolling investor. Comstock appoints most of its directors and consolidates it, but some PGS assets are unavailable for general corporate use and Starville holds special distribution and return provisions.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6

- The SOCAR transaction and associated maturity extension could change the debt trajectory, but both depend on a closing and debt repayment. The issuer’s quarterly filing and results release describe the assets sold differently, so the transaction’s precise upstream footprint remains unresolved.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21; EX99.1_2026-09-30_crk_crk-ex99-1-htm.htm.jsonl, page 3

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a creditor-adverse governance interpretation is that the majority stockholder participated in an equity placement, common dividends have not been paid since 2023, and the SOCAR proceeds are intended to retire debt rather than fund a shareholder distribution. PGS also obtained new third-party capital to fund a redemption of its prior noncontrolling interest. These actions support funding capacity and do not establish an extraction pattern. Confirmation of the SOCAR closing, actual debt retirement, current beneficial ownership and control rights, executed farmout and PGS agreements, and lender-permitted related-party capacity would determine whether those counterweights outweigh the related-party cash-flow priorities.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 56, 101; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 21, 30

### Limitations

- Current ownership and issuer-level governance cannot be determined from the last disclosed 71% beneficial ownership in 2024-Q3 and the filings’ role-only references to a majority stockholder. This limits the present control and alignment conclusion to the disclosed transactions and prevents a current ownership assessment.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, page 101; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21

- Executed partnership, PGS, SOCAR and lender documents and the legal-capacity analysis are not supplied. This prevents a conclusion on transfer, affiliate-transaction, lien, priming, consent or restricted-group capacity and limits the assessment of the farmout’s creditor effect to the issuer’s description.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 19–21; EX99.1_2026-09-30_crk_crk-ex99-1-htm.htm.jsonl, page 3

- The Q3 filing describes SOCAR acquiring 20% of the company’s interest in all other upstream assets, while the results release describes 20% of its Legacy Haynesville interest. This leaves the footprint and volume effect of the sale unresolved.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21; EX99.1_2026-09-30_crk_crk-ex99-1-htm.htm.jsonl, page 3

- The report does not direct the sequencing of other analyses; its conclusions are limited to Comstock and the evidence listed here.

### Governance view

Overall, the governance risk level is Insufficient Information. The last disclosed majority-stockholder ownership was 71% in Q3 2024, but current holder identity, ownership and issuer-level control rights are not established. A related-party equity placement, the absence of common dividends since 2023, and external PGS capital counter extraction concerns. The farmout provides development funding but gives a majority-stockholder-owned partnership a 15% return hurdle and contingent interests in the wells. The SOCAR sale and longer facility maturity depend on a future closing and debt repayment. The filings disclose these transactions sufficiently to identify their cash-flow mechanics, but do not include the executed agreements or legal-capacity evidence needed to assign a decision-useful overall governance level.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 56, 101; 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6, 21, 29–30

Governance assessment is complete with limitations. Risk level: Insufficient Information. Key gaps are current ownership and issuer control rights, legal capacity, and the exact SOCAR asset perimeter.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

On November 4, 2026, Comstock agreed to sell non-operated working interests to SOCAR for aggregate cash consideration of USD 1.65 billion, subject to customary purchase-price adjustments. The latest quarterly filing describes the sale as covering 15% of Comstock’s interest in Western Haynesville assets, 15% in PGS and 20% in all other upstream assets; the release instead describes the 20% interest as Legacy Haynesville. The sale is effective July 1, 2026, is expected to close by year-end subject to customary conditions and any required government approvals, and is expected to produce a USD 150 million to USD 250 million gain. SOCAR’s Western Haynesville interest is scheduled to reduce to 7.5% after five years and after a 15% return on investment. The sale is not reported as completed.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21; EX99.1_2026-09-30_crk_crk-ex99-1-htm.htm.jsonl, page 3

The same-day bank-facility amendment extends the upstream facility maturity to November 15, 2028; a further extension to November 15, 2031 depends on SOCAR closing and repayment of debt from the proceeds. No new debt issuance for this sale is disclosed. The extension and expected debt retirement are therefore conditional, and the supplied filings do not state a pro forma debt balance after closing.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 21, 29–30; EX99.1_2026-09-30_crk_crk-ex99-1-htm.htm.jsonl, page 3

The August farmout is a related-party development-funding arrangement, not a new debt issue in the supplied description. A partnership owned by Comstock’s majority stockholder funds most drilling and completion costs for specified wells, is entitled to a 15% return hurdle and receives 50% of the well interest after that hurdle; Comstock is to recover its stated development and lease-operating costs first. The transaction provides funding while diverting defined well cash flows and interests, and the executed agreement is not supplied.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 21
