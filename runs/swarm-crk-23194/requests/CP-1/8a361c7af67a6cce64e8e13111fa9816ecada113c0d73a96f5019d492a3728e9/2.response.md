## Findings

### Bottom line

Comstock's latest balance sheet shows 2.68x carrying debt to house-calculated LTM EBITDA, while cash capital spending left LTM free cash flow materially negative; creditors therefore remain exposed to continued drilling funding needs and the 2028–2030 debt wall. The announced SOCAR sale could change that profile, but it had not closed by the cutoff.

Latest reported liquidity is USD 868.6 million, including USD 150 million restricted to PGS midstream activity. The sale agreement states a USD 1.65 billion cash price but gives no pro forma debt or net leverage; none is inferred here.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 19–21 and 27–30; calculation C2; calculation C4; calculation C7

Capital spending remains the binding cash-flow pressure: nine-month cash capital expenditures substantially exceeded operating cash flow, leaving cash FCF negative before financing. Financing included a large PGS noncontrolling-interest contribution and a redemption; neither is recurring operating cash flow or issuer debt repayment.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5 and 27; calculation C4

The latest debt measure is balance-sheet carrying value, not principal. At 2026-09-30, contractual principal exceeds carrying debt because note discounts and bank debt issuance costs reduce the reported balance. This keeps the debt denominator consistent with the statements and avoids overstating it by using face value.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, page 19; calculation C3; calculation C5

Comstock's USD 1.5 billion facility had USD 800 million drawn at 2026-09-30 and USD 700 million of unused commitment; PGS had no drawing on its USD 150 million facility, whose availability is restricted to midstream activity. The combined unused amount and reported cash reconcile to reported liquidity of USD 868.6 million. Neither the parent availability nor the PGS availability is a substitute for actual cash, and the PGS portion is not freely fungible for upstream debt service.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20 and 27–29; calculation C7

The SOCAR agreement is the strongest potential deleveraging counterweight: it provides for USD 1.65 billion of cash consideration for specified upstream and PGS interests, effective 2026-07-01, with closing expected by year-end subject to customary conditions. Management says it plans to repay the bank facility and retire part of the 2029 notes. No sale proceeds had been received and no repayment split or pro forma debt was stated at the cutoff, so the reported LTM leverage above remains pre-transaction.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 21 and 30; EX99.1_2026-09-30_crk_crk-ex99-1-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1

The strongest contrary evidence is the Q3 production increase and management's disclosed USD 868.6 million liquidity, alongside the pending asset sale. Those factors could ease refinancing pressure if sustained production funds a larger share of drilling and SOCAR closes on the stated terms. The concern remains because cash capital spending exceeded operating cash flow through 9M2026 and the sale's proceeds have not been translated into a completed debt reduction.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_crk.htm.jsonl, pages 27–30; EX99.1_2026-09-30_crk_crk-ex99-1-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation C4
