## Findings

### Bottom line

Olin is a liquid but deteriorating credit: leverage of 7.76x on reported EBITDA, or 5.2x on the issuer's adjusted measure, and interest cover of 2.12x are held up by USD 1,244.4 million of liquidity and no maturity before November 2027.

That time is the creditor's main protection, and three events in the next twelve months decide whether it is enough: covenant relief ends by 30 September 2027, the receivables financing of USD 465.0 million falls due in November 2027, and the Huntsman merger would replace the bank facility without stating how the notes are treated. Management guides fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA to USD 80 million to USD 100 million, which would put adjusted leverage between 4.92x and 5.10x if net debt is unchanged.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against a cautious view is that earnings are near a trough set partly by events that may not recur. The outage cost USD 72.2 million of nine-month results and is not added back even in the issuer's 5.2x, treating it as non-recurring gives 4.60x, fourth-quarter guidance points to between 4.92x and 5.10x, Fitch has Olin on positive watch, no debt matures before November 2027 and the Winchester backlog stands at USD 1,331 million. The view would turn more positive if the merger documents show combined leverage below Olin's own and unchanged ranking for the notes, or if fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA exceeds USD 100 million; it would turn more negative if fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA falls below USD 80 million, if closing slips beyond the first quarter of 2027, or if covenant relief ends before earnings recover. ### Company description

Olin is a vertically integrated chemicals and ammunition group. Chlor alkali and vinyls produces 54% of sales from chlorine, caustic soda, vinyls and chlorinated organics, epoxy 20%, and Winchester 26%, including the Lake City plant it operates for the US Army. Cash generation rests on commodity chlor alkali spreads, on epoxy margins that were negative from 2023 to 2025, and on government and commercial ammunition demand. The model supports debt service only when chlor alkali earns a normal margin, because that segment funds most of the group's fixed charges, and it weakens refinancing confidence because 9 of the 10 chemical product lines sit at one site.

### Ownership and group structure

Olin Corporation is a listed Virginia company with no sponsor or controlling holder identified, so financial policy is set by a board answerable to dispersed holders. The bank facility is guaranteed by certain domestic subsidiaries and secured by liens until the relief period ends, which puts bank debt ahead of unsecured creditors for now, and the guarantees and ranking of the notes are not stated. The Huntsman merger would give existing Olin shareholders about 54.5% of the combined company, and the creditor effect depends on documents that were not supplied. Distributions above the regular dividend are limited during the relief period and the buyback authorization of USD 1,948.9 million is unused.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Chlor alkali revenue is cyclical and price-taking: nine-month sales fell on lower volumes, mainly the wound-down Blue Water Alliance trading, and lower prices, with chlorine sold in substantial quantities to a relatively small number of industrial users and no customer above 10% of group sales. Epoxy sales rose on higher volumes and prices but face subsidised Asian imports, so the gain is not yet shown to be durable. Winchester revenue is the most predictable because of its backlog and military contracts, yet its price increases were outrun by metal and propellant costs. Revenue is therefore cyclical in chemicals and contracted in ammunition, and the first is the larger and the more exposed to substitution by imports.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

The cost base is energy-linked and largely fixed. Electricity is the largest raw material and about 76% of it comes from natural gas or hydroelectric sources, raw material and utility purchase commitments total USD 5,559.4 million with 13.0% due within a year, and depreciation and amortization was USD 521.6 million in 2025. Operating cost actions delivered USD 118.7 million of savings in chlor alkali and epoxy over nine months, but raw material costs rose by USD 32.1 million in chlor alkali and USD 33.1 million in epoxy, so margins can be defended through cost actions rather than pricing. Chlor alkali gross margin fell to 9.5% from 14.0% in nine months, and restructuring will continue, with about USD 70.0 million of further charges expected through 2030.

### Porter's five forces

Rivalry is the force that matters most: Olin competes with large chlor alkali producers in North America and, in epoxy, with Huntsman, Westlake and Asian producers, and subsidised Asian exports are cited by management as a pressure on the fourth quarter, which transmits into lower prices and margins. New entrants are a weak force because plants need large capital investment and permits. Buyers are moderately strong: chlorine goes to a small number of industrial users and Winchester sells to retailers and governments. Suppliers matter for Winchester, which buys propellant mainly from one large supplier and metals at exchange-linked prices, and for energy, which is the largest raw material. Substitutes for chlorine and caustic soda are not established from the sources.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

For the next twelve to 24 months the outlook is a weak fourth quarter, then a recovery that depends on chlor alkali, with merger closing expected in the first quarter of 2027, covenant relief ending by 30 September 2027 and the receivables financing due in November 2027.
