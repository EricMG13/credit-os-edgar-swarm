## Findings

### Bottom line

Credit quality is weak and dependent on one-off funding: earnings cannot carry the debt, so creditors rely on liquidity of USD 2,397.2 million, covenant relief and pending asset sales, while noteholders with no stated security now rank behind new secured lenders.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C2-1

Last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA was negative USD 53.5 million at 2026-09-30, so leverage cannot be measured. Before USD 671.6 million of third-quarter pricing charges it would have been USD 618.1 million, close to the USD 618.9 million that a 6.75x ceiling requires at the September debt level when the leverage test returns on 2026-12-31. The covenant definition of earnings is not printed, so headroom is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C2-8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fmc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 23–24

### Key findings

Earnings no longer carry the debt, and the damage is wider than one quarter's charges. Third-quarter 2026 Adjusted EBITDA was negative USD 558.6 million, and even before the pricing charges it would have been USD 113.0 million against USD 236.1 million a year earlier, a fall of 52.1%. Revenue excluding India and before the charges fell 16.2% over nine months, and gross margin was 10.81% of revenue against 35.73%. Price pressure on legacy products and lower volumes sit under the channel charges, so the earnings base to which leverage returns is lower than the FY2025 figure of USD 842.7 million.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C2-9; calculation C2-10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fmc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 45–46; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fmc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 54

Revenue durability is weak because the main franchise has lost patent protection and demand is being destocked. The two diamide product families supplied about 34.6% of FY2025 revenue, their composition-of-matter patents have expired in the major markets, and the company names generic producers, particularly in China, as a continuing pricing threat. Brazil and the United States supplied 58.8% of FY2025 revenue, customers are running very low inventory in response to high financing costs and demand uncertainty, and growers are buying carefully because of low crop prices and elevated input costs, so a regional destocking cycle reaches group earnings directly.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a weak view is that outside capital arrived at the point of stress and the pre-charge earnings base is still positive. Tessenderlo paid USD 13.30 per share for about 20% of the company, Corteva prepaid USD 200.0 million, the pre-charge Adjusted EBITDA of USD 618.1 million covers annualised third-quarter interest expense of USD 294.4 million 2.10 times, capital expenditure was cut 57.8% over nine months, and the Project Foundation restructuring targets USD 200 million of annual savings by the end of 2027. The issuer also reports covenant compliance at 2026-09-30 and a revolver of USD 2,000.0 million with a 2028 maturity.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C2-7; calculation C2-15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fmc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 79

The view would improve if the fourth quarter shows no new pricing charges and channel inventory normalises, if the India and Newark proceeds repay debt, and if the credit agreement defines covenant earnings so that the 2026-12-31 leverage test is met with room. It would worsen if further pricing adjustments follow in North America, if accrued rebates are settled in cash, if the India sale slips beyond 2026, or if a further revolver amendment is needed at the first test date.

### Limitations

- The credit agreement is not supplied, so the conclusion about the 2026-12-31 leverage test rests on arithmetic from the reported ratios and on a ceiling of 6.75x; a definition that adds back less than the third-quarter charges could produce a breach at the first test date, and one that adds back more would widen the cushion.
- The USD 671.6 million add-back that gives pre-charge earnings is an analyst normalisation, and the company says it also changed its North American pricing and rebate model, so part of the charge may recur and underlying earnings could be below USD 618.1 million, which would weaken the free cash flow and cover conclusions.
- No peer set or sector benchmark is supplied, so the business profile is judged in absolute terms and its standing against typical leveraged issuers is not established.
- The equity cushion uses the USD 13.30 price paid in September 2026, not a current share price, and the only debt market measure is the issuer's aggregate fair value, so the conclusion on market confidence could change with a dated quote for any series.
- Management guidance is directional only, so the fourth-quarter free cash flow cap is a derived bound and the cash conclusion is bounded rather than forecast.
- The lien ranking among the revolver, the secured notes and the senior and subordinated notes is not printed, so the structural priority conclusion identifies the exposed holders but does not size recovery.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The main credit strength is liquidity and funding access in the near term: USD 2,397.2 million of cash and revolver availability, a revolver that runs to 2028, only USD 72.8 million of principal due within one year, and no maturity above USD 150.0 million before the USD 500.0 million senior notes in 2029. The mechanism is that equity, a licence prepayment and secured notes retired the 2026 maturity and repaid the revolver, which gives creditors time. The implication is that a payment default before 2028 would likely need a cash burn well above the 2026 pattern.

A second strength is cooperative financial policy. The dividend is down to USD 0.08 per share, repurchases are barred until 2028-12-31, new equity went to debt repayment, and capital expenditure was cut 57.8% over nine months. The implication is that cash is being kept for creditors; cash dividends paid fell by USD 188.0 million in nine months 2026, which is modest next to cash interest of USD 250.8 million over twelve months.

A third strength is cost action and optionality: Project Foundation targets USD 200 million of annual run-rate savings by the end of 2027 and the pipeline includes four new active ingredients, and pending sales add USD 366 million gross. These are conditional and arrive after cash costs, so they support the downside case only if delivered.

The main weakness is the earnings collapse. Third-quarter Adjusted EBITDA was negative USD 558.6 million, the last twelve months are negative USD 53.5 million, and even pre-charge earnings fell 52.1% in the quarter. The mechanism is generic competition on the diamides and destocking by customers who face low crop prices, and the implication is that leverage cannot be sized and covenants, not earnings, set the pace.

The second weakness is priority and structure: first-lien secured notes and a secured revolver put 32.0% of principal ahead of the holders of senior and subordinated notes, for which no security is stated, rising to 51.2% on a full revolver draw, with a rating-triggered lien provision and repeated amendments that release and add collateral. The third is thin capital: total equity of USD 9.5 million, claims outside debt in the form of rebates of USD 811.9 million, environmental liabilities of USD 566.0 million and supplier finance of USD 213.1 million, and free cash flow near zero after fixed cash claims. The fourth is concentration, in one patent-exposed franchise and in Brazil and the United States.

### Company, ownership and revenue drivers

The reporting entity is FMC Corporation, consolidated with its subsidiaries; the filings describe no separate finance subsidiary and print no guarantor financial information, so debt, earnings, leverage and cover are read at the consolidated level, with the parent's own figures not separable. FMC is a global agricultural sciences company with one business segment, selling insecticides, herbicides and fungicides and a plant health line of biologicals, crop nutrition and seed treatment. FY2025 revenue was USD 3,467.4 million, down 18.3% from USD 4,246.1 million, with insecticides 45.4%, herbicides 36.0%, fungicides 10.5%, plant health 5.5% and other 2.7%, and Latin America 39.0%, North America 31.8%, Europe, Middle East and Africa 25.1% and Asia 4.1%. The business model supports cash-flow visibility only weakly: sales are seasonal, flow through distributors that are holding elevated FMC inventory and cutting replenishment, and are priced against generic suppliers, so debt service depends on the pricing cycle rather than on contracted revenue.

### Cost structure, margin and cash conversion

Margins are not defended under volume and price pressure. Gross margin fell from 37.0% of revenue in FY2025 to 10.81% in nine months 2026, selling, general and administrative expense rose 3.9% while revenue fell 27.4%, and research and development was 7.7% of FY2025 revenue and USD 183.6 million in nine months 2026. Fixed overhead and research spending therefore deleverage quickly. Management levers exist: Project Foundation exits high-cost plants with a targeted USD 200 million of annual savings by the end of 2027, at a cost of USD 700 million to USD 775 million of total pre-tax charges, of which USD 490 million to USD 505 million is non-cash. Business & governance reported the savings target as not supplied; the third-quarter 2026 report prints it, and this answer corrects that point.

Capital intensity is low, with capital expenditure of USD 96.3 million in FY2025, USD 29.7 million in nine months 2026 and USD 45 million to USD 55 million guided for 2026, so capex flexibility is already used. Restructuring cash of about USD 180 million, environmental spending of USD 80 million to USD 100 million, cash interest of USD 250.8 million over twelve months and dividends of about USD 50.4 million a year absorb the earnings that remain, which is why free cash flow converts at close to zero.

### Competitive forces and external factors

Competitive rivalry and substitutes are the dominant forces. Generic producers, mainly Chinese, compete on the same active ingredients as patents expire, competitors have consolidated into larger groups, and seed-applied technologies are alternatives. The mechanism is price: generic entry lowers the price at which FMC can hold volume, which cuts margin on the diamide franchise of about 34.6% of revenue. Buyer power is high because distributors and co-operatives hold the inventory and can defer orders, and the channel charges show it. Supplier power is moderate: critical intermediates from China and India expose cost and continuity to tariffs and trade measures, and take-or-pay purchase obligations of USD 179.9 million at 2025-12-31 fix part of the cost. New entrants matter mainly as generic registrants, and formulation, mixture and data protection still run longer than the expired composition-of-matter patents.

### Outlook and qualitative downside scenario

Over 12 to 24 months the issuer needs three things: no further channel charges, closing of the India and Newark sales, and a leverage covenant definition that works at 2026-12-31. Tailwinds are lower cash taxes, the working-capital release in the seasonally strong fourth quarter, capex of USD 45 million to USD 55 million and the Project Foundation savings; headwinds are generic diamide competition, restructuring and environmental cash, the 8.00% and 8.45% coupons and weak grower economics. Over three to five years the credit rests on whether new active ingredients (Isoflex, fluindapyr, Dodhylex and rimisoxafen) replace diamide revenue before the 2029 and 2031 maturities. Events after the balance sheet date are a dividend of USD 12.6 million paid on 2026-10-15 and Amendment No. 8 of 2026-10-21, which requires net India proceeds to repay the revolver or fund general or working capital needs and conditionally releases some guarantors and security; neither is in the September figures.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_fmc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fmc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 49; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fmc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 78–79

The downside scenario is a second round of channel adjustments. Initiating condition: customers keep destocking and the North American pricing reset does not hold. First operational break: fourth-quarter 2026 orders stay near the third-quarter level and a further pricing charge is booked. Financial transmission: accrued rebates of USD 811.9 million are settled in cash rather than credit notes and the fourth-quarter working-capital inflow stays below the USD 123.6 million cap, leaving 2026 free cash flow worse than FY2025. The observable triggers are a new revenue charge, a revolver amendment, and the India sale slipping past 2026.

### Overall credit view

Overall, FMC presents an adequate but deteriorating business profile supported by a broad product and regional range, an extensive pipeline and a restructuring programme, but credit quality is constrained by collapsed earnings, a patent-exposed franchise, thin book equity and a secured structure that ranks lien holders first. Cash-flow durability appears weak because free cash flow is near zero at the pre-charge earnings base. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the India and Newark sales, the covenant definition and a recovery in pricing before the 2029 and 2031 maturities. The primary downside path is a second round of channel charges that turns the 2026-12-31 leverage test into an amendment request and draws on the revolver. Further analysis would require the credit agreement and indentures, the Tessenderlo Investor Agreement, rating levels, dated bond quotes and a peer set; the business profile against typical leveraged issuers is not established because no peer set is supplied. Credit synthesis completed. Top risk: covenant and liquidity dependence on earnings that are currently negative.
