## Findings

### Bottom line

After the USD 2,628 million Off-Highway purchase, Allison is a more diversified but lower-margin group with no sponsor, so creditors rely on observed behaviour, which has been broadly supportive, rather than on legal documents that are not supplied.

The acquired business is 45.10% of nine-month net sales but earns an adjusted EBITDA margin of 14.03% against 37.47% for the legacy business, and its implied pro forma sales growth is only 7.57%, so cash flow support still comes mainly from North American on-highway demand, which is 51.21% of legacy segment sales.

### Key findings

The purchase was funded almost entirely with new debt, and the new money is mostly secured, which moves existing noteholders further back. New principal of USD 2,000 million (a USD 1,200 million incremental term loan, USD 500 million of unsecured notes and a USD 300 million revolver draw) covered all but USD 628 million of the price, and cash fell from USD 1,495 million at year-end 2025 to USD 263 million after closing. Secured term debt rose by USD 1,190 million to USD 1,699 million, against USD 2,300 million of unsecured notes, so noteholders now sit behind a much larger secured tranche.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_alsn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_alsn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-12-31_alsn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 65; 10-K_2025-12-31_alsn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 67; 10-K_2025-12-31_alsn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 101; 10-K_2025-12-31_alsn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 104; calculation FD-1; calculation FD-2; calculation SD-1; calculation SD-2; calculation SD-3; calculation SD-4

Diversification is real but of lower quality than the legacy franchise. Off-Highway is 45.10% of nine-month net sales, spread across construction and material handling at 34.84%, agriculture at 21.46%, industrial at 13.83%, mining at 7.73% and service parts, specialty and other at 22.15%, but it earns an adjusted EBITDA margin of 14.03% against 37.47% for Allison Transmission, and the issuer calls agriculture and industrial demand subdued. Implied pro forma sales growth was 4.15% in the third quarter and 7.57% in the nine months, so the acquisition has not yet shown strong underlying growth.

The legacy business is the anchor of the credit. Allison Transmission net sales rose 8.84% in the nine months, defense sales rose 44.33%, and North American on-highway is 51.21% of segment sales under long-term agreements that cover over 90% of its unit volume. That strength comes with concentration: the top three OEM customers were 39% of 2025 net sales and the top five about 52%, so the loss of one customer or a turn in the truck cycle would hit cash flow directly.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The view that the diversification is of lower quality could be too harsh: Off-Highway has only three quarters in the numbers, the issuer says construction and mining demand is rebounding from trough levels and the third quarter is seasonally the softest, and the legacy business is at record sales with a 37.47% margin. If integration lifts the Off-Highway margin toward the legacy level, group cash generation would be stronger than the nine-month figures show.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-08-03_d95413dex991.htm.jsonl, page 1; EX-99.1_2026-11-02_d187329dex991.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation SG-2

The view turns more cautious if the Off-Highway margin stays near 14.03%, if buybacks exceed free cash flow, if the revolver is drawn again, if further debt-funded acquisitions follow, or if the executed debt documents permit large restricted payments or additional priming debt.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-11-02_d187329dex991.htm.jsonl, page 10; calculation SG-1

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Allison Transmission Holdings completed the purchase of Dana Incorporated's off-highway business on 2026-01-01 under a stock purchase agreement signed on 2025-06-11. The final price was USD 2,628 million: USD 2,664 million of cash was transferred at closing, USD 2 million of it settling an existing payable, and a USD 34 million post-closing adjustment came back. The annual report gave approximately USD 2,732 million and no source bridges the USD 104 million difference.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_alsn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-31_alsn-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 117–118; calculation FD-4; calculation FD-5

The purchase was funded with new debt and cash. Allison Transmission, Inc. issued USD 500 million of senior unsecured notes due December 2033 in November 2025. On 2026-01-02 it added a USD 1,200 million secured incremental term loan due 2033-01-02 at Term SOFR plus 1.75% (5.54% all-in at 2026-09-30), raised the revolver commitments from USD 750 million to USD 1,000 million with a January 2031 maturity and drew USD 300 million. A 364-day bridge commitment of up to USD 2,000 million, cut to USD 500 million by year-end 2025, was never drawn and ended at closing, and cash on hand covered the rest, falling from USD 1,495 million to USD 263 million.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_alsn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 65; 10-K_2025-12-31_alsn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 67; 10-K_2025-12-31_alsn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 104; 10-K_2025-12-31_alsn-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 118; 10-Q_2026-09-30_alsn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_alsn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_alsn-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation FD-1; calculation FD-6

Interest on the new debt cost USD 24 million in the third quarter alone, USD 17 million on the incremental term loan and USD 7 million on the 2033 notes.
