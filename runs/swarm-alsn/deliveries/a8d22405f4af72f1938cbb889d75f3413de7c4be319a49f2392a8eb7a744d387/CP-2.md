## Findings

### Bottom line

Allison's credit profile is adequate but weaker than before the Off-Highway purchase: a high-margin transmission franchise generates the cash to reduce acquisition debt, so creditors are exposed mainly to cycle and integration risk.

Free cash flow of USD 662 million in nine months and liquidity of USD 1,493 million leave room to keep repaying debt.

### Company description

Allison makes fully automatic transmissions and electric propulsion solutions for commercial vehicles, buses, motorhomes and U.S. defense vehicles, sells service parts and extended coverage through a network of independent distributors and dealers, and since 2026-01-01 also makes drivetrain and motion systems for construction, agriculture, mining, material handling and industrial equipment. The legacy business is cash generative and contractually anchored, while the acquired business broadens end markets at lower margins, so the model supports debt service but with a larger share of cyclical, lower-margin revenue than before.

### Porter's Five Forces

- Rivalry is limited in North American on-highway, where Allison supplies the fully automatic transmission, but vertically integrated OEMs and competitors such as Caterpillar, Komatsu and Volvo cap share in mining and construction, which limits growth at Off-Highway.
- New entrants face high engineering and manufacturing barriers, and fully electric propulsion remains in a developmental phase for medium- and heavy-duty vehicles, so electrification is a long-dated rather than near-term threat to cash flow.
- Substitutes are manual and automated manual transmissions, mainly in Class 8 line-haul where Allison has limited exposure, so substitution risk is concentrated in a small part of sales.
- Buyer power is high because a handful of OEMs buy most volume, offset by long-term agreements and by end users who often specify the Allison product.
- Supplier power matters through aluminium and steel castings and forgings and a direct-material share of about 65% of cost of sales that passes through only in part.

The issuer-specific downside begins with a North American truck downturn like 2025, when legacy net sales fell 6.67%, arriving while agriculture and industrial demand stay subdued. ### Overall credit view

Overall, Allison presents an adequate business profile supported by a high-margin transmission franchise, long-term OEM agreements and a large aftermarket, but credit quality is constrained by acquisition leverage of 3.31x, interest cover of 6.55x and an unproven lower-margin Off-Highway business. Cash-flow durability appears adequate due to free cash flow of USD 662 million in nine months and cost actions that held margin in 2025. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on continued repayment, stable ratings and the 2029 and 2031 maturities. The primary downside path is a North American truck downturn coinciding with weak off-highway demand. Further analysis would require pro forma EBITDA, the executed debt documents and ownership detail. Top risk: a cyclical volume decline that stalls deleveraging while interest and shareholder returns continue.
