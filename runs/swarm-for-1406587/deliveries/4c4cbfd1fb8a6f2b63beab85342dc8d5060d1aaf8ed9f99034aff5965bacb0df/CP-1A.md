## Findings

### Bottom line
Forestar is a parent-controlled lot developer: D.R. Horton owns about 62%, bought 82.79% of lot sales revenues in fiscal 2025 and can veto debt above 40% leverage, so creditors get steady contracted demand but carry conflict risk and no stated parent support.
Basis: calculation B01; calculation B10; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 41

No dividend, buyback or other extraction is documented, the 2025 refinancing moved the first note maturity to 2028, and the parent's lot contracts carry USD 2,095.1 million of remaining sales price, but a stockholder suit alleges breach of fiduciary duty in lot sales to the parent and the executed agreements are not supplied. Governance risk is medium.
Basis: calculation B04; calculation B11; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 70; 10-Q_2026-06-30_for-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 15

### Key findings

Control sits with one industrial parent, and no parent guarantee or support for the Company's debt is stated. D.R. Horton owns about 62% of the common stock, designates four of seven directors and the Executive Chairman, and while it holds at least 35% must consent to new equity classes, executive changes, certain acquisitions and debt that takes total leverage above 40%. The consent right limits releveraging by the Company alone, but it also gives the parent a veto over refinancing choices; the filings do not define total leverage for this test, so it is not established as a creditor protection.
Basis: calculation B10; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-06-30_for-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 4

Revenue is concentrated in the controlling shareholder, so the Company's cash flow is a function of one buyer's demand. D.R. Horton accounted for 82.79% of residential lot sales revenues in fiscal 2025 and 85.97% in the nine months to June 30, 2026, against 82.89% a year earlier, and 80.61% of total revenues once tract sales are counted. Lot bankers that expect to resell to D.R. Horton add indirect dependence: USD 83.4 million of lots in fiscal 2025 and USD 33.4 million in the nine months. Contract strength partly offsets this, with USD 2,095.1 million of remaining sales price on owned lots under contract to the parent and USD 195.3 million of earnest money, 9.32% of that price.
Basis: calculation B01; calculation B02; calculation B04; calculation B12; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-Q_2026-06-30_for-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-06-30_for-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-06-30_for-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 21

The supply relationship is long but not binding on the parent. The Master Supply Agreement runs until June 29, 2037 or until D.R. Horton falls below 15% of voting shares, and the parent holds a right of first offer on lots, not an obligation to buy; it is not required to present land opportunities and is not restricted from competing, and lot sales contracts require an earnest money deposit that the Company may retain or refund on cancellation depending on the contract. The deposit is a modest share of the remaining price, so contracted revenue rests on the parent's willingness to close as much as on the deposit.
Basis: calculation B04; calculation B10; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 14–16; 10-Q_2026-06-30_for-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 15

Related-party costs are modest, but the pricing of lots to the parent is contested. Shared-service, benefit and other costs paid to the parent were USD 23.3 million in fiscal 2025, 15.09% of selling, general and administrative expense, and the Company reimbursed the parent USD 47.1 million for earnest money and diligence costs on contracts it then closed itself; neither looks like extraction on this evidence. A derivative complaint filed on April 29, 2025 alleges breach of fiduciary duty in lot sales between the Company and the parent, no loss is estimated and the Company disputes it, so a settlement or ruling that changed lot terms could affect the Company's revenue; no such change is stated.
Basis: calculation B06; calculation B07; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 78–79; 10-Q_2026-06-30_for-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 13

Financial policy has been creditor-aligned on distributions and maturities. The Company pays no dividend and intends to retain earnings, raised USD 19.7 million of equity in fiscal 2024 and none since while holding USD 300.0 million of program capacity, and in 2025 issued USD 500.0 million of 6.5% notes due 2033 to retire the 2026 notes, so no note matures before March 2028. The cost is a higher coupon, and legal capacity for dividends, related-party payments and added debt cannot be assessed because the indentures and credit agreement are not supplied.
Basis: calculation B11; calculation B12; 10-K_2024-09-30_for-20240930.htm.jsonl, page 28; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 37; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 70; 10-Q_2026-06-30_for-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 12

The business model limits some risks and concentrates others. The Company develops entitled, short-duration projects in phases across 65 markets in 24 states, so no single market dominates, but substantially all development work is subcontracted and lot demand is under affordability pressure: in the nine months to June 30, 2026 lots sold fell 8.64% while the average price rose 8.13%, so residential lot sales revenues fell 1.37%. Real estate rose USD 378.9 million in fiscal 2025, which commits cash to land ahead of sales.
Basis: calculation B08; calculation B09; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-06-30_for-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 18–20

Disclosure supports monitoring of reported figures but not of control terms. The statements are audited with an unqualified opinion and effective internal control, lenders receive quarterly covenant reports, and the Company has not used its controlled-company exemptions, but director independence, pay, beneficial ownership and the executed agreements sit in documents that are not supplied.
Basis: Source readiness (CP-0); 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 57; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 81–82; 10-Q_2026-06-30_for-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 8

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest evidence for a lower risk view is behavioural and contractual: the Company has paid no dividend, the parent's leverage consent right works as a cap on debt, and its lot contracts carry USD 2,095.1 million of remaining sales price backed by USD 195.3 million of earnest money under a supply agreement that runs to 2037. The view would improve if the executed agreements showed firm purchase obligations and a defined leverage test, if the derivative suit were dismissed, and if the parent's own credit quality were shown to be strong. It would worsen if D.R. Horton fell below 35%, which ends its consent rights, or below 20%, which ends its board designation rights, if lot prices to the parent were reset, if its lot purchases fell, or if the Company paid a dividend or a related-party payment out of debt-funded cash.
Basis: calculation B04; calculation B10; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-06-30_for-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 15

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

D.R. Horton became the controlling shareholder in October 2017 under a merger agreement dated June 29, 2017 and owned 75% of the common stock afterwards; it owned about 62% at September 30, 2025 and at June 30, 2026, and the filings do not describe how the holding fell.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2025-09-30_for-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 83–84; 10-Q_2026-06-30_for-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 4

New debt in the period is a refinancing, not growth capital. In March 2025 the Company issued USD 500.0 million of 6.5% senior notes due 2033 and tendered for its 2026 notes, of which USD 329.4 million was tendered, and in September 2025 it redeemed the remaining USD 70.6 million, so no note matures before March 2028. The revolver grew from USD 410.0 million of commitments at September 30, 2024 to USD 640.0 million a year later, then to USD 665.0 million after October 30, 2025 and USD 715.0 million after March 2026, with USD 650.0 million maturing in December 2029 and USD 65.0 million in October 2026; a USD 9.9 million secured note was repaid in December 2025.

Equity access is unused: an at-the-market program raised USD 19.7 million in fiscal 2024, and a new program of USD 300.0 million under a USD 750.0 million shelf has issued nothing. No business acquisition, disposal, dividend or recapitalisation is reported in the period.
