## Findings

### Bottom line
Forestar is an adequate credit: modest leverage and large liquidity cover its debt, but its controlling shareholder, D.R. Horton, buys most of its lots, so creditors depend on that parent's continued purchases. Total debt of USD 793.8 million is 3.13x last-twelve-months (LTM) EBITDA (calculated here because the issuer prints none) and net debt is 1.57x, while liquidity of USD 1,064.8 million is 3.55x the USD 300.0 million of notes due March 2028, with no principal due for 20 months.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C02; 10-Q_2026-06-30_for-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-06-30_for-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 24; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The weak point is cash flow: free cash flow was USD 2.3 million over fiscal 2023 to 2025 against USD 766.4 million of EBITDA, because earnings were reinvested in land, so leverage depends on spending that management controls. The main downside path is a slower parent taking fewer lots at lower prices while development spending continues.

### Key findings

Dependence on one controlling customer is the dominant credit risk, because that customer sets both the volume and the price of most revenue. D.R. Horton owns about 62% of the common stock, bought 82.79% of residential lot sales revenues in fiscal 2025 and 85.97% in the nine months to June 30, 2026 (82.89% a year earlier), and has USD 2,095.1 million of remaining sales price under contract with earnest money of only 9.32% of it. The supply agreement gives the parent a right of first offer, not a purchase obligation, no parent guarantee of the debt is stated, and a derivative suit alleges breach of fiduciary duty in lot sale transactions with the parent, so creditors rely on the parent's choice to keep buying at acceptable prices.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest evidence for a better view is that the balance sheet improved in the same season: net debt to total capital was 17.7% at June 30, 2026 against 28.9% a year earlier, operating cash flow turned positive with no borrowing on the revolver, no dividend has been paid and the parent holds USD 2,095.1 million of contracted remaining sales price. If fiscal 2026 shows positive operating cash flow for the full year, EBITDA at or above USD 253.7 million and a replacement for the revolver tranche, the view would move toward strong on liquidity and leverage. The strongest evidence for a worse view is concentration: the parent's share of lot revenues rose from 82.89% to 85.97% between the two nine-month periods while lots sold fell 8.64%, and no purchase obligation exists. The view would weaken if that share passed the fiscal 2024 level of 87.30% while volumes fell, if net cash put into real estate returned to the nine-month 2025 pace of USD 561.0 million with EBITDA below USD 247.2 million, which would put net debt above 1.71x EBITDA, or if the parent's holding fell below 35%.

### Business model, ownership and financial policy

Forestar acquires and entitles land, develops it into finished residential lots and sells them mainly to homebuilders in 65 markets across 24 states, with a lot position of 91,700 lots, of which 62,200 are owned and 29,500 are controlled through purchase contracts. Projects are entitled, short-duration and phased, substantially all development work is subcontracted and fixed assets are negligible, so capital is tied up for a limited period per phase; the cost is that real estate of USD 2,711.5 million is 84.19% of total assets, financed by equity, debt and USD 202.2 million of customer deposits. Cash-flow visibility therefore comes from contracts and delivery timing, not from recurring revenue, and debt service capacity depends on lots closing.

D.R. Horton controls the company with about 62% of the common stock, designates four of seven directors, and must consent while it holds at least 35% to debt that takes total leverage above 40%; the Master Supply Agreement runs to June 29, 2037 and gives it a right of first offer on lots, and the company relies on it for shared services. No dividend, repurchase or parent funding is documented, equity of USD 19.7 million was raised in fiscal 2024 and none since, and the issuer aims to keep net debt to total capital at approximately 40% or less. The revolver carries financial covenants for minimum tangible net worth, minimum liquidity and maximum leverage whose levels are not printed, and the filings say the parent's interests may differ from those of other holders, so creditor alignment is partial: the parent is a supportive customer and a controlling shareholder at the same time.

### Revenue, margin and cash-flow durability

Revenue is transactional, cyclical, concentrated and partly contracted rather than recurring. Lot sales close three to eighteen months after contract, 37.78% of owned lots are under contract and the fiscal fourth quarter is seasonally the strongest, so no single quarter represents the year. Price and volume moved in opposite directions in the nine months to June 30, 2026, with lots sold down 8.64% and the average price up 8.13%, at a time when management says builders are using incentives to address affordability; revenue visibility is therefore up to eighteen months, and the pricing power shown depends on the parent accepting higher prices.

### Competitive forces

Competitive rivalry is high and the market is fragmented: the annual report names numerous local, regional and national developers and homebuilders, some larger or with a lower cost of capital, and says competition can make it hard to buy or sell land at acceptable returns. ### Outlook

Over the next 12 to 24 months management describes demand as held back by affordability and cautious consumer sentiment with elevated builder incentives, yet third-quarter revenues rose 4.23% and net cash put into real estate has been cut; the sector headwind is elevated mortgage rates and the company headwind is lower lot volume at higher prices. ### Downside scenario

The downside path starts with mortgage rates staying elevated and builder demand softening further, so D.R. Horton slows takedowns of the lots it has under contract and seeks lower prices. The first operational break is closings moving beyond the usual three to eighteen months, with earnest money of 9.32% of remaining sales price the only stated protection and the company free to retain or refund it on cancellation depending on the contract, while development spending on projects already started continues. Revenue and gross profit fall against overhead of USD 154.4 million in fiscal 2025, operating cash flow turns negative as it did in fiscal 2024 and 2025 even when revenue grew, and inventory rises.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Forestar presents an adequate business profile supported by modest leverage, USD 1,064.8 million of liquidity and contracted lots with its controlling shareholder, but credit quality is constrained by dependence on that shareholder for most revenue and by cash flow that follows inventory spending. Cash-flow durability appears adequate: EBITDA has stayed between USD 237.8 million and USD 281.4 million across three fiscal years and land spending is discretionary, but free cash flow was close to nil over those years. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on market access for the USD 300.0 million of notes due March 2028, the parent's continued purchases, and the borrowing base and covenants on the revolver. The primary downside path is a slower parent taking fewer lots at lower prices while development spending continues, which turns operating cash flow negative and raises net leverage. Further analysis would require cash interest paid, covenant levels and compliance certificates, the executed agreements with D.R. Horton, price by customer, results for the fiscal year ended September 30, 2026 and peer data. Top risk: dependence on D.R. Horton's lot purchases.
