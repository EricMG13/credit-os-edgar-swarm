## Findings

### Bottom line

SunCoke is a contract-anchored industrial credit whose main risks are customer concentration and recontracting rather than owner behaviour: Cliffs Steel supplied 61.6% of FY2025 revenue, and the filings identify no controlling holder.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 104–105; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 69; calculation C02

Take-or-pay coke agreements, a long run of unsatisfied performance obligations and USD 505.8 million of fixed-fee services revenue support earnings visibility, and the Phoenix Global purchase lifted Industrial Services to 19.4% of revenue in the nine months of 2026. Against that, Haverhill I was closed after Algoma Steel refused coke, the Granite City agreement runs only to 2026-12-31, and no debt agreement or proxy statement was supplied, so legal limits on dividends and acquisitions are not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 26; calculation C01; calculation C05

### Key findings

Cash flow is contract-anchored but concentrated in two customer groups. Take-or-pay terms oblige customers to buy the contracted tons or pay the contract price, and coal cost passes through when targeted yields are met, so volume risk and most coal-price risk sit with the customers, while yield shortfalls and operating-cost overruns stay with SunCoke. Cliffs Steel and U.S. Steel together supplied 75.8% of FY2025 revenue and owed 36% of the receivables balance at year-end, so the credit is in practice an exposure to the solvency and renewal decisions of those two groups.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 104–105; calculation C02

The contract calendar is the nearest business risk. The Granite City agreement with U.S. Steel was extended only to 2026-12-31, which is 43 days after the analysis date, the Algoma Steel volume at Haverhill II ends in December 2026 and is already in dispute, while the Cliffs Steel agreements run to 2028, 2032 and 2035 and the ArcelorMittal Brazil operating agreement to January 2028. A lapse or weaker repricing at Granite City would push volume at that plant into non-contracted sales, which are priced at spot and export market levels and carry none of the take-or-pay protection.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 11–13; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 73; calculation C06

The Algoma Steel breach and the Haverhill I closure show that contract protection is not complete. Algoma Steel refused further coke at the end of Q3 2025, the issuer recognised a USD 90.1 million impairment at Haverhill I in Q4 2025 and completed the shutdown in Q1 2026, and a settlement was reached in October 2026 whose terms are not stated. Until the terms are known, it is not established how much of the lost contract value can be recovered or whether the settlement brings cash.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 72–73; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 75; 10-Q_2026-03-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 26

The Phoenix Global acquisition changed the business mix and added integration risk. The USD 294.0 million purchase, completed on 2025-08-01 with cash and revolver borrowing, adds mill services and slag handling at about fifteen sites in four countries and took Industrial Services from 4.2% of FY2024 revenue to 19.4% of nine-month 2026 revenue, with international revenue rising from 2.9% to 5.5% of nine-month revenue. Integration of its processes and controls was completed on 2026-09-30 and site closures are under way, so cost and control risks are still being worked through.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 101–104; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 13–15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 37; calculation C01; calculation C07

Ownership is dispersed and no sponsor is identified, which removes the usual leveraged-buyout extraction risk but leaves governance largely undocumented in the supplied sources. The company is listed and has holders of record in the thousands, a separate chair and chief executive, and an audit by KPMG with effective internal control for FY2025 excluding Phoenix Global. A third party holds 14.8% of the Indiana Harbor facility and received cash distributions that are a leak from that subsidiary before they reach the parent, but board independence, pay and related-party dealings sit in a proxy statement that was not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 69; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 34–36; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 88–91; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 96; calculation C03

Financial policy has been steady and partly creditor-friendly, with one weaker year. The quarterly dividend of USD 0.12 per share continues, share repurchases have been suspended since the first quarter of 2020, and the revolver was repaid by USD 86.0 million in the nine months of 2026. In FY2025, however, dividends and distributions to the minority holder exceeded free cash flow, and the acquisition was debt-funded, so releveraging is a documented behaviour even though no related-party leakage or liability management transaction is stated in the filings read.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 69–70; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 35–36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 38; calculation C03; calculation C04

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a benign owner-behaviour reading is the combination of a debt-funded acquisition and a dividend paid through a weak free cash flow year: in FY2025 dividends and minority distributions of USD 48.8 million exceeded free cash flow of USD 42.3 million, and carrying debt rose after the Phoenix Global purchase. The view would change to a higher governance risk if the credit agreement or indenture shows wide restricted-payment, investment or asset-transfer capacity, if the proxy statement discloses a large holder or related-party arrangements, or if the board sustains the dividend while leverage stays high. It would change toward lower business risk if the Granite City agreement is renewed on similar economics for a multi-year term, and if the Algoma Steel settlement delivers cash or replacement contracts.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 91–93; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 69; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 26; calculation C03

### Limitations

- The proxy statement was not supplied, so board independence, director and executive pay, committee structure and any large holder are not established; the view that owner behaviour is a secondary risk rests on filings that do not describe them.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 135–136
- No credit agreement, indenture or guarantee document was supplied, so capacity to pay dividends, acquire businesses or transfer assets is not established; the reading of financial policy as creditor-neutral rests on observed behaviour, not on legal limits.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 113–115; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18
- The settlement of the Algoma Steel claim has no disclosed amount or terms, so whether the Haverhill I loss is partly recoverable, and how firm take-or-pay claims are in practice, are not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 26
- Contract expiry dates come from the issuer's facility table and extension notes, not from the contracts, so renewal options, termination rights and make-whole terms are not established; the recontracting risk at Granite City may be larger or smaller than the dates imply.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 73
- Several operating measures, such as the split of fixed and variable cost, customer-level margins and the weight of non-contracted coke sales, are not printed, so the earnings sensitivity to a single contract is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 13–14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 30–32
