## Findings

### Bottom line

Creditors hold a contract-backed credit that is deleveraging, with no debt maturing before 2029 and USD 275.6 million of liquidity, but its cash flow rests on two steel customers whose nearest agreement expires on 2026-12-31. Trailing net debt is 2.09x issuer-adjusted EBITDA and 3.35x reported EBITDA, or 3.42x pro forma for the dividend payable on 2026-12-01; both measures are improving after the Phoenix Global purchase, and no adjusted-basis pro forma is established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 35–36; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 115; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01; calculation C02

The view turns on two unresolved items: whether USD 97.4 million of receivable growth since year-end converts to cash, and whether the Granite City agreement is renewed on similar terms. Covenant headroom is not established because no debt agreement was supplied.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 12; calculation C05; calculation C10

### Key findings

Deleveraging is real and came mainly from operations, but the headline ratios depend on which EBITDA is used. Carrying debt fell USD 84.7 million to USD 600.8 million in nine months, USD 86.0 million of it net revolver repayment, and trailing net debt is 2.09x issuer-adjusted EBITDA against 3.35x reported EBITDA, which is 3.42x pro forma for the USD 10.2 million dividend payable on 2026-12-01. The gap is the FY2025 impairment still inside the trailing window: add-backs were USD 110.0 million in FY2025 but only USD 2.0 million in the nine months of 2026, so the difference between the two measures is a 2025 event, not a continuing habit of adjusting.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 35–36; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 79; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C02; calculation C12

Customer concentration, not leverage, is the binding constraint on the credit. Cliffs Steel and U.S. Steel supplied 75.7% of nine-month revenue and 79.3% of Q3 revenue, up 7.5 percentage points on the prior-year quarter, while revenue from all other customers fell USD 36.5 million (26.6%) in the quarter. Take-or-pay terms oblige these customers to buy the contracted tons or pay for them, so the exposure is first counterparty performance and renewal, and only second volume.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 104; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 12; calculation C09

The contract calendar links business risk to refinancing risk. The Granite City agreement with U.S. Steel ends on 2026-12-31, 43 days after the analysis date, and covers 20.6% of contracted tons; the Haverhill II agreement, at 17.5%, ends in December 2028, so 38.1% of contracted tons comes up for renegotiation by the end of 2028, ahead of the 2029 notes. FY2025 results already cite the economics of the Granite City extension as a drag, and the January 2026 extension was again only one year on similar terms, so repricing is a demonstrated risk, not a hypothetical one.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 73; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 75; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 78; calculation C10; calculation C11

Take-or-pay protection is real but has already been breached once. Algoma Steel's refusal to take coke triggered a USD 90.1 million impairment at Haverhill I, which was then closed in the first quarter of 2026; a settlement was reached in October 2026, but its amount and terms are not disclosed, so what a take-or-pay claim recovers in practice is not established. The coke backlog also fell 12.4% in nine months, so renewals rather than the existing book will set earnings after 2028.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 72; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 75; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 19; calculation C08

Cash conversion is the nearest test of the improvement. Receivables rose USD 97.4 million since year-end to USD 208.9 million on quarterly revenue that moved only from USD 455.1 million to USD 485.9 million, lifting receivable days from 21.4 days to 39.6 days, and the balance did not fall in Q3. The issuer attributes the build to the timing of customer payments without quantifying it. Nine-month operating cash flow of USD 140.3 million therefore rests on cash generated before working capital, with inventory release and tax inflows cushioning a USD 99.9 million receivable build.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-Q_2026-06-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-03-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 8; calculation C05; calculation C06

Liquidity is adequate and the maturity wall is distant, but liquidity is mostly undrawn revolver and the covenant tests cannot be checked. Cash of USD 57.6 million and undrawn revolver of USD 218.0 million give USD 275.6 million against trailing cash interest of USD 36.9 million; nothing matures before 2029 and the revolver runs to July 2030. The covenants are a 4.50x maximum net leverage ratio and a 2.50x minimum interest coverage ratio; compliance is reported, but the definitions are not supplied, so headroom is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 115; calculation C01; calculation C03

Phoenix Global diversified the products but not the end market, and Domestic Coke earnings are less fixed than the contract label implies. Industrial Services supplied 41.8% of reportable-segment adjusted EBITDA in the nine months against 17.7% in FY2024, yet its work is tied to steel-plant output. Domestic Coke adjusted EBITDA fell USD 64.7 million (27.6%) in FY2025 and USD 5.5 million in the nine months of 2026 against the prior-year period, and the price effect on adjusted EBITDA swung from negative USD 39.8 million in FY2025 to positive USD 22.4 million in the nine months of 2026 on the stated driver of the mix of contracted and non-contracted tons.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 77; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 72; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 30–32; calculation C07; calculation C08

Financial policy is creditor-neutral rather than conservative, and its legal limits are unknown. The quarterly dividend of USD 0.12 per share was held through the debt-funded acquisition, payouts exceeded FY2025 free cash flow by USD 6.5 million, and trailing free cash flow now covers payouts 2.45x; repurchases have been suspended since the first quarter of 2020. No exchange, priming or other creditor-adverse transaction appears in the filings read, but the indenture and credit agreement restrict dividends on terms that were not supplied.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better view is that the improvement is already visible in cash and in customer behaviour. Trailing free cash flow of USD 119.3 million covers dividends and minority distributions 2.45x, adjusted EBITDA covers cash interest 7.04x, the weighted contract term is about eight years, Cliffs Steel extended Haverhill II to 2028 after the Algoma dispute, and U.S. Steel has repeatedly extended Granite City on similar economics. The view would improve if Granite City is renewed for a multi-year term before 2026-12-31, if receivables fall back toward the year-end balance of USD 111.5 million without write-offs, or if the Algoma settlement brings cash.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 73; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 92; calculation C03; calculation C04

The strongest evidence for a worse view is that receivables have stayed near the same level at two quarter-ends (USD 209.3 million at Q2 and USD 208.9 million at Q3) while the one-year extensions leave U.S. Steel free to reprice every year. The view would weaken if Granite City lapses or is renewed on worse terms, if quarterly operating cash flow turns negative again as it did in Q2 (negative USD 27.2 million), if Domestic Coke sales volumes keep falling beyond the Haverhill I closure, or if a second customer refuses tons.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-06-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 73; calculation C05; calculation C06

### Business and company description

SunCoke is the largest independent producer of coke in the Americas by tons produced, with about 38% of U.S. blast furnace coke capacity in five U.S. cokemaking plants whose capacity and contracts are set out in the table below, plus a sixth plant in Brazil that it operates for ArcelorMittal Brazil for fees. Most coke is sold to Cliffs Steel and U.S. Steel under long-term take-or-pay agreements; unreserved capacity is sold as foundry coke and into spot and export markets. Since the August 2025 acquisition of Phoenix Global the group also provides mill services and slag handling at about fifteen steel-plant sites in four countries alongside its bulk-material terminals, and these Industrial Services produced 19.4% of nine-month 2026 revenue against 4.2% in FY2024.

### Ownership, group structure and financial policy

SunCoke is a widely held, New York-listed company with no controlling holder identified in the filings and about seven thousand holders of record, so there is no sponsor to extract cash or to supply equity in a downturn. The notes are obligations of SunCoke Energy, Inc. with subsidiary guarantors party to the indenture, and the revolver is guaranteed by certain subsidiaries and secured on substantially all assets of the company and the guarantors; the guarantor list sits in an exhibit that was not supplied, so the position of any non-guarantor, including the Brazilian operations, cannot be mapped. The minority holder of Indiana Harbor received USD 7.4 million in FY2025 and USD 5.9 million in the nine months of 2026, cash that leaves the group before it reaches debt service.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 69; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 114; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 137; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 93; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 10

Behaviour since 2020 has been to suspend repurchases (USD 96.3 million of the authorisation is unused), to keep a quarterly dividend of USD 0.12 per share, to fund the USD 294.0 million Phoenix Global purchase with cash and revolver, and then to repay USD 86.0 million of that revolver in nine months. The indenture limits borrowing, liens, dividends, asset sales and affiliate transactions and the credit agreement may limit dividends, so a legal ceiling on distributions exists but is not visible. This is an interpretation of conduct, not of capacity: leverage tolerance looks moderate because the revolver was drawn to USD 193.0 million at year-end and was cut to USD 107.0 million within nine months.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue in the contracted core is predictable, but the contract label overstates how fixed it is. Cokemaking was 75.7% of nine-month revenue against 91.3% in FY2024 and is priced as an operating-cost component, a coal component passed through while contractual yields are met, and a fixed per-ton fee that earns the return on capital, so revenue moves with coal prices without moving margin when yields are met. Domestic Coke sales volumes fell 7.1% in the nine months after the Haverhill I shutdown, and the price effect on adjusted EBITDA reversed from negative in FY2025 to positive in 2026 on the mix of contracted and non-contracted tons, which shows that tons sold at spot and export prices matter at the margin. Foundry coke is sold on annual agreements with no take-or-pay volume.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 28; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, pages 30–32; calculation C07; calculation C08

Industrial Services revenue combines fixed fees with volume-based charges tied to customer production, with periodic adjustment for macroeconomic indicators, and terminals earn per-ton fees; handling volumes rose 22.7% in Q3 and 19.3% in the nine months. Fixed-fee and take-or-pay revenue not yet recognised is USD 505.8 million over about eleven years, down USD 81.3 million since year-end. In credit terms revenue is predictable and recurring in the contracted core, concentrated in two customers, cyclical through steel output for services and non-contracted tons, and structurally exposed over a long horizon to electric arc furnace substitution.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Coal is the largest cost and passes through to customers when contractual yields are met; each ton of coke consumes more than one ton of coal, coal is bought on one-year contracts, and coal procurement obligations of USD 738.2 million at year-end ran through 2026. Operating costs are fixed with an inflation index under four agreements and passed through against a negotiated budget under the other, with overruns above caps or budgets borne by SunCoke. Yield shortfalls and the Middletown turbine failure, which cut energy revenue, show where cost and revenue leak, and 53% of the workforce is unionised under about ten agreements, which limits labour flexibility.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-K_2025-12-31_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 83; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sxc.htm.jsonl, page 28

Adjusted EBITDA margin was 14.10% in FY2024, 11.93% in FY2025 and 13.69% on a trailing basis, with Q3 2026 at 15.83% against 12.14% a year earlier. The pass-through defends margin against coal inflation, but not against yield shortfalls, volume loss at a plant whose operating costs are largely fixed, or repricing at renewal; the split between fixed and variable cost is not printed, so operating leverage is not quantified.

Competitive rivalry is moderate and sits at the edges, because capacity is largely committed under take-or-pay agreements. SunCoke holds about 38% of U.S. blast furnace coke capacity against steelmakers' own plants and merchant producers, so rivalry matters at the 2026 and 2028 renewals and for the spot-priced tons that drove the FY2025 price decline, not for the contracted base.

### Overall credit view

Overall, SunCoke presents an adequate business profile supported by take-or-pay contracts with cost pass-through, heat-recovery technology and a distant maturity profile, but credit quality is constrained by two-customer concentration, a contract calendar that starts to expire on 2026-12-31 and an unconverted receivable build.
