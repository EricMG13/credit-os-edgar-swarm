## Findings

### Bottom line

Net leverage on EBITDA calculated from statement lines fell to 3.05x at 2026-09-30 from 4.05x at FY2025, which eases refinancing pressure ahead of the 2029 maturities, but the improvement rests on debt paydown funded by unusually strong working-capital cash flow.

The issuer prints no EBITDA or adjusted EBITDA, so every ratio uses operating profit plus property depreciation and intangible amortization; rental-merchandise depreciation and other gains and charges stay deducted, and adding back the latter as an analyst normalisation would put net leverage at 2.49x.
Basis: calculation CALC-02; calculation CALC-11; calculation CALC-43

Covenant levels, ratings and executed debt documents are not supplied, so covenant headroom and the springing maturity terms are not established.

### Leverage and coverage

Net debt to EBITDA was 4.05x at FY2025, the highest of the five balance sheet dates examined, and has since fallen to 3.05x, with total debt to EBITDA falling from 4.39x to 3.36x on the same calculated EBITDA. Both legs of the ratio moved: net debt at 2026-09-30 is 15.66% below year-end, and last-twelve-months EBITDA is 12.05% above FY2025. The FY2025 peak came from the debt-funded Brigit acquisition lifting net debt 16.06%, while EBITDA fell 12.76% as other gains and charges rose 102.93% to 4.52% of revenue. For creditors the path matters as much as the level: the March and June 2026 readings of 3.56x and 3.46x show a steady paydown rather than a single-quarter move.
Basis: calculation CALC-35; calculation CALC-41; calculation CALC-42; 10-K_2025-12-31_upbd-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 121–122; 10-K_2025-12-31_upbd-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 123–124; 10-Q_2026-09-30_upbd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3

The deleveraging came from cash, not from earnings. Operating cash flow for nine months of 2026 was USD 457.1 million against USD 264.0 million a year earlier, up 73.15%, while the cash outflow for rental merchandise fell 12.14% and a tax refund of USD 35.1 million arrived. The lower merchandise purchases follow a 9.3% fall in Acima gross merchandise volume, so part of the cash release reflects buying less merchandise for a shrinking Acima book, and it reverses if volume recovers. Free cash flow was 108.18% of last-twelve-months EBITDA against 66.73% in FY2025 and 11.82% in FY2024, which is not a run rate; after Brigit customer cash advances it is 88.33%.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_upbd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_upbd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 56; calculation CALC-40; calculation CALC-38; calculation CALC-39; calculation CALC-51; calculation CALC-52

EBITDA covered cash interest 3.40x in FY2025 against 3.78x in FY2024, because EBITDA fell 12.76% while cash interest paid fell only 2.97%. Cash interest paid is printed only in the annual reports, so interim cover is not calculable; on accrued interest expense, last-twelve-months cover is 3.80x against 3.17x in FY2025, and cover after capital expenditure was 2.76x in FY2025 against 3.26x in FY2024. With 66.94% of principal at floating rates and cover below 4x, creditors are exposed to rate moves: the filing puts a one percentage point change at USD 9.1 million of annual pre-tax interest, and the term loan rate had risen to 6.83% by 2026-10-22 from 6.48% at 2026-09-30.

### Cash flow quality and liquidity

In nine months of 2026 operating cash flow of USD 457.1 million funded dividends of USD 69.5 million, capital expenditure of USD 47.6 million, customer cash advances net of collections of USD 69.0 million and the first USD 37.5 million Brigit deferred installment, and still left room to repay debt: repayments of USD 485.6 million exceeded new borrowing of USD 260.0 million. Customer advances are an investing outflow that behaves like Brigit working capital, so free cash flow after them is the better measure of what is available to creditors.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_upbd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_upbd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 55; calculation CALC-51

Liquidity at 2026-09-30 was cash of USD 125.1 million plus ABL availability of USD 451.2 million after USD 53.8 million of letters of credit, 0.43x of total debt against 0.23x at FY2025. By 2026-10-22 cash was USD 73.5 million and availability USD 456.2 million. Calls on that liquidity are the dividend paid 2026-10-27, the remaining USD 37.5 million of Brigit deferred consideration due no later than 30 days after the second anniversary of the 2025-01-31 closing, term loan installments of USD 8.75 million a year and estimated legal accruals of USD 57.9 million that include a non-binding agreement in principle with the multistate attorneys general. Availability is the filing's figure: the borrowing base is not printed, and use of the full amount depends on a fixed charge coverage maintenance covenant whose level is not stated. No facility expires within twelve months of 2026-09-30; the nearest maturity is the notes on 2029-02-15.

### Debt structure and claims outside debt

Debt at 2026-09-30 is three instruments with USD 1,361.25 million of principal: the ABL with USD 45.0 million drawn of USD 550 million of commitments, due 2029-06-07; the term loan with USD 866.25 million, due 2032-08-19; and USD 450 million of 6.375% senior notes due 2029-02-15. Nothing is printed as maturing before 2029, so the wall is USD 495 million in 2029 (the notes and the ABL drawings) and USD 866.25 million in 2032, but the term loan's 1.00% annual amortization of USD 8.75 million is stated in the notes and absent from the maturity table. Springing maturity provisions apply to both bank facilities and their terms are not supplied, so the 2029 and 2032 dates are contractual, not tested.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_upbd-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19–22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_upbd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 62; calculation CALC-46; calculation CALC-47; Source readiness (CP-0)

The ABL and the term loan are secured on separate collateral pools, each first-priority on its own pool, so both carry rank 1 here and holders of the notes sit behind USD 911.25 million of secured principal, 2.27x of EBITDA. The notes are unsecured, effectively subordinated to that debt and structurally subordinated to subsidiaries that do not guarantee them; cumulative leverage through the notes is 3.40x on principal against 3.36x on carrying value. The borrower named in the 2021 credit agreements is Rent-A-Center, Inc., the indenture is listed between Radiant Funding SPV, LLC and Truist Bank, and no guarantor is named, so the obligor at each rank is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_upbd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_upbd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-K_2025-12-31_upbd-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 179–180; calculation CALC-49; calculation CALC-19

A search of all five filings finds no pensions, retiree medical, factoring, supply-chain finance, securitization or purchase commitments, and the 2025 annual report states there are no financing leases.

The definition with the most effect is EBITDA: nine-month depreciation of rental merchandise is 3.27x nine-month EBITDA and is treated as the operating cost of the rental book, while the depreciation and amortization that sits inside other gains and charges is added back through the cash flow statement.
Basis: calculation CALC-56; calculation CALC-55; calculation CALC-44; 10-K_2025-12-31_upbd-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 121–124

The 2026 first-quarter report corrects the 2025 cash flow classification by USD 10.2 million, and the original 2025 report is not supplied. The maturity tables and the amortization text disagree on the term loan.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a durable improvement is that the cash behind it was largely a working-capital release: free cash flow of 108.18% of last-twelve-months EBITDA, a tax refund of USD 35.1 million and a 9.3% fall in Acima volume that cut merchandise purchases. If Acima volume recovers, merchandise purchases rise and cash conversion falls back toward the 66.73% of FY2025. A second point against is that legal exposure is not closed: a settlement above the USD 57.9 million accrued would be cash outside debt, since the multistate agreement in principle is not binding.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_upbd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_upbd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_upbd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 56; calculation CALC-38; calculation CALC-52; calculation CALC-54

In favour of the view, EBITDA itself rose: Q3 2026 EBITDA was 22.77% above Q3 2025, and on the analyst normalisation net leverage is 2.49x, so the improvement does not rest on cash flow alone. The view would weaken if net leverage rose back above 3.56x, the March 2026 level, or if ABL availability fell below its FY2025 level without a matching rise in cash; it would strengthen if the Acima volume decline stopped while free cash flow stayed above FY2025's 66.73% of EBITDA.
