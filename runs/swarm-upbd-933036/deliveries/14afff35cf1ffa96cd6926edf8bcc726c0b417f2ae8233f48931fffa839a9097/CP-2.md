## Findings

### Bottom line

Upbound's fundamental credit is adequate and improving, with moderate calculated leverage and no bullet maturity before 2029, but it leans on a shrinking Acima channel and unresolved legal settlements, so creditors should treat the deleveraging as unproven.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an adequate and improving view is that the improvement is largely cash timing: free cash flow of 108.18% of EBITDA includes a tax refund and a working-capital release that reverse when merchandise purchases normalise, LTM EBITDA margin of 8.48% is still below the 9.49% of FY2024, and the 2029 wall of USD 495 million meets springing maturity terms that are not supplied. If those terms bring bank maturities forward, the refinancing event arrives earlier than the printed dates.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation CALC-06; calculation CALC-04; calculation CALC-01

The case for a stronger view is that EBITDA itself recovered: third-quarter EBITDA was 22.77% above the prior year, interest expense is 6.65% lower than in FY2025, and adding back other gains and charges gives net leverage of 2.49x, for which a pro forma figure after the dividend is not established. The view would turn clearly positive if Acima volume stabilised and the legal matters settled within the accrual; it would turn negative if net leverage rose above 3.56x, ABL availability fell without a matching rise in cash, or the dividend continued while earnings fell below it.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation CALC-01; calculation CALC-05; calculation CALC-07

### Company description and ownership

Upbound leases durable goods to consumers who do not qualify for traditional credit, through Acima retailer locations and Rent-A-Center stores, and adds earned wage access and subscriptions through Brigit. Customers pay weekly to monthly with no long-term obligation and mostly take ownership through early purchase options, so revenue visibility is short and depends on renewals and charge-offs rather than on contracts. That gives quick cash conversion but leaves creditors exposed to consumer payment behaviour and to the pace of merchandise purchases.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_upbd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_upbd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10

Ownership is listed common stock with no controlling holder identified, and the board sets dividends within limits in the debt agreements. A third party reaching 40% of the votes is an event of default under the bank facilities, so control change is protected, but sponsor support cannot be assumed and ownership concentration is not established because the proxy statement is not supplied. Business & governance assesses sponsor and governance risk as Medium on incomplete evidence.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_upbd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 81; 10-K_2025-12-31_upbd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 89; 10-K_2025-12-31_upbd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 178

### Revenue durability, margin resilience and cash conversion

Revenue is recurring in form but discretionary and volume-sensitive. Acima volume fell 9.3% in nine months and revenue 2.37%, Rent-A-Center revenue is flat as stores close, and Brigit grew from a small base, so the durable base is Rent-A-Center rentals while Acima carries the growth and the risk. Three suppliers account for 58% of merchandise purchases with no minimum-purchase contracts, which adds a supply dependency without contractual protection.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation CALC-05; 10-K_2025-12-31_upbd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 18

Margins have been defended by loss control rather than by price. LTM EBITDA margin is 8.48% against 9.49% in FY2024 and 7.62% in FY2025; Acima margin rose to 12.27% because fewer customers used early purchase options and charge-offs eased, while Rent-A-Center charge-offs rose. Cost flexibility is real in merchandise purchases, which fell 12.14% as a cash outflow, but occupancy and labor are largely fixed, so a volume fall deleverages operating profit quickly.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation CALC-06; calculation CALC-05; 10-Q_2026-09-30_upbd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-Q_2026-09-30_upbd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 53

Capital expenditure is light at 1.42% of FY2025 revenue, so conversion depends on rental merchandise purchases inside operating cash flow. Free cash flow was 11.82% of EBITDA in FY2024, 66.73% in FY2025 and 108.18% on a last-twelve-months basis, which shows volatility more than a level, and cash taxes and the excess cash flow sweep on the term loan from fiscal 2026 will reduce what is retained. Creditors should therefore size debt service on EBITDA and interest cover, not on the current free cash flow.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation CALC-06; calculation CALC-03; 10-K_2025-12-31_upbd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 155

### Competitive forces and external factors

Rivalry is high: Upbound competes with other lease-to-own operators, retailers, buy-now-pay-later providers and subprime lenders, and Brigit competes with other earned wage access and credit-building providers, which caps pricing and rewards loss discipline. Buyers, the Acima retailers, hold bargaining power that cannot be sized because retailer concentration is not printed, suppliers are concentrated at 58% of purchases for three names, and new entrants and substitutes arrive through fintech and online channels. The credit consequence is that margin gains must come from loss control and volume, not price.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_upbd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-K_2025-12-31_upbd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 18; calculation CALC-05

Regulation is a primary credit driver and is treated as the material external factor: state rental purchase statutes, consumer-finance enforcement against Acima, and evolving state rules on earned wage access directly set legal cash costs and the terms on which Acima and Brigit can operate. Economic conditions matter through consumer payment behaviour and short-term rates, since 66.94% of principal floats; social and technology factors are not primary credit drivers on the supplied evidence.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_upbd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-K_2025-12-31_upbd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_upbd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; calculation CALC-02

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months the tailwinds are lower interest expense after repayment of the ABL, lower other gains and charges, store consolidation and Brigit growth, and the headwinds are Acima volume, the excess cash flow sweep, Brigit deferred consideration of USD 37.5 million due by 2027-Q1, legal settlements and a term loan rate that reached 6.83% by 2026-10-22. On the supplied evidence these roughly offset, so leverage is more likely to hold near current levels than to fall much further unless Acima volume stabilises.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation CALC-01; calculation CALC-02; calculation CALC-03; 10-Q_2026-09-30_upbd-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 59

Over three to five years the issue is the 2029 refinancing: USD 495 million of notes and ABL drawings must be refinanced in 2028, against a term loan to 2032 whose springing terms are unseen. Sector headwinds are regulatory, with the federal regulator's December 2025 advisory opinion lowering federal risk for earned wage access while state action continues, and the lease-to-own model remains exposed to state and enforcement reclassification risk. Company tailwinds are Brigit scale and data-driven underwriting, which management describes but which the filings do not yet quantify.
Basis: calculation CALC-04; 10-K_2025-12-31_upbd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-K_2025-12-31_upbd-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9

### Downside scenario

The issuer-specific downside begins with Acima volume continuing to fall at the third-quarter rate of 11.0% as retailer traffic and consumer payment behaviour weaken. The first operational break is lower Acima revenue and fewer early purchase options, which cut gross profit while store and occupancy costs stay fixed; charge-offs then rise, as they already have at Rent-A-Center. Replaying the FY2025 EBITDA decline of 12.76% lifts net leverage to 3.50x (3.56x pro forma for the dividend) and total debt to EBITDA to 3.85x, and interest cover falls below the 3.40x of FY2025.
Basis: calculation CALC-08; calculation CALC-05; calculation CALC-02

Liquidity absorbs the shock: availability of USD 451.2 million exceeds identified twelve-month calls of USD 195.10 million, and management levers include cutting the dividend, which runs at USD 90.95 million a year, reducing merchandise purchases and closing stores. An unplanned settlement of USD 100 million adds 0.25x to leverage and would be met from availability. The binding constraint is not liquidity but refinancing in 2028 at higher leverage, with an unseen covenant and springing provisions, so resilience looks sufficient to avoid liquidity distress inside two years but not to guarantee refinancing on current terms.
Basis: calculation CALC-08; calculation CALC-03; calculation CALC-04

### Overall credit view

Overall, Upbound presents an adequate business profile supported by two scaled lease-to-own platforms, a growing fintech segment and no sponsor, but credit quality is constrained by a shrinking Acima channel, legal exposure and thin interest cover. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because EBITDA recovered and capital intensity is low, but current free cash flow is flattered by working capital. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on market access in 2028 and on springing maturity terms that are not supplied. The primary downside path is Acima volume decline combined with settlement cash above the accrual. Further analysis would require ratings, covenant levels, the executed debt documents, the proxy statement and management projections. Fundamental credit completed with limitations. Top risk: Acima volume decline compounding legal settlement cost.
