## Findings

### Bottom line

Moog presents an adequate and improving credit profile: a diversified aerospace, defense and industrial book with low leverage and no large maturity before 2031 supports creditors, but free cash flow that leans on customer advances and an unremediated reporting-control weakness keep conviction moderate.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against a cautious reading is breadth and trajectory. All four segments grew in the third quarter, the twelve-month backlog rose 23%, leverage fell on every measure including 1.42x without the recovery, shareholder returns were covered at 0.60x of free cash flow in the first nine months of fiscal 2026, and management raised its fiscal 2026 guidance to net sales of USD 4.4 billion, an adjusted operating margin of 14.1% and free cash flow conversion of 70% of adjusted net earnings. That guidance implies fourth-quarter sales growth of 7.82%, about half the third-quarter pace, so even management expects growth to slow, and the guidance includes the tariff refund and a research tax credit. The view would improve if the control weakness is closed, full-year free cash flow conversion reaches the guided 70% with contract advances holding, and buybacks stay below free cash flow. It would weaken if the twelve-month backlog falls below USD 3,250 million, contract advances reverse while inventories and unbilled receivables keep rising, or distributions again exceed free cash flow.

### Limitations

- Management guidance is the only forward view supplied and it is rounded, so the outlook conclusion could shift on rounding alone: implied fourth-quarter sales growth runs from 3.05% to 12.58% across the rounding of USD 4.4 billion, and the free cash flow guidance is stated on adjusted net earnings, a different base from the EBITDA conversion used here.
- Covenant headroom is not calculated because the loan agreement adds non-cash and synergy items to EBITDA that the statements do not show; the conclusion that leverage headroom is wide rests on the company's statement that it complied with every covenant at June 27, 2026 and could narrow once the compliance figures are seen.
- Segment profit lines include a share of the tariff recovery that the filings do not split by segment, so the conclusion that Industrial and Military Aircraft margins improved most may be partly the recovery rather than business performance.
- The filings show that 70.43% of the opening advance balance was recognised as revenue within nine months but not how much of the June 27, 2026 balance relates to programs that deliver within twelve months, so the timing of any reversal is a range and the downside path is a sequence of events, not a dated forecast.
- No peer, market or ratings data is supplied, so the comparison with typical leveraged-credit issuers rests on leverage and margin levels alone and could change once peers are measured.

### Company description and ownership

Moog designs, manufactures and integrates precision motion and fluid controls for aerospace, defense and industrial customers in four segments, and sells to original equipment manufacturers and end users, with spares, repair and overhaul support at 14% of fiscal 2025 sales. The business model supports debt service because most revenue is recognised over time on long contracts and is partly funded by customer advances, and because the installed base of flight control systems creates aftermarket demand; it weakens liquidity when a customer delays deliveries, because revenue recognition, billing and advance receipts all slip together while costs continue. Moog is a listed company with no sponsor or parent, Class A and Class B shares carry different votes, trustees of company-funded trusts vote 29.47% of the Class B shares, which is 18.67% of all votes on a calculated basis, and acquisitions have been small, with COTSWORKS costing 1.59% of fiscal 2025 net sales. For creditors this means financial policy is set by management and by a board elected through the share structure, with a dividend of USD 0.30 per share declared on July 30, 2026 and buybacks at management's discretion.

### Revenue durability

Revenue is predictable over the next twelve months and cyclical beyond that. The twelve-month backlog equals 75.27% of last-twelve-months net sales, roughly nine months of sales, and the quarterly report attributes the 23% rise to higher narrowbody and widebody orders, higher military aircraft orders, orders across the whole Space and Defense portfolio and demand for data center cooling pumps in Industrial. Over the cycle the exposure is concentrated and regulated: U.S. Government contracts were 37.85% of fiscal 2025 sales and can be terminated, Boeing was 10.28%, and the 2025 annual report says Boeing and Airbus build-rate changes move demand for Moog's flight control systems. Pricing power is limited by the same facts: management's strategy is pricing and simplification, but the risk factors say Moog may be unable to raise prices as fast as supplier prices, and a large customer evaluates the cost of its supplier base. For creditors the implication is stable cash flow while backlog converts and a sharp, correlated fall if build rates or defense funding turn, because the commercial and government exposures do not offset each other in a build-rate pause.

### Margin resilience

Margins can be defended against moderate volume pressure but their resilience in a downturn is unproven, because fiscal 2023 to fiscal 2026 was an upcycle. Fixed-price contracts, 90% of over-time cost-to-cost sales, leave cost overruns with Moog, supplier price inflation and tariffs on steel and aluminum press margins, and Moog says it may be unable to pass them on. In fiscal 2025 gross margin fell 0.66 percentage points while EBITDA margin rose 0.68 percentage points, because research and development fell from 3.12% to 2.43% of sales and impairment, restructuring and other charges fell from 1.73% to 0.65% of sales, not because gross profit improved. In the third quarter of 2026 research and development rose 50.83% for Space and Defense growth programs, which is an investment that lowers cash flow before it lifts sales, and selling, general and administrative cost fell as a share of sales on higher volume, as the company states. For creditors the margin is a support while volume grows and an amplifier on the way down.

### Market frameworks

Competitive rivalry is considerable, with tier one and tier two suppliers and vertically integrated primes, and the annual report notes that suppliers have consolidated to secure long-term sole-source positions that may withstand downturns better than Moog; for creditors this limits pricing power and margin recovery in a slowdown. Buyer power is high because Boeing and the U.S. Government are large buyers that review their supplier base and can curtail programs, which caps the margin on the 37.85% of sales in government contracts. Supplier power matters because some inputs are sole-sourced and prices have risen, which pressures margins when fixed-price contracts prevent pass-through. The filings do not describe entry barriers or substitutes, so those two forces are not established. Political and economic factors are the material external drivers: the February 20, 2026 ruling that tariffs under the emergency powers act were invalid produced a USD 30 million recovery of previously paid tariffs, which is a catch-up rather than a run-rate item, further tariffs on steel and aluminum could raise costs, and defense appropriations and aircraft build rates set demand. Technology is a modest upside through data center cooling pumps in Industrial. In credit terms the strengths are liquidity, backlog and segment balance, the weaknesses are advance-dependent cash flow, control quality and fixed-price exposure, the opportunities are data center cooling and Space and Defense capture, and the threats are a build-rate pause, defense funding delay, tariffs and supplier inflation.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months management guides fiscal 2026 net sales of USD 4.4 billion, an adjusted operating margin of 14.1% and free cash flow conversion of 70%, all raised in July 2026, and the backlog supports the sales number. ### Qualitative downside scenario

The issuer-specific downside begins with a Boeing production pause or a defense appropriation delay, which the annual report names as risks, and the first operational break is Commercial Aircraft and Military Aircraft deliveries slipping while Moog keeps building. Because 90% of over-time sales are fixed-price, a slip raises cost to complete and cuts margin, and because EBITDA moved 1.94 times as fast as sales in the third quarter, earnings would fall by more than sales. Cash flow then falls twice: customer advances stop growing and begin to unwind, and because 70.43% of the opening balance was recognised as revenue within nine months the float would run down within roughly a year, while inventories and unbilled receivables build and dividends and buybacks continue. If only the nine-month advance inflow reversed and EBITDA stayed unchanged, net debt to EBITDA would be 1.53x, or 1.61x without the tariff recovery, and even a full reversal of the advance balance would take it to 2.14x, the level of fiscal 2023, so the effect on leverage is a return to earlier levels, not a break. Liquidity absorbs it, because unused capacity of USD 952 million exceeds the whole advance balance and no maturity falls before 2028; management can cut buybacks, hold capital expenditure at 3.14% of sales or lower and slow the dividend. Resilience therefore looks sufficient to avoid liquidity distress in this scenario, and default risk is not supported by the evidence. The observable triggers are contract advances falling, revolving loan drawings rising above USD 164 million and backlog falling below USD 3,250 million.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Moog presents an adequate business profile supported by a USD 3,250 million twelve-month backlog, balanced segments, low reported leverage and long-dated maturities, but credit quality is constrained by free cash flow that leans on customer advances, an unremediated control weakness and shareholder returns that have exceeded free cash flow. Cash-flow durability appears adequate but unproven, because conversion rose to 26.28% in fiscal 2025 and 56.44% over the last twelve months while customer advances supplied 71.14% of nine-month free cash flow. ### Strengths and weaknesses

Five strengths support the credit, in order of weight. First, unused capacity of USD 952 million and no sizeable bank or bond maturity before 2031 mean a slowdown cannot force a refinancing, which is the main protection of the debt. Second, the twelve-month backlog of USD 3,250 million equals 75.27% of last-twelve-months net sales, which protects the next year of revenue and operating cash flow. Third, no segment exceeds 28.83% of net sales, government revenue follows appropriations and aftermarket revenue follows fleet usage rather than new-aircraft build rates, so the book is not tied to a single build rate. Fourth, net debt to EBITDA of 1.36x and cash interest cover of 6.88x leave room for an earnings fall before debt service is stressed. Fifth, gross margin without the recovery rose to 28.41% from 27.52%, which shows the margin gain is not only a one-off.

Five weaknesses constrain it, in order of weight. First, free cash flow depends on customer advances and the final quarter: advances supplied 71.14% of nine-month free cash flow and equal 53.59% of total debt, so a reversal would raise net debt by the amount of the float. Second, an adverse opinion on internal control, restated history and cost-to-complete accounting on 64.28% of sales mean every ratio carries estimation risk. Third, customer concentration and fixed-price terms, with the U.S. Government at 37.85% and Boeing at 10.28% of sales, fixed-price terms on 90% of over-time cost-to-cost sales and EBITDA growth of 1.94 times sales growth, mean a funding cut, build-rate cut or overrun would cut earnings by more than sales. Fourth, shareholder returns of 1.57x and 1.40x free cash flow in fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2025 under trust-influenced dual-class control show that distributions can outrun cash flow. Fifth, finance leases and receivables sold lift leverage to 1.83x, and total claims including finance leases were little changed over nine months, so the fall in reported leverage came from earnings, not repayment.
