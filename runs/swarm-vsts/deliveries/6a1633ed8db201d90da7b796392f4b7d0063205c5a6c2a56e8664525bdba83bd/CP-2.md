## Findings

### Bottom line

Vestis is an adequate but fragile credit: recurring route revenue and recovering cash flow support debt service, but 4.62x leverage, thin margins and falling revenue leave little room if cost savings or the receivables facility renewal disappoint.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation L1; calculation L6; calculation F1

Deleveraging is real but recent: reported net debt to EBITDA fell from 5.89x at the fiscal 2025 year end to 4.62x at July 3, 2026, and free cash flow reached 61.27% of reported EBITDA over twelve months. Two dates matter more than the trend: the covenant maximum falls to 4.75x for the quarter ending October 2, 2026, and the USD 250 million receivables facility ends on August 2, 2027.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 37; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation L1; calculation L2; calculation F1

### Key findings

Leverage has fallen but is still the main constraint. Reported net debt was 4.62x EBITDA at July 3, 2026 against 5.89x at the fiscal 2025 year end and a 6.24x peak at January 2, 2026, and cash interest cover was 2.63x, with 38.00% of EBITDA paid as cash interest. The issuer's own ratio is 4.10x, on a wider debt definition and a higher EBITDA, while counting finance leases and receivables sold gives 6.29x reported EBITDA and 4.82x adjusted EBITDA, so the improvement is real but the base is thin.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-08-11_vstsq3fy26earningsrelease-.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 37; calculation L1; calculation L2; calculation L3; calculation L6; calculation L11; calculation L14; calculation L16; calculation L17

Cash conversion has improved sharply, but part of the improvement will not repeat. Free cash flow was 61.27% of reported EBITDA over the last twelve months against 3.02% in fiscal 2025, helped by working capital that released cash and by a larger sale of receivables, and fiscal 2024 was inflated by the one-time inflow when the receivables facility started. After finance lease principal, free cash flow was 44.61% of reported EBITDA over twelve months and negative in fiscal 2025, which shows how much lease financing flatters the headline figure.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 73, 91; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 50; calculation F1; calculation F2; calculation F4; calculation F5; calculation F6; calculation F7

Revenue durability is the weakest operating dimension. Revenue fell 2.53% in fiscal 2025, or 4.37% without the extra week, and 1.90% in the nine months to July 3, 2026, mainly because lost business exceeded new business, and net volumes fell 4.5% in the third quarter, partly by choice under the cost plan. Recurring rental is 95% of revenue and the largest customers are small, so the risk is steady account loss, not one concentrated customer.
Basis: 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 11, 68; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 44; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation R4; calculation R5; calculation R6; calculation R13

Margins are thin but recovering through cost action. Reported EBITDA margin was 10.67% in fiscal 2024, 6.98% in fiscal 2025 and 8.26% over the last twelve months, and the United States segment margin was 9.24% in the third quarter of 2026 against 6.63% a year earlier. Part of the recovery is the absence of the fiscal 2025 bad debt and severance charges, so the margin gain is not all new.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 68; calculation R1; calculation R2; calculation R3; calculation R8; calculation R9

Liquidity is ample and no term debt matures before fiscal 2028, but one refinancing comes earlier. Liquidity rose 18.09% from the fiscal 2025 year end, the revolver is undrawn, and Term Loan A-2 and the revolver mature in September 2028, but the receivables facility ends on August 2, 2027 and funds 19.34% of principal debt in sold receivables. Funding that balance on the revolver would cut liquidity by 60.34% and lift net debt to 5.57x reported EBITDA.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 37; calculation Q2; calculation Q3; calculation Q6; calculation Q7; calculation Q8

The covenant path tightens just as the distribution lock can lift. The maximum net leverage falls from 5.00x to 4.75x for the quarter ending October 2, 2026 and to 4.50x afterwards, against an issuer ratio of 4.10x, and the lock on dividends and share repurchases ends on the earlier of a compliant quarter after October 2, 2026 or two quarters at 4.50x or lower. Covenant headroom is not calculated here because the credit agreement is not supplied; Legal & covenants and Covenant capacity own that test.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 59; EX-99.1_2026-08-11_vstsq3fy26earningsrelease-.htm.jsonl, page 16; calculation L11

Financial policy is creditor-protective for now. No sponsor or controlling holder is identified, dividends paid in fiscal 2025 were 239.21% of free cash flow before the lock, and none has been paid since, but executive turnover, shareholder litigation with no loss estimate and a plan whose cost estimate rose 36.36% add execution risk around the covenant step-downs.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F11; calculation P1; calculation P2; calculation P3

The primary downside path is continued customer loss with delayed savings, followed by the covenant step-down and the 2027 receivables renewal arriving together. A 1% rise in interest rates on the floating-rate term loans would cut cash interest cover from 2.63x to 2.33x on unchanged EBITDA, and no interest rate hedge is described in the sources read.
Basis: 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 43, 83; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation S1; calculation S2

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a stronger credit is the earnings and cash trend: adjusted EBITDA is 30.46% above reported EBITDA over the last twelve months, the issuer reports 4.10x, its fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of USD 310.0 million to USD 315.0 million is 7.60% above the last twelve months at its midpoint, and it raised its free cash flow guidance to USD 160.0 million to USD 170.0 million. It would win if fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA reaches the implied USD 84.0 million to USD 89.0 million, revenue stabilizes once the targeted volume exits end, and the receivables facility is renewed, because leverage would keep falling through the covenant step-downs.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-08-11_vstsq3fy26earningsrelease-.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 16; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation L11; calculation L13; calculation G1; calculation G2; calculation G3

The strongest case for a weaker credit is that the recovery leans on working capital, add-backs and lease financing. Adjusted EBITDA includes transformation, severance and securitization add-backs, finance leases and sold receivables sit outside the issuer's net debt, and revenue is still falling. It would win if revenue keeps declining after the exits end, the plan estimate rises again, or the receivables facility is renewed only on worse terms, because cover near 2.00x and a 4.50x maximum would then be tested together.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 44, 50; calculation L13; calculation L16; calculation L17; calculation F4; calculation R6

### Strengths and weaknesses

The main credit strengths are undrawn liquidity with no term maturity before fiscal 2028, which covers earnings swings and the cost plan; a free cash flow recovery that repays floating-rate term loans, with net debt down 8.61% since the fiscal 2025 year end; scale and recurring weekly rental revenue with low customer concentration, which supports debt service through a slow decline; and the absence of a sponsor together with a lender-imposed distribution lock, which keeps cash in the company for now.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 59; calculation Q1; calculation F8; calculation L10; calculation E1; calculation F11

The main credit weaknesses are leverage and cover that are thin against falling covenant maximums; revenue erosion from lost customers that shrinks the EBITDA base; a receivables facility that ends within twelve months of the as-of date and must be renewed; a transformation plan that costs more than first stated with unproven savings; and claims outside term debt, namely finance leases, pensions and open litigation, that rank with operating costs and reduce free cash flow.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 37, 42; calculation L1; calculation L6; calculation R4; calculation Q3; calculation P1; calculation F4

### Company description

Vestis rents and services uniforms and sells workplace supplies, including restroom and first-aid products, mats, towels and linens, to a very large number of small and large customers in the United States and Canada, with Canada 8.98% of revenue. Weekly route service under multi-year contracts with exit costs gives cash flow visibility, but the business separated from Aramark only in September 2023, so it has a short record as a stand-alone borrower and its historic statements for fiscal 2023 are on a combined basis.
Basis: 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 10, 11, 12, 145; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B3

### Ownership and group structure

Ownership is dispersed: the shares were distributed to Aramark stockholders, no sponsor or controlling holder is identified, and a letter agreement with Corvex Management LP and a director is known only by its title. Creditors rely on guarantees and liens from wholly owned domestic material subsidiaries, a bankruptcy-remote entity holds the sold receivables, and whether the Canadian business guarantees the debt is not established because the subsidiary list is not supplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 72, 154; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 37

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is recurring and weakly cyclical but is being lost account by account. Uniforms revenue fell 7.57% in fiscal 2025 while workplace supplies rose 0.89%, rental revenue fell mainly because lost business exceeded new business, and direct sales fell with the loss of a national account. Net volumes fell 4.5% in the third quarter of 2026 and pricing offset part of it, but pricing power is contested: a shareholder class action alleges misleading statements about pricing practices, so price-led recovery carries legal and retention risk.
Basis: 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 68; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 28, 44; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation R12; calculation R13; calculation R15

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Cost of services is 73.50% of revenue and selling, general and administrative costs 18.92%, with routes, plants and a largely unionized workforce that make costs quasi-fixed, so a small volume loss costs margin. About 59.23% of employees are union-represented, tariffs and a single-supplier input expose unit cost, and pass-through to customers is limited by contract terms and by retention risk. Margins can be defended only by the plan savings, which the issuer claims are partly realized.
Basis: 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 15, 17, 89; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 4; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation R16; calculation R17; calculation B1; calculation P4

### Capital intensity and cash flow conversion

The business is capital intensive in fleet, plants and rental merchandise, and reported capital spending was cut 25.91% in fiscal 2025 while new vehicles were financed through finance leases. Cash conversion is therefore best read after lease principal: free cash flow was 44.61% of reported EBITDA over twelve months on that basis, against negative cash flow in fiscal 2025, and working capital released cash in 2026, which will not repeat at the same pace.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 91; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 17, 50; EX-99.1_2026-08-11_vstsq3fy26earningsrelease-.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation R14; calculation F4; calculation F5; calculation F6

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense and local: the industry is fragmented, two competitors of size are named, and many customers could run the service in-house. Buyer power is moderate because customers sign multi-year contracts with exit costs but can challenge pricing and contracts, supplier power is concentrated in single-supplier inputs and two plants in Mexico that make about 60% of uniforms and linens, and entry is limited by route density and scale. In-house operation is the main substitute. The result is price pressure that caps margin recovery and increases the importance of retention.
Basis: 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 11–14; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Macro and policy factors

Three outside factors change credit capacity: tariffs and trade policy on Mexican-made garments, wage and benefit inflation under union contracts and multiemployer plans, and interest rates on floating-rate term loans, where each 1% rise adds about USD 11.4 million a year on the annual report's principal and cuts cover to 2.33x on current figures. Other political and social factors are not a primary credit driver in the documents.
Basis: 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 83; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation S1; calculation S2

### Strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats

The strengths and weaknesses are those set out above. The opportunities are cross-selling, because customers take about 30% to 40% of the full product line, and the cost plan, which together could lift margin without revenue growth. The threats are further customer loss, tariffs, wage settlements, a ruling in the shareholder litigation, and the distribution lock lifting before leverage reaches 4.50x.
Basis: 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 29; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale and diversification are strong, competitive advantage and cost and capex flexibility are average, margin stability and free cash flow stability are weak, liquidity is strong, and refinancing access and financial policy are average. The trend is improving since January 2, 2026, with reported net debt to EBITDA falling from 6.24x to 4.62x, but the constraint is thin cover and the covenant step-downs, and the missing datapoints are the credit agreement, fiscal fourth-quarter results and any peer or market reference.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation L1; calculation L3; calculation L6

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months the issuer guides to fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA of USD 310.0 million to USD 315.0 million and free cash flow of USD 160.0 million to USD 170.0 million, with revenue flat to down 2% on a normalized basis. Tailwinds are the plan savings and continued repayment, and headwinds are volume losses, plan costs, the step-down to 4.50x, the lock lifting, and the receivables renewal in August 2027. Beyond fiscal 2027, when the plan is expected to be substantially complete, the documents give no outlook; Term Loan A-2 and the revolver mature in September 2028 and Term Loan B-1 in February 2031, and the sector tailwind of outsourcing is stated only in general terms.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-08-11_vstsq3fy26earningsrelease-.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2025-10-03_vsts-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 67; 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 42; calculation G1

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is account losses that continue after the targeted volume exits end, with plan savings arriving late. The first operational break is revenue falling faster than costs, so reported EBITDA stalls near its last twelve months level and cash interest, which takes 38.00% of EBITDA, stops falling; free cash flow after finance lease principal, 44.61% of EBITDA, would shrink toward the negative level of fiscal 2025. The financial transmission is leverage that stays near 4.62x while the covenant maximum falls to 4.75x and then 4.50x, with each 1% rise in rates cutting cover to 2.33x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 44; calculation L1; calculation L14; calculation F4; calculation F5; calculation S2

The liquidity consequence arrives in August 2027: if the receivables facility were not renewed and the sold balance had to be funded on the revolver at July 3, 2026 levels, liquidity would fall by 60.34% and net debt to reported EBITDA would be 5.57x, an illustration that assumes unchanged balances and available revolver capacity. Management levers are capital spending, which was cut 25.91% in fiscal 2025, the distribution lock, asset sales and cost action, so resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress through fiscal 2027 if the facility is renewed. The observable triggers are listed in the monitoring register.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 37; calculation Q5; calculation Q6; calculation Q7; calculation Q8; calculation R14

### Overall credit view

Overall, Vestis presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, recurring weekly route revenue, low customer concentration and ample undrawn liquidity, but credit quality is constrained by 4.62x leverage, thin cover, falling revenue and claims outside term debt. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow has recovered, but part of the recovery is working capital and lease financing. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on renewal of the receivables facility before August 2027 and on leverage staying below the falling covenant maximums. The primary downside path is customer loss with delayed savings. Further analysis would require the credit agreement, fiscal fourth-quarter results and a peer or market reference. The top risk is continued revenue erosion.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-07-03_vsts-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 37; calculation L1; calculation L6; calculation F4; calculation Q3
