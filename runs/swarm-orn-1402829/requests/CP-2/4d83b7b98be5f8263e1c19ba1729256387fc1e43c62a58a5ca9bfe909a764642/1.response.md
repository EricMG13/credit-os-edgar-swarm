## Findings

### Bottom line

Orion presents an adequate but thinly protected credit: a USD 645 million backlog supports revenue, but negative cash conversion, a debt step-up to 3.13x EBITDA and unproven covenant headroom leave repayment dependent on working capital reversing in the fourth quarter.

Cash and revolver availability of USD 33,671 thousand compare with a nine-month free cash outflow of USD 38,831 thousand, so liquidity is adequate only if the USD 53,122 thousand build in contract assets unwinds; guidance of USD 50 million to USD 54 million of adjusted EBITDA implies a fourth quarter close to the third. The company states covenant compliance, but headroom cannot be computed because the executed agreement and the reported ratios are not supplied.

### Key findings

Liquidity is adequate only on the assumption that working capital reverses. Unrestricted cash was USD 5,671 thousand and revolver availability about USD 28.0 million at 2026-09-30, together USD 33,671 thousand, which is 0.87x of the nine-month free cash outflow of USD 38,831 thousand, and the revolver is 48.33% drawn before letters of credit; management states that liquidity is adequate for at least the next twelve months.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_orn-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 14, 30; calculation CALC-01; calculation CALC-02; calculation CALC-03; calculation CALC-10

Cash conversion has broken down while margins are thin. Last-twelve-month operating cash flow was USD 3,084 thousand against EBITDA of USD 34,714 thousand, a conversion of 8.88% against 82.47% in FY2025, as contract assets rose to 9.08% of last-twelve-month revenue from 3.65% at the 2025 year-end; EBITDA was only 3.74% of last-twelve-month revenue, so a small cost overrun on fixed-price work removes a large share of earnings.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_orn-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 5; calculation CALC-04; calculation CALC-05; calculation CALC-06

Leverage has stepped up and the ratio flatters. Total debt was 3.13x last-twelve-month EBITDA and 2.97x net of cash at 2026-09-30, rising to 3.62x if finance lease liabilities are counted as debt, and cash interest cover of 4.10x predates most of the new borrowing; swaps fix the rate on an amount equal to the USD 40,000 thousand acquisition term loan, while the revolver and equipment loan float at a weighted average rate of 6.2%, 4.50 percentage points below the 2025 weighted rate on the facility it replaced.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_orn-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 14–16, 32; 10-K_2025-12-31_orn-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, page 88; calculation CALC-07; calculation CALC-08

Refinancing risk sits in liquidity, not in a maturity wall, but the wall is not fully visible. The bank facility matures in December 2030 and no bonds are outstanding, so repayment depends on the revolver and on cash generation rather than on market access; the term loan repayment schedule is not disclosed, the secured lenders hold substantially all assets, and the seller note and contingent payments add claims that are paid from the same cash.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_orn-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 14–16; 10-K_2025-12-31_orn-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, pages 87–89

Business risk is project execution on fixed-price work, increasingly in concrete. Most revenue is fixed-price and competitively bid, concrete carries 42.08% of nine-month revenue after growing 36.76%, and Navy-linked marine work is falling, so earnings now lean on private data-center and commercial demand; the USD 645 million backlog and positions on large Navy contract vehicles give visibility but not contracted margin.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_orn-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 10–12, 26–27; EX-99.1_2026-10-27_orn-ex99d1.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3

Governance and financial policy cannot be rated, and the observable record is mixed. No sponsor or controlling holder is stated, no dividend or repurchase is shown, and equity raised in 2024 repaid debt, but the 2026 acquisition was funded with debt and contingent payments of USD 16,267 thousand, and ownership, independence and compensation sit in a proxy statement that is not supplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_orn-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, pages 43, 60–61, 94; 10-Q_2026-09-30_orn-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 7–10, 13, 16

The primary downside path starts with a marine project overrun or an unapproved change order that stays in contract assets. Claims and unapproved change orders were USD 17.5 million in the balances, so a settlement below book value cuts earnings and holds billed cash back, availability of USD 28.0 million is drawn to cover payroll and equipment, interest rises on the floating part of the debt, and fixed charge coverage and senior leverage covenants of 1.20x and 3.00x then bind; the observable triggers are availability, contract assets and the next quarter's operating cash flow.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_orn-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 14–15; calculation CALC-01; calculation CALC-06

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against caution is guidance and order intake: full-year adjusted EBITDA is guided to USD 50 million to USD 54 million, which implies fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA of USD 15,473 thousand to USD 19,473 thousand against USD 17,917 thousand in the third quarter, and at that level the 2026-09-30 debt would be 2.01x to 2.18x adjusted EBITDA. Backlog of USD 645 million, third-quarter awards of USD 179 million and management's statement of adequate liquidity for twelve months support the same reading. The view would improve if the fourth quarter shows contract assets and receivables turning to cash and availability rising, and would worsen if the next report shows a further operating cash outflow, a guidance cut like the one in July, or a covenant amendment.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-27_orn-ex99d1.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 11; EX-99.1_2026-07-28_orn-20260728xex99d1.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_orn-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 30; calculation CALC-09; calculation CALC-11

### Limitations

- No credit rating, market price or peer data is supplied, so the view that leverage is elevated is not benchmarked and could be softer against the sector.
- The executed credit agreement is not supplied, so the conclusion that liquidity is adequate cannot be tested against covenant limits and any step-downs.
- Seasonality of working capital is not stated, so the expectation that contract assets unwind in the fourth quarter rests on guidance rather than on a pattern.
- The downside path is qualitative because the documents give no stress parameters, so no stressed leverage or liquidity figure is stated.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strength: order book and end-market demand. Backlog stood at USD 645 million and third-quarter awards were USD 179 million, concrete revenue grew 36.76% in nine months on data-center and commercial demand, and the company holds positions on two large Navy contract vehicles; this supports revenue visibility over 6 to 24 months, though not margin.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_orn-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 12, 27; EX-99.1_2026-10-27_orn-ex99d1.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; calculation CALC-12

Strength: cheaper and simpler funding after the refinancing. The 2025 refinancing replaced a facility that cost 10.70% with a bank facility at a weighted average rate of 6.2%, no bonds are outstanding, and last-twelve-month cash interest cover is 4.10x; lower interest eases debt service while the business rebuilds cash flow.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_orn-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, pages 87–88; 10-Q_2026-09-30_orn-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Strength: no evidence of cash leaving for owners. No dividend or repurchase appears and equity raised in 2024 repaid debt, so cash stays available to creditors.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_orn-20251231x10k.htm.jsonl, pages 43, 74, 94

Weakness: negative cash conversion. Nine-month operating cash flow was an outflow of USD 10,722 thousand and capital expenditure USD 28,109 thousand, so free cash flow was negative USD 38,831 thousand and the revolver funds the gap; repayment depends on working capital reversing.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_orn-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation CALC-02; calculation CALC-04

Weakness: leverage and contingent claims after a debt-funded acquisition. Total debt is 3.13x EBITDA, the seller note and contingent payments rank as further claims, and goodwill is large against equity; an execution miss lands on a more leveraged balance sheet.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_orn-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 7–10; calculation CALC-07

Weakness: thin fixed-price margins and concentration. EBITDA is 3.74% of last-twelve-month revenue, most work is fixed-price, and earnings depend on a few large projects, Texas-centred concrete and private demand.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_orn-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 10–12, 26; calculation CALC-05

Weakness: unknown covenant headroom and debt amortisation. Covenants of 1.20x fixed charge coverage and 3.00x senior leverage are stated with compliance, but the reported ratios, definitions and the term loan schedule are not supplied, so headroom and the maturity wall are not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_orn-20260930x10q.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15; Source readiness (CP-0)
