## Findings

### Bottom line

Solstice is a moderately leveraged credit with a sound liquidity cushion, and its main exposure is financial policy rather than near-term distress, because refrigerant margins are falling while buybacks and acquisitions compete for the same cash.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation D03; calculation D08

Net debt is USD 1,253 million, 1.44x LTM reported EBITDA, or USD 1,264.79 million and 1.45x after the dividend declared on 2026-11-03; cash plus the undrawn revolver is USD 1,714 million, and no scheduled principal beyond amortisation falls due before 2032. Adjusted EBITDA was flat at USD 804 million for nine months on sales 9.87% higher, and up to USD 400 million of buybacks remains authorised.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 47–49, 52; calculation D01; calculation D02; calculation D03

Reported LTM EBITDA would have to fall 28.24% before net debt reached 2.00x with debt unchanged, an illustration rather than a covenant, so a margin setback alone would not threaten debt service in the next twelve months.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against concern over margins is the third quarter: Adjusted EBITDA rose 12.29% to USD 265 million and its margin was 25.33% against 24.36% a year earlier, after 25.13% and 25.26% in the first two quarters of 2026, so the nine-month decline is mostly the gap to a first half of 2025 that opened at 30.21% on a carve-out basis. Part of the cost increase is a step-up for standalone operation, with selling and administrative spending USD 41 million higher on added headcount, that need not repeat, and Electronic & Specialty Materials is widening its margin to 21.2% from 18.5%.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-03-31_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 38; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 32, 39–45; calculation D03; calculation D05; calculation D14

The strongest evidence against concern over financial policy is its restraint so far: management ended the acquisition within 52 days, returned only USD 136 million in nine months and kept adjusted net leverage near 1.26x, and completing the remaining USD 400 million of buybacks from cash would still leave cash plus the revolver at USD 1,314 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11, 24, 49; calculation D02; calculation D12

The view would improve if LTM Adjusted EBITDA held near USD 993 million with the refrigerants margin stabilising above 33.8% while buybacks were paid from operating cash flow rather than the cash balance. It would worsen if the refrigerants margin fell a further 4.2 percentage points, which would lower LTM Adjusted EBITDA to USD 867.13 million and lift adjusted net leverage to 1.44x, or 1.91x after the remaining buyback; if the working-capital inflow reversed; or if a new acquisition were financed with secured debt ahead of the notes.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 42–43; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D01; calculation D06; calculation D08

### Company description and credit linkage

Solstice makes low global warming potential refrigerants, blowing agents, uranium hexafluoride conversion, healthcare packaging, semiconductor materials and protective fibres in two segments, with FY2025 net sales of USD 3,886 million, 71.77% of them in Refrigerants & Applied Solutions. The business reaches more than 3,000 customers in about 120 countries, with no customer above 10% of sales, and carve-out reporting until the spin-off on 2025-10-30, so cash flow is dispersed and visible but the stand-alone record is about eleven months long. That supports debt service through the cycle and limits what history can show about stand-alone margins and working capital.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 7–10, 97, 138; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation D10


### Ownership, group structure and financial policy

No sponsor or controlling holder is named, Honeywell holds no shares, and the Tax Matters Agreement may restrict equity issuance, mergers and being acquired, particularly for the two years after the spin-off, that is until about 2027-10-30. The notes are guaranteed by the domestic subsidiaries that guarantee the senior credit facilities, so 60.78% of cash held by foreign subsidiaries sits outside that perimeter, and noncontrolling interest takes 13.19% of nine-month net income while dividends and buybacks are the issuer's alone. Creditors therefore rely on documents and covenants rather than a sponsor, and a lapse of the agreement's limits in late 2027 widens the room for equity-funded deals.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 74, 101, 145; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6, 52; calculation D02; calculation D13


### Revenue durability and pricing power

Volume added 6.2% and price 2.8% to nine-month sales, led by stationary refrigerants for data centres, automotive refrigerants, nuclear conversion and memory-related electronic materials, while Building Solutions & Intermediates sales fell 4.46%, the construction-linked line. Original equipment contracts carry fixed pricing and cost recovery clauses, traditional hydrofluorocarbons are relatively commoditised, and incumbents in the United States and Europe have regulatory protection, so pricing power rests on the low global warming potential molecules and long contracts, and the nine-month price gain of USD 62 million in refrigerants did not stop its segment EBITDA falling.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18, 40, 43; 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 21, 23; calculation D04


### Cost structure and margin resilience

Gross margin fell to 32.40% from 34.22% over nine months on raw material inflation, with research spending up USD 13 million, selling and administrative spending up USD 41 million on standalone headcount, and Corporate and All Other cost 21.21% higher at USD 160 million. Raw materials are chlor-alkali based materials, ethylene, polyethylene, wet spar and copper, mostly under contract with fixed and market-based pricing, and the issuer reports dual sourcing and pricing actions, so margins can be defended against modest input shocks but did not absorb this year's inflation, and the 20 manufacturing sites likely carry fixed cost that would magnify a volume fall.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 32, 37, 39–41; 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 30–31; calculation D05


### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capex incurred was USD 408 million in FY2025, 10.50% of net sales, with Electronic & Specialty Materials capex rising to USD 173 million from USD 63 million, and the 2026 range is USD 420 million to USD 440 million on a basis the issuer does not state; paid capex was 9.90% of LTM net sales. LTM free cash flow of USD 446 million is 44.91% of LTM Adjusted EBITDA against 11.90% in FY2025, but working capital supplied USD 156 million of the nine-month operating cash flow, so durable conversion is lower and capex flexibility, mostly in the expansion projects, is the main cash lever.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 162; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8, 52; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D06

  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 21, 32–33
- Substitutes are the not-in-kind alternatives the issuer names among competitors, and the low global warming potential transition is the regulatory driver that favours the issuer's molecules, so substitution risk is a pricing risk and not a volume risk today.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 21, 23
- Buyer power is low to moderate: no customer exceeded 10% of sales and the ten largest were below 20%, but original equipment contracts run one to five years with fixed pricing mechanisms, which limit how fast price follows cost, as the nine-month refrigerant margin shows.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over twelve to twenty-four months, the issuer expects to complete the buyback by the end of the first quarter of 2027, spend USD 420 million to USD 440 million of capex in 2026, and see the Tax Matters Agreement's two-year restrictions end in late 2027; the term loan can be prepaid without premium, but the issuer says only that it will repay debt over time and states no deleveraging target. Tailwinds are the regulatory transition and semiconductor and data-centre demand, and headwinds are cost inflation, trade measures and construction demand, so the likely path is stable leverage with a thinner cash cushion unless refrigerant margins recover. Over three to five years, the 2032 term loan and 2033 notes set the refinancing horizon, with the revolver due first, and patent expiries in the 2030s would test the molecule franchise.

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is that raw material and freight inflation continues and stationary refrigerant pricing stops offsetting it. The first operating break is a further 4.2 percentage-point fall in refrigerants margin, equal to the nine-month gap, which on LTM refrigerant sales of USD 2,997 million would remove USD 125.87 million of EBITDA and take LTM Adjusted EBITDA to USD 867.13 million. Debt is unchanged, so total debt to adjusted EBITDA rises to 2.27x from 1.98x and net debt to 1.44x from 1.26x, or 1.91x if the remaining USD 400 million of buybacks is paid from cash; cash plus the revolver would fall to USD 1,314 million, with working capital reversal as a further drain. Management can suspend the buyback, trim expansion capex and keep the small dividend, so resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress and the observable triggers are the segment margin, working capital and buyback pace. First lien leverage and interest coverage against the revolver tests cannot be calculated because their definitions are not supplied.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 42–43, 49, 52; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation D01; calculation D02; calculation D08


### Overall credit view

Overall, Solstice presents an adequate business profile supported by customer dispersion, contract visibility and positions in low global warming potential refrigerants and nuclear conversion, but credit quality is constrained by falling refrigerant margins, cash generation that leans on working capital, and a financial policy that has shown appetite for debt-funded acquisition and buybacks. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because LTM Adjusted EBITDA covers interest expense 7.36x and contractual run-rate interest 8.88x, expense-based proxies as cash interest is not printed, but depends on holding margins. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on keeping adjusted net leverage near 1.26x and on the revolver being extended before it expires about 2030. The primary downside path is a further refrigerant margin fall combined with a completed buyback. Further analysis would require the executed credit agreement and indenture, a rating or market pricing, sector data, and the size of the Metropolis settlement; the issuer's business profile against typical leveraged-credit issuers in its sector is not established. Top risk: financial policy acting on a thinner earnings base.
