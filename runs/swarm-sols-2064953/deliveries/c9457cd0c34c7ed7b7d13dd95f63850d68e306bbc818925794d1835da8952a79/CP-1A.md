## Findings

### Bottom line

Solstice is a diversified specialty materials business with a young capital structure and a management team that has already shown appetite for debt-funded growth and shareholder returns. Creditor protection beyond the revolver's two maintenance tests rests on debt terms that are not in the supplied documents.
Diversification is real by customer, since none exceeded 10% of 2025 net sales, but earnings rest on one segment, which produced 82.85% of segment Adjusted EBITDA.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 97, 162; calculation A01

Within nine months of the spin-off the company agreed to buy Element Solutions with a bridge commitment of up to USD 4,685 million, then terminated the deal on 27 August 2026 and authorised a USD 500 million buyback instead. Honeywell no longer owns shares, but uncapped indemnities to Honeywell and limits on equity issuance and mergers until 2027 remain.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 32–33; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11, 24; 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 74, 79

### Key findings

- Earnings depend on refrigerants more than the customer count suggests. Refrigerants & Applied Solutions was 71.77% of FY2025 net sales and 82.85% of segment Adjusted EBITDA, and its segment margin fell from 38.9% in FY2024 to 35.2% in FY2025, then was 33.8% in the first nine months of 2026 against 38.0% a year earlier, while Electronic & Specialty Materials reached 21.2% against 18.5%. The regulatory phase-down of older hydrofluorocarbons and the shift to low global warming potential products underlie its volume growth and pricing, while raw material and freight inflation and higher research cost drove the margin decline, so a pricing or cost setback reaches group earnings quickly.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 46, 97, 162; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 32, 42–45; calculation A01; calculation A02
- Most of the new debt paid Honeywell rather than funding the business. Of USD 2,000 million raised in 2025, USD 1,500 million went to Honeywell as a distribution and USD 38 million to financing costs, which left USD 462 million before other cash flows, so leverage at the spin-off came from the distribution to the former parent rather than from operating needs.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, page 117; calculation A04
- Management has shown that it will pursue a large debt-funded acquisition, and the termination removed the transaction, though the filings do not say why. The July 2026 agreement paid USD 10.00 in cash and half a Solstice share for each Element Solutions share, rested on a bridge of up to USD 4,685 million, equal to 4.72x LTM Adjusted EBITDA, and planned permanent financing from a senior secured term loan B and unsecured notes, which would have added secured debt ranking ahead of the unsecured notes. The company cut the plan within two months, with no payment either way, after spending USD 61 million on fees and advisers, and the annual report still says it expects to evaluate selective acquisitions.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 32–33, 48–49; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11; 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, page 8; calculation A05; calculation A06
- Shareholder returns have started and now compete with deleveraging. The company paid USD 136 million in the first nine months of 2026, USD 100 million of buybacks and USD 36 million of dividends, equal to 36.96% of operating cash flow less capex paid, and it intends to substantially complete the USD 500 million authorisation by the end of the first quarter of 2027, leaving up to USD 400 million to come from cash and operating cash flow. The terms of its debt limit cash dividends, but their size is not in the supplied documents.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8, 24–25, 49, 56; 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, page 90; calculation A07; calculation A08
- Governance is thinly evidenced and no controlling holder is named to support creditors or to extract from them. Honeywell holds no shares, the company is a non-accelerated filer with a public holder base, directors and officers are named on the annual report's signature page, and the preliminary information statement describes a classified board until 2028, a board in which all directors but the chief executive were expected to be independent, and independent audit, compensation and nominating committees. Directors, compensation and ownership are deferred to a proxy statement that is not supplied, so protection beyond the revolver's maintenance covenants rests on debt documents that are also not supplied.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 90, 165, 169–170; PREDECESSOR_2024_10-12BA_EX99.1_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 212–214, 275–276; Source readiness (CP-0)
- Contract visibility is a real strength. Remaining performance obligations were USD 3.3 billion at 2026-09-30, 29% due within a year, the ConverDyn joint venture reports a backlog of about USD 2.2 billion, original equipment contracts run one to five years with fixed pricing and cost recovery clauses, the majority of raw material supply is under contract, and no customer exceeded 10% of 2025 net sales with the ten largest below 20%.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 19, 23, 30–31
- The only US-based uranium hexafluoride conversion service is both a strategic asset and the largest contingency the filings leave unquantified. Nuclear conversion was 9.32% of nine-month net sales and grew 21.22%, the Department of Energy shares expansion costs and intends to extend reimbursement for certain litigation and settlement costs, and a global settlement of the Metropolis litigation was agreed in principle in September 2026, but no amount, funding split, court date or insurance recovery is stated, and the issuer carries uncapped indemnities for environmental and toxic tort liabilities assumed from Honeywell.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 29–30; 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20, 79; calculation A03; calculation A11
- Takeover and equity-funded acquisition risk is restrained for about 11 months and then loosens. The Tax Matters Agreement may restrict equity issuance, mergers and being acquired, particularly for two years after the spin-off, Honeywell held a consent right that the merger agreement named, and the classified board ends at the 2028 annual meeting, so the window for a transformative equity-funded deal opens in late 2027 and for a contested sale in 2028.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, page 74; 10-Q_2026-06-30_sols.htm.jsonl, page 32; PREDECESSOR_2024_10-12BA_EX99.1_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 275–276; calculation A12

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against caution is financial. Financial foundation shows total debt at carrying value of USD 1,967 million, net debt of 1.44x LTM reported EBITDA, cash plus the undrawn revolver of USD 1,714 million and no scheduled principal beyond amortisation before 2032, and the company reported compliance at 2026-09-30 with the revolver's tests of first lien leverage not above 3.50x and interest coverage not below 2.75x, while the company did not complete the Element Solutions acquisition and returned only USD 136 million to shareholders. Those facts suggest a company that can absorb a setback, and the deal that would have raised leverage sharply was ended before closing.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11, 22, 24; 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, page 103; calculation A07; calculation A13

Management also states that it does not anticipate the Metropolis matters will have a material adverse effect on financial condition, results of operations or cash flows, citing the Department of Energy and insurance claims, which weakens the case that the contingency is large.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, page 30

The view would change if the Metropolis settlement amount and its funding were disclosed and proved small against reimbursement from the Department of Energy and insurers, or large; if the executed credit agreement and indenture show tight limits on restricted payments, new debt and guarantees that bind buybacks and acquisitions; if a new acquisition is announced with a bridge above 4x LTM Adjusted EBITDA after the Tax Matters Agreement restrictions lapse; or if a controlling or activist holder appears once the classified board ends.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 29–30; 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, page 74; calculation A05; calculation A12

### Limitations

- The executed credit agreement, its first amendment and the indenture are not supplied, so the conclusion that creditor protection beyond the maintenance tests is thin cannot be tested against actual limits on restricted payments, debt incurrence and guarantees; only the filings' summaries, which print the two revolver tests, and the preliminary description of the notes are available.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 101–103, 166–167; PREDECESSOR_2024_10-12BA_EX99.1_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 256–257
- The proxy statement is not supplied, so board independence, compensation design and holders above any threshold could not be read, and the governance finding rests on the signature page and the preliminary information statement, which the final charter may have changed.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, page 165; PREDECESSOR_2024_10-12BA_EX99.1_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 212–214
- The amount, funding split, timing and recoveries for the Metropolis settlement are not stated, so the contingency finding cannot be sized and may be small or material to liquidity.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 29–30
- No earnings release, presentation or call transcript is supplied, so management's reasons for ending the Element Solutions agreement and its intended capital allocation after the buyback are not established, which limits the finding on appetite for leverage.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11, 48–49; Source readiness (CP-0)
- Periods before 2025-10-30 are carve-out statements, so behaviour under Honeywell's ownership cannot be read as the company's own financial policy, which limits the finding that most of the new debt funded the distribution to the former parent.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 119–121

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The capital structure dates from the spin-off. On 2025-09-30 the company issued USD 1,000 million of 5.625% senior notes due 2033, sold to qualified institutional buyers and outside the United States, with the proceeds held in escrow until the spin-off; the notes are senior unsecured and guaranteed on a senior unsecured basis by the domestic subsidiaries that guarantee the senior credit facilities. On 2025-10-29 it entered a credit agreement with a seven-year first lien term B loan of USD 1,000 million, a five-year first lien revolver of USD 1,000 million and uncommitted bilateral letter of credit facilities of USD 750 million, secured on substantially all assets of the company and its domestic guarantors. The revolver lenders have maintenance covenants of first lien leverage not above 3.50x, stepping up to 4.00x after a material acquisition, and interest coverage not below 2.75x, and the company reported compliance at 2025-12-31 and at 2026-09-30. The spin-off on 2025-10-30 then distributed all shares to Honeywell shareowners.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 101–103, 145–146; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, page 22; PREDECESSOR_2024_10-12BA_sols.htm.jsonl, page 4

Of USD 2,000 million of debt issued in 2025, USD 1,500 million was paid to Honeywell as a distribution in connection with the separation and USD 38 million was paid in financing costs. Honeywell had been the sole source of funding before the spin-off and its debt was never attributed to the company. The notes may be called from 2028-09-30 and carry a change of control put at 101% under the preliminary description; the final indenture is not supplied, so those terms are not confirmed.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, page 117; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, page 12; PREDECESSOR_2024_10-12BA_EX99.1_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 256–257; calculation A04

In 2026 the company tried to change that structure. On 2026-07-06 it agreed to acquire Element Solutions for USD 10.00 in cash and 0.500 shares per share, backed by a commitment letter from Goldman Sachs for a 364-day first lien bridge of up to USD 4,685 million and a USD 1,000 million backstop revolver that was never established because the credit agreement was amended on 2026-07-24 to permit the bridge. Permanent financing was to be a senior secured term loan B and unsecured notes. Termination fees of USD 385.0 million or USD 513 million were payable by the company in specified circumstances, including if Honeywell revoked its consent under the Tax Matters Agreement, and USD 376.0 million by Element Solutions. The agreement and the commitments were terminated on 2026-08-27 with no payment between the parties; the company recorded USD 34 million of bridge and backstop fees in interest and other financial charges and USD 27 million of professional fees in transaction-related costs over the nine months.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 32–34, 48–50; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11, 48; calculation A05; calculation A06

Capital returns followed. On 2026-08-27 the board authorised a USD 500 million buyback; 1,685,339 shares were bought at an average of USD 59.32 for USD 100 million up to 2026-09-26, and the company intends to substantially complete the authorisation by the end of the first quarter of 2027. Quarterly dividends of USD 0.075 per share have been declared since 2026-02-11, the latest on 2026-11-03 for payment on 2026-12-10. No new debt has been issued since the spin-off.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sols.htm.jsonl, pages 24–25, 48–49, 56; 10-K_2025-12-31_sols.htm.jsonl, page 163; calculation A08
