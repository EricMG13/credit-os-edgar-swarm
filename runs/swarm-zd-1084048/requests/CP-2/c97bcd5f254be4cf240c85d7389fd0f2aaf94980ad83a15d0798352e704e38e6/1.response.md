## Findings

### Bottom line

Ziff Davis can meet its debt service from cash for the next 12 months to 24 months, but the cash came from selling Connectivity rather than from earnings, so noteholders depend on management not spending it. Cash was USD 1,407.7 million against debt principal of USD 872.3 million at September 30, 2026, and pro forma cash after the 1.75% convertible notes were repaid after that date is not established.

Earnings fell sharply: nine-month EBITDA was USD 62.0 million against USD 188.6 million a year earlier, and USD 146.4 million against USD 206.2 million before goodwill impairment, while combined free cash flow was an outflow of USD 49.3 million and USD 265.0 million went to share repurchases. The view weakens if repurchases continue at the third-quarter pace.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against caution is the balance sheet and the calendar. Cash exceeded debt principal by USD 535.4 million, the revolving facility was undrawn, after the 1.75% repayment nothing drawn matures before March 2028, net interest turned to income in the third quarter, and at the September average repurchase price the equity was worth about USD 1,818.3 million, 2.08x debt principal, an analyst illustration and not a market quotation. The fourth quarter is the seasonal peak: it earned 34.97% of FY2025 total-entity EBITDA, so the trailing earnings the filings cannot yet show may recover, and the company states that cash, operating cash flow and revolver availability are sufficient for at least the next 12 months.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The first strength is liquidity. Cash of USD 1,407.7 million exceeded debt principal by USD 535.4 million and the undrawn revolving facility added USD 348.9 million, so near-term debt service does not depend on earnings; pro forma cash after the later repayment of the 1.75% convertible notes is not established. The mechanism is that cash held in treasury funds and demand deposits can be applied to any maturity without a refinancing, which keeps the probability of default low over the next 12 months to 24 months.

The second strength is a plain debt structure. The remaining principal of USD 723.2 million is fixed rate and unsecured, with 36.39% due in 2028 and 63.61% in 2030, scheduled coupons of USD 30.82 million a year, no hedging contracts and no drawn secured debt, so a rate rise cannot reset the cost of the existing notes.

The third strength is the absence of a sponsor and of a dividend. No controlling shareholder is stated, no dividend was paid in 2023, 2024 or 2025, and the notes carry change-of-control repurchase rights, so there is no owner with an incentive to extract cash through the capital structure and creditors can exit on a takeover.

A comparison with typical leveraged-credit issuers in the sector is not established because no peer set is supplied. Further analysis would require recast continuing-basis annual results, the executed credit agreement and covenant definitions, the date and effect of the 1.75% repayment and tax payment, and management guidance.
