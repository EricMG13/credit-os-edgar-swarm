## Findings

### Bottom line

GFL's business quality is steady but its balance sheet is stretched: net debt reached 5.04x filed trailing Adjusted EBITDA at 30 September 2026 after SECURE, and liquidity cannot be measured, so the credit depends on acquired earnings arriving.

Price added 5.7 percentage points to third-quarter organic growth and the Adjusted EBITDA margin held at 31.5%, which supports cash interest cover of 4.70x for nine months. Against that, net debt rose 55.0% since December 2025 while trailing Adjusted EBITDA rose 13.7%, free cash flow after capital expenditure was only CAD 37.3 million, and the take-private review leaves ownership and financing open. The primary downside is acquired earnings falling short before the first maturities arrive in 2028.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better credit is operating momentum and a guided deleveraging path. Management reports underlying margin expansion of 115 bp excluding acquisitions and diesel prices, nine-month Adjusted EBITDA rose 18.4%, equity paid 80.1% of the SECURE price, Adjusted EBITDA covered cash interest paid 4.70x, the issuer reports covenant compliance, and guidance puts net leverage in the mid 3s by the end of 2026. The guided path also requires fourth-quarter Adjusted EBITDA of about CAD 744.1 million, 9.6% above the third quarter, which a full quarter of SECURE, owned only from 1 September, could explain, and Adjusted Free Cash Flow of about CAD 535.1 million against CAD 272.5 million in the third quarter, nearly double, so delivery is not yet evidenced. The view would improve if a full quarter shows SECURE earnings at the run-rate management assumes, if undrawn availability is disclosed as ample, and if the review ends without a leveraged transaction. It would worsen if price growth fell below cost inflation, if the Adjusted EBITDA margin slipped below the 31.5% of the third quarter, if revolver drawings kept rising, or if a bidder financed a purchase with new debt.

### Limitations

- The judgement that leverage is stretched is relative to GFL's own 2025 and 2026 history and its covenant level only: no peer, rating agency or market price evidence is supplied, so how GFL compares with other leveraged waste issuers is not established.
- The view that near-term refinancing pressure is low rests on maturity timing alone: the sources give no committed undrawn availability and no split of letters of credit by facility, so the cash runway behind that view is not measured.
- The covenant reading in the downside scenario is qualitative: the executed credit agreement and indentures are not supplied, so whether SECURE sets the higher 5.00x level, how the ratio is defined and how much headroom remains cannot be tested.
- The leverage trend uses the issuer's Adjusted EBITDA, which is 45.3% above reported EBITDA for nine months, and the 2025 income statement is not supplied, so a reported-earnings version of the trend cannot be built and the stretch may be larger than shown.

### Downside scenario

The realistic downside starts with acquired earnings and price growth disappointing together. If SECURE and the 2026 acquisitions deliver less than the CAD 730.9 million of run-rate earnings management assumes, and price growth slows below cost inflation while volume stays flat, the Adjusted EBITDA margin of 31.5% would compress. Trailing Adjusted EBITDA would then stay near the filed level while cash interest, guided at about CAD 455 million for the year, and capital expenditure absorb operating cash flow, leaving free cash flow near the CAD 37.3 million seen for nine months. Leverage would stay above 5x on the filed basis, the revolver drawings of CAD 868.0 million would rise to cover acquisitions and interest, and if SECURE counts as a Material Acquisition the covenant level of 5.00x falls to 4.50x after four quarters, a definition the sources do not supply. The first maturities, the 2028 notes of CAD 2,128.2 million, would then need refinancing from a weaker position. Management levers are slowing acquisitions, funding them with shares as with SECURE, trimming growth spending of CAD 182.4 million and keeping share repurchases small, which were CAD 71.0 million for nine months. Resilience looks adequate through 2027 on maturity timing alone, because only CAD 14.2 million falls due within twelve months, but headroom against the covenant and undrawn availability are not established, so that conclusion is bounded. Observable triggers are price growth, organic growth, the margin, revolver drawings, the compliance statement and the outcome of the take-private review.

### Overall credit view

Overall, GFL presents an adequate business profile supported by price-led pass-through and a diversified service mix, but credit quality is constrained by leverage that rose to 5.04x on filed trailing earnings, thin free cash flow after investment and unmeasured liquidity. Against typical leveraged issuers in the sector the business is sound but the balance sheet is weaker, although no peer evidence is supplied to test that. Cash-flow durability appears adequate on the adjusted measure and weaker on reported figures. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on lender access and acquired earnings arriving before the 2028 maturities. The primary downside path is acquired earnings and price growth falling short while leverage stays above the covenant step-down. Further analysis would require the 2025 audited statements, undrawn availability, the debt documents and the outcome of the take-private review. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: leverage on filed earnings after the SECURE acquisition.
