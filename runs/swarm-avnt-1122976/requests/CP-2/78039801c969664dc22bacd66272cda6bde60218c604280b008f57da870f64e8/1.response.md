### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an adequate and improving profile is how recently it was weaker. Total debt was 4.94x EBITDA and net debt 3.63x at the end of 2025, operating income fell 38.2% in that year on flat sales, and the 2026 recovery rests partly on pricing that is not yet tested by a downturn, while 99.95% of principal comes due within three years of 2029. The strongest evidence for a better view is that net debt is falling, liquidity covers the 2029 maturity 1.98x and free cash flow of USD 239.0 million is 45.84% of EBITDA. The view would weaken if the last-twelve-months EBITDA margin fell below the 14.59% of normalised fiscal 2025, if the Calvert City accrual rose again, if the swaps were settled in cash, or if new management announced debt-funded buybacks or an acquisition. It would strengthen if the 2029 term loan were refinanced well before maturity, if cash interest were disclosed for interim periods, or if the debt documents showed covenant headroom and priority terms.

Revenue is order-driven with short manufacturing lead times, contract tenor and renewal rates are not supplied, and no customer exceeded 3% of revenue, so visibility comes from breadth rather than from a stated backlog, with defense tenders the main lumpy exception. ### Industry forces and credit-translated SWOT

Competition is global and local: the company competes with global companies and local niche producers on performance, innovation, service, quality, delivery and price, and with polymer and non-polymer alternatives, so rivalry limits price increases beyond cost pass-through. Buyer power is dispersed because no customer exceeds 3% of revenue, while supplier power sits with large chemical producers, whose resin and additive prices Avient cannot control and, for some strategic materials, cannot easily replace. Trade policy and currency are the material external factors: the annual report names tariffs and retaliatory measures as risks to demand and results, 61% of sales are outside the United States, and the euro net investment is hedged with swaps whose liability is USD 302.0 million, so a stronger dollar or new tariffs would pressure translated earnings, while plastics and environmental regulation add compliance cost. In credit terms the strengths are liquidity, cash generation and diversification, the weaknesses are the maturity wall, legacy claims and swap-supported interest cost, the opportunities are growth in defense, healthcare, electronics and infrastructure applications where growth-vector sales are outpacing the rest of the company, and the threats are input-cost inflation without pricing, tariffs and a weaker refinancing market in 2029 to 2031.

### Downside scenario

The realistic downside starts with raw-material, energy and freight costs rising faster than selling prices in packaging, consumer and industrial markets, which the annual report names as a risk. The first operational break is gross margin, because cost of sales is most of the cost base and pass-through has only been shown in an upswing. If EBITDA fell back to its fiscal 2025 reported level, a fall of USD 132.0 million, total debt would be 4.69x EBITDA and net debt 3.55x, and free cash flow after dividends would be about USD 6.6 million before tax relief and working-capital release, so debt reduction would stop and the prepayment would have to come from cash. Management levers are the dividend of USD 100.4 million a year, capital spending that runs at about 57% of depreciation and amortization, restructuring and the unused buyback authority, and liquidity of USD 932.4 million would absorb the shortfall, so liquidity distress before 2029 is not indicated. The consequence is on refinancing: the USD 470.7 million term loan due in 2029 becomes current in 2028 and would be refinanced at 3.55x to 4.69x leverage and with Calvert City costs still open. The observable triggers are a falling EBITDA margin, a lag between price and cost, a rising environmental accrual and a swap settlement.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Avient presents an adequate business profile supported by diversified customers, steady free cash flow and USD 932.4 million of liquidity, but credit quality is constrained by a USD 1,847.6 million maturity wall from 2029 to 2031 and by legacy claims that are not bounded. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow of USD 239.0 million covers dividends of USD 100.4 million and low capital spending, although part of the 2026 earnings gain is charges leaving the window. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on market access in 2028 and 2029, the margin holding, the Calvert City accrual stabilising and the treatment of the swap liabilities. The primary downside path is a pricing squeeze that returns EBITDA toward its 2025 level and leaves less cushion for refinancing.
