## Findings

### Bottom line

Clean Harbors is a moderately leveraged, permit-protected waste network whose credit quality is constrained by debt-assisted acquisitions, which lift net leverage from 1.86x at 2026-09-30 to 2.22x pro forma after the 2026-10-02 EnviroServe purchase, and by earnings that lean on oil prices.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C01; calculation C14

Liquidity gives time: USD 759,044 thousand of cash and undrawn revolver at 2026-09-30, with pro forma cash not established, and no note due before 2029. The primary downside path is lower oil product prices and fewer one-off projects while integration costs and interest rise, so creditors should watch pro forma leverage and the oil segment margin.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_clh.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-K_2025-12-31_clh.htm.jsonl, page 133; calculation C06; calculation C15

### Business profile, ownership and group structure

Clean Harbors collects, treats, recycles and disposes of hazardous and non-hazardous waste across North America through permitted incinerators, landfills and treatment sites and re-refines used oil; Environmental Services supplies 83.44% of nine-month 2026 revenue and the oil segment 16.56%. The issuer describes its permits as hard to replicate and customers keep generator liability, so established vendors hold recurring volumes, which supports cash-flow visibility and debt service. Ownership is dispersed with no sponsor or controlling holder named, so creditor protection rests on board and management policy, and the proxy statement that would show holdings and independence is not supplied. The notes are guaranteed by domestic subsidiaries and rank behind secured debt and non-guarantor subsidiaries, and Canada, at 8.56% of revenue, sits outside the note guarantee as the debt note words it.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_clh.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-K_2025-12-31_clh.htm.jsonl, page 137; calculation C17

### Revenue durability and pricing power

Revenue is mostly recurring and generator-driven, with cyclical and non-scheduled parts. Technical Services and the Safety-Kleen core services follow industrial volumes and pricing, Field and Emergency Response varies with events, and Safety-Kleen Oil follows product prices, with revenue down 15.10% in 2025 and up 26.70% in nine months 2026; emergency response and oil together are 28.93% of nine-month revenue. Customers face a financial burden in switching hazardous waste vendors, which supports pricing, and the ten-year disposal contract adds visibility at USD 60.0 million a year, 0.99% of 2025 revenue, so the core is durable but the 2026 growth rate is not a run rate.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_clh.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_clh.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_clh.htm.jsonl, page 37; calculation C18

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Costs are labour, equipment, fuel and transport on top of the fixed cost of permitted assets, and the issuer passes much of the inflation through pricing and fuel surcharges. In Q3 2026 Environmental Services cost of revenues rose 16.4% against revenue growth of 16.5%, with labour and benefits up USD 75.0 million, equipment and supplies up USD 33.2 million and fuel and transport up USD 31.3 million, yet the segment margin held at 27.4% against 26.8%. Margins can be defended at current volumes; under volume pressure the fixed cost of ten incinerators and seven landfills would deleverage margins, and the oil segment margin depends on product spreads that the issuer does not control.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_clh.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-Q_2026-09-30_clh.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-Q_2026-09-30_clh.htm.jsonl, page 37; 10-Q_2026-09-30_clh.htm.jsonl, page 38

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is real but split by service: national firms such as Veolia North America, Enviri, Republic Services, Waste Management, GFL Environmental and Crystal Clean compete in parts of the offer and hundreds of private firms compete locally, while the issuer states no one competitor offers its full range, which limits price competition in the integrated disposal business. New entry is constrained by permitting, regulatory approval and capital cost of incinerators and landfills. Substitutes such as in-house disposal are limited because regulatory requirements can make captive disposal too capital intensive or complex, and the issuer lists market incineration capacity, including captive incineration closures, among the factors that drive revenue. Buyer power is low by concentration, with no customer above 10% of revenue, and generator liability makes customers cautious about switching, while supplier power sits mainly in fuel, labour and used-oil feedstock, where fuel and transport cost rose USD 31.3 million in Q3 2026 and was passed through.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

In the next 12 to 24 months the issuer guides fourth-quarter Adjusted EBITDA growth of 23% to 26%, full-year 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of USD 1.44 billion to USD 1.48 billion and adjusted free cash flow of USD 600 million to USD 660 million, with the ten-year contract started in Q4 and the PFAS pipeline described as robust; these support revenue and deleveraging if debt stops rising. ### Downside scenario

The downside begins with oil product prices easing as supply disruption ends while the large emergency response and PFAS projects fade. ### Overall credit view

Overall, Clean Harbors presents a strong business profile supported by permit-protected assets, customer spread and liquidity, but credit quality is constrained by debt-assisted acquisitions and earnings that lean on oil prices. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on delivering synergies and holding leverage near the 2.22x pro forma level before the 2029 maturities and the swap expiry on 2027-09-30. The primary downside path is an oil price reversal with fading project revenue on a higher debt base. Further analysis would require ratings, covenant levels, the proxy statement and the reported earnings of the acquired businesses. Fundamental credit completed.
