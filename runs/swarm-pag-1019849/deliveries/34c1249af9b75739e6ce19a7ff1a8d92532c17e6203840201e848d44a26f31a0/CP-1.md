## Findings

### Bottom line

Penske Automotive Group's non-vehicle debt is modest, at 1.50x reported EBITDA after cash and with 5.49x interest cover at September 30, 2026, but the pending take-private could add debt that none of these figures include.
Basis: calculation R1; calculation R2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–11, 25

Floor plan notes payable of USD 4,262.0 million and operating lease liabilities of USD 2,509.8 million sit outside that debt, and counting floor plan lifts leverage to 3.92x EBITDA before floor plan interest. Net debt was USD 2,298.9 million, or USD 2,394.8 million pro forma for the dividend payable on December 1, 2026, and liquidity was about USD 1,568.3 million.
Basis: calculation R6; calculation N5; calculation X2; calculation LQ5; calculation Z4

### Key findings

- Net leverage rose from 1.15x at year end 2024 to 1.38x at year end 2025 and peaked at 1.69x on March 31, 2026, after USD 669.7 million of acquisitions paid in cash, then fell to 1.50x by September 30, 2026 as total debt fell by USD 269.7 million and last-twelve-month EBITDA edged up to USD 1,533.6 million. The improvement comes from repaying debt, not from earnings growth: issuer-adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,458.8 million is level with USD 1,459.2 million at year end 2025.
  Basis: calculation R1; calculation R4; calculation R5; calculation Z1; calculation L6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 22

- Interest cover has held between 5.43x and 5.63x across the five balance-sheet dates, but the measure mixes perimeters: cash interest paid of USD 279.2 million over the last twelve months is close to floor plan plus other interest expense of USD 274.4 million, so it evidently includes floor plan interest that EBITDA is already struck after. On a like-for-like basis, EBITDA before floor plan interest covered cash interest 6.05x over the last twelve months. The pressure point is other interest expense, USD 117.9 million over the last twelve months against USD 91.6 million in 2025, because revolver borrowing paid for dealership acquisitions.
  Basis: calculation R2; calculation R8; calculation L16; calculation K3; calculation Z2; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 117; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4, 65

- Free cash flow was USD 497.9 million over the last twelve months and USD 507.5 million in the first nine months of 2026, which covered dividends of USD 281.0 million with USD 226.5 million to spare. Operating cash flow fell USD 174.8 million to USD 724.6 million: a USD 244.2 million inventory build was only partly funded by a USD 110.8 million rise in floor plan payable, and lower net income and a larger non-cash disposal gain made up most of the rest, so the cash conversion of EBITDA depends on inventory and floor plan funding staying in step. Net borrowing under revolver and mortgage facilities of USD 347.4 million helped fund the acquisitions.
  Basis: calculation F3; calculation F4; calculation F5; calculation Z2; calculation Z3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4

- Reported EBITDA of USD 1,207.6 million for the first nine months of 2026 includes USD 166.4 million of equity earnings from affiliates, led by Penske Transportation Solutions, which paid USD 73.0 million in cash distributions, and a USD 90.9 million gain on dealership sales. Issuer-adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,129.7 million was flat on USD 1,130.1 million a year earlier, and selling, general and administrative expense rose to 73.2% of gross profit from 71.5%.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 64, 70; EX-99.1_2026-10-28_q326exhibit99_1.htm.jsonl, page 17

- Liquidity at September 30, 2026 was cash of USD 68.3 million plus about USD 1,500 million of issuer-stated borrowing availability, USD 1,568.3 million in all. The last printed maturity schedule, at December 31, 2025, put USD 939.3 million of debt in 2028, the largest year, but the printed expiry of the U.S. credit agreement has since moved from 2028 to 2029, so the 2028 wall is probably smaller today; no current schedule is printed.
  Basis: calculation LQ5; calculation M1; 10-K_2025-12-31_pag-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 143–144; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25, 67

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against the benign leverage reading is that vehicle floor plan and leases are real claims that the issuer's non-vehicle measure leaves out. Counting floor plan notes payable lifts leverage to 3.92x EBITDA before floor plan interest, against 3.69x at year end 2025, and operating lease liabilities of USD 2,509.8 million are on top. The second case is the transaction: committed debt financing of up to USD 1.8 billion, of which up to USD 1.1 billion could be added to the U.S. credit agreement, comes with a termination fee of USD 383.0 million that the issuer would owe in specified circumstances, and the financing that becomes the issuer's own debt at closing is not established.
Basis: calculation R6; calculation R7; calculation Z4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_pag-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 25, 90–91

The benign reading would be overturned by a printed covenant threshold with thin headroom, a fall in adjusted EBITDA while debt stays at today's level, or a financing structure at closing that adds most of the committed debt to the issuer's balance sheet. It would be confirmed by a maturity schedule at the latest balance sheet date that shows the extended revolver and no new near-term wall.
