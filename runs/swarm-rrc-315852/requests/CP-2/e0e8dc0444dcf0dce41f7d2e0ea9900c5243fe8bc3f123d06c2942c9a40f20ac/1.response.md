## Findings

### Bottom line

Range's fundamental credit profile is strong on leverage and liquidity and adequate on business risk, so creditors are well covered on current numbers, but dependence on gas prices, fixed transport costs and the unresolved EQT merger are the threats to watch. Total debt is 0.49x derived EBITDA for the last twelve months at 2026-09-30, EBITDA covers cash interest 20.33x, no debt matures before 2030 and the undrawn revolver gives USD 1,540.90 million of liquidity. The main downside is a gas price fall that cuts EBITDA while minimum transport payments stay due: a fall that returned EBITDA to its FY2024 level of USD 727.72 million would lift total debt to 1.07x EBITDA and turn free cash flow negative, but would not threaten liquidity or the 2030 maturities. Creditor treatment in the merger is not established.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against the favourable view is that the leverage figures are flattered by a strong price year and ignore fixed claims. Derived EBITDA includes USD 60.38 million of non-cash derivative income, excludes USD 378.82 million of divestiture and lease claims that lift leverage to 0.73x, and sits ahead of minimum transport payments of at least 10.28x LTM cash interest each year. ### Company description

Range is an independent producer of natural gas, NGLs and oil that operates almost all of its production from the Marcellus Shale in Pennsylvania as one operating segment, with a holding company whose subsidiaries guarantee the revolver and the notes. Proved reserves equal 22.23 years of FY2025 production, so reinvestment risk is low and the asset base can support debt over the maturity horizon, but production, pipeline access and political and regulatory exposure sit in one basin. Revenue is market-priced: in 9M 2026 natural gas was 57.78%, NGLs 36.45% and oil 5.78% of product sales, so credit quality follows Appalachian gas and NGL prices more than oil. The business therefore generates durable volume but not durable price, which supports debt service capacity and refinancing confidence at current prices and offers little revenue visibility beyond the hedged portion.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is cyclical, price-taking and not contractually recurring, so it is unpredictable beyond the hedged portion. Range sells at index-based prices to utilities, marketers, midstream companies and industrial users and has no pricing power; in 9M 2026 realized prices before settlements rose 11.95% and production rose 5.74%, but in Q3 2026 the average NYMEX gas price fell 3.91% year on year and the realized gas price fell 7.03%, which management attributes to US production growth and uncertain winter demand. Management expects LNG export growth and power demand to keep longer-term gas prices constructive, which is a view and not a contract; the credit consequence is that revenue durability depends on a market the issuer cannot influence, with hedges smoothing only part of the next two years.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins can be defended against moderate volume or price pressure but not against a deep fall, because the largest cost is fixed. Transport, gathering, processing and compression is 54.86% of costs, rests on minimum commitments, and per-unit cost rose to USD 1.55 per mcfe; direct operating and general and administrative costs are small per unit and rose on workovers, water hauling and employee costs. Processing charges that are a percentage of proceeds fall with prices, which is the main automatic cost relief, and tariff changes on intrastate pipelines could raise fixed charges. The credit consequence is that cash flow available for debt service falls faster than revenue.

### Porter's five forces

Rivalry is intense but works through costs and acreage rather than customers, so the credit effect is on margin durability. The annual report describes substantial competition from larger companies with greater financial resources for leases, services, personnel and pipeline capacity, and says there is little long-term price advantage for a commodity producer; this means Range's margin relies on cost position and balance-sheet strength, which low leverage supports. New entrants matter less than supply response by existing producers: management attributes the lower Q3 2026 gas price to US production growth, which pressures realized prices and the borrowing base value of reserves. Substitutes such as wind, solar and nuclear are cited as a risk if technology and policy support grow, but management expects power demand and LNG exports to support gas demand, so the substitution threat is a long-dated pressure on gas demand and refinancing appetite. Buyers have limited power because alternative purchasers are readily available and Range sells at index prices to many users, so buyer risk is price, not volume or credit. Suppliers hold the most power: third-party pipelines and processors set capacity and fees under long minimum commitments, and capacity constraints can lead to shut-ins, which is the channel from supplier power to cash flow and liquidity.

### PEST factors

Policy and economic factors are material to credit quality; social and technology factors are not primary drivers on the supplied evidence. The methane waste emissions charge was postponed until 2034 by legislation and the regulator extended some compliance deadlines, which lowers near-term compliance cost but leaves a possible later cost; the federal administration has restarted reviews of LNG export projects, which supports demand for Appalachian gas. Changes in intrastate pipeline rate design could raise fixed transport charges and reduce netbacks, and tariffs on imported goods can raise service costs; the economic exposure is commodity price volatility, inflation in diesel and services, and the floating rate on the revolver, where a rise of 1 percentage point costs USD 2.94 million a year. The credit consequence is that policy risk is mostly about the timing of demand and cost, not about solvency, while price volatility is the dominant economic risk.

### Downside scenario

The realistic downside is a return of low Appalachian gas prices of the kind seen in FY2024, when the NYMEX benchmark used for reserves was 37.17% lower than in 2025, while minimum transport payments stay due. As an illustration, sized to return EBITDA to the FY2024 level of USD 727.72 million and not as a forecast, a fall of 27.10% in realized prices on LTM product sales removes USD 866.05 million of EBITDA. Revenue falls by the same amount, the EBITDA margin falls to 27.31%, and operating cash flow falls to USD 553.21 million, so free cash flow before dividends becomes a deficit of USD 138.86 million at current capital expenditure and a deficit of USD 230.94 million after LTM dividends of USD 92.08 million. Total debt over EBITDA rises to 1.07x, or 1.59x including claims outside debt, and EBITDA still covers cash interest 9.28x. ### Overall credit view

Overall, Range presents an adequate business profile supported by a long reserve life, an operated Marcellus position and hedges over part of the next three years, but credit quality is constrained by single-basin gas price exposure, fixed transport commitments of USD 5,857.88 million and a single bank facility. Cash-flow durability appears adequate at current prices, with USD 727.19 million of LTM free cash flow, and weaker if gas prices return to FY2024 levels. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on holding leverage low, keeping the borrowing base above the commitments and the terms of the merger, since the revolver and the notes both mature in 2030. The primary downside path is a gas price fall that meets fixed transport payments and a borrowing base cut. Further analysis would require the credit agreement, the indenture, the merger documents, ratings and peer data. Top risk: a gas price fall combined with fixed transport commitments and an unstated treatment of creditors in the merger.
