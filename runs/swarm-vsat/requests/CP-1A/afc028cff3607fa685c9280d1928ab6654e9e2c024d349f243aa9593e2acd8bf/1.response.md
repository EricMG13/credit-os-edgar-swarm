## Findings

### Bottom line

Viasat is a listed, founder-led company with no sponsor or parent identified, no dividend and no documented extraction, so creditors face execution and refinancing risk more than owner risk, but ownership, board and covenant detail is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_vsat-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 42, 66, 83; Source readiness (CP-0)

Communication services produced 71.11% of fiscal 2026 revenue of USD 4,640.3 million and defense and advanced technologies 28.89%. Aviation, at 25.68% of revenue, is the growth line while U.S. fixed broadband shrinks because satellite bandwidth is rationed in favour of aviation. The credit turns on the ViaSat-3 satellite ramp and the Inmarsat integration, and fiscal 2026 deleveraging coincided with non-recurring Ligado and Navarino receipts.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_vsat-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 104, 106, 205; calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C3

### Key findings

- Control rests with a founder-led board and public holders, not a sponsor, but the holder register is not supplied, so concentration is not established. The founder has led the company since 1986, the board is classified with staggered three-year terms, blank-check preferred stock is authorised and action by written consent is barred, which slows any hostile change of control. A change of control lets noteholders put their notes at 101% and is an event of default under the secured credit facilities. About a third of today's shares were issued to Inmarsat's former owners in 2023, and an outside investor signed a cooperation agreement on May 6, 2026 whose terms are not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_vsat-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 42, 66, 124; calculation C16
- Financial policy has been creditor-aligned so far: no dividend has been paid, buybacks are limited to shares withheld for employee taxes, and debt principal fell USD 619.16 million to USD 6.6 billion in fiscal 2026. The fall coincided with non-recurring receipts, a USD 420.0 million Ligado lump sum and USD 203.0 million from the Navarino sale, and the filings do not tie those receipts to the repayments, so a similar reduction is not assured.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_vsat-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 83, 110, 114, 173; calculation C15
- Growth and risk concentrate in aviation, although revenue is spread across several service and product lines. Aviation services rose 13.66% in fiscal 2026 and 10.75% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027 while fixed services fell 18.06% and 27.08%, and installed commercial aircraft grew 9.46% on the approximate counts printed for the year to June 30, 2026. Recurring aviation revenue supports durability, but no airline customer or contract term is disclosed, so dependence on any one airline is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_vsat-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 106, 205; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vsat-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 48, 67; calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C6; calculation C7; calculation C9
- Government exposure is material but not dominant. The U.S. Government was 16% of fiscal 2026 revenue, the five largest contracts about 19%, and substantially all U.S. Government backlog can be terminated for convenience. Defense and advanced technologies revenue grew 9.79% in fiscal 2026 and its firm backlog rose from USD 1,211.9 million at March 31, 2026 to USD 1,401.6 million at June 30, 2026.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against a benign owner-risk view is what is missing rather than what is documented. The proxy statement is not supplied, a cooperation agreement with an outside investor dated May 6, 2026 is listed as a material contract, about a third of the share count was issued to former Inmarsat holders, and the Chairman and chief executive adopted a share-sale plan in March 2026 running to March 2027. Any of these could precede board change, buybacks or asset sales that favour shareholders over creditors. The company also admits it violated covenants in former revolving facilities and obtained waivers, with no date given.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_vsat-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 76, 119, 124; calculation C16

The strongest operating case in favour is improving segment profitability: communication services segment operating profit rose from USD 40.9 million to USD 62.4 million in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, an 8% margin against 5%, and ViaSat-3 service would end the bandwidth rationing that is shrinking fixed broadband. A debt-funded distribution or buyback, a covenant waiver or amendment, board changes tied to the cooperation agreement, failure to extend or replace the Inmarsat revolving facility before March 28, 2027, delay or impairment of ViaSat-3 service, or failure to collect the USD 100 million Ligado payment still outstanding would move the view toward higher risk. Evidence of dispersed ownership, a refinanced revolver and ViaSat-3 service in commercial operation would move it toward lower risk.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_vsat-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 67; 10-K_2026-03-31_vsat-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 110, 176

### Limitations

- The proxy statement is not supplied, so board composition, independence, pay and the holder register are not established; this limits the owner-risk conclusion to the absence of documented extraction, not proof of dispersed ownership.
- The terms of the cooperation agreement dated May 6, 2026 are not supplied, so any board, standstill or strategy commitments are not established; this could reverse the finding that control sits with the board and public holders.
- Executed credit agreements and indentures are not supplied and covenant levels are printed in no filing, so capacity for dividends, investments and asset transfers is not established; the financial-policy conclusion rests on observed behaviour, not on what the documents permit.
- No document is dated after August 6, 2026, so the ViaSat-3 service starts, any Ligado resolution of the USD 100 million outstanding and any extension of the Inmarsat revolving facility are unconfirmed at the as-of date; the execution and refinancing conclusions may be out of date.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_vsat-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 34, 71
- Airline customers, contract lengths and renewal terms are not disclosed, and backlog excludes recurring in-flight connectivity revenue, so the durability of the aviation growth that drives the business view cannot be tested for single-customer dependence.
  Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_vsat-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 108, 134
- Management's own earnings framing is not supplied because the shareholder letters and slides behind the three results notices are absent, and no issuer-defined adjusted EBITDA exists in any filing; segment profit lines are the only earnings measure used.
- Debt is shown by borrower group from the notes, with no obligor-group statements, so the split of earnings and cash between the Viasat and Inmarsat groups is not established and structural subordination conclusions are qualitative.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The defining transaction is the Inmarsat acquisition completed on May 30, 2023, in which Viasat bought all outstanding shares of Inmarsat Holdings for consideration transferred of USD 2,679.092 million, made up of USD 550.7 million of cash and Viasat shares valued at about USD 2.1 billion. Viasat assumed long-term debt of USD 3,519.774 million and recorded goodwill of USD 1,462.881 million and acquired intangible assets of USD 2,570.0 million. Two financings were arranged around the closing: a USD 616.7 million term loan in May 2023 and a USD 733.4 million unsecured bridge loan that the 2031 senior notes replaced in September 2023. The former Inmarsat owners received shares equal to about one third of today's share count, and their current holdings are not established.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_vsat-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 169, 175, 185; calculation C16

Inmarsat's own debt has been refinanced twice since closing. In March 2024 a USD 1.3 billion term loan and a USD 550.0 million revolving facility replaced most of the earlier term loan and the earlier USD 700.0 million revolver, and in September 2024 USD 1.975 billion of 9.00% secured notes due 2029 were issued to redeem the 6.75% secured notes due 2026, a coupon increase of 2.25 percentage points. In fiscal 2026 Viasat redeemed the remaining USD 442.6 million of its 2025 senior notes in May 2025, repaid USD 300.0 million of the original Inmarsat term loan in November 2025 and repaid the former export-credit loan at maturity on October 15, 2025.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_vsat-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 111, 176, 178, 180, 181, 184; calculation C26

The only new debt issue in the period is the export-credit facility of USD 188.7 million agreed on January 21, 2026 with the Export-Import Bank of the United States. It financed up to 85% of the launch and insurance costs of the first ViaSat-3 satellite (USD 175.8 million) plus USD 12.9 million of exposure fees, carries a fixed 4.78% rate (7.52% effective), is repayable in 16 semi-annual installments from May 2026 to a final maturity on November 25, 2033, is guaranteed by Viasat, Inc. and is secured by first-priority liens on the ViaSat-2 satellite and a pledge of the borrower's shares. The first installment reduced the balance to USD 176.934 million by June 30, 2026, and the financial covenants are a maximum total leverage ratio and a minimum interest coverage ratio whose levels are not printed.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_vsat-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 178, 179; 10-Q_2025-12-31_vsat-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 56; 10-Q_2026-06-30_vsat-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 32, 36; calculation C27

At June 30, 2026 total debt was USD 6,570.503 million: the Inmarsat group's term loan and 9.00% secured notes (USD 3,245.75 million), the Viasat group's two term loans, 2027 secured notes, 2028 and 2031 unsecured notes and export-credit facility (USD 3,182.075 million) and finance leases (USD 142.678 million). Both revolving facilities were undrawn: Viasat's USD 647.5 million facility had USD 47.2 million of letters of credit and USD 600.3 million available, and Inmarsat's USD 550.0 million facility, available in full, expires on March 28, 2027, 143 days after the as-of date. The 2027 secured notes of USD 600.0 million were classified as current at June 30, 2026, and the documents show no refinancing after August 6, 2026.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_vsat-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 32, 34, 37, 38; calculation C20; calculation C24

Other transactions shape the credit. The Link-16 tactical data link business was sold to L3Harris for about USD 1.96 billion in cash in January 2023; the energy services system integration business was divested in December 2024; Inmarsat's June 2025 settlement with Ligado Networks provides a USD 420.0 million lump sum received in October 2025, a USD 100 million payment due in March 2026 that remains outstanding pending resolution, and quarterly payments of about USD 16 million rising 3% a year through 2107; and Navarino UK was sold in March 2026 for USD 203.0 million with a gain of USD 168.1 million. Events after March 31, 2026 are the launch of the third ViaSat-3 satellite in April 2026, the cooperation agreement of May 6, 2026 and the first export-credit installment in May 2026; the March 31, 2026 balance sheet reflects none of them.
