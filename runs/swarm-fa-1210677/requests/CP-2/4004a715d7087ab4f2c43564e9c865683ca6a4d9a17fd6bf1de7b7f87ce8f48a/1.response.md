## Findings

### Bottom line

First Advantage's credit profile is adequate and improving but still levered: net leverage is 3.91x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA, or 4.19x pro forma for the 4 November 2026 acquisition, so creditors rely on earnings growth and the sponsor's restraint rather than on a cushion.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F01

The supports are a sticky customer base, USD 490.2 million of liquidity with no principal due before 2028 and cash interest cover of 3.17x. The constraints are hiring-driven revenue without minimum volumes, floating-rate debt with 74.00% unhedged after 2026, thin free cash flow conversion and a controlling sponsor that has paid out cash and keeps buying businesses. Cash after the acquisition is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F01; calculation F02; calculation F03; calculation F05; calculation F07

### Key findings

The credit is adequate and improving, but leverage is still close to four times EBITDA. Total debt to reported EBITDA fell from 5.47x at the end of 2025 to 4.45x on the last twelve months to 30 September 2026, and net debt to reported EBITDA from 4.84x to 3.91x at 30 September 2026, which becomes 4.19x pro forma for the acquisition of 4 November 2026. Of the improvement in total leverage, 77.10% came from higher EBITDA and 22.90% from lower debt, and on the issuer's adjusted measure total leverage moved from 4.71x to 4.09x. The pro forma figure counts the full cash consideration and no earnings from the target, so it is an upper bound, and pro forma cash is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F01; calculation F04

Liquidity is the strongest support. Cash of USD 240.9 million and an undrawn revolver of USD 249.3 million give USD 490.2 million, 24.62% of total debt, and no scheduled principal falls due before 2028, with the revolver due in October 2029 and the term loan in October 2031. The acquisition of 4 November 2026 used part of this buffer, and the cash that remains after it is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F02

Cash flow covers interest comfortably but converts only about a third of adjusted EBITDA into free cash flow. Reported EBITDA covers cash interest 3.17x, up from 2.35x at the end of 2025, but last-twelve-months levered free cash flow is 36.39% of adjusted EBITDA and 8.90% of total debt, after cash taxes that rose 67.50% and capital expenditure that rose 56.85% in the nine months to September 2026. Prepayments and share repurchases together used 104.05% of nine-month free cash flow, so deleveraging is funded by free cash flow only if buybacks and deals do not take more.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F01; calculation F03

Business risk is moderate: customers are sticky but revenue follows hiring. Gross retention was about 96% in both 2024 and 2025 and no customer reached 10% of FY2025 revenues, but most revenue is pre-onboarding screening ordered per individual, contracts carry no minimum volumes and customers can leave at short notice. The United States produces 85.81% of revenues, Sterling now supplies 49.07% of gross segment revenues, and the International segment, whose revenues fell 6.17% in the nine months with a margin of 5.95% against 16.12%, adds little cushion.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F07

Rate risk is a real constraint on the improvement. Interest is floating, three swaps mature on 31 December 2026, and 74.00% of principal is unhedged after that date, so a rise in SOFR would lift cash interest. The issuer estimated that a 100 bp move changes twelve months of interest on the first lien facility by about USD 21.1 million, which is 14.94% of last-twelve-months cash interest paid; it does not say whether the swaps are in that estimate.
Basis: calculation F05; calculation F08; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 118; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Sponsor control makes financial policy the swing factor. Silver Lake holds 51.4% and must approve debt above USD 100.0 million while it holds 25% or more. The record is mixed: a USD 217.7 million special dividend in 2023, voluntary prepayments of USD 65.0 million in 2025 and USD 95.0 million in the nine months to September 2026, a USD 100.0 million repurchase programme and three acquisitions in three years, the latest for USD 125.0 million with USD 45.0 million drawn on the revolver. Deleveraging is therefore a choice and not a commitment.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F07

Information gaps keep several conclusions provisional. No rating, market quote, executed credit agreement or proxy statement is supplied and the 2026 target is unnamed, so market access, covenant capacity and post-deal leverage are judged on reported figures only.
Basis: Source readiness (CP-0); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an adequate and improving credit is that part of the improvement comes from lower one-off charges and part is already reversed by the acquisition. Reported EBITDA grew 17.76% against 10.37% for adjusted EBITDA because the charges the issuer adds back shrank, free cash flow converts only 36.39% of adjusted EBITDA, and the acquisition of 4 November 2026 lifts net leverage to 4.19x pro forma from 3.91x before any earnings from the target are counted. The view would be confirmed if net leverage stayed below the 4.84x of end-2025 once the acquisition is consolidated and cover stayed above 2.35x. It would turn negative if reported EBITDA fell 13.43% on a pro forma net debt basis, which returns net leverage to 4.84x, or 25.84%, which returns cover to 2.35x, or if a further debt-funded deal or payout used the liquidity.
Basis: calculation F01; calculation F03; calculation F04; calculation F06; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Company description

First Advantage sells employment background screening and identity verification to employers, covering criminal, education and work-history checks, identity and drug screening, driver and healthcare credentials, continuous monitoring and adjacent products such as fleet compliance and hiring tax credits. Revenue is transactional, since an employer orders a package of checks for one person, so it follows hiring volumes, yet relationships are long, gross retention is about 96% and no customer reached 10% of FY2025 revenues. The United States produces 85.81% of revenues and the Sterling acquisition of October 2024 made Sterling 49.07% of gross segment revenues. For creditors this means cash-flow visibility is good at the relationship level and weak at the volume level, so debt service depends on earnings tracking hiring and refinancing confidence rests on leverage, not on contracted revenue.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F07

### Ownership and group structure

Silver Lake beneficially owns 51.4% of the common stock and holds approval rights over debt above USD 100.0 million, joint ventures above USD 100.0 million and change of control while it keeps 25% or more. The listed registrant is a holding company with no liabilities of its own, and the debt sits at First Advantage Holdings, LLC, guaranteed by its direct parent and material wholly owned domestic restricted subsidiaries according to the 2024 annual report, which is the only filing to describe the guarantee and collateral. Restricted-group figures are not supplied, so structural subordination to non-guarantor subsidiaries is not sized. Leverage tolerance has been shown by the 2024 acquisition financing and then by USD 160.0 million of voluntary prepayments over 2025 and the nine months to 2026-09-30, while payout behaviour is a 2023 special dividend and a 2026 repurchase programme.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2024-12-31_fa-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 158; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 174–175; calculation F07

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is driven by volumes: most revenue is pre-onboarding screening that follows employer hiring, growth in the nine months to September 2026 came from existing customers at 8.4% and new customers at 4.2%, and the issuer says pricing was relatively stable, so pricing power is not demonstrated. Revenue is recurring in relationship but not in volume, because contracts carry no minimum volumes and customers can end them at short notice. It is cyclical with hiring and exposed to customers taking screening in-house or using AI-driven hiring tools. That means a hiring slowdown reduces revenue without any contract being lost, which is the first break in the downside path.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F09; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fa-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Cost of services, which is mostly third-party data, government fees, fulfilment and customer care, is 54.33% of FY2025 revenues, so a large part of cost varies with volume and protects margin when orders fall. The issuer says third-party fees are typically invoiced to customers as pass-through costs, but its risk factors state that fee increases cannot always be passed on, so pass-through is partial. Adjusted EBITDA margin was 28.5% in the nine months to September 2026 against 28.1% a year earlier, with automation and operating leverage credited by management, while the International segment margin fell to 5.95% from 16.12%. Margins can be defended against a moderate volume fall, but technology and overhead costs are largely fixed, so a deeper fall would cut EBITDA faster than revenue.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F04; calculation F07; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 90

### Capital intensity and FCF conversion

The business needs little physical capital but invests continuously in software, and capital expenditure, mostly capitalized software, rose 56.85% in the nine months to September 2026. Last-twelve-months operating cash flow is 51.89% of adjusted EBITDA and levered free cash flow 36.39%, after cash interest, cash taxes that rose 67.50% and working-capital outflow as revenue grew. The issuer does not split maintenance and growth capital expenditure, so the capex that could be cut in a downturn is not established. EBITDA therefore translates into positive but modest free cash flow, 8.90% of total debt, which supports debt service and some deleveraging but not rapid repayment.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F03

### Porter's five forces

Rivalry is moderate to high: the market is fragmented, with a limited number of multinational firms, and the issuer competes on accuracy, turnaround, compliance and price, so competitors can force price concessions that would cut margin. New entrants are a live threat from applicant tracking and payroll providers that build screening into their products, which could erode volumes and the integrations that sustain retention. Substitutes are customers insourcing screening and AI-driven hiring tools that lower hiring volumes. Buyer power is high, because customers can leave at short notice, buy from several providers and are often steered by human capital software partners. Supplier power is meaningful, since data providers and laboratories are non-exclusive, some are owned by competitors, and fee increases cannot always be passed on, which pressures margin at the cost-of-services line.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 40; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 54; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 56

### PEST analysis

Regulatory and economic factors are material and the others are covered under competition. Consumer-reporting law, state fair-chance laws and privacy laws shape which checks customers may use, and the company remains bound by consent decrees with the US Federal Trade Commission, so a compliance failure would add cost or restrict products; the accrued legal liability is USD 8.5 million. The hiring cycle is the economic driver of volumes, and floating-rate debt makes interest rates the second economic factor.

### SWOT analysis

- Strengths: retention of about 96% and a broad customer base; USD 490.2 million of liquidity with no principal due before 2028; positive levered free cash flow; scale as one of a limited number of multinational providers.
- Weaknesses: leverage near four times EBITDA with floating rates; revenue tied to hiring without minimum volumes; thin free cash flow conversion; sponsor control and an acquisitive record.
- Opportunities: continued voluntary paydown and earnings growth could bring leverage lower, and identity-verification and monitoring products add recurring-type revenue according to management.
- Threats: a hiring slowdown, in-sourcing and AI-driven hiring tools, regulatory action, SOFR rising once swaps expire, and a further debt-funded acquisition or payout.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The strongest credit support is a sticky, broad customer base, because gross retention of about 96% and the absence of any customer at 10% of revenue steady cash flow and make a sudden revenue loss unlikely; the implication is better debt-service visibility than the transactional contracts alone suggest. Second, earnings growth and voluntary prepayments have cut leverage, lifting cover to 3.17x. Third, USD 490.2 million of liquidity with no principal before 2028 gives time to absorb a downturn. Fourth, levered free cash flow is positive at 8.90% of total debt.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F01; calculation F02; calculation F03; calculation F07

The main weaknesses are, first, leverage of 3.91x net, or 4.19x pro forma for the acquisition of 4 November 2026, with 74.00% of principal floating and unhedged after 2026, which makes interest cost and leverage sensitive to rates and earnings. Second, a sponsor-controlled financial policy with a record of a payout and repeated acquisitions means cash and debt can move away from creditors. Third, revenue follows hiring and contracts carry no minimum volumes, so a downturn cuts revenue and EBITDA quickly. Fourth, free cash flow is only 36.39% of adjusted EBITDA while capital spending and cash taxes rise.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F01; calculation F03; calculation F05; calculation F07

### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale and market position are average, with revenue large enough to rank among the few multinational providers but a fragmented market. Competitive advantage is average: integrations with human capital platforms, proprietary data and 96% retention are offset by short-notice termination and entrants. Diversification is average, with a broad customer base and no concentration but one service line, one country at 85.81% and hiring-driven demand. Cost and capex flexibility is average, since variable cost of services helps but software spending is rising. Margin stability is average and free cash flow stability average, with a negative levered free cash flow year in the acquisition period and a positive trend since. The ability to refinance is average with low confidence because no rating or market data exist and no principal falls due before 2028. Liquidity is strong at USD 490.2 million before the acquisition. Financial policy and governance is average, weighed down by sponsor control but helped by a stated no-dividend policy and voluntary paydown. The main supports are liquidity, retention and the deleveraging trend; the constraints are leverage, rate exposure and hiring sensitivity; the trend is improving; the missing datapoints are the credit agreement, ratings and the 2026 target.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over twelve to twenty-four months the tailwinds are continued earnings growth, lower interest from the 2025 repricing and 2026 prepayments, and no principal due before 2028; the headwinds are the expiry of swaps on 31 December 2026, integration and funding of the 2026 acquisition, and any weakening of hiring. Over three to five years the first scheduled instalment falls in 2028, the revolver matures in October 2029 and the term loan in October 2031, so refinancing arrives only if leverage has not fallen. Management cites identity fraud, contingent workforces and analytics as sector tailwinds; headwinds are in-sourcing, AI-driven hiring tools, privacy and fair-chance rules and competition. No company guidance is supplied, so the outlook is qualitative.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F02; calculation F05; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 13–15; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 39

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a hiring slowdown across retail, transportation and healthcare customers. Orders fall at once because contracts carry no minimum volumes, revenue follows, and EBITDA falls faster than revenue because technology and overhead costs are largely fixed, while cost of services at 54.33% of revenue flexes. Cash interest does not fall with volumes and rises if SOFR rises after the swaps expire, so cover narrows first: a 25.84% fall in reported EBITDA returns cover to 2.35x. Free cash flow then shrinks and prepayments slow, so net leverage rises, and a fall of 13.43% on pro forma net debt returns it to the 4.84x of end-2025. Management levers are to stop buybacks and acquisitions, defer software capex and keep prepaying; liquidity of USD 490.2 million before the acquisition and no principal due before 2028 mean resilience appears sufficient to avoid distress in this scenario, though the buffer and the revolver covenant test above 40.0% use must be watched. Observable triggers are screening volumes, adjusted EBITDA margin, cash after the acquisition and any new payout.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F01; calculation F02; calculation F03; calculation F05; calculation F06; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fa-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 12

### Overall credit view

Overall, First Advantage presents an adequate business profile supported by sticky customers, scale among multinational screening firms and ample liquidity, but credit quality is constrained by leverage near four times EBITDA, floating-rate exposure, hiring-driven volumes and a controlling sponsor with an acquisitive record. Cash-flow durability appears adequate: interest is covered 3.17x and free cash flow is positive, but conversion is thin. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on leverage at 2028 and later maturities and on whether the sponsor keeps using cash for deals. The primary downside path is a hiring slowdown that cuts EBITDA faster than revenue while rates rise on unhedged debt. Further analysis would require the credit agreement, ratings and market data, the target and cash after the 2026 acquisition, and the proxy statement.
