## Findings

### Bottom line

Silver Lake's control, at 51.4% with an approval right over debt above USD 100.0 million, makes financial policy a sponsor decision, and the record since 2023 mixes debt repayment with acquisitions and cash returns, so creditors cannot rely on steady deleveraging.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 72; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 156; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fa-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fa-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23

The operating business is the stronger credit point: a broad customer base, no customer at 10% of FY2025 revenues and gross retention of about 96%, set against revenue that follows hiring volumes and a regulatory regime that includes consent decrees. Sponsor and governance risk is medium: no related-party payments or sponsor fees are disclosed, but cash was paid out in 2023 and acquisitions continue.

### Key findings

Silver Lake control decides financial policy, and its consent right is a sponsor check, not a creditor protection. Silver Lake beneficially owned 51.4% of the common stock at the end of 2025, down from 51.7% a year earlier, and under the stockholders' agreement its approval is needed for debt above USD 100.0 million, joint ventures above USD 100.0 million and a change of control for as long as it holds at least 25%. A large debt-funded acquisition or recapitalisation therefore cannot happen without the sponsor, so financial policy follows the sponsor's view. Creditors rely on the credit agreement for protection, and that agreement is not supplied, so capacity to pay out cash, move assets or prime lenders is not assessed.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 72; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 73; 10-K_2024-12-31_fa-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 73; calculation B06

Documented sponsor behaviour is mixed, not adverse. The only large payout was the USD 217.7 million special dividend of August 2023, equal to 133.73% of that year's operating cash flow and paid from cash without any borrowing, before the Sterling acquisition. Since the acquisition the company has made voluntary term loan prepayments of USD 65.0 million in 2025 and USD 95.0 million in the nine months to September 2026, says it expects to pay no dividends, and has authorised a USD 100.0 million repurchase programme that excludes shares held by SLP Fastball Aggregator, L.P. and its affiliates, whom the filing does not describe further. No management fees, monitoring fees or related-party payments are disclosed, though the proxy statement that would show them is not supplied.
Basis: calculation B05; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 75; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 156; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 169; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 170; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fa-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fa-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47

Acquisitions are a repeated strategy, so integration and funding risk recurs. Infinite ID was bought for USD 41.0 million in September 2023, Sterling in October 2024 at a value of about USD 2.2 billion, with 22.30% paid in stock and the rest in cash funded from cash on hand and a new term loan of USD 2,185.0 million, and a data platform business on 4 November 2026 for USD 125.0 million in cash with USD 45.0 million drawn on the revolver. Each deal brings integration cost, with transaction charges of USD 125.7 million in FY2024 and integration expense of USD 18.1 million in FY2025. The latest deal is small at 5.69% of Sterling's consideration, but it followed USD 95.0 million of paydown, so creditors should expect leverage to rise again when a suitable target appears, and the target, its earnings and the funding of the other USD 80.0 million are not stated.
Basis: calculation B04; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 102; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 151–152; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 154; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 155; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fa-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fa-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23

The business is a high-retention, transactional screening franchise whose revenue still moves with hiring. Gross retention was about 96% in both 2024 and 2025, the customer count stayed in the tens of thousands in both years, and no customer reached 10% of FY2025 revenues, although one customer was about 12% in FY2024 and FY2023. Against that, most revenue is pre-onboarding screening ordered per individual, contracts carry no minimum volumes and customers can leave on short notice without penalty, so a hiring slowdown lowers revenue without any contract being lost. The annual reports state customers, retention, tenure and screens for both years, which corrects the earlier view in Financial foundation that no such series is stated; two years show stable retention but are too short to show a trend.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 54; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 145; 10-K_2024-12-31_fa-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-K_2024-12-31_fa-20241231.htm.jsonl, page 26

Revenue is concentrated by country and split almost evenly between the legacy and acquired businesses. The United States generated 85.81% of FY2025 revenues, and Sterling supplied 49.07% of gross segment revenues against 44.30% for First Advantage Americas and 6.62% for First Advantage International. The International segment is the weak part: its revenues fell 6.17% in the nine months to September 2026 and its Adjusted EBITDA margin dropped to 5.95% from 16.12%, so it adds little cushion.
Basis: calculation B01; calculation B02; calculation B03; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 103; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 173; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fa-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 41

Regulatory and legal exposure is structural but contained today. The company remains bound by consent decrees with the US Federal Trade Commission inherited from a 2013 acquisition, requiring compliance with consumer-reporting law and a biennially audited security programme, and it carries an accrued legal liability of USD 8.5 million. A breach would add cost or restrict products, but the filings document no breach.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fa-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19

Governance disclosure is strong where it is audited and thin where it is incorporated by reference. Statements are audited, with an opinion that they present fairly, and internal control was effective, quarterly reports are filed and no material related-party transactions are reported, but directors, pay and independence sit in a proxy statement that is not supplied. Overall, First Advantage presents medium sponsor and governance risk: the key supported behaviours are the 2023 payout and repeated acquisitions, which matter because they move cash and debt at the sponsor's discretion, and imply that deleveraging is a choice, not a commitment. Countervailing evidence is the voluntary paydown and the stated policy of no dividends. Further analysis requires the proxy statement and the credit agreement.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against reading sponsor behaviour as mixed, not adverse, is the 2023 special dividend, paid to shareholders of whom Silver Lake is the largest, and the speed with which a new acquisition followed the 2026 paydown; together they suggest cash is returned or redeployed whenever liquidity allows. The view would turn adverse if the company announced a debt-funded dividend or buyback, if a new amendment widened sponsor consent to higher leverage, or if the credit agreement proved to allow wide payouts or asset transfers. It would improve if the proxy statement showed no sponsor fees or related-party payments, if the 2026 target proved modest and low-risk, and if voluntary prepayments continued at the 2026 pace. A fall in Silver Lake's holding below 25% would remove its approval rights and change who decides debt questions.
Basis: calculation B04; calculation B05; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 72; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 169; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fa-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fa-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23

The strongest evidence against the stability of the business is that customers can end contracts at short notice and that revenue follows hiring. A rise in lost accounts, the loss of a large customer or gross retention falling below the recent level of about 96% would change the view of revenue visibility.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 54

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

First Advantage completed the acquisition of Sterling Check Corp. on 31 October 2024. The transaction valued Sterling at about USD 2.2 billion and was paid with cash on hand, new debt and common stock: Sterling's holders received about USD 1,168.1 million in cash and shares with a fair value of USD 494.6 million, and Sterling's own debt was repaid. At closing the company refinanced its existing credit agreement into a first lien facility with a term loan of USD 2,185.0 million due in 2031 and a USD 250.0 million revolver due in 2029, which is the only funded debt.
Basis: calculation B04; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 151–152; 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 155

On 30 July 2025 the margins were reduced to 2.50% to 2.75% over SOFR on the term loan and 2.25% to 2.75% on the revolver, and prepayments made in connection with repricing carried a 1.00% premium until 30 January 2026. The company prepaid USD 65.0 million of the term loan in 2025 and USD 95.0 million in the nine months to September 2026, with no penalties.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_fa-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 156; 10-Q_2026-09-30_fa-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 12

Effective 4 November 2026 the company acquired a U.S.-based data platform business and a small employment background screening operation for USD 125.0 million in cash, subject to working-capital adjustments, and borrowed USD 45.0 million on the revolver. The filing does not name the target, state its earnings or cash, or say how the other USD 80.0 million was funded.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_fa-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23
