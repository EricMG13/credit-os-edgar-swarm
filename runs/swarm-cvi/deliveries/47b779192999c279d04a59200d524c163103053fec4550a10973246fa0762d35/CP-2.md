## Findings
### Bottom line
CVR Energy's liquidity exceeds its total debt today, but its earnings are cyclical and driven by commodity prices and regulation, so creditors are covered now and exposed to a sharp reversal and to how the pending USD 4,200 million acquisition is financed.

Last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA of USD 919 million, of which Q3 2026 supplied 63.33%, compares with USD 393 million in FY2025, so total debt of USD 1,781 million is 1.94x Adjusted EBITDA today and would be 4.53x at the FY2025 level. Liquidity is USD 1,853 million and no note matures before 2028. Net debt of USD 503 million is USD 513 million pro forma for the dividend declared on 2026-11-03; net debt pro forma for the acquisition and the CVR Partners distribution is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C3

### Key findings
Earnings are at a cyclical peak and one quarter carries most of it. Adjusted EBITDA was USD 317 million in FY2024 and USD 393 million in FY2025, then USD 828 million in the first nine months of 2026, and Petroleum Adjusted EBITDA rose from USD 127 million to USD 621 million as the NYMEX 2-1-1 crack spread averaged USD 55.16 against USD 25.62. The issuer takes the crack spread as given, so the mechanism is an external margin that can reverse within a quarter, and creditors should treat last-twelve-months coverage of 6.96x as a peak reading.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-11-03_d897437dex991.htm.jsonl, pages 2 and 9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 56; calculation C2

Leverage is low today but roughly doubles on earlier-year earnings. Total debt of USD 1,781 million is 1.94x last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA, 4.53x the FY2025 figure and 5.62x the FY2024 figure, and annual fixed coupons of about USD 120.71 million are covered 7.61x by the last twelve months but only 3.26x at the FY2025 level. The leverage reduction came from earnings, not debt repayment, because total debt moved from USD 1,765 million to USD 1,781 million over the nine months, so earnings at the FY2025 level would put leverage close to the 4.49x of year-end 2025.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C2; calculation C4; calculation C5

Liquidity and maturities give a long runway on the existing structure. Cash of USD 1,278 million (cash pro forma for the declared dividend, the CVR Partners distribution and the acquisition is not established) and undrawn credit facilities of USD 575 million make USD 1,853 million, 2.53 times the USD 733 million of notes that fall due in 2028, and the largest credit facility matures in 2031. The runway is narrower than it looks because USD 158 million of the cash sits in the consolidated nitrogen partnership and the company has agreed to hold at least USD 500 million of unrestricted cash until the acquisition closes or the agreement ends. Net debt of USD 503 million is USD 513 million pro forma for the dividend declared on 2026-11-03, and the pro forma figure after the acquisition and the CVR Partners distribution is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 59; calculation C1; calculation C3; calculation C6

Free cash flow is real but volatile and partly timing. House free cash flow after capital and turnaround spending was USD 872 million in nine months of 2026 against negative USD 238 million in FY2025 and USD 172 million in FY2024, so conversion of Adjusted EBITDA ranged from 105.31% to negative 60.56% to 54.26%. The 2026 figure benefits from USD 132 million of income tax that was accrued but not yet paid and from only USD 2 million of turnaround spending, with the next major turnaround scheduled for 2027, so durable conversion is lower than the nine-month figure suggests.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 27 and 61; calculation C7

The Renewable Fuel Standard is a second earnings driver and a cash contingency. Waivers removed obligations of about USD 488 million in August 2025 and about USD 253 million in August 2026 and appear as non-cash gains in reported EBITDA, while the accrued obligation is USD 230 million; if challenges succeed, reinstating the USD 253 million would equal 27.53% of last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA in cash. This is why Adjusted EBITDA, not reported EBITDA, is the better coverage base, and why a ruling date, not a quarter's results, can reset the cash position.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 23–25 and 58; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C8

The acquisition would change the credit more than any operating trend. The cash consideration of about USD 4,200 million is 4.57x last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA and 2.36 times existing total debt, and the named backstops include a secured bridge of up to USD 2,500 million that alone equals 2.72x last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA. The sources give no sources and uses table, no target earnings and no bridge draw, so pro forma debt and leverage are not established, and secured bridge debt would rank ahead of the USD 1,183 million of CVR Energy unsecured notes on pledged assets.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12 and 32; Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C9

Control and structure add leakage that consolidated figures hide. One holder owns about 71% of the stock, the controlled-company exemptions are in use, and the nitrogen partnership paid USD 67 million of its USD 110 million of nine-month distributions to public unitholders and USD 3 million to the controlling holder, so CVR Energy received about 19.51% of nitrogen EBITDA. Creditors of the CVR Energy notes therefore hold a claim on a group whose consolidated cash and EBITDA overstate what reaches them, and the documented sponsor record, a dividend suspended in October 2024 and no priming or drop-down disclosed, is mixed rather than adverse.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 11 and 62

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest case against the peak-earnings view is balance-sheet strength that does not depend on the cycle. Total debt is USD 72 million below liquidity, no note matures before 2028, the credit facilities are undrawn, the company states compliance with all covenants, and Q3 free cash flow on the issuer's own definition was USD 593 million, so even a sharp earnings fall leaves several years of runway on the existing structure. The Wynnewood subsidiary has also been relieved by waivers in two successive Augusts, and the refineries ran at 100.1% of crude capacity against 75.6%, which suggests operations, not only price, improved. The view would soften if fourth-quarter results hold near the third-quarter level, because the weak fourth quarter of 2025 drops out of the last twelve months, and if the acquisition closes with more equity than debt. It would harden if the bridge is drawn in full, if the waivers are reversed, or if the closing financing leaves unrestricted cash below the USD 500 million undertaking after the tax and dividend calls already identified.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17 and 59; EX-99.1_2026-11-03_d897437dex991.htm.jsonl, pages 9 and 11; calculation C10

### Limitations
- No rating, peer financials or market price was supplied, so the strong and weak judgements below are relative to the issuer's own record and absolute measures, and the comparison with typical leveraged-credit issuers in refining is not established.
- Three years of annual data cannot establish mid-cycle earnings, so the FY2025 and FY2024 sensitivities are reference points rather than forecasts and do not bound the downside.
- The acquisition target's earnings, working capital and purchase accounting are not supplied, so the favourable liquidity reading describes the existing group and not the combined group after closing.
- Fourth-quarter 2026 results and the closing financing arrive after the 2026-11-05 cutoff, so the near-term outlook rests on company guidance and seasonality rather than on reported figures.

### Strengths and weaknesses
The strongest support is liquidity and the maturity profile. Liquidity of USD 1,853 million exceeds total debt, all credit lines are undrawn and nothing falls due before 2028, so debt service in the next two years depends on earnings only at the margin.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C6

A second strength is a low-cost regional crude position. The gathering system supplied about 67% of Coffeyville and 94% of Wynnewood crude demand in FY2025, and the two refineries ran at 100.1% of capacity in 2026, so margin capture rests on owned logistics, which the annual report describes as a transportation cost advantage over crude sourced elsewhere.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cvi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 20; EX-99.1_2026-11-03_d897437dex991.htm.jsonl, page 9

A third strength is a second earnings stream with separate funding. The nitrogen partnership earned USD 205 million of EBITDA in nine months against USD 191 million, funds itself through its own secured notes and credit facility, and diversifies away from refining margins, although its cash is only partly available to CVR Energy.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 56–57 and 62

A fourth strength is a refinancing record that cleared the 2027 to 2029 maturities. The February 2026 issue of USD 1,000 million of notes and the credit facility extension moved the nearest note maturity to 2028 and the facility to 2031, which reduces refinancing risk before the acquisition.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The main weakness is earnings that depend on external margins and rulings. Last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA is 2.34x the FY2025 figure, 63.33% of it from one quarter, and waivers that can be challenged lifted reported results, so the leverage ratios can reverse faster than debt can be repaid.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C2; calculation C8; calculation C13

A second weakness is the pending acquisition, which adds debt, execution and integration risk while funding terms are unknown. Secured bridge debt of up to USD 2,500 million would sit ahead of the unsecured notes, and no pro forma leverage is stated.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12; calculation C9

A third weakness is concentrated control with weak visibility into limits. One holder controls the issuer, relies on the controlled-company exemptions, and the indentures, credit agreements and board independence are not in the sources, so protection against leakage cannot be tested.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A)

A fourth weakness is unquantified legal, environmental and operating exposure. An ammonia release, class claims in Kansas, a guaranty dispute and an EPA settlement project are open, and the filings do not size them, so they limit confidence in the liquidity runway.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25–26 and 34

### Company description
CVR Energy is a holding company whose cash flow comes from two mid-continent refineries, a controlling interest in a nitrogen fertilizer partnership and, until December 2025, a renewable diesel unit. Refining supplied 93.40% of nine-month 2026 sales and nitrogen 6.60%; the renewables business, which had an EBITDA loss of USD 22 million in FY2025, ended when the unit reverted to hydrocarbon processing. Bulk refined products are sold to retailers, railroads, farm cooperatives and other refiners at spot prices based on a regional differential, and nitrogen products are sold under fixed-price contracts of less than one year, so revenue visibility is limited to a quarter or two and debt service depends on liquidity rather than contracted revenue. The business is seasonal: refining earnings are lower in the first and fourth quarters, and nitrogen sales are higher in the first half.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cvi-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 21–22, 26 and 116; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Ownership and group structure
Icahn Enterprises and its affiliates own about 71% of the common stock, the issuer is a controlled company relying on the exemptions from independent board requirements, and a sale of the controlling stake could trigger a repurchase offer at 101% of principal and a default under both credit facilities. The issuer owns about 37% of the common units and all of the general partner of CVR Partners, which is consolidated, has its own secured notes and credit facility, and is excluded from the guarantees of the CVR Energy notes, while no CVR Energy guarantee of its notes is described. The implication is a two-tier creditor structure in which structural priority at the nitrogen assets sits with CVR Partners creditors, and leverage tolerance, dividends and acquisitions are set by one holder; the sponsor record is mixed, with dividends suspended in October 2024 and equity of up to USD 500 million committed to the acquisition, and the legal limits on leakage are not established.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_cvi-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 76–78

### Revenue drivers and pricing power
Revenue is price and volume driven with little pass-through protection. Nine-month 2026 net sales rose 49.48% to USD 8,000 million on higher gasoline and distillate prices and higher throughput volumes after the 2025 turnaround, and the refining margin was USD 19.28 per barrel against USD 15.93, yet the issuer is a price taker on both crude and products. The largest petroleum customer was 12% of petroleum sales and the top two nitrogen customers 28% of nitrogen sales in FY2025, so concentration is moderate, and nitrogen prices followed global commodity markets, with UAN at USD 348 per ton against USD 307. Revenue is therefore cyclical, commodity-linked and not contracted, which is why creditors should lean on liquidity and maturities rather than revenue visibility.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cvi-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 22 and 28; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9 and 46; EX-99.1_2026-11-03_d897437dex991.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C11

### Cost structure and margin resilience
Most cost is crude and feedstock, so margins depend on the spread, not on cost control. Cost of materials was 79.98% of nine-month 2026 net sales and direct operating expenses 7.16%, direct operating cost per barrel fell to USD 6.06 from USD 6.62 while utilization rose, and commodity derivative losses of USD 516 million in the nine months show that hedging transfers risk between periods rather than removing it. Adjusted EBITDA margin was 10.35% in nine months of 2026 against 5.49% in FY2025, so margins can be defended in a strong spread year but not under a spread squeeze, because fixed operating cost and a turnaround cycle remain.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9 and 19; EX-99.1_2026-11-03_d897437dex991.htm.jsonl, page 2; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C12

### Capital intensity and free cash flow conversion
Capital spending is moderate but lumpy and the cash conversion shown for 2026 is flattered. Capital spending of USD 147 million in nine months of 2026 was 66.67% maintenance, the 2026 estimate is USD 220 million to USD 240 million, and growth projects include an alkylation unit of about USD 155 million due in late 2027 and a flare gas system of about USD 53 million. Turnaround cash was USD 197 million in FY2025 and USD 2 million in nine months of 2026, and the next major turnaround falls in 2027, so house free cash flow of USD 872 million should not be annualised. Cash interest of USD 132 million over the last twelve months, finance leases and the unpaid income tax are the other main calls on that cash.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 34 and 60–61; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C7; calculation C12

### Porter's five forces
Rivalry is price-based: the issuer competes with several regional refineries and with product moved in by pipeline, and nitrogen is a global commodity market with larger producers, so margins are set by the market and cannot be defended by the issuer. The sources do not discuss new entry; as an Analyst Judgement the capital intensity of refining limits it, which supports the issuer's regional position. Substitution has already hit the renewable diesel unit, which the issuer reverted for poor economics, and the regulatory credit regime, not product substitution, is the live threat. Buyer power is moderate: the largest refining customer is 12% of sales, though nitrogen contracts are short. Supplier power is contained by the owned gathering system, but the remaining crude depends on pipelines subject to proration, and pet coke for the Coffeyville fertilizer plant came 36% from the adjacent refinery in 2025, so an outage at one site affects the other.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cvi-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 20–22, 27–29

### PEST analysis
Regulation and the commodity economy are primary credit drivers; social and technology factors are not. The Renewable Fuel Standard, waiver decisions and pending appeals move earnings and cash, an EPA settlement requires a flare gas project, and agricultural and refining prices set both segments' margins, so these two categories are included. Other categories do not appear to be primary credit drivers based on the provided materials.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 23–25 and 34

### SWOT
Strengths: liquidity and a 2028 maturity profile, regional crude supply, a second earnings stream in nitrogen, and a refinancing record, each of which supports debt service on the existing structure.
Weaknesses: earnings concentrated in one quarter, dependence on external margins and rulings, controlled-company governance with limits not visible, and unquantified legal and environmental exposure, each of which raises the chance that leverage reverses.
Opportunities: closing the acquisition with a large equity component, a restart of growth capital funded from cash flow, and the nitrogen capacity expansion of about 5%, each of which would improve scale or earnings if executed without adding unsecured claims behind secured debt.
Threats: reversal of the RFS waivers, a sharp fall in crack spreads, a bridge drawn in full on closing, and a change of control, each of which would hit liquidity or leverage.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12, 25 and 34

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds
Over the next 12 to 24 months the tailwinds are strong spreads and full utilization, with fourth-quarter guidance of 95% to 100% crude utilization, and the headwinds are seasonally weaker refining quarters, the 2027 Wynnewood turnaround, the unpaid tax and the closing of the acquisition. Over three to five years the main supports are the extended credit facility and the 2028 notes being the next maturity, while the 2028 cluster of USD 733 million, a larger post-acquisition debt stack and a possible reinstatement of waived obligations are the main pressures. Each tailwind supports free cash flow and deleveraging only if margins hold, and each headwind pressures liquidity or refinancing confidence, so the outlook is balanced on the existing group and negative on a combined group whose financing is not stated.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-11-03_d897437dex991.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 34 and 61; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Qualitative downside scenario
The scenario starts with the crack spread reverting from USD 55.16 toward the USD 25.62 of nine months of 2025. Refining margin falls first, and Petroleum Adjusted EBITDA returns toward the USD 199 million of FY2025, taking consolidated Adjusted EBITDA back to the USD 393 million of FY2025. Leverage on existing debt becomes 4.53x and fixed-coupon cover 3.26x, and house free cash flow turns negative as it did in FY2025 if a turnaround resumes. The calls already identified, USD 132 million of accrued income tax, a USD 10 million dividend and, if waivers were reversed, USD 253 million, total USD 395 million, or 21.32% of liquidity, so the existing structure would not reach liquidity distress inside the next year; management levers are a dividend suspension as in October 2024, deferred growth capital and the undrawn facilities. The first real break is market access, not cash: with the acquisition closed and a secured bridge drawn, USD 2,500 million of secured debt would rank ahead of USD 1,183 million of unsecured notes at a time when earnings had fallen, and the observable triggers are the crack spread, Petroleum Adjusted EBITDA, the bridge draw and any waiver reversal. Primary downside path: a crack-spread reversal coinciding with a debt-funded closing.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.1_2026-11-03_d897437dex991.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_cvi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 115; calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C10

### Overall credit view
Overall, CVR ENERGY INC presents an adequate business profile supported by liquidity, regional crude supply and a second earnings stream, but credit quality is constrained by earnings concentrated in a peak quarter, a regulatory contingency and a pending debt-funded acquisition. Cash-flow durability appears adequate on the existing group and not fully assessable for the combined group, because free cash flow swung between negative USD 238 million and USD 872 million across periods. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on crack spreads, the closing financing and the outcome of the waiver challenges, and the business profile against typical leveraged-credit issuers in the sector is not established. The primary downside path is a margin reversal that meets a debt-funded closing. Further analysis would require the indentures and credit agreements, the closing financing, target earnings and ratings. Fundamental credit analysis completed. Top risk: refining-margin reversal combined with the acquisition's secured financing.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)
