## Findings
### Bottom line
Icahn Enterprises controls this refining-led issuer and its documented record toward creditors is mixed rather than adverse, but the pending USD 4,200 million refinery acquisition, partly financed by the controlling holder, will test that alignment.

Refining supplied 93.40% of sales in the nine months of 2026, and the nitrogen partnership that is 37% owned adds a separate creditor group. Dividends resumed at USD 10 million a quarter in 2026 and no priming, drop-down or creditor-adverse amendment is documented, but director independence, covenant terms and the acquisition's funding mix are not established in the sources. Sponsor and governance risk is assessed as Medium.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12, 18 and 28; calculation C1

### Key findings
One holder controls the issuer, and the limits on that control sit in documents that were not supplied. Icahn Enterprises and its affiliates owned about 71% of the common stock at September 30, 2026 against about 70% at the end of 2025, the company relies on all three controlled-company exemptions from the independent-board requirements, and a sale of the controlling stake could oblige the issuers to offer to repurchase the notes at 101% of principal and could be an event of default under both credit facilities. The controlling holder therefore influences board composition, dividends, financing and acquisitions, and creditors can only test that influence through indentures and credit agreements that are not in the source set.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cvi-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 76–78; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 11 and 34

Refining margin and regulation, not scale, drive creditor risk. Refining supplied 93.40% of sales in the nine months of 2026 and 89.58% in FY2025, and the two refineries ran at 100.1% of crude capacity against 75.6% a year earlier, when the 2025 Coffeyville turnaround took capacity out. The refining margin was USD 19.28 per barrel against USD 15.93, and the NYMEX 2-1-1 crack spread averaged USD 55.16 against USD 25.62, so the improvement owes much to price, which the issuer does not control, as well as to the volume recovery. The nitrogen business is only 6.60% of sales but produced 22.45% of nine-month EBITDA, so a small revenue line carries a large part of earnings and sits in a separate creditor group.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18; EX-99.1_2026-11-03_d897437dex991.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 8 and 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_cvi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 159; calculation C1; calculation C2

The Renewable Fuel Standard turns a regulatory ruling into earnings and into a cash claim. The Wynnewood subsidiary is an obligated party, and the EPA waived its obligation by about USD 488 million in August 2025 and by about USD 253 million in August 2026, while the accrued obligation was USD 230 million at September 30, 2026 against USD 72 million at the end of 2025. Waiver gains are non-cash and can be challenged: challenges are pending in the federal appeals court and the EPA proposed in October 2026 to spread the 2025 exempted volumes over 2026 and 2027 or add them to 2027, so a successful challenge would reinstate an obligation and turn an earnings gain into a cash outflow on RIN purchases, which the company already accrues in full for 2026.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 23–25 and 58

Sponsor payout behaviour is moderate against cash generation and responded to weaker earnings. Total dividends were USD 453 million in 2023 and USD 151 million in 2024, of which the controlling holder received USD 311 million and USD 100 million, and house free cash flow after capital and turnaround spending was USD 686 million and USD 172 million. The board suspended the dividend in October 2024 as margins fell, none was paid in 2025, and payments resumed in 2026 at USD 10 million in each of the second and third quarters, USD 20 million over nine months against house free cash flow of USD 872 million. That record supports a mixed rather than extractive reading, but it is also a record of discretion: nothing supplied limits the next distribution.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cvi-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 120–121, 133 and 178; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 10 and 28; calculation C3

The acquisition shifts the creditor question from past payouts to how the next USD 4,200 million is funded. On November 2, 2026 the issuer agreed to buy two Marathon Petroleum refineries and related assets for about USD 4,200 million in cash. The named backstops are a senior secured 364-day bridge of up to USD 2,500 million, a USD 800 million convertible preferred placement with a commercial counterparty and up to USD 500 million of equity from the controlling holder, which total USD 3,800 million, so about USD 400 million plus fees would come from cash on hand, credit-facility borrowings or an equity offering. The sources give no sources and uses table, no pro forma EBITDA and no bridge draw, and the controlling holder's commitment ends if the company completes an equity offering of at least USD 500 million, so sponsor support is committed rather than funded. The company has agreed to hold at least USD 500 million of unrestricted cash until closing or termination.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12, 31–32 and 59; calculation C4

Management changed in mid-2026 while the finance chair is interim. A separation agreement with the former chief executive and an employment agreement with the former chief financial officer, now chief executive, are both dated June 18, 2026, and the third quarter report is signed by an interim chief financial officer who is also the vice president of financial planning and investor relations. The auditor issued an unqualified opinion with no critical audit matters, but a thin finance bench during a USD 4,200 million transaction raises execution and reporting risk that creditors cannot test, because the proxy statement with board independence and compensation is not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cvi-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 128–129 and 191–192; 10-Q_2026-06-30_cvi-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 57; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 73

CVR Partners is consolidated but only partly reachable by CVR Energy creditors. The issuer owns about 37% of the common units and all of the general partner, public unitholders hold about 60% and the controlling holder about 3%, and the partnership's own executives are CVR Energy employees under a services agreement that can be ended on 90 days' notice, which creates a stated conflict of interest. Of USD 110 million distributed in nine months of 2026, CVR Energy received USD 40 million, public unitholders USD 67 million and the controlling holder USD 3 million, so CVR Energy received about 19.51% of the nitrogen EBITDA of USD 205 million. Consolidated cash and EBITDA therefore overstate what is available to holders of the CVR Energy notes, which the CVR Partners subsidiaries do not guarantee.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cvi-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 80–81, 151, 153 and 157; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 62 and 57; calculation C5

Unquantified legal and environmental matters sit beside the debt. An ammonia release at the Coffeyville fertilizer facility in October 2025 has produced consolidated suits in Texas and a further suit in August 2026, class claims over environmental conditions in Kansas are in discovery, a guaranty dispute over alleged historical well contamination is stayed during mediation, and a flare gas recovery project of about USD 53 million is being installed under the 2023 EPA settlement. The company describes these matters as early-stage and possibly material, so the exposure is a limit on certainty and a possible call on liquidity rather than a number.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25–26 and 34

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest evidence against a mixed reading is the combination of control, discretion and a large pending deal. The controlling holder received USD 311 million of dividends in 2023, relies on the controlled-company exemptions, is considering further strategic transactions that include options for CVR Partners, and is providing up to USD 500 million of equity into the acquisition at the same price per share as an outside investor. The evidence for a more favourable reading is that the dividend was suspended when earnings fell, no uptier, drop-down, priming or creditor-adverse amendment appears in the sources, and the 2026 refinancing replaced a secured term loan and the 2029 notes with unsecured notes. The view would turn adverse if the bridge is drawn in full and dividends continue, if the equity commitment is replaced by a public offering that is smaller than the need, or if the indentures show wide restricted-payment capacity; it would improve if the sponsor funds equity before closing and the final structure keeps unsecured creditors' position intact.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cvi-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 76–78 and 178; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17, 32 and 34; calculation C3; calculation C4

### Limitations
- Director independence, committee composition and executive pay are in a proxy statement that was not supplied, so the conclusion on board alignment with creditors rests on filing text and signatures only and is not a finding that the board is independent or not.
- No indenture or credit agreement was supplied, so the conclusion that no priming or drop-down is documented means none is disclosed in the filings, not that the documents forbid one; restricted-payment and investment capacity are not established.
- The purchase agreement, debt commitment letter and subscription agreements are known only through the company's description, so deal conditions, the funding mix at closing and the size of any bridge draw are not established and the acquisition view stays provisional.
- The sponsor's own financial position, fund terms and any other holdings are outside the sources, so the sponsor-support conclusion is limited to what the issuer's documents state and does not assess the sponsor's capacity to fund its commitment.
- The dates of the chief financial officer's departure and the interim appointment are not stated in the filings read, so the management-transition conclusion rests on signature pages and exhibit lists.
