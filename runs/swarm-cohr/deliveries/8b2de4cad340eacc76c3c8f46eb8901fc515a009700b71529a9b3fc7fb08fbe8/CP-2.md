## Findings

### Bottom line

Coherent's credit profile is strong on scale, earnings growth and equity but weak on cash conversion, so creditors rely on earnings growth and outside funding, not free cash flow, and are exposed if AI datacenter demand pauses before the build-out pays back. Total debt was 2.07x derived EBITDA of USD 1.56 billion in fiscal 2026, yet free cash flow was negative USD 1.02 billion and capital spending over the last twelve months, USD 1.77 billion, exceeded EBITDA.

Refinancing risk is low until 2029, after which USD 2.14 billion falls due in fiscal 2030, and liquidity of USD 2.22 billion would cover about 2.56 quarters at the latest quarter's free cash outflow. Governance and sponsor risk is low.

### Company description

Coherent makes lasers, optical transceivers and engineered materials for AI datacenter, communications and industrial customers, and it has become mainly a datacenter and communications business: that segment supplied 74.10% of fiscal 2026 revenue and 82.34% of the latest quarter, while the Industrial segment shrank through two disposals. Manufacturing is vertically integrated and mostly in Asia, which supports cost and supply control but concentrates production in a region exposed to trade and export policy. The credit linkage is that growth and a multi-year NVIDIA agreement improve cash-flow visibility, while concentration in one end market and a handful of buyers weakens it.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_iivi-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 14, 128; 10-Q_2026-09-30_iivi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Ownership and group structure

The company is widely held with no controlling shareholder or sponsor disclosed, and the preferred stock that ranked ahead of common equity converted in the quarter ended 2025-12-31, so there is no sponsor with an extraction motive and also none to cure a shortfall. The secured credit facilities are guaranteed by the borrower and certain subsidiaries and secured on substantially all their assets, the 2029 notes are guaranteed by the domestic guarantors, and the foreign facilities of USD 803 million have no parent guarantee, so lenders to those subsidiaries have first call on their assets. Silicon Carbide LLC is owned about 75% by the company, and USD 581 million of restricted cash at 2026-09-30 is usable only inside that subsidiary.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_iivi-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 105, 107, 116; 10-Q_2026-09-30_iivi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 41, 42; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Revenue durability

Revenue is cyclical and concentrated and follows customer capital spending; the filings show no recurring or contracted revenue base. The mechanism is that AI datacenter customers order capacity ahead of their own buildouts, so revenue follows their spending, and the company itself says the upturn may not be sustainable and that orders can be cut with little notice. Customer prepayments rising to USD 333 million and the NVIDIA agreement raise visibility, but backlog, price and volume and contract terms were not disclosed, so revenue durability is average rather than strong.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_iivi-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 28, 29; 10-Q_2026-09-30_iivi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 7; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Margin resilience

Margins expanded sharply, but the company states that certain sales, research and overhead costs are relatively fixed, so operating leverage cuts both ways. The mechanism is that gross margin of 39.1% in the latest quarter and an EBITDA margin excluding gains and charges of 23.66% reflect the cost reductions, yield gains and pricing optimisation the company cites, whereas large customers have sought price concessions in the past and some key inputs come from sole or limited sources. Margins can probably be defended under moderate volume pressure but not under an abrupt order pause, and management's target of non-GAAP gross margin above 45% before the end of calendar 2028 is a target, not a result.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_iivi-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 28, 29; EX-99.1_2026-11-04_d176836dex991.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 3; calculation C03

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

EBITDA is not converting into free cash flow: capital spending of USD 1.10 billion in fiscal 2026 and USD 1.77 billion over the last twelve months, plus inventory growth, absorbed all of it. The mechanism is a build-out of indium phosphide and optics capacity and of work in progress ahead of revenue, with USD 527.73 million of capital spending still unpaid at 2026-09-30 and the NVIDIA capacity agreement possibly requiring investment through 2030. Capital spending is flexible only for the part not committed, and the filings do not say how much that is, so cash conversion should be treated as weak until operating cash flow turns positive.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_iivi-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 60, 79, 88; 10-Q_2026-09-30_iivi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 5, 42; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C06

### Competitive forces

Competitive rivalry and technology change matter through pricing and share: the company says its markets change fast, alternatives can be more cost-effective, and large customers have bargained for concessions, so margin gains can erode. On an analyst reading, new entry is limited by the capital and process know-how that indium phosphide and laser manufacturing need, which protects margin, but the AI efficiency risk the company names could reduce compute and optical demand, and buyer power is high because a few customers dominate revenue. Supplier power is also real for sole and limited-source inputs and an industry-wide shortage of indium phosphide, which can cap output and raise cost.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_iivi-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 13, 26, 27, 28, 29, 60

### Policy, trade and macro factors

Trade and regulatory factors are a material credit driver here. Tariffs and export controls are named as risks, an export-control inquiry into past sales to Huawei is unresolved, long-lived assets in China rose to USD 0.97 billion, and a preliminary USD 50 million CHIPS Act memorandum supports the Texas expansion. On rates, the issuer's own sensitivity is USD 7 million of extra interest expense a quarter for a 100 bp change had it not hedged, and an interest rate cap of USD 1.5 billion notional covers part of that exposure, while the balance sheet foreign exchange hedging programme has been paused indefinitely, so currency moves on balance sheet exposures are not covered by it.

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a delay or cut in orders from one or two large AI datacenter customers, or an efficiency shift that reduces optical demand, both named by the company as risks. The first operational break is underused new capacity and inventory that stops turning, with USD 3.34 billion of inventory, USD 11.8 billion of purchase commitments and USD 527.73 million of unpaid capital spending already in place. Revenue would fall below next-quarter guidance of USD 2.55 billion to USD 2.75 billion, gross margin would be at risk of falling below the 40% low end of guidance because costs are relatively fixed, and free cash flow would stay negative as capital spending is paid. Liquidity of USD 2.22 billion lasts about 2.56 quarters at the latest quarter's outflow, before undrawn foreign facilities, and if EBITDA fell back to its fiscal 2025 level net debt would be 2.15x EBITDA. Management levers are uncommitted capital spending, slower purchasing, drawing foreign lines, further equity of the kind NVIDIA provided, and selling the entity still held for sale. Whether resilience is sufficient to avoid liquidity distress is not established, because at the current outflow liquidity lasts under three quarters and avoiding distress would depend on management levers and market access that the filings do not evidence; observable triggers are a sequential fall in datacenter revenue, inventory growing faster than revenue, unpaid capital spending rising and foreign facility draws rising.
