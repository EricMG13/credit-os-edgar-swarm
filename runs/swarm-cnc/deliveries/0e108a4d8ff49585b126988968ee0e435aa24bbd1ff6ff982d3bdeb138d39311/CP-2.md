## Findings

### Bottom line

Centene's credit quality is adequate but not yet secure: the 2026 earnings recovery and falling debt support creditors, but 2027 revenue is shrinking, parent-level cash is thin and leverage of 4.11x rests on nine months of recovered earnings.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F3; calculation F4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 46

Default risk within twelve months appears low because the USD 4,000 million revolver is undrawn, no senior note matures before July 2028 and EBITDA covers cash interest 6.03x. The risk is slower deleveraging if Marketplace, Medicaid contract and Medicare Star rating losses outrun margin gains, and if the fourth quarter proves to be a loss quarter as guidance allows.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 48; EX-99.1_2026-10-27_a20261027ex991pressrelease.htm.jsonl, page 4

Earnings therefore have to come from margin, not growth.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is that total debt is falling and the 2025 loss was largely non-cash. Total debt fell USD 1,859 million in nine months, EBITDA could fall 83.42% before cash interest cover reached 1.00x, the USD 6.7 billion goodwill impairment in 2025 did not use cash, operating cash flow was USD 5,088 million that year, and the issuer raised its 2026 earnings guidance and reports a faster recovery than expected. The view would improve if the fourth quarter stays within the 2026 health benefits ratio range of 90.4% to 91.1%, final 2026 risk adjustment transfers confirm the 2026 margin, the Georgia and Texas protests succeed and Medicaid rates keep pace with acuity. It would weaken if the ratio exceeds the top of that range, if risk adjustment reverses, if the protests fail, if unregulated cash available for corporate use falls below USD 175 million or if buybacks resume while leverage is above the 2024 level of 2.99x.

### Limitations

- The issuer states no EBITDA and the last-twelve-months figure is derived from statement lines, so the 4.11x leverage and 6.03x cover are measures built here that may differ from a lender definition, and a different definition could move the leverage conclusion.
- The fourth quarter of 2026 is not reported and guidance gives only floors and ranges, so year-end leverage, the full-year health benefits ratio and the cash effect of the expected fourth-quarter CMS settlement of USD 2,668 million of 2025 plan year receivables and of the USD 1,000 million participating interest are not established, and the improving trend could reverse.
- Marketplace risk adjustment for 2026 is still an estimate that the issuer says will be updated through 2027, so the conclusion that Commercial margin has been repaired could be overstated by a later transfer, and the size of any change cannot be judged from the filings.
- Premium by state is disclosed only for Florida and New York, each about 10% or more of Medicaid premium revenues in 2025, so the size of the 2027 revenue loss from the Hawaii, California and commercial group exits is not quantified and the weak-visibility conclusion rests on direction rather than amount.
- Statutory capital is disclosed only as an aggregate at the end of 2025 with a risk-based capital level above 350% of the authorized control level, and entity-level dividend capacity and approvals are not supplied, so the conclusion that parent liquidity is thin cannot be tested against how much subsidiary cash is truly surplus.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Scale and program breadth are the strongest support. Centene describes itself as the largest Medicaid and Marketplace insurer and the largest stand-alone prescription drug plan provider, with Medicaid at 52.49%, Medicare at 24.34% and Commercial at 20.59% of nine-month premium and service revenues, so no single program decides debt service and gains in one can absorb losses in another, as Medicare gross margin rose USD 813 million in the nine months of 2026 while Marketplace membership shrank.
Basis: calculation F2; 10-K_2025-12-31_cnc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 94; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 44–45

Deleveraging and liquidity access are the second support. Total debt is USD 1,859 million lower than at year end 2025, the debt-to-capital ratio is 41.1% against a stated covenant maximum of 0.60 to 1.00, the revolver is undrawn to March 2030 and the issuer states that cash, operating cash flow and the revolver are sufficient for at least 12 months, so near-term repayment risk is low even though covenant capacity is not inferred.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 47–48

Cost discipline is the third support. The adjusted SG&A ratio fell to 7.0% from 7.3% for the nine months and capital expenditures were USD 568 million, or 13.25% of nine-month operating cash flow, so fixed spending leaves room to protect cash if revenue falls, although enterprise optimization costs of USD 358 million absorb cash first.
Basis: calculation F9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 43; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 45

The weakest material dimension is revenue visibility, and the primary downside pathway runs through it. Contract exits, Marketplace attrition and lower Star ratings lower premium from 2027 and 2028 while Medicaid margin is only 6.44% of premium and service revenues, so there is little cushion if medical costs run ahead of rates, and the monitor is the quarterly health benefits ratio against the 2026 range and the 2027 contract awards.
Basis: calculation F2; calculation F11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 37–38; EX-99.1_2026-10-27_a20261027ex991pressrelease.htm.jsonl, page 15

Earnings depend on risk adjustment and reserve conservatism. Commercial supplies 38.79% of gross margin on a shrinking membership base, favourable claims development of USD 1,860 million is 1.41% of premium, and the issuer attributes it to reserving under moderately adverse conditions, so a change in either would reduce margin faster than revenue.
Basis: calculation F2; calculation F6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19

Parent liquidity and structure visibility are weak. Cash available for general corporate use is USD 175 million against total debt of USD 15,542 million, regulated subsidiaries paid USD 1.7 billion of dividends in the nine months and received USD 623 million of capital even though their statutory capital and surplus exceeded the required minimum at the end of 2025, and the legal rank, guarantors and security of the credit facilities are not stated, so structural priority cannot be judged.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 46; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 49; 10-K_2025-12-31_cnc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 113

Cash flow is volatile. Operating cash flow ranged from USD 154 million to USD 5,088 million between 2024 and 2025 because of pharmacy rebate timing, risk adjustment payables and state receivables, and the third quarter of 2026 used USD 3,669 million, so free cash flow cannot be relied on to service debt in a single period.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cnc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 109; EX-99.1_2026-10-27_a20261027ex991pressrelease.htm.jsonl, page 3

### Company description and ownership

Centene is a government-programs managed care insurer that operates through regulated health plan subsidiaries, which means cash is generated in entities whose dividends and capital are restricted. Premium is set by states and the federal government, so revenue depends on contract awards, rate setting and rule changes rather than on pricing power, while the holding company relies on subsidiary dividends and its own revolver for debt service. No controlling shareholder or sponsor is disclosed and governance risk is rated Medium by Business & governance, so creditors rely on the operating company and cannot look to a parent for support.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_cnc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 94; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 49

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is regulated, recurring and concentrated in government payers. Premium and service revenues grew 19.98% in 2025, helped by 26% Marketplace membership growth and Medicare drug plan growth, and only 3.30% in the nine months of 2026, because rate increases and drug plan growth offset Marketplace and Medicaid membership losses. States and the federal government set rates and award contracts, so pricing follows acuity data and procurement outcomes, and the 2027 changes in California, Hawaii and commercial group, the unresolved Georgia and Texas protests and the Arizona long term care rebid, with an award expected in early 2027, make the contract base less predictable than the 2026 guidance suggests. Medicaid contracts with Florida and New York each supplied about 10% or more of Medicaid premium revenues in 2025, so state outcomes matter more than the number of states suggests, although no customer exceeded 10% of total revenues. Illinois was renewed from January 2027 and several dual-eligible contracts started in 2026, which partly offsets the losses.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Medical costs are variable and driven by acuity and utilisation, so margin resilience depends on matching rates to costs. In 2025 the health benefits ratio rose to 91.9% from 88.3% because of lower Marketplace risk adjustment revenue, higher Marketplace costs, drug plan program changes and higher Medicaid behavioural health, home health and high-cost drug costs, which cut derived EBITDA before impairment to USD 2,536 million in 2025 from USD 6,200 million in 2024. In 2026 the ratio improved to 89.9% for nine months, but the third quarter was 92.7%, the same as a year earlier, and full-year guidance of 90.4% to 91.1% implies a higher ratio in the fourth quarter, which fits the issuer's note that Medicare earnings are higher early in the year and lower late in the year as cost sharing progresses.
Basis: calculation F7; calculation F3; 10-K_2025-12-31_cnc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 107; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; EX-99.1_2026-10-27_a20261027ex991pressrelease.htm.jsonl, page 1; EX-99.1_2026-10-27_a20261027ex991pressrelease.htm.jsonl, page 4

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital intensity is low and capital expenditures are flexible, but cash conversion is dominated by working-capital timing. Capital expenditures of USD 568 million for nine months are mainly systems and hardware, and about USD 200 million more is expected in the rest of 2026, so they take only 13.25% of operating cash flow. Operating cash flow instead moves with the timing of pass-through payments, pharmacy rebates, risk adjustment and the USD 21,495 million claims float, which means a fall in days in claims payable from 48 days would absorb cash that earnings do not replace.

### Industry forces and policy

Buyer power rests with governments. States and the federal programs set rates, award or withdraw contracts and set Star ratings, so Centene faces concentrated buyers and a loss of a contract removes premium without negotiation, as the Florida and Hawaii outcomes show. Rivalry is most visible in Marketplace, where corrective pricing covered 95% of membership and the market contracted, and in Medicaid procurement, where protests are the contest mechanism. Providers and the pharmacy benefit manager are the main suppliers, and the January 2024 change of pharmacy benefit manager delayed rebate receipts and cut 2024 operating cash flow. Substitution takes the form of state fee-for-service in California, and Centene is also entering off-exchange employer-funded individual coverage arrangements in 13 states.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 37; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 30; 10-K_2025-12-31_cnc-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 109

Political and regulatory factors are the primary credit driver. The expiry of enhanced Marketplace subsidies and the Marketplace rule raised projected morbidity, federal law adds Medicaid work requirements and more frequent redeterminations that could raise morbidity largely from 2027 subject to state plans, and provider tax and state directed payment changes from 2028 may reduce federal Medicaid funding, which could raise acuity faster than rates. Economic factors matter through medical cost trend and lower interest rates, which cut investment income, and social and technology factors are not primary credit drivers on the evidence supplied. Medicare dual-eligible integration from 2030 is both an opportunity and a procurement risk.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 29–31; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 43

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months the tailwinds are rate increases, Marketplace repricing that covered 95% of membership, drug plan growth, the retirement of the 2027 notes and cost programs, and the headwinds are the 2027 contract exits, lower Star ratings that reach 2028 revenue, Marketplace attrition and risk adjustment updates through 2027. Over three to five years the outcome depends on whether Medicaid rates keep pace with acuity after the 2027 work requirements and the 2028 funding changes, and on whether dual-eligible integration from 2030 favours Centene. Together these point to flat revenue and margin-led earnings, so deleveraging is likely to be gradual and driven by debt repurchases rather than growth.
Basis: calculation F1; calculation F13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 36–38; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47

### Downside scenario

The most plausible downside starts with a lower than expected final 2026 Marketplace risk adjustment transfer or a Medicaid acuity shift after the 2027 eligibility rules, which would lift the health benefits ratio above the 2026 range of 90.4% to 91.1%. The first operating break is a negative gross margin swing in Commercial or Medicaid while contract exits already shrink premium, and the financial effect is a fall in EBITDA that lifts leverage above 4.11x even if debt keeps falling. The USD 1,860 million of favourable claims development, which the issuer attributes to reserving under moderately adverse conditions, could also shrink if reserving normalised. Consolidated cash stays large, but only USD 175 million is freely available at the parent. Liquidity would then rest on the USD 4,000 million revolver, on regulated subsidiary dividends, which were USD 1.7 billion in the nine months of 2026, and on a pause in note and stock repurchases, and a drawn revolver would be the first observable trigger. The 2028 notes with a carrying value of USD 2,101 million would then be refinanced from a weaker earnings base. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress within 12 months, because the issuer states that cash, operating cash flow and the revolver cover that period.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F4; calculation F8; calculation F6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 47–48; EX-99.1_2026-10-27_a20261027ex991pressrelease.htm.jsonl, page 4

### Overall credit view

Overall, Centene presents an adequate but not fully secure business profile supported by government-program scale, a lower debt balance and an undrawn revolver, but credit quality is constrained by shrinking 2027 revenue, narrow Medicaid margins and thin parent liquidity. Cash-flow durability appears weak to adequate because operating cash flow swings with timing and risk adjustment. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the 2028 and later notes, market access and ratings that are not supplied. The primary downside path is a Commercial and Medicaid margin shortfall on a shrinking revenue base. Further analysis would require premium by state, executed debt documents, agency ratings and the fourth quarter results.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F2; calculation F4
