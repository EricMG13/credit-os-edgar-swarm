## Findings

### Bottom line

Terex's fundamental credit is adequate but unproven: net leverage eased to 3.06x at 2026-09-30 (3.01x pro forma for the sale completed on 2026-10-23) and liquidity is USD 1,207 million, yet REV earnings are only partly in the ratio and Aerials margins are weak.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

No significant debt maturities until 2029 protect creditors from a refinancing squeeze. The primary downside path is a cyclical fall in rental and infrastructure demand that compresses Aerials and equipment margins while REV integration is still unproven, pushing leverage back toward the 4.59x seen at 2026-03-31. Free cash flow of USD 253 million in nine months covered USD 133 million of shareholder returns.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F1; calculation F11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 47

### Key findings

Leverage has improved quickly but on a base that is not yet like-for-like. Net leverage fell from 4.59x at 2026-03-31 to 3.06x at 2026-09-30, with net debt of USD 2,171 million (3.01x and USD 2,138 million pro forma for the sale of two Specialty Vehicles businesses completed on 2026-10-23), but it was 2.89x at 2025-12-31 before REV, and the last-twelve-month earnings include REV only from 2026-02-02 with no pro forma EBITDA printed.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Source readiness (CP-0)

Liquidity and maturities are the main support. Company-stated liquidity was USD 1,207 million at 2026-09-30 with the USD 800 million revolver undrawn (pro forma cash after the October 2026 sale is not established), and the first principal maturity of size is the USD 600 million of 5% notes due in 2029, 23.1% of the principal ladder. The only maintenance covenant is tested above 30% revolver usage, a trigger of USD 240 million against a peak quarter-end drawing of USD 170 million.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F6; calculation F7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 47

Cash generation covers shareholder returns, but the merger consumed the cash. Free cash flow of USD 253 million in nine months 2026 was 47.4% of EBITDA (Reported), the unadjusted measure used for leverage, against 51.9% in FY2025 and 31.4% in FY2024. Dividends and buybacks took USD 133 million, leaving USD 120 million, yet net debt rose USD 359 million to USD 2,171 million (USD 2,138 million pro forma for the 2026-10-23 sale) because the REV cash price was USD 467 million.
Basis: calculation F1; calculation F2; calculation F3; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 48

Add-backs flatter Adjusted EBITDA and funding outside debt qualifies the leverage picture. Adjusted EBITDA of USD 727 million for nine months includes USD 193 million of add-backs, 26.5% of the total, mostly deal costs and purchase accounting, so the leverage ratios here use the smaller EBITDA (Reported), which is conservative. Receivables sold of about USD 562 million, customer advances of USD 447 million, supplier finance of USD 51 million and repurchase and bond commitments of USD 460 million and USD 619 million sit outside debt, so a slowdown in orders or in factoring would reach liquidity before it reaches leverage.
Basis: calculation F4; calculation F12; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 48; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, pages 29–30

Margin stability is the weakest material dimension. Aerials earned a 5.4% Adjusted EBITDA margin on 30.0% of nine-month sales, its FY2025 segment operating margin fell 6.2 percentage points to 5.0% from 11.2%, and the Environmental Solutions margin fell to 17.0% from 19.8% on unfavourable mix, a utilities production ramp-up and lower refuse vehicle volumes, so the group Adjusted EBITDA margin slipped to 11.8% from 12.1% even with REV added.
Basis: calculation F9; calculation F14; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_tex.htm.jsonl, pages 115–116; 10-K_2025-12-31_tex.htm.jsonl, page 58; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, pages 39–42

REV is now the largest earnings contributor and its integration is the central uncertainty, while the older businesses are well spread. Specialty Vehicles produced USD 262 million, 36.0% of group Adjusted EBITDA of USD 727 million, inside a purchase price allocation that is still provisional and with consumer recreational vehicles alongside public-service vehicles; management gives no REV synergy figure. Revenue is otherwise diversified, with no customer above 10% of sales and a USD 7.0 billion backlog at 2026-09-30.
Basis: calculation F5; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-K_2025-12-31_tex.htm.jsonl, page 18

Financial policy is the swing factor. Buybacks restarted in Q3 and up to USD 500 million more was authorised in October 2026, the 2.5x net leverage target of the FY2024 report is no longer stated, and legal limits on restricted payments were not supplied, so willingness to put debt reduction first cannot be shown; Business & governance rates governance and financial-policy risk as medium.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2024-12-31_tex.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 34

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a more positive view is that leverage may already be lower than 3.06x: the last-twelve-month base holds REV for about eight of twelve months, Adjusted EBITDA was USD 286 million in Q3 2026 against USD 186 million a year earlier, and the 2026 outlook of USD 930 million to USD 1 billion of EBITDA, if met, would exceed the USD 710 million of EBITDA (Reported) in the ratio. With USD 1,207 million of liquidity and no maturities until 2029, the profile could then be judged strong on cash flow. The outlook dates from 2026-02-13 and has not been updated.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-K_2025-12-31_tex.htm.jsonl, page 59

The view would weaken if net leverage on the enlarged perimeter stays near 3.06x once a full twelve months of REV is in the base, if the Aerials margin falls below 5.4%, if buybacks outrun free cash flow or if revolver usage approaches the USD 240 million test trigger. It would strengthen if the company prints pro forma EBITDA that confirms leverage near 3x, restates a numeric leverage target or applies Aerials sale proceeds to debt.
Basis: calculation F6; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Strengths and weaknesses

Liquidity and a back-ended maturity profile are the strongest support. Company-stated liquidity of USD 1,207 million and an undrawn USD 800 million revolver mean a cyclical downturn would not force a refinancing before the USD 600 million of 5% notes fall due in 2029, which are 23.1% of the ladder, and the term loan and 2032 notes fall due later, so liquidity distress inside two years would need a sustained shock to earnings and cash flow, not a maturity.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 47

Cash generation covering distributions is the second strength. Free cash flow of USD 253 million exceeded dividends and buybacks of USD 133 million by USD 120 million in nine months 2026, so shareholder returns were covered by free cash flow on the evidence available, and the working capital ratio improved to 15.0% from 23.2%, which supports further cash release but could reverse.
Basis: calculation F1; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, pages 48–49

Product and customer diversification is the third strength. Four segments each carry 20.5% to 30.0% of nine-month sales, no customer exceeds 10%, and the backlog of USD 7.0 billion gives visibility for the enlarged group, so no single end market decides debt service.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-K_2025-12-31_tex.htm.jsonl, page 18

A share-funded merger is the fourth strength. REV was paid 83.6% in shares, so the larger of the two acquisitions added little debt, and net debt moved by USD 359 million rather than the USD 3,384 million consideration.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F1; calculation F13

The first weakness is leverage on an unproven base. Net leverage of 3.06x (3.01x pro forma) sits above the 2.89x of 2025-12-31, includes REV earnings for only part of the period, and relies on EBITDA (Reported) because 26.5% of Adjusted EBITDA is add-backs, so lenders cannot yet test a like-for-like ratio.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F4

The second weakness is Aerials. At a 5.4% margin and 13.8% of group Adjusted EBITDA it cannot absorb a volume fall, and production cuts, unfavourable customer mix and tariffs cut its FY2025 operating margin by 6.2 percentage points, which makes group margin stability the weakest dimension.
Basis: calculation F5; calculation F9; 10-K_2025-12-31_tex.htm.jsonl, page 58; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, pages 41–42

The third weakness is dependence on REV. Specialty Vehicles supplies 36.0% of group Adjusted EBITDA inside provisional purchase accounting and brings customer advances and dealer repurchase commitments, so integration slippage would hit earnings and liquidity together.
Basis: calculation F5; Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1)

The fourth weakness is financial policy. Resumed buybacks, a new authorisation of up to USD 500 million and a leverage target that is no longer numeric weaken the anchor creditors can hold management to while legal limits are untested.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-K_2024-12-31_tex.htm.jsonl, page 11

The fifth weakness is interest burden. EBITDA over interest expense fell to 3.54x in FY2025 from 6.80x in FY2024 and was 3.70x on a last-twelve-month basis, and the floating-rate term loan means each 100 bp adds about USD 12.27 million of interest before any hedge.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 29

### Company description and ownership

Terex makes capital equipment in four segments: Environmental Solutions (refuse bodies, utility equipment), Materials Processing (crushing, screening, material handling), Specialty Vehicles (fire and ambulance vehicles, terminal trucks, recreational vehicles, from REV) and Aerials (aerial work platforms, telehandlers). Equipment is sold through distributors, rental companies and direct sales, so cash flow follows customer capital spending, and the installed base supports parts and service whose share of revenue is not printed. The acquisitions of the Environmental Solutions Group in October 2024 and REV in February 2026 moved the mix toward waste, utilities and emergency vehicles that management describes as less cyclical, which supports cash-flow visibility if the claim holds.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-K_2024-12-31_tex.htm.jsonl, page 11

Terex is listed, with no controlling holder named in the supplied filings, so there is no sponsor whose support or extraction must be judged; creditors depend on a board with a non-executive chairman and on the debt documents, which were not supplied. Business & governance rates governance and financial-policy risk as medium.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is cyclical and project-driven: in 2025 Aerials sales fell 14.5% on weaker demand from independent rental customers exposed to smaller, rate-sensitive projects, and Materials Processing sales fell 11.6% on macro uncertainty, high interest rates and weak European demand, while national customers and large projects drove the 2026 recovery in Aerials. The mechanism is that rental and dealer customers defer fleet purchases when rates are high, which cuts volumes and absorption; the implication is that revenue durability is average, supported by replacement demand in waste, utilities and emergency vehicles that management calls stable but that is only management language. Price-cost discipline is cited in Materials Processing, and no pass-through figure is printed, so pricing power is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tex.htm.jsonl, page 58; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 39; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins are the weakest dimension. Group Adjusted EBITDA margin was 11.8% for nine months 2026 against 12.1%, operating margin fell to 4.7% from 8.2% mainly on purchase accounting, and Aerials earned 5.4%, so a volume fall with under-absorbed plants would hit Aerials first. Management cites cost-out actions, a plant closure in Aerials and the Terex Operating System, and gross profit margin was 17.6% for nine months 2026 against 20.8% on mix and the REV addition, but fixed versus variable cost is not split in the filings, so operating leverage cannot be sized.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, pages 15–16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 42; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital intensity is low: capital expenditure was USD 90 million in nine months 2026 and USD 118 million in FY2025, so most EBITDA can reach cash, and conversion to free cash flow was 31.4%, 51.9% and 47.4% of EBITDA (Reported) in FY2024, FY2025 and nine months 2026. The 2025 and 2026 figures were helped by a working capital release, from 24.0% of sales at 2024-12-31 to 20.8% a year later and 15.0% at 2026-09-30, and by about USD 562 million of receivables sold in nine months 2026, so a reversal would cut cash conversion; capex is the main lever, with dividends and buybacks of USD 133 million the next. REV transaction costs of USD 19 million in nine months 2026 are another claim on cash, and cash interest paid is not printed for 2026.
Basis: calculation F3; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_tex.htm.jsonl, page 59; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 48

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense and varies by product: the company names no single competitor across segments, and in Aerials the primary competitors named include Oshkosh (JLG), Haulotte, JCB, Manitou and Dingli, which limits price increases and exposes margin to tariffs. Buyer power is high because national rental companies buy a material part of Aerials and Environmental Solutions output and customer consolidation increases pricing pressure, while supplier consolidation limits sources for some parts. Substitution risk and the threat of new entry are not established from the filings, so the credit effect is margin pressure from the largest buyers rather than a loss of volume.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tex.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; 10-K_2025-12-31_tex.htm.jsonl, page 41; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Macro and policy factors

Tariffs and interest rates are material. Higher tariff costs cut Aerials nine-month Adjusted EBITDA, USD 40 million of tariff refunds were recognised in nine months 2026 and further claims await customs review, and high interest rates weigh on rent-to-own conversions and smaller projects, so policy shifts move margins and fleet purchases. Government infrastructure funding and municipal budgets support Aerials, Materials Processing and emergency vehicles, which is management's view only. Other social and technology factors do not appear to be primary credit drivers on the supplied materials.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 42; 10-K_2025-12-31_tex.htm.jsonl, page 58; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 35

### Credit-translated SWOT

Weaknesses are leverage on an unproven base, Aerials margin and dependence on REV, which cap the profile at adequate. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are demonstrated REV synergies, an Aerials sale applied to debt and continued working capital release. Threats are a rental and infrastructure downturn, tariffs, resumed buybacks and a customer-advance reversal.

Financial policy and governance is average, with medium risk in Business & governance. ### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over 12 to 24 months the outlook rests on REV integration, tariffs and the Aerials review. Management's 2026 outlook of USD 930 million to USD 1 billion of EBITDA implies USD 203 million to USD 273 million in the fourth quarter if it uses the Adjusted EBITDA basis, against USD 286 million in Q3, a gap that suggests the outlook is cautious or out of date, and it was not updated. Tailwinds are a USD 7.0 billion backlog, replacement demand and utility grid investment, which support revenue and deleveraging. Headwinds are tariffs, rental customers managing capital spending and absorbing delivered fleets, and higher interest on the floating term loan. Over three to five years the filings give no guidance; the structural sector tailwinds are management's view, so the medium-term view is not established.
Basis: calculation F10; 10-K_2025-12-31_tex.htm.jsonl, page 59; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 39

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a cyclical fall in rental-company fleet purchases and infrastructure projects, as happened to Aerials in 2025 when sales fell 14.5% and production was cut. The first operational break is under-absorption and lower shipments in Aerials, Materials Processing and refuse bodies, followed by weaker orders at Specialty Vehicles and slower customer advances. Financial transmission is a lower EBITDA with margins already thin in Aerials, a working capital build that reverses the release, and less room from receivable sales; a fall of 33.4% in last-twelve-month EBITDA (Reported) would return net leverage to the 4.59x seen in March 2026 (reported net debt is used), and a smaller fall would leave it between 3.06x and 4.59x, the range of pressure this scenario implies. Liquidity would still be supported by cash and the revolver, whose test applies only above USD 240 million of drawings against a peak of USD 170 million, and no maturity falls before 2029. Management levers are suspending buybacks and dividends (USD 133 million in nine months 2026), low capex, working capital release, restructuring in Aerials and the Aerials sale. Resilience therefore appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress, with the credit effect showing as leverage and margin pressure. Observable triggers are falling backlog, Aerials margin below 5.4%, revolver usage rising and buybacks above free cash flow.
Basis: calculation F6; calculation F11; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_tex.htm.jsonl, page 58; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tex.htm.jsonl, page 27

### Overall credit view

Overall, Terex presents an adequate business profile supported by USD 1,207 million of liquidity, product and customer diversification and a share-funded REV merger, but credit quality is constrained by leverage on an unproven earnings base, weak Aerials margins and resumed shareholder returns. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow covered returns by USD 120 million yet depends on working capital and receivable sales. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on REV delivering earnings before the USD 600 million of 2029 notes approach maturity and on the Aerials outcome. The primary downside path is a rental and infrastructure downturn that compresses Aerials and group margins while REV is bedding in. Business profile against typical sector issuers is not established because no peer data were supplied. Further analysis would require pro forma EBITDA, the executed debt documents, ratings and the Aerials outcome. Top risk: leverage rising again on an unproven earnings base.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F1; calculation F7
