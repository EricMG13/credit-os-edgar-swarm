## Findings

### Bottom line

Aecom is a widely held infrastructure consultancy with no sponsor or controlling holder disclosed, so creditors rely on management's financial policy, and the main weakness is two delayed Construction Management projects that are absorbing earnings and cash.

The wider business is diversified: governments supply 50% of revenue, no client reaches 10%, and International earnings before taxes and amortization reached a 10.35% margin in the nine months to 2026-06-30. Against that, one project took a loss of USD 336.8 million, management expects net cash outflows of USD 600 million to USD 800 million to finish two projects, and in those nine months repurchases plus dividends were 3.29x operating cash flow. Management says delayed claims could constrain repurchases, none were made from April to June 2026, and governance risk is medium.

### Key findings

No sponsor stands behind the credit, so support in a downturn has to come from the company's own cash flow and bank lines. Aecom is listed on the New York Stock Exchange with widely held shares, the Chairman is also the Chief Executive Officer, and holder percentages are not established because the proxy statement is not supplied. Shares held by non-affiliates were worth about $12.2bn on 2025-03-28.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_acm_acm-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2; 10-K_2025-09-30_acm_acm-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 132

The core consulting business is diversified and mostly public-sector funded, which is the main support for creditors. Governments supply 50% of revenue and private clients 50%, no client reached 10% of revenue in the past five years, and backlog was $39.7bn at 2025-09-30, $2.3bn higher than a year earlier. Backlog is a gross figure that includes pass-through costs and is $20.0bn above the remaining performance obligations, so it measures visibility, not profit.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_acm_acm-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11; 10-K_2025-09-30_acm_acm-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 13; calculation A13

Contract terms, not demand, are where the losses come from. Guaranteed-maximum-price and fixed-price work was 61.61% of fiscal 2025 revenue and on both types cost overruns and delay costs can fall on Aecom, while about half of revenue (53% in fiscal 2025) is pass-through subcontractor cost, so one project can erase the margin earned on a large amount of other work. In the quarter to 2026-06-30 the Americas segment reported a gross loss of USD 143.1 million against a gross profit of USD 238.8 million a year earlier.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_acm_acm-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12; 10-K_2025-09-30_acm_acm-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 53; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 48–50; calculation A01

Two Construction Management projects decide credit quality over the next year, and the business was kept, not sold. Claims for recovery recorded in contract assets rose from about $180 million at 2024-09-30 to about $400 million at 2025-09-30, peaked at about $680 million at 2026-03-31 and stood at about $620 million at 2026-06-30, one project took a loss of USD 336.8 million, and management expects net cash outflows of USD 600 million to USD 800 million to finish two projects, USD 175 million to USD 225 million of it in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026, before any claim inflows. Aecom has also given the project clients corporate guarantees, USD 403.9 million of letters of credit and USD 1.0 billion of surety bonds, and says clients may file a material claim. The strategic review of the business ended with a decision to keep it, so no sale will transfer the exposure.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_acm_acm-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 95; 10-Q_2026-04-03_acm_acm-20260403.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 35–36; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 55; 10-Q_2026-04-03_acm_acm-20260403.htm.jsonl, page 40; calculation A06; calculation A07

Capital allocation has favoured shareholders, but management has named repurchases as the first lever. In the nine months to 2026-06-30 repurchases plus dividends were 3.29x operating cash flow against 0.38x a year earlier, the quarterly dividend rose to USD 0.31 per share from USD 0.26 and USD 0.22, and the USD 1.0 billion authorization had USD 884 million left. Management says further delay in collecting claims could constrain the timing of repurchases, repurchases are discretionary, and no shares were bought from April to June 2026.

The guarantor group is weaker than the consolidated company, so the structure matters more than the consolidated figures suggest. The subsidiary guarantors are wholly owned and guarantee the 2033 senior notes, and the bank facilities are secured by liens on substantially all assets of the company and the guarantors. In the nine months to 2026-06-30 the parent and guarantors recorded revenue equal to 55.66% of the consolidated total, a net loss of USD 168.5 million against consolidated net income attributable to Aecom of USD 167.7 million, and a fall in combined equity of USD 237.9 million to USD 362.0 million. Cash held in consolidated joint ventures is restricted to those ventures.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 21–23; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 42; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 63–64; calculation A08

Disclosure on structure is good, but the claims disclosure trailed the cash exposure. The auditor has named contract cost and claim recovery estimates a critical audit matter in both annual reports, claims grew each quarter before a loss was recorded, and the second-quarter report noted a longer than anticipated claims resolution process, but the loss and the expected cash outflows appear for the first time in the third-quarter report. Management's confidence that the claims will be recovered is management language, not evidence of recovery, and internal control over financial reporting was assessed effective at 2025-09-30.

Acquisitions are small and are not the driver of the credit story, but goodwill makes book equity a thin cushion. Two acquisitions in fiscal 2025 cost $375.9 million in total, of which $146.4 million was paid in Aecom shares, and goodwill is 1.72x AECOM stockholders' equity.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against caution is that the loss is concentrated and the rest of the business is improving: the International margin rose to 10.35% from 8.79%, remaining performance obligations were $20.4bn at 2026-06-30, and two legacy claims have been collected or settled, USD 51.1 million in the third quarter of fiscal 2026 and USD 58.5 million agreed in July 2026, which shows that large claims can be collected, although each followed a write-down of expected proceeds of USD 53.0 million and USD 61.8 million, so collection has been slower and lower than first expected. Claims recorded in contract assets of about $620 million are close to the low end of the expected outflows of USD 600 million to USD 800 million, so full and timely recovery would offset much of the cash cost, which is an analyst inference and not a management position.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 37; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 55; calculation A03

The view would improve if the claims are collected, if the fourth-quarter outflow stays within USD 175 million to USD 225 million, and if repurchases stay paused until then. It would weaken if either project needs a further loss estimate, if a client files a claim against Aecom, if the outflows exceed USD 800 million, or if repurchases resume while the claims remain uncollected.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 35–36; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 55

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

On 2026-03-10 Aecom replaced its revolver and term loans with a $1.5bn revolving credit facility and a $950 million term loan A, both maturing on 2031-03-10, and a $500 million term loan B maturing on 2031-04-19. Both term loans were borrowed in full and the proceeds repaid the previous facilities, which had a revolver and term loan A maturing in 2029 and a term loan B maturing in 2031. Bank debt outstanding was USD 1,448.8 million at 2026-06-30 against USD 1,439.9 million at 2025-09-30, so the amendment extended maturities without adding material debt.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_acm_acm-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 62; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20; calculation A17

On 2026-06-10 a separate $500 million revolving facility maturing on 2028-06-09 was added under a new agreement, and it was undrawn with no letters of credit at 2026-06-30. Both bank agreements are secured by liens on substantially all assets of the company and the guarantors, and a consolidated leverage ratio of at most 4.00x is tested quarterly; the covenant does not apply to the term loan B and the company reported compliance at 2026-06-30.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 20–22

On 2025-07-22 Aecom issued $1.2bn of unsecured senior notes due 2033 and used the proceeds to retire the 2027 senior notes: most were bought in a July 2025 tender offer and the remainder was redeemed in August 2025, with make-whole payments of $9.1 million in total. The notes' estimated fair value at 2026-06-30 was $1,201.5 million, close to principal.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_acm_acm-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 80; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 22; calculation A16

Acquisitions and disposals have been small or already completed. Two businesses were bought in fiscal 2025 for $375.9 million, including $146.4 million of Aecom shares; the Management Services and self-perform construction businesses were exited earlier and remain in discontinued operations; and the AECOM Capital team moved to a third-party platform in fiscal 2024. The strategic review of the Construction Management business, which the 2025 annual report said could include a sale, was later reported complete with a decision to keep and operate it.
