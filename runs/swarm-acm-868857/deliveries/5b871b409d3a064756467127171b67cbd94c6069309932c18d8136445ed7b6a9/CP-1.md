## Findings

### Bottom line

Leverage has risen in fiscal 2026 because earnings fell, not because Aecom borrowed more: net debt reached 2.31x last-twelve-months EBITDA at 2026-06-30 against 1.11x at fiscal 2025 year-end, so creditors now carry a thinner cushion ahead of large expected project cash outflows.

Gross leverage was 3.21x against 2.26x because the third quarter absorbed a USD 336.8 million loss on one Construction Management project while debt stayed flat and cash fell. Cash interest cover cannot be measured on the same basis for the latest twelve months because the quarterly report prints no cash interest. Two dated events after quarter-end, a dividend paid on 2026-07-17 and a settlement due in July 2026, take net leverage to 2.36x or 2.24x if each is taken alone.

### Financial interpretation

Leverage is the main concern in the financial foundation, and the cause is earnings rather than borrowing. Gross debt was almost unchanged between 2025-09-30 and 2026-06-30 while calculated EBITDA for the last twelve months fell as the third quarter absorbed a loss of USD 336.8 million on a Construction Management project and nine months of restructuring and acquisition costs, so gross leverage moved from 2.26x to 3.21x and net leverage from 1.11x to 2.31x as cash fell. For creditors the cushion built in fiscal 2025 is being spent on shareholder returns and project cash outflows, not on debt reduction.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_acm_acm-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 77; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 16; calculation C03; calculation C05

The strongest offsetting evidence is that fiscal year leverage was low and falling before this year: gross leverage fell from 2.50x to 2.26x between FY2024 and FY2025, and cash interest cover on interest actually paid rose from 5.67x to 7.21x. Cover for the latest twelve months cannot be stated on cash paid because the quarterly report omits it; on accrued interest expense it fell from 6.52x to 4.18x, which gives the direction but is not a cash measure. Recovery of the project claims would turn contract assets into cash and ease net debt, whereas further delay would leave a heavier interest burden carried on a smaller earnings base.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_acm_acm-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 78–80; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 3; calculation C05

Cash generation weakened sharply in the nine months to 2026-06-30. Operating cash flow of 169.234 (US$m) compares with 625.472 a year earlier, with the filing attributing a 283.5 million increase in outflow to two Construction Management projects, while payments to repurchase shares of 441.862 and dividends of 115.469 (US$m) together exceeded it. Free cash flow after capital expenditure was 203.449 for the last twelve months against 684.927 in FY2025 (US$m), a conversion of 0.24x of EBITDA against 0.57x. Receivables and contract assets absorbed cash, days sales outstanding rose from 74 to 86 and claims inside contract assets rose, so recovery of the claims is the observable test of whether working capital reverses.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 55–56; calculation C08

Liquidity is adequate against scheduled maturities but is being drawn down, and none of the dated bank facilities expires within the next twelve months. Cash excluding joint-venture cash of 762.018 (US$m) plus undrawn revolvers of 1995.6 gives 2757.618, set against scheduled debt maturities of only 117.8 in total through fiscal 2030 (36.3 in the rest of fiscal 2026 and 34.4 in fiscal 2027), because the March 2026 amendment moved the large bank maturities to 2031, with the 2033 senior notes after them. Against that, management expects net cash outflows of between USD 600 million and USD 800 million to finish two projects, of which USD 175 million to USD 225 million falls in the fourth quarter, before any inflow from claims. Those outflows are not in net debt and would raise it by the amount paid; the filing's maintenance test of 4.00x leverage is not computed here because its definition is not printed.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 21–23; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 55; calculation C09

Customer billings and receivable sales are non-debt funding that carries risk of its own. Contract liabilities were 1,129.26 million at 2026-06-30 and trade receivables sold stood at 348.9 million against 268.2 million at 2025-09-30, so reported net debt does not capture all of the funding that supports operations. If a client dispute or a slowdown in advance billings removed either source, the gap would fall on revolver drawings, which is why days sales outstanding and the sold-receivable balance should be watched with debt.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 56

Two dated events after 2026-06-30 are kept out of the period figures. The quarterly dividend paid on 2026-07-17 would lift net leverage to 2.36x and the Department of Energy settlement, if received in full, would lower it to 2.24x, each taken alone against the same EBITDA; the settlement date is stated only as July 2026. The 2026-03-10 refinancing is a multi-figure event: the cash flow statement shows gross borrowings and repayments of about 5.7 billion because the revolver is drawn and repaid regularly and the old term loans were replaced, so gross flows overstate new debt, and the debt note is the source for outstanding amounts.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-07-03_acm_acm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 56; calculation C07
