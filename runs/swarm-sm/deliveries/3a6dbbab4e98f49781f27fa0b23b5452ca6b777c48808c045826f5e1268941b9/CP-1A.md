## Findings

### Bottom line

SM Energy's creditors face a widely held, merger-enlarged producer that has so far used asset sales and cash flow to reduce debt while also returning capital, so the main governance risk is how far unseen debt documents let that policy go. No sponsor or management fee appears in the sources, which is a strength.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21; 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 97

Board composition, ownership concentration and restricted-payment capacity are not established because the proxy statement and the debt documents are not supplied. Sponsor and governance risk is Medium, resting on behaviour alone: in the nine months to 30 September 2026 the company retired USD 2,160 million of notes and paid USD 353 million to stockholders.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8; calculation C10; calculation C13

### Key findings

Control is dispersed and no sponsor is evidenced, so extraction by an owner is not the risk. The company is a listed Delaware corporation, and former Civitas stockholders held 52 percent of its shares at the merger closing, but the sources name no large holder, and the proxy statement that would show holders, directors and related-party dealings is not supplied.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 97; 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 89

Management is deleveraging and returning capital at the same time, which makes shareholder policy the creditor's main watch point. Debt reduction came from asset sales and cash flow: the final South Texas proceeds of USD 894 million funded redemption of USD 819 million of 2026 notes, and cash flow funded the USD 417 million 2027 notes. Stockholder payments of USD 353 million were 41.8% of free cash flow after capital expenditure, and the base dividend was raised by 10 percent in February 2026.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8; calculation C10; calculation C12; calculation C16

The 2026 tender offer was a creditor-neutral refinancing rather than a distribution. The company bought USD 894 million of its higher-coupon Civitas notes due 2028 at a premium to principal, paying USD 30 million of premium and fees, and funded it with new 2034 notes, so interest cost fell and the 2028 maturity shrank. The offer was open to all holders, and USD 456 million of those 2028 notes stayed outstanding.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19; calculation C15

Integration risk is moderate and mostly behind the company, but the evidence is management's own. The company says integration is substantially complete and that it has actioned about USD 400 million of run-rate synergies against an original target of USD 200 million, with one-time cash costs of USD 227 million inside nine-month adjusted free cash flow; oil volumes were held back by downtime and by takeaway limits in the Uinta Basin of about 4 MBbl per day that management expects to clear in early 2027.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-11-03_exhibit99111032026er.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2; EX-99.1_2026-11-03_exhibit99111032026er.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36

The business is an oil-weighted onshore producer in four areas, which ties creditor cash flow to the oil price. Oil was 88.1% of production revenue in the nine months to 30 September 2026, the Permian Basin supplied 51 percent of revenue, and one purchaser took 28% of FY2025 production revenue. Hedges are a net liability of USD 460 million and settlements cost USD 457 million in the nine months, so hedging cushions the price but has so far cost cash.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 13–14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 24–25; 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 139; calculation C1

Disclosure is good enough to monitor cash flow and debt but not to judge legal capacity. Results, reconciliations and debt tables are published each quarter, yet the covenant definitions were amended on 30 January 2026 without being printed, no covenant ratio is stated, and the credit agreement and indentures are not supplied, so capacity to pay dividends, raise debt or release liens is not established and the rating separates capacity from evidenced behaviour.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 161

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a lower risk level is that every documented action so far is creditor-aligned or neutral: no sponsor, no debt-funded distribution, USD 1,236 million of notes redeemed at par and a tender funded by cheaper debt, with stockholder payments at 12.3% of operating cash flow. The conduct evidence alone would support a Low rating. The rating stays Medium because distribution capacity under the debt documents, holder concentration and related-party dealings are not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21; calculation C14; calculation C11

Sight of the credit agreement and indentures showing tight restricted-payment limits, or leverage reaching the company's stated target of low one-times by year-end 2026, would move the rating to Low. A debt-funded acquisition, faster repurchases while net debt is still elevated, or a concentrated holder pressing for returns would move it to High.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-11-03_exhibit99111032026er.htm.jsonl, page 2

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

SM Energy completed its merger with Civitas Resources, Inc. on 30 January 2026, under a merger agreement signed on 2 November 2025. Each Civitas share became 1.45 SM Energy shares, about 124 million shares were issued, equal to 52 percent of the enlarged share count, and total consideration was USD 2,664 million: cash of USD 226 million, shares of USD 2,409 million and replacement awards of USD 29 million. The company also assumed about USD 4.9 billion of Civitas senior notes at fair value of USD 5,090 million and repaid the Civitas revolver for USD 201 million. The purchase price allocation is preliminary.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 97; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18

The same day the Credit Agreement was amended to permit the assumed notes, add the Civitas subsidiaries as guarantors, raise commitments from USD 2,000 million to USD 2,500 million and the borrowing base from USD 3,000 million to USD 5,000 million, and extend elected commitments to 2031; it also changed financial covenant definitions that are not printed. New debt in 2026 was one issue: USD 1,000 million of 6.625% senior notes due 2034, issued on 9 March 2026 at par for net proceeds of USD 984 million, used mainly to repurchase USD 894 million of higher-coupon 2028 Civitas notes. USD 1,236 million of 2026 and 2027 notes were then redeemed at par, leaving no senior note maturity until mid-2028.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21; 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 158; calculation C14; calculation C15

The South Texas assets of about 61,000 net acres were sold to Caturus Energy, LLC under an agreement of 17 February 2026 at a price of USD 950 million, closing on 30 April 2026. A final post-closing settlement after 30 September 2026, with its day not stated, brought net cash proceeds to USD 894 million and the final gain to USD 260 million. After the quarter end the lenders also reaffirmed the borrowing base and commitments, with the next redetermination on 1 April 2027. Both events fall after the balance-sheet date and are not blended into period figures.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 97; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_sm-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17
