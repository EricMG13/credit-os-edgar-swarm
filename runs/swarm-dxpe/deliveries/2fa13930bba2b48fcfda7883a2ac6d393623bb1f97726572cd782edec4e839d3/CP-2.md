## Findings

### Bottom line

DXP is an adequate fundamental credit whose main exposure is financial policy, not liquidity: broad customers, an 11.17% EBITDA margin and USD 411.0 million of liquidity protect debt service, while acquisitions and a new USD 150.0 million buyback could push floating-rate secured leverage up.
Basis: calculation B01; calculation B03; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_dxpe-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33

Net leverage was 2.45x at 2026-09-30 and cash interest cover 4.20x, and pro forma net debt for the acquisition agreed on 2026-11-01 and for the new buyback is not established. The view rests on ownership, a covenant level and acquired earnings that the documents do not settle.
Basis: calculation B02; calculation B03; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an adequate view is the structure of the debt, not today's ratios. Total leverage of 3.33x, floating-rate secured debt with no hedges, one USD 812.0 million maturity in 2030 and B-category agency ratings leave little room for error if earnings fall, and the twelve-month cash flow that supports coverage includes unusually low taxes and capital spending while acquisitions keep absorbing cash. If a 100 bp margin squeeze arrives as the quarterly gross margin trend suggests, net leverage would reach 2.69x before any buyback.
Basis: calculation B02; calculation B04; calculation B05; calculation B06; calculation B09; 10-K_2025-12-31_dxpe-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 64; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dxpe-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 14

The view would strengthen if executed agreements confirmed a wide covenant cushion and baskets that limit the buyback, if acquired EBITDA showed the 2026 deals earning enough to lower leverage, and if gross margin recovered to its nine-month 2026 level of 31.58%. It would weaken if the buyback were paid from cash while acquisitions continued, if organic sales growth turned negative, if gross margin fell below the FY2024 level of 30.87%, or if the backlog in water and wastewater pumping fell below USD 325.0 million. Strength in liquidity of USD 411.0 million would still delay any liquidity event, so the weakening path is leverage and refinancing, not a near-term cash shortfall.

### Company description

DXP distributes maintenance, repair and operating products to industrial customers from a branch network in the United States and Canada, and also engineers custom pump packages and runs customers' procurement and storerooms. Service Centers supplied 68.10% of FY2025 sales, Innovative Pumping Solutions 19.36% and Supply Chain Services 12.54%, and sales grew from a business founded in 1908 to USD 2,016.4 million in FY2025 largely through acquisitions, 64 of them since 2004.
Basis: calculation B08; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_dxpe-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-12-31_dxpe-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_dxpe-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 99

The model supports debt service because products are replacement and consumable items sold to a wide base, with no customer at 10% of sales, but it ties up working capital: receivables rose USD 52.6 million in nine months 2026. Cash flow is therefore durable in a stable economy and sensitive to industrial activity and to supplier terms.

### Ownership and group structure

DXP is a listed Texas corporation with no sponsor or parent named in the documents. Its chairman, president and chief executive has led it since 1996 and acquired control of the predecessor company in 1986, but his current holding, the board's independence and the identity of the Series B preferred holders are not established; related-party leases are small at 0.50% of selling, general and administrative expense.

Leverage tolerance has been moderate, with net leverage between 2.39x and 2.45x, but acquisition appetite is high at USD 407.7 million across 18 businesses since 2024, and creditors' protection rests on covenants because no outside equity backstop is shown.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is transactional and cyclical, not contracted. Customers buy on purchase orders, DXP traditionally has no long-term customer contracts, and the Supply Chain Services contracts that exist can be cancelled on little or no notice; demand follows industrial production, maintenance budgets and energy capital spending, so sales rose 11.89% in FY2025 and 13.19% in nine months 2026, of which 9.26% was organic.
Basis: calculation B01; calculation B08; 10-K_2025-12-31_dxpe-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-12-31_dxpe-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dxpe-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_dxpe-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 24

Pricing power is moderate. Management says it has kept strong margins despite vendor price increases, and the nine-month gross margin held at 31.58%, but the third quarter shows a lag: input costs cut the gross margin by 65 basis points. Backlog in Innovative Pumping Solutions of USD 325.0 million at the end of 2025 gives some visibility for that segment, though backlog can be delayed or cancelled.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Cost of sales is 68.42% of nine-month 2026 sales, and after product cost labor is the largest expense. Selling, general and administrative expense of 22.65% of sales is mostly payroll, rent, insurance and professional fees, which are semi-fixed: they produced operating leverage in the third quarter, when the ratio fell to 21.81% from 22.88%, and they would work against margin if volumes fell.

Margins can be defended against modest volume pressure because capex and acquisitions are discretionary, but not fully against input-cost inflation or adverse mix: the quarterly gross margin fell from 32.3% to 30.7% during 2026, and a further 100 bp of EBITDA margin would cost USD 22.1 million, 8.95% of twelve-month EBITDA.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is high: the market is highly fragmented, DXP ranked 17th among U.S. distributors of these products, and several competitors have greater resources, so pricing is a constant constraint on margin and on the cash flow that services debt.

### Overall credit view

Overall, DXP presents an adequate business profile supported by customer breadth, an 11.17% EBITDA margin and a growing water and wastewater platform, but credit quality is constrained by acquisition-led growth funded with secured debt, floating-rate debt with one 2030 maturity and financial policy that cannot be verified. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because capital intensity is low and twelve-month free cash flow was USD 105.0 million, tempered by working capital and acquisition draws. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on EBITDA growth and market access in 2029 and 2030. The primary downside path is a gross margin squeeze from input costs in a softer industrial cycle while cash goes to acquisitions and buybacks. The business profile against typical leveraged-credit issuers is not established because no peer data are supplied. Further analysis would require the proxy statement, the executed agreements, acquired EBITDA and peer data. Fundamental credit analysis completed. Top risk: leverage drift from acquisition and buyback spending.
