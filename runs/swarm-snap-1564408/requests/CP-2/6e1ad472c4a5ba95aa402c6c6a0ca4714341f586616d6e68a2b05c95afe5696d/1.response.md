## Findings

### Bottom line

Snap presents an adequate, improving fundamental credit anchored by liquidity rather than earnings: USD 2.8 billion of cash and securities covers debt due before 2030 more than four times, while earnings before stock-compensation add-backs are near zero and litigation is unsized.

Leverage of 2.85x on issuer-adjusted EBITDA hides that this measure excludes USD 1,025,256 thousand a year of stock-based compensation, which Snap offsets with buybacks that exceeded free cash flow in every year since 2023; liquid assets fell 17.94% from the end of 2024. The first maturities are modest, USD 106,318 thousand of convertible notes and a USD 250.0 million facility tranche in May 2027, so the credit is more exposed to slow erosion of liquidity and to the platform-harm cases than to a refinancing squeeze.

### Key findings

Liquidity is the anchor of the credit and is large against what falls due, but it is being spent. Cash and cash equivalents of USD 1,019,215 thousand plus marketable securities of USD 1,751,432 thousand give liquid assets of USD 2,770,647 thousand, which is 4.47x the USD 620,509 thousand of notes due by 2028 and 0.81x all principal of USD 3,420,509 thousand, before an undrawn USD 1.05 billion revolving facility. Liquid assets nonetheless fell by USD 605,632 thousand, or 17.94%, from the end of 2024, a period in which buybacks exceeded free cash flow by USD 590,658 thousand, and the cushion will keep shrinking if buybacks continue to run above free cash flow.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious reading is the speed of improvement. Third-quarter 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of USD 382,647 thousand was 110.20% above the prior-year quarter, the quarter produced net income of USD 56,498 thousand against a loss of USD 103,541 thousand, nine-month free cash flow more than doubled, no debt is secured, no maturity falls in 2029, 2031 or 2032, and the issuer says existing cash is enough for at least twelve months. Buybacks are discretionary and can be stopped at once, which would turn the liquidity trend around without any change in operations, and the 2030 convertible notes' conversion price makes equity dilution from the debt very unlikely.

The view would weaken on advertising revenue turning negative year on year, North America daily-user declines spreading, a ruling for the insurers on 10 December 2026, a sizeable verdict or settlement in the 2027 trials, buybacks continuing above free cash flow while cash and cash equivalents approach the USD 800.0 million covenant floor, or any debt-funded payout or secured debt. It would strengthen if EBITDA before add-backs stays above cash interest through the seasonally weak quarters, if stock-based compensation falls as a share of revenue, if subscription revenue is shown to be recurring, and if the executed documents show tight limits on payouts and liens.

### Limitations

- No peer set, agency rating or market price series is supplied, so the judgement that the profile is adequate is absolute rather than relative to comparable high-yield issuers and could shift when benchmarked.
- The covenant definition and any other maintenance test are known only from a summary, and the sources do not say whether standby letters of credit reduce the revolving facility's availability, so the liquidity finding counts the facility at its face amount and no covenant headroom is computed.
- Loss estimates, accruals and insurance recoveries for the platform-harm and regulatory cases are not stated, so the downside path is qualitative and the cash that could leave outside debt cannot be bounded.
- The split of other revenue between subscriptions and the partner contract is not reported, so the revenue durability finding cannot judge how much of the 85.31% growth continues after the first quarter of 2027.
- The fourth quarter of 2025 is derived from the annual report and nine-month comparatives, and interim cash interest is given to one decimal, so the last twelve months cover and seasonality figures carry rounding error of roughly USD 0.1 million on interest.
- The outlook rests on trend because the issuer provides only limited financial guidance, so the improving direction in the view may not hold if the fourth-quarter advertising season disappoints.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The five main strengths rank as follows. First, liquid assets of USD 2,770,647 thousand cover the USD 620,509 thousand of notes due by 2028 4.47x, so near-term default and refinancing risk is low. Second, operating leverage is working: nine-month 2026 costs rose 4.62% against revenue growth of 15.07%, which lifted free cash flow to USD 570,998 thousand. Third, all debt is unsecured and ranks equally, with no maturity in 2029, 2031 or 2032, so no creditor class is prime and the wall is spread to 2034. Fourth, capital intensity is 2.94% of revenue, which keeps conversion of earnings to cash high and gives capex flexibility. Fifth, the platform reaches 977 million monthly and 495 million daily users, which supports advertising and subscription volumes.

### Company description

Snap runs Snapchat, a camera-first messaging application used by 977 million people a month and 495 million a day in the third quarter of 2026, and monetises it mainly through advertising with a growing subscription and partnership line; it reports one operating segment, employed 5,261 people at the end of 2025 and in April 2026 announced a plan to cut about 16% of staff. Revenue is recognised as advertisements are served or ratably over periods of under 30 days for fixed-fee advertising, ratably over a year or less for subscriptions and ratably over longer terms for partnerships, so visibility is short: unsatisfied performance obligations at the end of 2025 were only USD 387.9 million, all from one partner agreement, and deferred revenue was USD 246.6 million at 30 September 2026 against USD 145.2 million at the end of 2025. For creditors the model gives fast revenue recognition and weak contractual visibility, which supports cash generation in good markets but offers little protection if advertisers cut budgets, and the history includes a 2023 AR Enterprise wind-down, a 2024 restructuring and the 2026 headcount cut, so cost resets are a recurring lever.

### Ownership and group structure

Snap is a listed company controlled by its two founders, who hold over 99% of the votes through Class C shares and about 19.59% of the shares; the Class A shares that carry the public equity are non-voting. The consolidated group is Snap Inc. and wholly owned subsidiaries, with a United Kingdom base holding USD 256,209 thousand of long-lived assets and about 11% of cash and securities held by foreign subsidiaries; no restricted or unrestricted group, finance subsidiary or guarantor is described, so the structural position of the unsecured notes against subsidiary liabilities is not established. Creditors face leverage tolerance that is set by the founders alone, a revolving facility that limits debt, liens, dividends and buybacks, and indentures that do not, which leaves financial policy to discretion; the Business & governance review rated governance risk medium because behaviour so far has been creditor-neutral to supportive.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue growth in nine months 2026 was 15.07%, made of advertising up 5.33% and other revenue up 85.31%. Volume grew 4% in monthly and daily users and price or mix improved, with revenue per daily user of USD 3.48 against USD 3.16, up 10.13%, driven by native ad surfaces such as Sponsored Snaps and Promoted Places and by subscriptions. Revenue is discretionary, short-cycle and exposed to platform policy: advertisers spend on impression-based and short fixed-fee contracts with no long-term commitments, the issuer cites Apple's tracking changes, competition for advertising budgets and its own 2025 ad platform change, and substitution risk is high because the product is free and users and advertisers can move. There is no pass-through, customer concentration is not disclosed, and subscription retention is not reported. The conclusion is that revenue is not structurally declining but is cyclical, discretionary and partly dependent on a fixed-fee partner contract with limited remaining life, so durability supports cash flow in the next 12 to 24 months more than over the 2030 to 2034 maturities.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

The cost base is mostly people and hosting. Personnel-heavy operating expenses and stock-based compensation make up the largest part, research and development alone was 30.89% of nine-month 2026 revenue, infrastructure costs for Google Cloud and AWS were 25.07% and scale with users, while content and developer partner payments fell to 7.01% of revenue from 11.09% as advertising shifted to native surfaces and advertising partner and transaction costs rose to 8.43% with subscriptions. Operating leverage is high both ways: costs were nearly flat while revenue grew, but non-cancelable commitments of USD 3,423,854 thousand at the end of 2025, 78.42% of them due in 2026 and 2027, and a payroll partly paid in stock granted at an average value of USD 5.53 in 2026 make short-run flexibility smaller than the headcount cut suggests. Margins can be defended under moderate volume pressure through further cuts, marketing and hiring restraint, but not through the hosting commitments or the stock award pipeline, and a revenue decline would hit operating income at roughly the incremental rate of 65.08%.

### Porter's Five Forces

Competitive rivalry is intense and asymmetric: Alphabet, Apple, ByteDance, Meta, Pinterest, Reddit and X compete for the same attention and advertising budgets with larger resources, and the issuer says demand growth on its platform has slowed, which caps pricing and margin durability. The threat of new entrants is moderate, because AI platforms and new social formats can divert users but the installed base of 977 million monthly users and the Lens ecosystem raise the cost of entry. Substitutes are strong, since default smartphone cameras, messaging and short-video products meet the same need for free. Buyer power is high because advertisers have no long-term commitments and can move budgets quickly, which makes revenue volatile. Supplier power is high in three places: Apple and Google control operating systems, app stores and data signals, Google Cloud and AWS host nearly all computing under non-cancelable commitments of USD 3,423,854 thousand, primarily hosting, at the end of 2025, and content partners share advertising revenue, so each can raise cost or cut reach. The net credit effect is a business that earns good margins when attention is high but has little pricing power in a downturn.

### PEST analysis

Regulatory and social factors are material and change probability of default more than macro factors do. The platform-harm cases, state attorney general trials in 2027, the European Commission's Digital Services Act proceeding, the Federal Trade Commission consent order of December 2014 with biennial audits for 20 years and a 2025 referral that has since returned to the FTC all convert a social issue, the safety of minors, into possible fines, product changes and cash claims outside debt. Technological factors cut both ways: AI features, a third-party AI partnership and SPECS glasses offer new revenue but add hosting cost, regulatory and execution risk, and Apple and Google policy changes have already reduced targeting and measurement.
