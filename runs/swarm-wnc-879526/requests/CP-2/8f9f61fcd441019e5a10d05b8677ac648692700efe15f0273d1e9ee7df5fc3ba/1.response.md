## Findings

### Bottom line

WABASH NATIONAL Corp is a cyclical manufacturer in an earnings trough whose creditors rely on liquidity and back-ended maturities rather than on operating cash flow, so the credit hinges on converting a rebuilt order book into cash before the 2028 refinancing.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is that the turn may already be under way. Quarterly reported EBITDA losses have narrowed for three consecutive quarters, Q3 2026 trailer shipments rose 43.3% and net sales 24.8%, the issuer sold USD 150.0 million of convertible notes in 2026 after exercise of the full over-allotment option, the revolver is undrawn, and forecasters expect 2027 industry production 30.10% and 16.34% above their 2026 estimates. If backlog converts at stable prices and fixed cost is absorbed, reported EBITDA could turn positive and the 2028 refinancing would then depend mainly on market access. The view would improve on two consecutive quarters of positive reported EBITDA with positive operating cash flow, or on an extension of the 2028 notes or the revolver; it would worsen if total backlog fell back toward the USD 705 million of year end 2025, if the revolver were drawn to fund dividends, or if liquidity moved toward the USD 90 million minimum.

### Credit-relevant company description

Wabash designs, manufactures and services dry van, refrigerated, platform and tank trailers, truck bodies and composite products, and sells aftermarket parts and services through two reportable segments. Last twelve months net sales were USD 1,518.33 million, sold through factory-direct accounts to large fleets and through independent dealers, so cash flow follows fleet replacement cycles rather than contracted recurring revenue. The business model supports debt service only when trailer orders are strong: backlog gives visibility for twelve months, but orders can be changed or cancelled and the parts and services business that management expects to be less cyclical is small.

### Ownership and group structure

Wabash is a publicly listed company with no controlling shareholder or sponsor identified, so there is no sponsor backstop and no sponsor-driven recapitalisation risk, and the governance rating is Medium because shareholder returns continued through losses. The Senior Notes and Convertible Notes are guaranteed by domestic restricted subsidiaries and rank behind a revolver secured on substantially all personal property of the borrowers and guarantors, while the notes are structurally junior to any debt of subsidiaries that do not guarantee them, such as foreign subsidiaries, but no such debt and no guarantor financial information is printed to size that.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_wnc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18–22

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is volume-driven, cyclical, discretionary and moderately concentrated, with price increases passed on but occasionally with delay. Trailer demand follows the amount of freight to be carried, new trailer shipments rose 13.4% in nine months 2026 while truck body shipments fell 50.4%, and the five largest customers were 35% of FY2025 net sales. Because pricing power is limited by customers that know lifecycle costs and by imports, volume recovery supports revenue but margin recovery depends on cost, which is why revenue durability is judged average and margin stability weak.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins cannot be defended under further volume pressure or cost inflation. Operating leverage should work in reverse if volumes recover, but fixed cost, input cost and tariff exposure on steel, aluminum and components mean that the benefit is not quantified in the filings and credit cannot rely on it.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months the order book is the main tailwind and tariffs, truck bodies and margins are the headwinds. Forecasters expect 2026 United States trailer production 3% and 5% above 2025 and 2027 production 30.10% and 16.34% above their 2026 estimates, backlog supports early 2027 production, and duty orders on Chinese imports are in force, but part of the order surge may be tariff-driven, truck body shipments fell 50.4% in nine months 2026, and the issuer is carrying production-readiness cost ahead of volumes. Over three to five years the same forecasters expect production in 2029 to be 58.67% and 47.52% above their 2026 estimates, which the issuer calls generally consistent with historical levels, so a recovery would restore volume, but the forecasts have been revised before and the filing says actual production may differ materially.

### Downside scenario

The realistic downside begins with orders that are cancelled or deferred after the early 2027 order cycle, for example because customers pulled demand forward ahead of tariff price increases. The first operational break is the dry van lines that were staffed and prepared for higher 2027 production, which would run under-absorbed while truck bodies stay weak, so gross margin would stay near the 3.12% of Q3 2026 and reported EBITDA would remain negative. Free cash flow would stay near the USD 144.30 million twelve month burn or the USD 75.01 million nine-month burn, so liquidity above the USD 90 million minimum liquidity test would last between 15.55 and 22.44 months from 2026-09-30, which brackets the 2028-07-16 revolver date and ends before the Senior Notes could be refinanced from earnings. Management levers are limited capital spending already near USD 9.19 million for nine months, suspension of the USD 0.08 per share quarterly dividend, the unused repurchase authorization, further asset sales and cash that could be released from working capital, with receivables of USD 198.61 million and inventory of USD 195.33 million at 2026-09-30, so resilience appears sufficient to avoid a liquidity shortfall through most of 2027 but not sufficient to reach the 2028 maturities without an earnings recovery or an extension.

### Overall credit view
