### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better view is that debt-only leverage has fallen to 1.58x, free cash flow turned positive and refinancing is largely done, so on a debt-only reading PENN looks comfortable. The strongest evidence for a worse view is that the fixed-charge load is far larger than the debt: lease-adjusted leverage is 5.6x, cover of interest after the rent gap is 1.97x and Interactive supplied 73.19% of the earnings gain, so the recovery is less proven than the headline. ### Company description

PENN owns, manages or holds interests in 42 gaming and racing properties in 19 states and runs online sportsbook and iCasino businesses in the United States and Canada. The retail segments produced 81.32% of FY2025 revenue and 80.62% of nine-month 2026 revenue, and most of that revenue is cash-based slot and table gaming at mature regional casinos, which gives good cash flow visibility. Most of the real estate is leased from two REIT landlords, so a large part of that cash flow is committed to rent before debt service, and Interactive adds growth in Ontario, Alberta and U.S. iCasino without yet funding itself.

### Ownership and group structure

PENN is a widely held, Nasdaq-listed parent with no controlling holder disclosed, so no sponsor behaviour can be inferred from identity, and Business & governance rates governance risk medium. The unsecured notes carry no subsidiary guarantees, while the credit facilities have subsidiary guarantors and are collateralised by interests in owned real property and the AR PENN and 2023 master leases carry a parent guarantee and cross-default each other; creditors of the notes therefore rely on parent-level claims, and the guarantor list is not supplied. The 2025 repurchases of 13.20% of opening shares show some tolerance for returning cash, while development was financed through sale-leasebacks rather than new borrowing.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is driven by visits and spend per visit at regional casinos, supported by a loyalty program whose membership is reported in the tens of millions, and by online play in Ontario, Alberta and the United States. It is discretionary, local-market dependent and exposed to substitution from online gaming, prediction markets, sweepstakes and illegal operators that the company names; revenue is largely transactional, with market access agreements with partners in six states the only contract-based stream described, and pricing power is limited to promotional reinvestment. Retail revenue grew 0.69% in FY2025 and 3.93% in nine months of 2026, with the Midwest and West up 10.27% and 9.66% on new projects, the Northeast up 2.11% and the South down 0.75%, so growth outside new projects was 2.27%. Customer concentration is not disclosed. Revenue is predictable at the portfolio level, cyclical at the local level and structurally exposed to regulation.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Gaming taxes take 31.47% of retail revenue, compensation 17.07% and other segment items 18.45%, leaving a retail margin of 33.01% in FY2025 before rent, which is fixed. Only gaming taxes move with revenue, so a 5% fall in retail revenue, taken as an assumption, would lower retail earnings by 10.38%, and margins have so far held between 33.01% and 33.95%. In Interactive, media and advertising spend fell by $133.9 million, 56.98%, and compensation by $30.0 million between nine months of 2025 and 2026, which explains most of the narrowing loss; margins can be defended under moderate volume pressure at retail but not if Interactive must restore user acquisition spend.

### Porter’s five forces

Rivalry is intense in mature markets: the company says most properties operate in mature, competitive markets, and a retail margin that stays within a narrow band shows limited pricing power rather than a moat. The threat of new entrants is low at retail because licences and taxes protect incumbents, and higher online, where prediction markets and sweepstakes platforms are named competitors. Substitutes include online gaming, video gaming terminals outside casinos and illegal machines, which cap retail growth to the 2.27% seen outside new projects. Buyers face no contractual switching costs and the loyalty program is the main retention tool. Suppliers matter mainly through the landlords, whose rent rises by escalators of up to 2% a year, and through technology and media partners; the ESPN arrangement showed how a supplier can carry $212.7 million of advertising cost into one year.

### Overall credit view

Overall, PENN presents an adequate business profile supported by a cash-generative regional retail portfolio and a narrowing Interactive loss, but credit quality is constrained by fixed rent and lease obligations and by a recovery that rests on Interactive cost cuts. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because retail margins held between 33.01% and 33.95% and free cash flow turned positive. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the January 2027 notes being refinanced at a coupon near the 6.75% of the 2031 notes and on the board keeping repurchases suspended. The primary downside path is a fall in retail earnings against fixed rent, which would lift lease-adjusted leverage from 5.6x toward the 6.8x seen at the end of 2025. Further analysis would require the credit agreement, the proxy statement, lease texts and any rating. Top risk: fixed rent claims on retail earnings.
