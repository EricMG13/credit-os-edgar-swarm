## Findings

### Bottom line

Gray Media's credit is adequate on business quality but weak on balance sheet strain, with net debt at 8.42x reported EBITDA and cover of 1.74x, so creditors depend on election-year earnings and timely refinancing of the 2028 maturities.

Net debt was USD 5,632 million at 2026-06-30 and, pro forma for the cash paid for acquisitions on 2026-07-01, USD 5,675 million, or 8.48x; the effect of the revolver-funded repurchase of 2026-07-21 on net debt and on cash is not established. The offsets are top-ranked stations in diversified markets, no principal due before 2028 and, at 2026-06-30, revolver availability of USD 745 million.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C4

### Key findings

Leverage is high on both reported and adjusted earnings. Net debt over reported EBITDA was 4.90x at FY2024, 8.55x at FY2025 and 8.42x on the last twelve months, and 7.94x on the issuer's adjusted EBITDA, because reported EBITDA fell 43.84% as political advertising fell 91.55%, so creditors carry the debt on off-year earnings until 2026 political revenue is booked. The pro forma figure after the 2026-07-01 acquisition payment is USD 5,675 million, or 8.48x; the figure after the 2026-07-21 repurchase is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C10; calculation C11

Interest cover is the thinnest dimension. Reported EBITDA covered cash interest paid 2.84x at FY2024, 1.60x at FY2025 and 1.74x on the last twelve months, and adjusted EBITDA covered it 1.84x, so a repeat of the off-year trough would leave little room for a cost or revenue miss. Cash interest takes 12.23% of last-twelve-months revenue.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C12

Free cash flow is positive but small relative to the debt. Operating cash flow of USD 250 million less capital spending of USD 104 million gave USD 146 million over the last twelve months, and after USD 87 million of common and preferred dividends only USD 59 million remained, so deleveraging from internal cash flow is slow unless earnings rise. The FY2025 off-year left USD 96 million after the same items.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C9

The maturity profile is the main support. No principal falls due before 2028, the USD 739 million term loan and the USD 750 million revolver are both due on 2028-12-01, and the USD 1,125 million of 2029 first lien notes follow, so USD 1,864 million, or 31.77% of principal, comes due by the end of 2029. Secured debt is 65.78% of principal, so the unsecured notes of USD 2,008 million rank behind a large secured layer.
Basis: calculation C5; calculation C6; calculation C8; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 37

Liquidity at 2026-06-30 was USD 921 million, made of cash and revolver availability, before the 2026-07-01 acquisition payment and the July 2026 repurchase funded partly from the revolver, so cash after those events is not established. The receivables securitization of USD 400 million is fully drawn, so it adds no further funding, and the revolver amount drawn is not stated, so liquidity today is not established.
Basis: calculation C4; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 25, 33–34; EX-99.1_2026-08-07_ex_998938.htm.jsonl, page 5

Revenue durability depends on fee contracts and the election cycle. Retransmission consent and network affiliation economics set the cash margin, and political advertising swings earnings between even and odd years, so the 2027 off-year and the renewals of network agreements from mid-2027 to the end of 2028 are the periods in which refinancing and earnings pressures meet. Management guidance for the third quarter of 2026 points to revenue of USD 935 million to USD 965 million against USD 749 million a year earlier, which is guidance and not an outcome.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C13; EX-99.1_2026-08-07_ex_998938.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 5

Governance and financial policy are mixed and add to the balance sheet risk. The earlier assessment rates sponsor and governance risk as medium because acquisitions were funded partly with first lien notes while dividends continued, and because a ten-vote share class concentrates voting power; the preferred stock repurchase at a discount and the debt repurchases are the countervailing actions.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a better view is that 2026 is an election year with political advertising already at USD 113 million in the first half against USD 22 million, and the company guides third quarter political revenue of USD 165 million to USD 185 million and the guided revenue implies growth of 24.83% to 28.84% over the prior-year quarter. If those results are met and the revolver balance proves small, last-twelve-months earnings would rise sharply and leverage would fall, and the issuer's covenant total net leverage of 5.73x already sits below its 7.00x incurrence level. The view would improve if reported results meet guidance and debt is reduced from cash flow, and worsen if third quarter revenue misses the low end of guidance, if the revolver is drawn further, or if affiliation renewals raise network fees faster than retransmission revenue.
Basis: calculation C13; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 37; EX-99.1_2026-08-07_ex_998938.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6

### Limitations

- The financial profile rests on twelve-month figures that include a quarter in which acquired stations contributed only part of a period, so the leverage and cover ratios may overstate or understate the run rate of the enlarged group.
- Ratings, market prices and peer benchmarks were not part of the evidence, so the view is an absolute fundamental assessment and says nothing about how the debt trades or compares with similar broadcasters.
- The tests that govern distributions, additional debt and liens are known only from filing summaries, so the downside path assumes management policy rather than testing what the documents would allow.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Scale and market rank are a strength. The group reaches about 37% of US households and holds the top-rated station in 77 markets, with no market above 5% of revenue, so a local downturn or one lost advertiser does little to debt service and the stations keep bargaining power with distributors, which supports refinancing access.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, page 4

A back-ended maturity profile is a strength. No principal is due before 2028 and USD 1,864 million falls due by the end of 2029, so the company does not need the capital markets in the 2026 cycle, which lowers near-term default risk even though leverage is high.
Basis: calculation C5; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, page 16

A path to higher earnings in the current cycle is a strength. Political advertising of USD 113 million in the first half of 2026 and guided third quarter political revenue of USD 165 million to USD 185 million would lift twelve-month earnings and cover if realised, which gives management a credible route to deleveraging without asset sales.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, page 11; EX-99.1_2026-08-07_ex_998938.htm.jsonl, page 5

Leverage and cover on reported earnings are the main weakness. Net debt of 8.42x reported EBITDA (8.48x pro forma after the 2026-07-01 acquisition payment) and cover of 1.74x leave little tolerance for a miss, so a fall in earnings would raise leverage and squeeze free cash flow before any covenant is tested.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1)

Dependence on fee contracts and the cycle is a weakness. Retransmission consent revenue was 46.17% of FY2025 revenue and network fees took a large share of it, with political advertising 1.36% of revenue in the off-year, so earnings can fall by 43.84% between an election year and the following year and renewals can reset fees.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C10; calculation C11; calculation C15; calculation C16

Policy and secured-debt priority are a weakness. Acquisitions funded partly with first lien notes and maintained dividends raise secured debt ahead of the unsecured and second lien notes, and the family-influenced control structure means these choices are decided by shareholders, so recovery for junior creditors is more exposed.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C8

### Business and operating model read

The revenue base is a large station group with two fee lines and one cyclical line. Core advertising of USD 1,452 million and retransmission consent revenue of USD 1,429 million supply 46.91% and 46.17% of FY2025 revenue, political advertising swings by even and odd years, and network affiliation fees of USD 882 million are the largest cost, so net retransmission revenue of USD 547 million is the margin that matters. Pricing power sits partly with the group, which negotiates retransmission with distributors, and partly with the networks that set affiliation fees at renewal.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C13; calculation C15; calculation C17; EX-99.1_2026-02-26_ex_924265.htm.jsonl, page 2

Cost flexibility is limited because network fees and station staffing are largely fixed in the short run, so earnings move more than revenue: reported EBITDA fell 43.84% when revenue fell 15.07% between FY2024 and FY2025. Capital intensity is modest, with USD 108 million of purchases of property and equipment in FY2025, which leaves operating cash flow available for interest, dividends and acquisitions, but conversion of EBITDA to free cash flow is low after USD 385 million of cash interest.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C10; calculation C14; calculation C12; 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, page 75

### Industry forces

Supplier power is high: the four major networks set affiliation fees and the agreements expire between mid-2027 and the end of 2028, so renewals can move USD 882 million of annual cost. Buyer power rests with distributors: retransmission revenue fell in the first half of 2026 on subscriber declines, a station moving to independent status and a resolved dispute, so the largest fee line is under pressure. Entry barriers are real because broadcast licences are limited in each area and ownership rules cap national reach, but the same rules limit the group's ability to buy more stations.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 14; EX-99.1_2026-08-07_ex_998938.htm.jsonl, page 3

Policy and the economy matter through two channels. Advertising budgets follow the economy, and the election cycle supplies political revenue in even years, so regulation of ownership and the timing of pending station approvals affect growth plans that management has linked to expected ownership reform. These factors support a view that growth depends on regulators and the cycle rather than on organic share gains.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 14; EX-99.1_2026-02-26_ex_924265.htm.jsonl, page 1

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months the tailwind is the 2026 election cycle, with first-half political advertising of USD 113 million and guided third quarter political revenue of USD 165 million to USD 185 million, and the headwinds are the 2027 off-year, subscriber declines and the maturity of the term loan and revolver on 2028-12-01. Over three to five years the outcome depends on whether network and distributor renewals leave net retransmission revenue intact and whether acquisitions add earnings that exceed their secured-debt cost, which is not disclosed.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, page 11; EX-99.1_2026-08-07_ex_998938.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 5; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Downside scenario

A realistic downside starts with the 2027 off-year: political advertising reverts to the FY2025 level of USD 42 million, and retransmission revenue falls further as subscriber declines continue while network renewals begin. Revenue and reported EBITDA would then move toward the FY2025 outcome, when reported EBITDA of USD 629 million covered cash interest only 1.60x. Free cash flow after dividends was USD 96 million in that off-year, so the group avoids liquidity distress if it holds acquisitions and repurchases to cash flow, but a revolver-funded repurchase or purchase would reduce the USD 745 million of availability just as the term loan and revolver approach their 2028-12-01 maturity. Management levers are suspending the common dividend of USD 33 million, using the USD 250 million repurchase authorization more sparingly and delaying acquisitions; these are possible but not documented.
Basis: calculation C9; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, page 75; EX-99.1_2026-08-07_ex_998938.htm.jsonl, page 5

### Overall credit view

Overall, GRAY MEDIA, INC presents an adequate business profile supported by top-ranked stations, diversified markets and a back-ended maturity profile, but credit quality is constrained by net debt of 8.42x reported EBITDA (8.48x pro forma after the 2026-07-01 acquisition payment), cover of 1.74x and secured debt of 65.78% of principal. Cash-flow durability appears adequate in election years and weak in off-years because political revenue and fee renewals drive earnings. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the 2026 election-year result, the revolver balance and renewals of network and distributor contracts. The primary downside path is an off-year earnings trough in 2027 that coincides with a drawn revolver ahead of the 2028-12-01 maturity. Further analysis would require the revolver balance after 2026-07-21, third quarter 2026 results, the executed debt documents and the proxy statement. Top risk: refinancing the 2028 maturities from a weak off-year earnings base with a drawn revolver.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C5; calculation C8
