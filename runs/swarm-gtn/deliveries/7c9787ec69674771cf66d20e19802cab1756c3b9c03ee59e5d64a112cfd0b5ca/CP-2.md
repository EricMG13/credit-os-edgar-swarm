## Findings

### Bottom line

Gray Media's credit profile is weak to adequate: scale and liquidity are real strengths, but leverage near eight and a half times derived EBITDA and thin interest cover leave little room for an off-political year before the December 2028 maturities. Net debt was 8.42x derived twelve-month EBITDA at 2026-06-30, or 8.48x pro forma for the first station closings of 2026-07-01, and cash interest cover was 1.74x; the effect of the 2026-07-21 note repurchase on net debt is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation B1; calculation B3

The support is USD 921 million of cash and undrawn revolver at 2026-06-30 and no principal due before December 2028; the largest risk is a second off-year, when political revenue falls back and retransmission revenue keeps shrinking as affiliation agreements come up for renewal from 2027.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, pages 15-16; calculation B5; calculation B10

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a better view is timing. Twelve-month EBITDA includes only the first half of a political year; the issuer guides third-quarter 2026 revenue to USD 935 million to USD 965 million against USD 749 million a year earlier, with political advertising of USD 165 million to USD 185 million against USD 8 million, and net retransmission revenue rose to USD 292 million from USD 281 million in the first half. The covenant ratio of 5.73x sits 1.27x below its 7.00x incurrence limit, the first lien ratio of 2.55x is far below the 4.25x limit that applies when the revolver is drawn, and management places the first maturity after the 2028 election cycle, when earnings should be at a peak. If twelve-month derived EBITDA at the end of 2026 moved back toward the USD 1,120 million of the last political year and the 2028 maturities were refinanced early, net leverage would move toward the 4.90x seen at the end of 2024 and the view would improve. It would worsen if the next off-year repeated the USD 629 million of 2025, if retransmission revenue fell faster than network fees, or if further debt-funded distributions or a covenant-limited refinancing left less room before 2028.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-08-07_ex_998938.htm.jsonl, pages 1-6; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, pages 29, 35, 37; calculation B1; calculation B13; calculation B17

### Limitations

- Leverage, cover and free cash flow are trailing figures on derived EBITDA with no forward model, so reported leverage could move by several turns in either direction once political-year earnings enter the twelve-month base, and the weak-to-adequate view would move with it.
- No credit ratings, market prices, peer figures or executed debt documents were supplied, so market access rests on the issuer's own fair-value disclosure to a tenth of a billion and refinancing flexibility cannot be tied to what the documents permit.
- The earnings of the stations bought in 2026 are not disclosed, so the effect of the acquisition programme on leverage is directional and the downside scenario uses the 2025 base without the new stations.
- Evidence stops at the second-quarter reports, so the revolver balance and the cash position after the July repurchase and station payments are not established and could lower the liquidity support.
- Ownership percentages and board independence are not established, so the financial-policy judgement could move up or down once the proxy statement is read.

### Company description

Gray Media owns local television stations in more than a hundred US markets that reach about 37% of US television households, with the top-rated station in most of the markets Nielsen measures, plus a small production and studio business. Revenue comes in roughly equal parts from advertising and from fees that cable, satellite and streaming distributors pay to carry its stations, and political advertising adds a large layer every second year. Retransmission contracts and network affiliation agreements run for years, which gives cash-flow visibility, but advertising is sold on short contracts and the political layer disappears in odd years, so debt service in an off-year rests on a smaller, fee-based core. The group is also changing shape: it closed station purchases of USD 280 million in the first half of 2026 and swapped stations with a peer, so the earnings base is being rebuilt faster than the filings show.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 9; calculation B15; calculation B19

### Ownership and group structure

Gray is a public company controlled through votes, not through a sponsor: ten-vote Class A shares held 51.45% of votes on 2026-07-31, and an insider family group is described as able to exert significant influence. The borrower is a holding company with no material independent assets, substantially all subsidiaries guarantee the debt, and the credit facility and the first and second lien notes are secured on substantially all assets other than real estate. Two structural claims sit outside the debt figures: USD 400 million of sold receivables that the notes rank behind, and USD 600 million of redeemable preferred stock that ranks ahead of common equity and below all debt. Business & governance rates sponsor and governance risk as medium because distributions, a debt-funded preferred repurchase and acquisitions coincide with high leverage, offset by par repurchases of debt and a longer maturity profile; holder identities, board independence and the executed documents are not established.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 18, 35; 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, pages 37-38, 93-94; calculation B20

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Volume is set by audience share and by the number of distributor subscribers, and price by the rates in retransmission contracts and advertising rate cards. Retransmission rates rise with each renewal, but subscriber losses, an Atlanta affiliation change and a satellite blackout from March to May 2026 contributed to a USD 50 million fall in first-half retransmission revenue; core advertising was flat and carries two cyclical categories, services at 26% and automotive at 17% of 2025 core advertising. Revenue is therefore cyclical, partly structurally declining in its fee line and not concentrated by customer or market; the consequence is that pricing power exists in carriage negotiations but is being spent offsetting subscriber decline, which limits growth in the cash that supports debt.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 10, 49; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 29; 10-Q_2026-03-31_gtn20260331_10q.htm.jsonl, page 25; calculation B10

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Network affiliation fees, compensation, benefits and programming are the main costs, and a large portion is fixed. The result is operating leverage: from 2024 to 2025 broadcasting expenses fell only USD 78 million against a USD 549 million fall in revenue, and adjusted EBITDA fell 42.3%. Margins can be partly defended through network fees, which fell USD 50 million in 2025 and fell further in the first half of 2026 as an Atlanta station went independent, but not against a political-year swing, so margin resilience is weak across the cycle and adequate within it.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, pages 48-50; EX-99.1_2026-02-26_ex_924265.htm.jsonl, page 2; EX-99.1_2026-08-07_ex_998938.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation B9

### Capital intensity and cash flow conversion

Capital intensity is low outside construction: purchases of property and equipment were USD 108 million in 2025, USD 104 million over the last twelve months, and the issuer guides USD 120 million to USD 130 million for 2026. Conversion of EBITDA to free cash flow is volatile, with free cash flow of USD 608 million in FY2024, USD 181 million in FY2025 and USD 146 million over the last twelve months, because cash interest of about USD 385 million and cash taxes absorb most of an off-year's EBITDA. Capital expenditure could be cut in a downside, which is a lever, but the guided 2026 uses of interest, dividends, capital expenditure and taxes of USD 723 million to USD 753 million already exceed trailing adjusted EBITDA of USD 709 million.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry from cable networks, streaming and digital platforms takes advertising and audience, and cord cutting removes the subscribers who generate fees, so rivalry works on both revenue lines; local news and sports programming are the issuer's response. Distributors have bargaining power, shown by the March to May 2026 satellite blackout, while networks hold supplier power over 61.7% of retransmission revenue through affiliation fees. New entry is limited by scarce licences, but substitutes are close: advertisers can move spend to digital platforms, and viewers to streaming. The credit consequence is that pricing power in the fee line is shared with networks and contested by distributors, so net retransmission revenue is a more reliable guide to cash flow than gross revenue.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, pages 11-13, 31; 10-Q_2026-03-31_gtn20260331_10q.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, page 29; calculation B10

### Macro and regulatory factors

Two outside factors matter. Regulation shapes deleveraging: station acquisitions need regulatory approval, the national audience cap is 39%, a court vacated the top-four ownership restriction in 2025 and the ownership review remains open, so rule changes could widen or narrow the room for further duopoly deals. The economy matters through advertising: core advertising was flat in the first half of 2026 with the issuer citing macroeconomic softness, and the risk factors name trade policy as a threat to advertiser budgets. Other factors, such as technology, are not a primary credit driver on the evidence supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 23, 39; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, page 29

### Credit SWOT

- Strengths: scale across many markets; USD 921 million of cash and undrawn revolver; no principal due before December 2028; net retransmission revenue of USD 547 million that has held; audited reporting.
- Weaknesses: net leverage of 8.42x on derived EBITDA; cover of 1.74x, lower on forward interest; fixed costs that make EBITDA swing; ten-vote control and a record of distributions at high leverage.
- Opportunities: on-year political revenue; duopoly acquisitions and cost sharing if approvals and prices allow; par repurchases of debt under a USD 250 million authorisation; an early refinancing of the 2028 term loan.
- Threats: a second off-year at the 2025 earnings level; retransmission erosion and network fee resets from 2027; carriage disputes; a refinancing in 2028 or 2029 at higher cost or with less room under the covenants.

### Financial profile and credit quality

The financial profile is weaker than the business profile. The main supports are scale, committed liquidity of USD 921 million at the quarter end, a clear runway to December 2028 and on-year earnings power, shown by net leverage of 4.90x at the end of the last political year. The main constraints are leverage of 8.42x and cover of 1.74x, free cash flow that fell from USD 608 million to USD 181 million between 2024 and 2025, margins that are fragile across the cycle because adjusted EBITDA fell 42.3% on a 15.1% revenue decline, and a financial policy that keeps paying dividends and has funded a preferred repurchase with first lien notes while also buying back debt. Capital-market access is not tested by a ratings view or market prices, and the issuer's own fair-value estimate puts the debt below carrying value. The trend is improving in 2026 as political revenue arrives, from a weak 2025 base, and the missing datapoints are the revolver balance and cash after 2026-06-30, the earnings of the acquired stations, ownership percentages and the executed debt documents.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months, 2026 is a political year, so revenue and earnings should rise: the issuer guides third-quarter revenue to USD 935 million to USD 965 million, and 2027 is an off-year when political revenue falls back and the derived leverage figures will look worst again. The tailwinds are political spending, acquisitions that add stations in markets where the group is already strong, and lower network fees; the headwinds are subscriber losses, carriage disputes, higher cash interest after the 2025 refinancing and affiliation renewals from the second half of 2027. Over three to five years the issuer's ability to refinance the 2028 and 2029 maturities in the 2028 political year, at market rates that are not known today, and to turn acquisitions into lower leverage will decide credit quality; ownership-rule changes could help but are unresolved.

### Downside scenario

The downside is a second off-year that repeats the conditions of 2025. The initiating condition is political revenue falling back from USD 113 million in the first half of 2026 toward the USD 42 million of 2025, with retransmission revenue still falling and no new carriage gains. The first break is operational: fixed costs mean that a revenue fall of 15.1% cut adjusted EBITDA by 42.3% in 2025. The financial transmission follows from the 2025 outcome: derived EBITDA of USD 629 million against cash interest of USD 393 million is cover of 1.60x and total debt to EBITDA of 9.13x, and free cash flow of USD 181 million less USD 85 million of dividends leaves USD 96 million.
