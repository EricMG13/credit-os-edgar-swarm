## Findings

### Bottom line

Gray Media is a sturdy station operator whose scale and market positions support creditors, but family-influenced control, a ten-vote share class and debt-funded station purchases beside maintained dividends make sponsor and governance risk medium.

Creditors rely on management policy rather than documented limits, because the debt agreements and the proxy statement were not supplied. The risk would rise if acquisitions keep being funded with secured debt while dividends continue, and would fall with documented debt reduction.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, pages 37–38; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, page 15

### Key findings

The operating business is the credit's strength: the stations are well diversified, with the largest market at 5% of revenue and the ten largest markets at about 25%, and most markets are led by a first or second ranked station. That breadth lowers the chance that one local market or one advertiser damages debt service.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, page 4

Half of revenue is now fee income negotiated with distributors and networks rather than advertising, which makes renewals the main business risk. Retransmission consent revenue was 46.17% of FY2025 revenue and network affiliation fees took 28.50% of it, so net retransmission revenue of USD 547 million depends on subscriber trends and on affiliation agreements that expire from mid-2027 to the end of 2028, the same window in which the 2028 term loan and revolver mature.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C5; calculation C13; 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, page 5; EX-99.1_2026-02-26_ex_924265.htm.jsonl, page 2

Earnings swing with the election cycle, so a single year says little about debt service. Political advertising was USD 497 million, or 13.64% of revenue, in FY2024 and USD 42 million, or 1.36%, in FY2025, so creditors should read leverage across the full two-year cycle and not on an election year alone.
Basis: calculation C3; calculation C4; EX-99.1_2026-02-26_ex_924265.htm.jsonl, page 2

Voting control sits with the Class A holders and the Howell-Robinson Family, whose stake is not established. Each Class A share carries ten votes, which gives that class 51.45% of votes on the share counts at 2026-07-31, and the filing states that the Howell-Robinson Family can exert significant influence over policies and management. Creditors therefore face shareholder decisions on acquisitions, dividends and leverage that are not checked by an outside controlling holder, and a change of control would trigger mandatory redemption of the USD 600 million Series A preferred stock.
Basis: calculation C6; 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, pages 37–38, 98; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, page 2

Financial policy is mixed: growth is funded partly with secured debt while shareholders are paid. The company bought stations for USD 280 million of stated consideration in the first half of 2026, issued USD 70 million of first lien notes to fund the American Spirit Media closing and a preferred stock repurchase, and paid common dividends of USD 33 million and preferred dividends of USD 52 million in FY2025. The preferred repurchase paid USD 30 million for USD 50 million of liquidation preference, which cut junior claims and the cash dividend by USD 4 million a year, but the new first lien debt ranks ahead of the unsecured and second lien notes.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C9; calculation C10; calculation C12; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13, 15, 18

Reporting quality supports monitoring. The auditor has served since 2006, disclosure controls were found effective at year end, and the quarterly report and earnings release print the covenant leverage calculation in full, so creditors can follow covenant ratios each quarter even though the debt documents were not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, pages 72, 115; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, page 37

Licence dependence and regulation tie the asset value to the FCC. Broadcast licences and goodwill were 78.15% of total assets at 2026-06-30, the national ownership limit of 39% of households (with a discount for UHF stations) caps further scale, and the second closings of the latest acquisitions await regulatory approval, so deal-funded growth depends on rule changes that management expects but the filings do not guarantee.
Basis: calculation C7; 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, page 25; EX-99.1_2026-02-26_ex_924265.htm.jsonl, page 1

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a lower risk reading is the operating record and the preference for reducing junior claims: the preferred repurchase was done at a 40.00% discount to liquidation preference, the board authorized up to USD 250 million of debt repurchases through 2027, and the company repurchased USD 120 million of notes at par in July 2026. The view would move to low risk if debt reduction continued from operating cash flow without secured debt issuance, and to high risk if debt-funded acquisitions or a distribution were funded ahead of the unsecured and second lien notes, or if the proxy statement showed the family holding a controlling majority of votes.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 25; EX-99.1_2026-08-07_ex_998938.htm.jsonl, page 5

### Limitations

- The family's percentage holding, board independence, committee structure and pay arrangements are not established because the proxy statement was not supplied, so the control assessment rests on voting rights and risk factor wording and could be understated or overstated.
- Funding for most of the 2026 station purchases is described only as cash or new notes, and acquired earnings are not disclosed, so the leverage effect of the acquisition programme on creditors cannot be sized here.
- The holder of the Series A preferred stock is not named in the supplied documents, so whether the preferred holder aligns with creditors in a stress or in a sale is not established.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

In the first half of 2026 the company closed station purchases with stated consideration of USD 280 million: WBBJ USD 27 million, three Allen Media stations USD 56 million, seven Allen Media markets USD 115 million, Block Communications stations USD 80 million and Sagamore Hill stations USD 2 million. It also swapped three stations to Scripps for two others without cash, recording a non-cash loss of USD 22 million.
Basis: calculation C10; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, pages 11–13

On 2026-06-30 the company issued USD 70 million of additional 7.25% first lien notes due 2033 at par in a private placement. The proceeds paid USD 40 million of the first closing of the American Spirit Media acquisition and USD 30 million to repurchase 50,000 Series A preferred shares with USD 50 million of liquidation preference. On 2026-07-01 the first closing of American Spirit Media and WHPM was paid in cash, and USD 11 million remains payable at the second closings, which need regulatory approval.
Basis: calculation C11; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 18, 25; EX-99.1_2026-08-07_ex_998938.htm.jsonl, page 5

In 2025 the company refinanced most debt: USD 900 million and USD 250 million of 9.625% second lien notes due 2032, USD 775 million of 7.25% first lien notes due 2033, and a revolving credit facility raised to USD 750 million and extended to 2028-12-01. The proceeds redeemed the 2027 notes at par, repaid term loans and redeemed part of the 2029 first lien notes at 103% of par. Later in 2026 the company repurchased USD 100 million of 2029 first lien notes and USD 20 million of 2031 notes at par.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gtn20251231_10k.htm.jsonl, pages 92–94; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gtn20260630_10q.htm.jsonl, page 25
