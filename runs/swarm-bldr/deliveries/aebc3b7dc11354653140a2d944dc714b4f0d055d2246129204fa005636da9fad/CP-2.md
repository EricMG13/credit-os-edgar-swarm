## Findings

### Bottom line

Fundamental credit has weakened sharply but is not distressed: reported leverage reached 5.11x and interest cover fell to 3.18x as earnings fell, yet USD 1,514.11 million of liquidity and no note or revolver maturity before 2030 give creditors time.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27

The weakest points are margin and cash conversion in a single-family housing downturn and a shareholder policy that has been partly debt funded; the strongest are liquidity, long-dated fixed-rate debt and a diversified customer base. The primary downside path is a further fall in single-family volumes and prices that cuts earnings faster than sales, which would push leverage higher well before it threatens liquidity.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.1_2026-10-29_bldr-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C5

### Key findings

Liquidity and the maturity profile are the main credit support, and they remove near-term refinancing risk. Cash of USD 55.01 million and net borrowing availability of USD 1,459.10 million sit under a borrowing base of USD 1,731.70 million, and the USD 4,300.00 million of senior unsecured notes are 92.30% of principal and fixed rate. The first principal due is USD 550.00 million in March 2030, so note and revolver debt service before then is interest only; the floating-rate revolver is only 4.23% of principal.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27; calculation C12; calculation C13

Earnings erosion is the weakest dimension: margins have roughly halved since FY2024. Reported EBITDA margin fell from 13.15% to 9.07% and then 6.30% over the last twelve months, and last-twelve-month reported EBITDA of USD 904.40 million is 34.35% below FY2025. The mechanism is fixed cost: nine-month EBITDA fell 5.80x as fast as net sales, so a small further sales decline compounds into a large EBITDA decline and a higher leverage ratio.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26; calculation C5

Cash conversion has fallen sharply, so deleveraging cannot yet rely on free cash flow. Free cash flow was 28.74% of reported EBITDA over the last twelve months against 63.44% in FY2025, and a nine-month working-capital outflow of USD 198.77 million took 65.81% of operating cash flow. Purchases of property, plant and equipment fell 42.65% and the issuer guides full-year free cash flow of USD 400.00 million to USD 450.00 million, which would imply USD 249.13 million to USD 299.13 million in the fourth quarter on the analyst's arithmetic as seasonal working capital releases.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_bldr-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation C15; calculation C1

Leverage is high on the reported basis and the issuer's headline understates it. Total debt was 5.11x reported EBITDA, 4.09x adjusted EBITDA, and 3.22x at FY2025; the issuer's own net measure is 4.1x against 2.3x a year earlier. Cash interest paid of USD 284.75 million over the last twelve months is 51.10% above FY2024, so EBITDA covers it only 3.18x (3.97x on adjusted EBITDA).

Buybacks and acquisitions since the start of 2025 were 1.91x free cash flow and total debt rose USD 915.05 million, while no shares were bought under the programme in the second or third quarter even though USD 500.00 million of authority remains; governance risk is medium, with board, ownership and pay detail not established.

The revolver is drawn for USD 197.00 million but its secured claim could rise towards USD 1,731.70 million, the notes are guaranteed by unnamed subsidiaries and are structurally behind non-guarantors, and USD 161.17 million of other finance obligations carry no stated ranking. Cumulative leverage through the notes is 4.97x; sizing recovery is left to the priority and recovery work.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a weakening view is that the decline is narrowing and the market is not pricing distress. Net sales fell 10.13% in the first quarter, 8.77% in the second and 2.21% in the third, the issuer's adjusted EBITDA decline eased from 42.1% to 28.3%, and the five note series were valued at 88.25% to 96.38% of principal in the third-quarter filing, below the 95.12% to 104.75% range at year-end but not at distressed levels (the 5.00% 2030 notes moved least). The view would improve if fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA reaches the USD 220.80 million to USD 270.80 million the outlook implies and free cash flow repays revolver borrowings.

### Company description

Builders FirstSource is a U.S. supplier and manufacturer of building materials, components and installation services for professional homebuilders, with about 575 locations in 43 states, one reportable segment and four product categories of similar size. The model supports cash-flow visibility through breadth and local density: the ten largest customers were 14% of 2025 net sales and the largest 4%. It weakens visibility through cyclicality, because orders follow housing starts and lumber prices; management calls the housing market underbuilt over the long term, which is Management Language and not evidence of near-term demand. Acquisitions of eight businesses in 2025 and four in 2026 have added scale at the cost of leverage and goodwill, and the notes' refinancing confidence rests on liquidity and a 2030-plus maturity profile rather than on demand visibility.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 57; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 59

### Ownership and group structure

The company is publicly held and listed on the NYSE and NYSE Texas, with no sponsor or controlling holder identified in the sources read; beneficial ownership, board independence and pay sit in a proxy statement that was not supplied. The group is one consolidated entity with unnamed subsidiary guarantors of the revolver and notes, so notes are structurally behind the liabilities of any non-guarantor subsidiary whose size is not disclosed. Debt agreements restrict dividends and buybacks, and the company pays no dividend. Financial policy has favoured buybacks and acquisitions funded partly by debt, which lifts leverage and shows a tolerance for higher leverage; the governance review rates the overall risk medium.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 53; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 93–94

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is cyclical, transactional and driven by single-family starts, repair and remodel and multi-family activity, with no contracted backlog or recurring element disclosed. In the third quarter of 2026 core organic single-family sales fell 6.2%, multi-family rose 2.0% and repair and remodel was up 0.3%, so volume is the main driver; commodity deflation reduced nine-month sales by 2.0% and acquisitions added 1.4%. Pricing power is limited because production builders press suppliers on price and costs are passed on only with a lag. The credit consequence is that revenue offers little visibility: sales declines flow quickly to earnings and cash, and the debt service capacity depends on cost and working-capital levers.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 28–29; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_bldr-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Costs are largely fixed in the short run: selling, general and administrative expenses fell only 1.6% against a 7.0% fall in nine-month sales and rose to 25.8% of sales from 24.4%, while gross margin fell to 28.1% from 30.5%. Raw materials are lumber, OSB, plywood and other purchased products whose price swings are passed on with delay. Margins cannot be defended under volume pressure on the evidence: the fixed-cost base, wage inflation and fuel and insurance costs offset productivity savings of about USD 57 million. The implication is high operating leverage, measured at 5.80 times, so each point of lost sales costs several points of EBITDA.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 23–24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_bldr-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 3; calculation C5

### Capital intensity and FCF conversion

Capital intensity is moderate and flexible: purchases of property, plant and equipment were 2.39% of FY2025 sales, fell 42.65% in the nine months of 2026, and the 2026 outlook is USD 175 million to USD 225 million against USD 250 million to USD 300 million in the annual report. Working capital is the swing factor: it released cash in FY2024 and FY2025 but absorbed USD 198.77 million in nine months 2026, and the first half is seasonally heavy. Technology implementation and acquisition spend add cash costs that are added back in adjusted EBITDA. EBITDA converted to free cash flow at 69.82% in FY2024 and 63.44% in FY2025 but only 28.74% over the last twelve months, so free cash flow available for debt service and deleveraging is currently thin and depends on a working-capital release.

### Porter's five forces

Competitive rivalry is high: the industry is fragmented and the annual report names national dealers, component manufacturers and home-centre chains expanding into the professional segment, so price competition limits margin recovery and weakens debt service capacity in a downturn. The threat of new entrants is moderate because barriers and costs of entry are described as low, although scale in manufacturing and procurement protects incumbents. The threat of substitutes arises from manufacturers selling directly to builders and builders integrating, which could erode distribution margin. Buyer power is high: production builders concentrate purchasing and press prices, though no customer exceeds 4% of sales. Supplier power is moderate: the largest suppliers are national producers and agreements are terminable on limited notice, but the company says it does not depend on any one supplier.

### PEST analysis

Economic and trade factors are material and the rest are not primary credit drivers. Interest rates, mortgage affordability and consumer confidence set housing demand, and the composite forecast for 2026 shows total starts down 3.5% and single-family starts down 6.4%, which lowers volumes and so earnings and cash flow; tariffs on imported goods and commodity prices move input costs and margins. Regulation (building codes, trade policy, labour rules) adds cost but the sources do not show it changing probability of default on its own, and social and technological factors, such as skilled-labour scarcity and the new ERP system, matter mainly through cost and the recurring technology add-back.

### SWOT analysis

Strengths: liquidity of USD 1,514.11 million and no maturity before 2030; fixed-rate debt for most of the structure; diversified customers and products; a manufacturing and distribution footprint; no sponsor and restrictions on dividends. Weaknesses: reported leverage of 5.11x and interest cover of 3.18x; thin cash conversion; high operating leverage; reliance on management-defined add-backs. Opportunities that could improve credit quality: a housing recovery from an underbuilt base, lower interest rates, continued cost savings and deleveraging from free cash flow. Threats that could weaken it: a further fall in single-family starts, price deflation, renewed debt-funded buybacks or acquisitions, a shrinking borrowing base and the ERP programme's cost and disruption.

Short term (12 to 24 months): the issuer expects 2026 net sales of USD 14.2 billion to USD 14.6 billion, adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,075 million to USD 1,125 million and free cash flow of USD 400 million to USD 450 million, on assumptions of lower single-family and multi-family starts and repair and remodel down 1%; that points to a stabilising level of earnings and leverage near 4x on the issuer's measure, so deleveraging relies on free cash flow and not on earnings growth. Medium term (three to five years): the 2030 maturities of USD 747.00 million, the revolver and the 5.00% notes, are the refinancing test. Company tailwinds are productivity savings, lower capex and the 2025 financing; company headwinds are fixed costs, add-backs and the ERP programme.

### Qualitative downside scenario

The scenario is a further leg down in single-family starts and lumber and building-product prices, extending the decline already visible in 2026. Sales fall and, because EBITDA has fallen 5.80 times as fast as sales, reported EBITDA falls faster than sales and gross margin slips below the 27.82% of the third quarter while selling and administrative costs stay fixed. Free cash flow then depends on a working-capital release, which has occurred in FY2024 and FY2025 and in the downturns shown, but the borrowing base falls with receivables and inventory: in 2025 it fell 6.26% against a 7.38% fall in net sales, so availability shrinks roughly in step with sales. Management levers are capex (already down 42.65%), the paused buyback and acquisitions, and cost actions. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress; the exposure is a credit-quality deterioration through leverage and the notes' subordination, not a cash shortfall, unless buybacks resume on the revolver.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Builders FirstSource presents an adequate but weakening business profile supported by liquidity, a long fixed-rate maturity profile and diversified customers, but credit quality is constrained by falling margins, thin cash conversion and reported leverage above 5x. Cash-flow durability appears weak at present because free cash flow was 28.74% of EBITDA over the last twelve months. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on earnings stabilising before the 2030 maturities and on the board holding back buybacks. The primary downside path is a further fall in single-family volumes and prices that cuts EBITDA faster than sales and lifts leverage while the secured revolver ranks ahead of the notes. Further analysis would require peer data, the covenant documents and the proxy statement. Fundamental credit review completed. Top risk: earnings erosion in the single-family housing cycle, amplified by fixed costs and a policy that has been partly debt funded.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)
