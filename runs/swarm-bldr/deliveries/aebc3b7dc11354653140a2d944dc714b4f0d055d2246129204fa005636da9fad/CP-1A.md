## Findings

### Bottom line

Governance risk is medium and comes from capital allocation, not ownership: no sponsor or controlling holder is identified, but debt-funded buybacks and acquisitions raised leverage while earnings fell.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 53; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22; calculation C5; calculation C6

Debt agreements restrict dividends, cash buybacks stopped after the first quarter of 2026 and reporting is audited and regular, which limits extraction risk. Board, ownership and pay detail sits in a proxy statement that was not supplied, so control and independence are not established.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 53; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 68; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 106–108; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 31–32; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

No sponsor or controlling owner is identified, so the creditor risk is board policy, not an owner's extraction. The common stock is listed on the NYSE and NYSE Texas with 62 stockholders of record, the company pays no dividends and its debt agreements restrict dividends and buybacks. Beneficial ownership sits in a proxy statement that was not supplied, so a large holder cannot be ruled out. With no sponsor there are no monitoring fees or fund-life pressure to assess; the risk is a policy that a board can change quickly.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 53; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 106–108; Source readiness (CP-0)

Shareholder returns and acquisitions have outrun free cash flow since the start of 2025, and the gap was largely debt funded. Buybacks of USD 717.48 million and acquisitions of USD 1,240.01 million were 1.91x free cash flow of USD 1,024.91 million, while total debt rose USD 915.05 million. The mechanism is that returns and deals set in the strong cash generation of 2023 and 2024 continued as earnings fell, so the issuer's own net leverage measure reached 4.1x at 2026-09-30 against 2.3x a year earlier; bondholders therefore depend on board restraint, and the limits in the debt agreements could not be tested because the executed documents were not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 72–73; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_bldr-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 3; calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C6

The first-quarter 2026 buyback is the clearest creditor-adverse action, and it has not been repeated. The company repurchased USD 302.90 million of stock at an average of USD 92.25 a share and drew a net USD 200.00 million on the revolving facility while adjusted EBITDA was falling and the issuer's net leverage measure stood at 3.2x against 2.0x a year earlier. No shares were bought under the programme in the second or third quarter, although the board authorised up to USD 500.00 million on 2026-04-29; the pause is supportive, and a repurchase funded on the revolver would be the trigger to watch.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-04-30_bldr-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-03-31_bldr-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 31–32; calculation C7

Acquisitions are a repeated, mostly cash-funded growth route that raises leverage in a downturn, but 2026 activity is far smaller. In 2025 the company completed eight acquisitions using USD 1,123.33 million of cash net of cash acquired, funded by cash and revolver borrowings and then termed out with the USD 750.00 million notes of May 2025; in the first nine months of 2026 it completed four acquisitions for about USD 129.30 million. The earnings contribution and pro forma leverage of these deals are not disclosed because the company calls them immaterial, so their effect on creditors cannot be separated from the organic decline.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 86–87; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 92; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; calculation C3

Disclosure is regular and audited, but the largest EBITDA adjustment is management-defined and recurring. The auditor gives an unqualified opinion on the statements and on internal control, and each release reconciles its non-GAAP measures; however technology implementation expense of USD 149.00 million over the last twelve months is added back while the new ERP system is rolled out in phases over several years, so more such add-backs should be expected. Adjusted and reported EBITDA should therefore be read side by side.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 68; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 105–106; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_bldr-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 13

The business is cyclical and housing-start driven but not concentrated in customers. The ten largest customers were 14% of 2025 net sales and the largest 4%, and the four product categories each hold between 22.45% and 26.78% of sales. Demand follows single-family starts: in the third quarter core organic sales fell 6.2% in single-family, while multi-family rose 2.0%; production builders press prices, so margins and cash flow swing with the cycle, but no single customer default drives credit risk.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 28; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 77; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_bldr-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C9; calculation C11

Management is adapting capacity and cost to the downturn. The location count fell by about ten between the annual report and the third-quarter release, the three operating divisions were merged into two in the second quarter of 2026, productivity savings reached about USD 57.00 million in the first nine months, and purchases of property, plant and equipment fell 42.65% year on year; this gives some flexibility, though the cost base remains largely fixed in the near term.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_bldr-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 3; EX-99.1_2026-10-29_bldr-ex99_1.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation C8

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a lower risk level is that the company has no sponsor, pays no dividend, is bound by restrictions on dividends and buybacks and has made no cash buybacks since the first quarter of 2026, while holding liquidity of USD 1,514.11 million and having no note or revolver maturity before 2030. The 2026 acquisition spend is also far below that of 2025. The view would move to low if the proxy statement shows an independent board and no large controlling holder, leverage falls through earnings recovery or debt repayment, and the buyback authorisation lapses unused.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 53; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 95; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 31–32; calculation C25

The view would move to high if buybacks resume funded by the revolver while availability falls, if new debt is raised to fund distributions, or if an amendment weakens the restricted-payment limits; none of these is documented in the sources read.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 92; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27

### Limitations

- The proxy statement was not supplied, so board independence, ownership concentration, executive pay and related-party dealings are not established; each could move the medium governance view in either direction.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 106–108; Source readiness (CP-0)

- The executed credit agreement and indentures were not supplied, so capacity to pay dividends, repurchase shares, invest or incur debt is not assessed; the view rests on documented behaviour and not on legal capacity.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 109–111

- The earnings contribution and pro forma EBITDA of acquisitions are not disclosed, so the leverage effect of deal spending cannot be separated from the organic decline and the acquisition finding is directional only.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 87; 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 9

- Executive incentive design is known only for performance share units, which reward return on invested capital and relative shareholder return, so whether pay encourages leverage reduction or further buybacks is not established.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_bldr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 15

- Customer contract terms and pricing power are not disclosed beyond the ten-customer share and the five largest named customers, so pricing pressure is described and not measured.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-12-31_bldr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 28
