## Findings

### Bottom line

Tidewater is a publicly held operator with no sponsor or controlling holder identified in the supplied documents, so creditor risk comes from earnings concentration in a short-contract vessel business and from the Wilson acquisition, not from owner behaviour.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 67; 10-K_2025-12-31_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 40; Source readiness (CP-0)

West Africa produced 40.93% of segment vessel operating profit in FY2025 on 27.09% of vessel revenue, and many contracts run a year or less with termination on 90 days or less notice. The Wilson acquisition on 2026-08-31 added 22 vessels in Brazil and USD 224,506 thousand of loans, USD 189,678 thousand of which the parent guarantees.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tdw.htm.jsonl, pages 141–142; 10-K_2025-12-31_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 40; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, pages 13–15; calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C06

The unused USD 492,355 thousand of the share repurchase authorization is the main route for value to leave creditors, and the proxy statement and executed debt documents were not supplied, so shareholder control and legal capacity are not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 52; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

- Revenue visibility is short, so cash flow follows utilization and day rates quickly. Many contracts run a year or less and can be ended on 90 days or less notice, no backlog amount is stated, and nine-month 2026 utilization was 78.1% against 75.3% with an average day rate of USD 23,007 against USD 22,753, while vessel operating cost per active day rose 8.70% to USD 9,694.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 40; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, pages 38–39; calculation C09

- Earnings are concentrated in West Africa, which produced 40.93% of FY2025 segment vessel operating profit on 27.09% of vessel revenue. Its nine-month 2026 operating profit then fell 20.17% as the segment ran 55 vessels against 68 and day rates fell 10.4%, and about 21% of cash held by foreign subsidiaries was awaiting conversion to dollars, so local-currency receipts delay the cash that reaches the parent.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tdw.htm.jsonl, pages 141–142; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, pages 42–43; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 44; calculation C02; calculation C04

- Customer concentration is moderate. The five largest customers were 29.8% and the ten largest 47.9% of FY2025 revenue, no customer reached 10% in 2025 against 12.3% for one customer in 2024, and a Mexican customer that paid USD 54.0 million of overdue amounts in the fourth quarter of 2025 still owed USD 12.4 million at 2025-12-31.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-K_2025-12-31_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 86

- Operating cost shocks and uninsured idle time weigh on cash flow. Drydock costs are deferred and amortized over 30 months, cash drydock spending was USD 87,898 thousand in the nine months of 2026, the Iran conflict added about USD 5.4 million of crew and travel cost, USD 1.8 million of insurance and USD 1.8 million of fuel in the Middle East from March 2026, and the issuer does not generally buy business interruption insurance.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 71; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 31

- The Wilson acquisition changes the credit profile more than any owner behaviour. The issuer paid USD 283.1 million in cash against a base price of USD 500.0 million, assumed USD 229,047 thousand of debt at face, and now guarantees the BNDES construction loans and the Banco do Brasil loan, so a default on those loans could trigger cross-default into the 2030 notes and the revolver, and the purchase accounting is provisional.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, pages 15–18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, pages 51–52; calculation C05

- Capital returns are the owner behaviour that matters to creditors. About USD 215.7 million of shares were bought back over three years, USD 90.0 million in 2025 and USD 7.9 million in the nine months of 2026, funded from cash, no dividends were declared, and USD 492,355 thousand of the August 2025 authorization remained at 2026-09-30.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 46; calculation C07

- History shows debt-funded growth followed by refinancing, not distributions. The 2023 purchase of 37 vessels from Solstad cost about USD 594.2 million and was funded by cash, a term loan and notes, and in July 2025 the issuer replaced three instruments with USD 650.0 million of senior unsecured notes due 2030, paying redemption premiums of USD 15.0 million and USD 4.5 million.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 7

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against the view that owner behaviour is not the risk is the size of the repurchase authorization against liquidity: USD 492,355 thousand remains while cash was USD 390,844 thousand at 2026-09-30, and the issuer repurchased USD 90.0 million of shares in 2025 in the year it refinanced. The view would change if the proxy statement names a large holder or a controlling relationship, if the indenture permits distributions beyond the authorization, or if buybacks resume while Wilson integration costs and the Middle East cost increases persist. The strongest evidence against the concentration view is that Europe/Mediterranean produced USD 71,836 thousand of nine-month 2026 vessel operating profit, 31.04% of the total and up 49.79% on a year earlier, so the profit base is broadening away from West Africa.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, pages 40–42; 10-K_2025-12-31_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 8; calculation C03; calculation C04; calculation C07

### Limitations

- The proxy statement, which holds ownership, board and related-party detail, was not supplied, so the conclusion that no controlling holder exists rests on none being named in the annual report and would change if a large holder is named.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tdw.htm.jsonl, pages 145–147; Source readiness (CP-0)

- No indenture, credit agreement or BNDES loan agreement was supplied, so legal capacity for shareholder payments, new debt, liens and asset transfers is not established and the buyback conclusion describes an authorization, not a permitted amount.
  Basis: Source readiness (CP-0); 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, pages 13–14

- The Wilson Companies are in the statements for one month and their purchase accounting is provisional, so conclusions about the acquired earnings and the leverage effect of the acquisition are provisional until a full quarter and the final accounting are reported.
  Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, pages 7–9

- No investor presentation, earnings release, backlog figure or management outlook with numbers was supplied, so the revenue-visibility conclusion rests on contract terms in the risk factors and not on contracted days.
  Basis: Source readiness (CP-0); 10-K_2025-12-31_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 40

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The Wilson acquisition is the one transaction that changes the creditor position in the period. Completed on 2026-08-31 under an amended and restated purchase agreement dated 2026-08-25, it bought all the capital stock of two Brazilian companies owning 22 platform supply vessels from three sellers, for a base price of USD 500.0 million reduced by the acquired companies' net debt and adjusted for working capital, which left USD 283.1 million paid in cash. The consideration was cash plus assumed loans, with no equity issued to the sellers.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 50; calculation C05

No new debt was issued in the nine months of 2026: the debt table adds USD 224,506 thousand of acquired BNDES and Banco do Brasil loans that the issuer assumed, the parent signed a guarantee agreement on 2026-09-21 that replaced the former guarantors of the BNDES construction loans from 2026-10-01, and the revolving credit facility was undrawn when the report was signed. The USD 650.0 million of senior unsecured notes due 2030 and the revolver were issued and signed on 2025-07-07, so they are not new in the period.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, pages 13–15; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 43; calculation C06

The purchase accounting is provisional and open for up to a year, and the issuer expects to leave the acquired companies out of its internal-control assessment at 2026-12-31, so figures for the acquired business may change.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 47; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tdw.htm.jsonl, page 49
