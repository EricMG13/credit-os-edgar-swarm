## Findings

### Bottom line

Energizer's credit profile is weak: leverage is high, earnings are falling and partly supported by items that may not repeat, and creditors depend on refinancing USD 2,117.2 million of notes that fall due in 2028 and 2029. Liquidity of USD 635.8 million against USD 8.6 million of debt due within a year means the first test is refinancing, not a cash shortfall, and the 2028 notes fall due no later than 590 days after the as-of date.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a weak assessment is that earnings still cover interest and liquidity is adequate. Trailing adjusted EBITDA is 4.03 times the next twelve months' contractual interest of USD 142.8 million and reported EBITDA is 2.88 times, no note matures before the 2028 notes, the company refinanced successfully in 2025, capital intensity is low at 2.24% of sales, and about USD 53.1 million of the tariff refund is still to be collected. The production credit, expected at USD 55 million to USD 65 million a year until its phase-out begins in 2030, is recurring rather than one-off, and the guided fourth quarter needs only USD 4.6 million more adjusted EBITDA than the prior-year quarter, against USD 7.0 million to USD 17.0 million of programme benefit still to be realised. The view would move toward average if fiscal 2026 results confirm adjusted EBITDA of USD 580 million or more with add-backs below USD 164.5 million, if adjusted net leverage returns to the 5.12x of 2025-09-30, if the refund is collected in cash and if the 2028 notes are refinanced early near the 6.00% coupon of the 2033 notes. It would weaken if adjusted EBITDA falls short of USD 580 million, add-backs rise again, free cash flow fails to cover the dividend as in fiscal 2025, revolver borrowings rise above USD 30.0 million, or the 2028 notes approach their final twelve months unrefinanced.

### Company description

Energizer makes and sells household and specialty batteries, portable lights and auto care products under the Energizer, Eveready and Rayovac brands, with Varta in Latin America and Asia Pacific, and Armor All, STP and A/C PRO in auto care. Sales go through mass merchants, food, drug, hardware, automotive and e-commerce channels, and about 40% of fiscal 2025 sales arose outside the United States. In the nine months to 2026-06-30, Batteries & Lights was 78.03% of segment net sales and Auto Care 21.97%. Battery demand follows device usage, consumer demographics and disasters and auto care follows the car parc and miles driven, so cash flow is needs-based but seasonal: batteries peak around the first fiscal quarter and auto care in the third and fourth, which makes working capital swing within the year. The business model supports debt service through steady replacement demand and low capital intensity, but it gives little pricing protection, because the sources give no backlog or customer contract term and sales depend on retailer shelf space.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is recurring in the sense that batteries and auto care products are replaced or topped up, but it is price-sensitive and moves with promotion. Organic growth was 0.7% in fiscal 2025, made of volume growth of 1.5% and pricing declines of 0.8%, then fell 2.3% in the nine months to 2026-06-30 as volumes fell 2.1% on softer U.S. consumer demand and a storm-heavy prior year, with pricing down 0.2%. The third quarter turned positive, with organic sales up 2.7% from volume gains of 4.1% against pricing declines of 1.4%, so recent volume has been bought with promotion. North America, 52.76% of nine-month sales, fell 2.93% in the nine months though it grew 4.31% in the third quarter. The largest customer, at 12.8% of fiscal 2025 sales, and the retailer trend toward private label limit pricing power; the expired acquired brand licence cut third-quarter sales by USD 17.2 million.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

The sources give no fixed-versus-variable cost split, but cost of products sold was 63.08% of net sales in the nine months to 2026-06-30, SG&A 19.09% and advertising 5.1%, and the plant network is being reshaped under Project Momentum. Principal inputs are manganese dioxide, zinc, silver, nickel, lithium, steel and plastic for batteries and refrigerant R-134a for auto care; zinc hedges cover a notional of about USD 24 million against nine-month cost of products sold of USD 1,360.3 million, so on the information supplied most input-cost and tariff exposure is not hedged and passes to margin unless priced. Margins have not been defended: adjusted gross margin fell 2.7 percentage points on mix, input cost and production inefficiency, partly offset by the tariff refund. Cost flexibility exists in advertising, which fell 6.55% to USD 109.9 million, and in the programme's savings of about USD 15 million in the first nine months of its fourth year, but both are modest against the margin loss.

### Competitive forces

Competitive rivalry is intense and mature: the annual report calls the categories highly competitive, names competitors with greater resources and describes aggressive competition in the United States, so promotion and price investment cap margin recovery and weigh on cash flow. The threat of new entrants comes mainly from retailers' private label and from e-commerce and discounters, which the company expects to shift consumption between channels and which can force lower prices or loss of distribution. Substitutes are not described beyond private label, so no further credit effect is claimed. Buyer power is high: customers frequently seek pricing concessions and better trade terms, one retailer is 12.8% of sales, and retailer consolidation is cited, which supports the view that pricing is a structural margin headwind. Supplier power is moderate: raw materials are commodities with fluctuating prices but available from several sources, and the company hedges part of its zinc, so supplier power matters mainly through tariffs and input-cost swings.

### Macro, policy and trade factors

Policy and trade factors are material to credit; social and technological factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver based on the provided materials. On the economic side, U.S. consumer demand is soft, currency moves reduce margin when local currencies weaken because much product cost is dollar-linked, and floating-rate debt of USD 791.9 million carries interest-rate risk.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Energizer presents an average but pressured business profile supported by scale, established brands, low capital intensity and adequate liquidity, but credit quality is constrained by leverage of 7.70x reported and 5.50x adjusted EBITDA, falling earnings that rely on add-backs, a tariff refund and a production credit, and a maturity wall in which USD 2,117.2 million of notes fall due in 2028 and 2029. Cash-flow durability appears weak because free cash flow swung between 10.95% and 99.79% of reported EBITDA and leaves little after the dividend. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on fiscal 2026 results meeting the USD 580 million guide, on collection of the refund, and on a coupon near the 6.00% of the 2033 notes. The primary downside path is margin erosion before the 2028 refinancing. Further analysis would require the fiscal 2026 results, the financing documents, the cash held by the U.S. borrowers, a rating and market spreads. Top risk: refinancing the 2028 and 2029 notes on weaker earnings at a higher coupon.

Capital expenditure is only 2.24% of trailing sales, and free cash flow after the dividend was USD 65.6 million, so ordinary operations were funded from cash flow over the trailing period. The 2025 issue of USD 400.0 million of 2033 notes and the extension of the bank facilities to 2030 and 2032 show that lenders and investors supported the credit when adjusted net leverage was 5.12x, below the 5.50x of today.
