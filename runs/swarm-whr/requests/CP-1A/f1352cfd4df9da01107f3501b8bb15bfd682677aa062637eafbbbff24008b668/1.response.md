## Findings

### Bottom line

Whirlpool is a listed appliance maker with no sponsor or controlling holder named in the filings read, and its 2026 financial policy has favoured creditors, but weak earnings and new secured debt leave unsecured creditors dependent on a large fourth-quarter cash recovery.

Common shares outstanding rose 16.31% from February to October 2026 after the equity raise, and the new mandatory convertible preferred converts into common stock in February 2029, adding 10.79% to 12.68% to the October share count, so it is a cash cost of about USD 48.88 million a year until then and not a maturity.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against a cautious view is that management has already acted on most levers. Dividends are suspended, USD 1,081 million of equity was raised, the USD 2,000 million asset-based facility was undrawn at 2026-09-30, cash was USD 1,103 million and only USD 14 million of debt falls due within twelve months, which gives liquidity of USD 3,296 million before any borrowing-base cap. The weakness also has identifiable causes in tariffs, soft North American demand and a customer bankruptcy, while small domestic appliances held an 18.6% EBIT margin and price increases of 7% and up to 10% are announced.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_whr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_whr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21; EX-99.1_2026-10-26_exhibit991pressrelease0930.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2; calculation C18; calculation C45

The view would improve if fourth-quarter cash flow brings net debt below USD 4,500 million, if the proxy statement shows an independent board and no related-party leakage, and if the executed documents show that the older notes share the new security or that restricted-payment capacity is small. It would worsen if the fourth-quarter inflow falls short, if a further covenant amendment or waiver is needed, if dividends or buybacks resume before net debt falls, or if the India, China or Arcelik stakes are sold below the USD 674 million at which they stood at 2026-09-30.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-10-26_exhibit991pressrelease0930.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_whr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–22; calculation C44; calculation C34

### Limitations

- The proxy statement was not supplied, so board composition, independence, any holder above 5%, executive pay and the related-party approval policy are not established; the finding that creditor risk comes from management policy rather than a holder could change if one holder controls the votes.
- Only the issuer's summaries of the asset-based facility and the second-lien indenture were read, so restricted-payment, debt and lien capacity, the facility maturity and the ranking of the older unsecured notes are not established; the conclusion that covenants limit leakage could be weaker than stated.
- Fourth-quarter cash flow and year-end net debt are management guidance and a derived arithmetic gap, not reported results; the deleveraging conclusion holds only if that guidance is met.
- The sales split by product category is not carried in the text read, so the business concentration conclusion rests on segment, country and customer figures only.
- Segment figures for 2025 include the former Asia business until its deconsolidation in November 2025, so year-on-year segment changes mix a changing perimeter and the share of the 2026 sales fall attributed to the Other column is approximate.
- Nothing after 2026-10-27 is read, so the effect of the fourth-quarter price increases, any later rating action and the date the private placement ended are not established; each could shift the earnings-recovery conclusion either way.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Whirlpool's 2026 funding changed in three steps. In February it sold common stock and 8.50% mandatory convertible preferred stock for USD 1,081 million net and used about USD 900 million to repay part of its revolving credit facility. In May it amended that facility, dropping the first-quarter interest coverage test and cutting commitments from USD 3,500 million to USD 2,250 million, with refinancing required before July 1. On June 16 it replaced the facility with a USD 2,000 million asset-based facility and issued USD 2,000 million of second-lien notes in two equal series due 2031 and 2034.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-03-31_whr-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 31–32; 10-Q_2026-03-31_whr-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 43; 10-Q_2026-03-31_whr-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 57; 10-Q_2026-09-30_whr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–20; calculation C18; calculation C19; calculation C28

The asset-based facility and the second-lien notes are guaranteed by each domestic and Canadian subsidiary that is an obligor under the facility. The facility has first-priority liens and the notes second-priority liens on the same collateral pool of eligible accounts receivable, inventory, intellectual property and machinery and equipment. The facility carries a springing fixed charge coverage test of 1.00x that applies when availability falls below the greater of 10% of the line cap and USD 135 million. Nothing was drawn on it at 2026-09-30, and borrowing-base availability and letters of credit are not stated.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_whr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20–22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_whr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 54
