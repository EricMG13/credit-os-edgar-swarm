## Findings

### Bottom line

Whirlpool's credit is weak and deteriorating but liquid: net debt of 7.10x last-twelve-months EBITDA and negative free cash flow leave creditors reliant on a fourth-quarter cash inflow that guidance sets well above last year's.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against a weak view is that management has pulled most levers early and the operating cash flow target has held at about USD 700 million through three releases. Dividends are suspended, USD 1,081 million of equity was raised, nothing was drawn on the asset-based facility, maturities are cleared until 2028 on management's statement, ongoing EBIT margin has risen each quarter, and the 25% tariff on imported appliances favours a business that makes about 80% of the major appliances it sells in the United States. Small domestic appliances earned an 18.6% EBIT margin in the third quarter and October orders in the United States were reported up about 40%.

### Limitations

- Borrowing-base availability, letters of credit under the asset-based facility and the facility maturity are not stated, so the liquidity conclusion rests on committed amounts that could overstate what can be drawn.
- The fourth-quarter figures are derived from guidance stated as approximate round numbers, so the implied cash inflow, the implied earnings and the gap to the net debt target are approximate.
- No peer, sector or market data were supplied, so the view that the business profile is adequate rests on the issuer's own disclosure and cannot be set against typical leveraged-credit issuers.
- The issuer states no EBITDA, so leverage rests on EBITDA built from statement lines that includes one-offs worth USD 129 million over the last twelve months, and the leverage conclusion would look worse on the measure that excludes them.
- The proxy statement and executed debt documents were not read, so the governance direction, restricted-payment capacity and the ranking of the older unsecured notes are not established and could change the view of loss given default.
- A recovery value, legal capacity and covenant headroom are not assessed here, so no conclusion on loss given default, basket capacity or compliance is drawn.

### Company description

Whirlpool makes and sells major home appliances (laundry, refrigerators and freezers, cooking and dishwashing) and small domestic appliances led by the KitchenAid stand mixer, under brands led by Whirlpool and KitchenAid. It sells mainly to retailers, distributors and builders and directly to consumers, and most products are not sold under long-term contracts. North America is the core: MDA North America was 65.43% of 2025 net sales, the United States alone 65.06%, and Lowe's about 15% of sales and about 44% of receivables; Latin America was 21.08%, with Brazil 15.46%, and small domestic appliances 7.14%. The portfolio has narrowed through the 2024 exit from Europe and the Middle East and North Africa, the cut of the India stake to about 40% in November 2025 and the sale of the last 25% of Beko Europe in June 2026.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_whr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 4–8; 10-K_2025-12-31_whr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_whr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13

Demand is cyclical and discretionary and moves with housing and consumer confidence, so cash-flow visibility is low outside the seasonal pattern of a first half of cash outflows and a fourth quarter of cash inflow. That pattern supports debt service mainly in the fourth quarter, and it makes refinancing confidence depend on a recovery in North American earnings rather than on contracted revenue.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_whr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10; EX-99.1_2026-05-06_exhibit991pressrelease0331.htm.jsonl, page 1; EX-99.1_2026-10-26_exhibit991pressrelease0930.htm.jsonl, page 1

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is cyclical, discretionary, concentrated and not contractual. Volume is the dominant driver: MDA North America sales fell 8.3% in the third quarter and 5.9% in nine months on soft industry demand, and the first-quarter release describes a recession-level industry decline in the United States after consumer confidence collapsed in late February and March, which it attributes to the war in Iran. Price and mix swing the result: unfavourable in the first quarter, when the Supreme Court tariff ruling and expected refunds disrupted industry pricing, favourable in the third quarter after the largest price increase in a decade, with a further 7% increase effective in the fourth quarter and up to 10% in Latin America.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Whirlpool presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, brands, domestic manufacturing behind a 25% import tariff and a small domestic appliance segment earning an 18.6% EBIT margin, but credit quality is constrained by leverage of 7.10x EBITDA, negative free cash flow, concentration in North America and one customer, and secured debt that ranks ahead of the older unsecured notes unless they share it. Cash-flow durability appears weak due to a first half of outflows, a fourth-quarter release that must reach USD 1,526 million and margins that have not yet recovered. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on delivery of the fourth-quarter cash inflow, the recovery of North American margins in 2027 and ratings of Ba3 at Moody's, BB- at Fitch and BB with a negative outlook at S&P. The primary downside path is a fourth-quarter shortfall that leaves net debt near USD 4,760 million, a seasonal first-quarter outflow on a shrinking borrowing base and a springing covenant that warns late. Further analysis would require borrowing-base availability, the executed debt documents, the proxy statement, instrument-level debt after June 2026, cash interest paid in 2026 and peer data.
