## Findings

### Bottom line

LGI Homes is a management-led, listed homebuilder with no sponsor or controlling holder identified, so creditors face housing-cycle risk rather than sponsor extraction risk, but board and ownership protections cannot be assessed without the proxy statement.

Shareholder distributions have been modest: no dividend has been paid, repurchases were USD 30,974 thousand in FY2024 and USD 23,639 thousand in FY2025 and none were made in the nine months of 2026, and the revolver restricts repurchases and dividends through December 31, 2026. Pay for the three senior executives is linked to net debt to capital, which was 41.6% at June 30, 2026.

### Key findings

No sponsor, parent or controlling holder is identified, so there is no outside owner to rely on for equity support and no sponsor extraction to fear; ownership percentages and voting control are not established because the proxy statement was not supplied. The chief executive officer is also chairman and a founder, and the board of seven includes a lead independent director.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 14–16; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 127; Source readiness (CP-0)

The business is an entry-level spec homebuilder whose credit risk is the housing cycle rather than governance: retail home sales were 0.86% of FY2025 homebuilding revenues and wholesale bulk sales to institutions 0.14%. Buyers need mortgages at rates near 7%, so cancellations of 48.9% and a 47.5% fall in backlog in the nine months to September 30, 2026 feed straight into future closings and cash conversion.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C2; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 4–7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47

Revenue is spread across five regions and no region dominates: Central was 0.27% of nine-month 2026 revenues and West 0.25%. Florida reported a loss before income taxes in both nine-month periods and inventory impairments were recorded in Florida and Central, so regional diversification limits, but does not remove, local-market shocks.
Basis: calculation C5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23

Shareholder distributions are modest and recently paused, which limits extraction risk: no dividend has ever been paid, and repurchases were USD 30,974 thousand in FY2024 and USD 23,639 thousand in FY2025 with none in the nine months of 2026. The FY2025 repurchases coincided with a rise in net debt to capital from 41.2% to 43.2%, which shows willingness to return cash while leverage was rising, although the amounts were small relative to the debt. The revolver restricts repurchases and dividends through December 31, 2026.
Basis: calculation C9; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 61; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 73; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 82; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 53

Executive pay is partly tied to a creditor-relevant measure: retention payments for the chief executive, president and chief financial officer depend on net debt to capital at June 30, 2026, and the reported ratio of 41.6% falls in the band that pays 100% of target under the printed rule. A separate bonus kicker adds 25% of the 2026 bonus if return on equity exceeds 10%, which rewards earnings rather than deleveraging.
Basis: calculation C8; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 126–127; 10-Q_2026-03-31_lgih-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 51–52; 10-Q_2026-06-30_lgih-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 40

The structure is a holding company, so debt service depends on distributions from subsidiaries; the revolver and the senior notes rely on subsidiary guarantees, and the LGI Living loan sits in a ring-fenced subsidiary with a limited recourse guaranty from the company, which adds secured, non-recourse debt outside the revolver guarantee.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 43–44; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11

Recent events are neutral to mildly positive for creditors: the dual listing on Nasdaq Texas from July 9, 2026 changes trading rather than credit, and the amendment of October 9, 2026 moved part of the revolver to 2030 but modified covenants whose terms are not printed.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_lgih-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a benign governance view is that a management-led company with USD 157.3 million of repurchase authorization remaining and a bonus kicker tied to return on equity could restart buybacks once the revolver restriction lapses after December 31, 2026, while leverage is still high; cash has already been returned to shareholders while net debt to capital was rising. The view would turn to concern if the proxy statement shows a concentrated holder, or if repurchases or dividends restart before net debt to capital falls. It would improve if the amended revolver keeps the restriction on distributions, or if the retention and bonus plans stay tied to leverage.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 56; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 73; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 126–127; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 53
