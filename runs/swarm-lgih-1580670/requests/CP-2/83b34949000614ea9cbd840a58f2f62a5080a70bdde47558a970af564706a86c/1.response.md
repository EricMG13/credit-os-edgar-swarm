## Findings

### Bottom line

LGI Homes has a weak financial profile on an adequate business: earnings cover debt service thinly and orders are falling, so creditors rely on liquidity, inventory turning into cash and an extended revolver, with no maturity before 2028.

Net debt was 9.27x last-twelve-months EBITDA at September 30, 2026 and EBITDA covered cash interest 1.39x, or 0.91x without the capitalized-interest add-back. Backlog fell 47.5% and cancellations reached 48.9%, so the 2026 fall in net debt may not repeat. Covenant headroom after the October 2026 amendment is not established.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C3; calculation C11; calculation C10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10

### Key findings

The financial profile is weak because earnings cover debt service thinly: net debt was 9.27x last-twelve-months EBITDA and EBITDA covered cash interest 1.39x at September 30, 2026, against 4.69x and 2.69x in FY2024. The issuer's EBITDA adds back capitalized interest charged to cost of sales, equal to 34.93% of that EBITDA; without the add-back cover is 0.91x, so cash interest is not met by operating earnings on the stricter measure. EBITDA could fall 28.29% before cover on the issuer's measure reaches 1.00x.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C5

Demand is the primary risk to the view and it is worsening: net orders fell 30.0% in the nine months to September 30, 2026, the cancellation rate rose from 28.1% to 48.9% and the backlog of homes fell 47.5%. Entry-level buyers need mortgages at rates near 7%, so weaker orders and more cancellations cut the closings that turn inventory into cash. The backlog equals 52.65% of the homes closed in the fourth quarter of 2025, so year-end closings depend more on homes sold and closed within the quarter, although third quarter 2026 homebuilding revenues were 10.31% higher than a year earlier.
Basis: calculation C10; calculation C11; calculation C12; calculation C13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47

Operating leverage is high and margins are falling: in FY2025 EBITDA fell 51.22% while homebuilding revenues fell 22.57%, and homebuilding gross margin was 19.7% in the nine months to September 30, 2026 against 21.9% a year earlier. Pre-tax income was flat only because lower selling and administrative costs of USD 12,886 thousand and higher other income of USD 10,445 thousand, mostly from unconsolidated entities, offset a USD 23,577 thousand fall in gross profit, so earnings quality is lower than the flat result suggests.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C9; calculation C18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 38

The recent fall in net debt came from inventory turning into cash, not from stronger earnings: operating cash flow was USD 184,732 thousand over the last twelve months after outflows of USD 283,712 thousand in FY2024 and FY2025 together, because inventory spending fell 83.49% in the nine months and payables rose. Net debt fell USD 120,527 thousand from December 31, 2025, and that continues only while land and construction spending stays low and homes keep closing.
Basis: calculation C14; calculation C15; calculation C17; calculation C6

Liquidity is adequate and the maturity profile is distant but concentrated: nothing is due before 2028, USD 378.3 million was available to borrow under the revolver and the October 2026 amendment moved 73.3% of commitments to 2030, yet senior notes of USD 700,000 thousand fall due in 2028 and 2029, which is 44.84% of principal. The issuer estimates the fair value of the 2029 notes at 94.06% of carrying value, and refinancing them at the rate of the 2032 notes would add about USD 9,000 thousand of interest a year, an illustration equal to 7.88% of last-twelve-months cash interest.

The amendment modified covenants and neither its levels nor the covenant definitions are printed, so headroom is not established. Availability is also capped by a borrowing base of about USD 1.9 billion that counts the USD 1,100,000 thousand of senior notes, so a fall of about 20% in the base would remove all availability.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a weak view is cash generation and balance-sheet flexibility: last-twelve-months operating cash flow was 116.04% of EBITDA, the company repaid a net USD 116,459 thousand of notes in the nine months, cash plus available revolver capacity of USD 447,737 thousand is 3.92x last-twelve-months cash interest, and third quarter 2026 homebuilding revenues rose 10.31%; management also states that a significant portion of the backlog is in homes further along in construction and expected to close in the near term. If closings keep converting inventory to cash while land spending stays low, leverage can keep falling without any earnings recovery, and the stated plan to meet liquidity needs for twelve months is credible.
Basis: calculation C16; calculation C25; calculation C13; calculation C30; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 52

The view would turn more positive if net orders stabilise and cancellations fall back toward 28.1%, if gross margin holds at 19.7% or better, and if the revised covenants leave clear room; it would turn more negative if operating cash flow becomes negative again as land spending resumes, if cover on the issuer's measure approaches 1.00x (an EBITDA fall of 28.29%), or if further impairments follow the USD 4,681 thousand recorded in 2026.
Basis: calculation C5; calculation C10; calculation C9; calculation C22

### Strengths and weaknesses

The first credit strength is liquidity with a distant maturity profile. Nothing is due before 2028, USD 378.3 million was available to borrow under the revolver at September 30, 2026, and the amendment of October 9, 2026 moved 73.3% of commitments to 2030, so a further fall in orders is unlikely to open a funding gap before the 2028 maturities. Availability is limited by a borrowing base built on land and lots that also counts the senior notes, so the support is real but conditional.
Basis: calculation C25; calculation C24; calculation C37; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10

The second strength is cash conversion that is working in creditors' favour. Operating cash flow of USD 184,732 thousand over the last twelve months and USD 116,459 thousand of net note repayments cut net debt by USD 120,527 thousand and net debt to EBITDA from 10.76x at December 31, 2025 to 9.27x, and the company used released inventory cash to repay debt rather than to make distributions. The gain reverses if orders stop supporting closings.
Basis: calculation C14; calculation C6; calculation C30; calculation C1; calculation C2

The third strength is cost and land-spend flexibility. Selling and administrative costs fell USD 12,886 thousand in the nine months, inventory spending fell 83.49%, capital spending on property and equipment was USD 2,509 thousand over the last twelve months, and option contracts cap the loss from walking away from land at the USD 17,907 thousand of deposits, so management can protect cash quickly if demand weakens further.
Basis: calculation C18; calculation C17; calculation C14; calculation C20

The fourth strength is financial policy: there is no sponsor, no dividend has been paid, repurchases are paused and were restricted through December 31, 2026 before the latest amendment, and senior executive retention pay is tied to net debt to capital, which aligns management with creditors on leverage.
Basis: calculation C30; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 126

The fifth strength is the asset base: inventory at carrying value is 88.59% of total assets and 2.36x net debt with impairments of USD 4,681 thousand so far, which supports loss absorption even though carrying value is not realisable value.
Basis: calculation C21; calculation C22

The main weakness is thin earnings cover on high leverage: net debt is 9.27x EBITDA, cover of cash interest is 1.39x and falls to 0.91x without the capitalized-interest add-back, so interest paid in cash while homes are built is not covered by operating earnings on the stricter measure. A 28.29% fall in EBITDA would bring cover on the issuer's measure to 1.00x, which leaves little room for a weaker second half.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C5

The second weakness is demand: net orders fell 30.0%, cancellations reached 48.9% and backlog fell 47.5%, so the closings that release inventory cash are less visible than a year ago, and the commitments to buy land rose 36.73% in the same period, which raises the cash needed if orders do not recover.
Basis: calculation C10; calculation C11; calculation C20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 47

The third weakness is margin and earnings quality: EBITDA fell 51.22% on a 22.57% fall in revenues in FY2025, gross margin fell to 19.7% in 2026, and other income, whose increase came mainly from unconsolidated entities, was 23.57% of nine-month pre-tax income, which is less repeatable than home sales.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C9; calculation C19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 38

The fourth weakness is refinancing concentration: senior notes of USD 700,000 thousand, 44.84% of principal, fall due in 2028 and 2029, 26.7% of revolver commitments still mature before 2030, and the 2029 notes carry the lowest coupon, so refinancing at the coupons paid on the later issues would raise cash interest on a cover ratio that is already thin.
Basis: calculation C23; calculation C24; calculation C27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10

The fifth weakness is what is not known: covenant definitions and headroom after the amendment, with an interest cover test that was due to step up to 1.50x against 1.33x on interest incurred, the rank and security of the senior notes and ownership percentages are not established, so the view cannot be tested against the contract terms that decide when lenders can act.

### Company description

LGI Homes designs, builds and sells entry-level and active adult homes under the LGI Homes brand and luxury homes under the Terrata Homes brand in 36 markets in 21 states, reported in five segments, and has built homes since 2003. Revenue is almost entirely the sale of homes to individual buyers, mostly renters converting to owners who need a mortgage, plus bulk sales to institutions buying single-family rental homes, a small land and rental line, and mortgage and insurance joint ventures. The business is transactional, discretionary and highly sensitive to mortgage rates, so cash-flow visibility is limited to the backlog and debt service depends on continued closings.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 3–7

The company owns and controls a lot position equal to 12.47 years of FY2025 closings, which lowers the land spending needed to keep operating and supports cash conversion in a slow market, but also means inventory is tied up for years; a new community takes three to four years to develop from raw land. Seasonality concentrates closings in the second to fourth quarters, so first-quarter results are not representative.
Basis: calculation C32; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 78

### Ownership and group structure

LGI Homes, Inc. is a listed holding company with no sponsor, parent or controlling holder identified in the filings, so there is no outside owner to supply equity support and no sponsor extraction risk, but ownership percentages and voting control are not established because the proxy statement is not supplied. Debt sits at the parent (revolver and senior notes, guaranteed by subsidiaries) and in a ring-fenced subsidiary, LGI Living - SFR 1, LLC, whose secured loan has only a limited recourse guaranty from the parent; the chief executive officer is also chairman and a founder. For creditors the structure means debt service depends on subsidiary cash flows and that the secured loan is small relative to the group.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 14–16

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is volume driven and price is flat: homes closed rose 1.51% and the average sales price rose 0.69% to USD 366,449 in the nine months to September 30, 2026, so growth came from volume while homebuilding costs rose 5.08% against revenues up 2.21%. Retail home sales are 85.37% of homebuilding revenues and wholesale bulk sales 14.63%; bulk contracts are lumpy, the buyers are institutions that are not named or sized, and FY2025 year-end backlog included 506 homes under bulk agreements against 146 a year earlier, so bulk demand can swing the backlog and the bulk share of the 2026 backlog is not stated.
Basis: calculation C28; calculation C34; calculation C33; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 77–78

The revenue base is therefore cyclical, discretionary and rate-sensitive rather than recurring, with a small rental line (operating lease income of USD 11.144 million in the nine months) that adds a little recurring income. With orders down 30.0% and cancellations at 48.9%, pricing power is weak: the company is closing the homes it has sold at flat prices while demand weakens, and any price cut or incentive to sustain orders would reduce margin further.
Basis: calculation C10; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Costs are mostly variable but there is no evidence that they are passed through: homebuilding costs were 80.31% of homebuilding revenues in the nine months, third-party subcontractors supply both labor and materials, the filings name labor, commodities and lumber as the price changes that matter most, and costs rose faster than revenues, so cost inflation or tariffs reach margin before they reach the sales price. Gross margin fell from 24.2% in FY2024 to 20.7% in FY2025 and to 19.7% in the nine months to September 30, 2026 (21.9% a year earlier), including a USD 4.681 million impairment in 2026.
Basis: calculation C9; calculation C33; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 59

Operating leverage is high but selling and administrative costs have flexed: EBITDA fell 2.27 times as fast as revenues in FY2025, yet the company cut selling and administrative costs by USD 12.886 million in the nine months of 2026 (selling down 5.89%, general and administrative down 6.59%) and held pre-tax income flat with help from USD 10.445 million of higher other income. Margins can be defended against a modest volume decline through overhead cuts but not against a combined volume and price fall, because gross profit fell USD 23.577 million while only USD 12.886 million of overhead came out.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 38

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

The business needs little capital spending but a great deal of working capital: purchases of property and equipment were USD 2.509 million over the last twelve months while inventory is 88.59% of total assets, so free cash flow is set by inventory spending, not by capital expenditure. In FY2024 and FY2025 operating cash flow was an outflow of USD 283.712 million against EBITDA of USD 452.443 million as inventory was built, and it turned to an inflow of USD 184.732 million over the last twelve months (116.04% of EBITDA) as inventory spending was cut by 83.49%.
Basis: calculation C14; calculation C15; calculation C16; calculation C17; calculation C21

EBITDA therefore translates into durable free cash flow only when the company is shrinking its inventory, which is the state of the last twelve months, and not when it is growing; cash interest of USD 114.165 million is paid while homes are built and the capitalized interest returns through cost of sales only as homes close. Land purchase commitments of USD 390.569 million, up 36.73% since December 31, 2025, show that the company is still committing to buy land while orders fall, although the cash at risk on walking away is the USD 17.907 million of deposits.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C20; calculation C4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense and entry barriers are low: the company competes with numerous national, regional and local homebuilders for buyers, land, financing, materials and labor, and with sales of existing homes and rental housing, and some competitors are more geographically diversified and better placed to ride out a regional downturn. For creditors this limits pricing power, so a fall in demand shows up in margin and volume together, as in the 2.2 percentage points fall in gross margin in 2026.
Basis: calculation C9; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 20

Buyer power is high because retail buyers are mortgage dependent and price sensitive and wholesale buyers are institutions that can negotiate bulk contracts; supplier power is moderate because subcontractors, most of them non-unionized, supply labor and materials and the filings flag shortages and tariff effects on labor, land and raw materials. Substitutes (existing homes and renting) matter most when mortgage rates are near 7%, which is the condition reported for 2026, and they cap the price the company can ask when buyers cannot qualify for a mortgage.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months the outlook is cautious: backlog is down 47.5% and the cancellation rate is 48.9%, so fourth quarter 2026 closings rest on a backlog that management says is largely near completion plus homes sold and closed within the quarter, and first quarter 2027, the seasonally weakest quarter, starts from a much smaller backlog than a year earlier; management says it can fund its liquidity needs for twelve months. Net debt can keep falling if closings convert inventory to cash, but with orders and backlog falling EBITDA is unlikely to rise materially, so the current 9.27x is the reference point for the period; this is an analyst reading, as the filings give no earnings guidance.

### Downside scenario

The realistic downside starts with mortgage rates staying near 7% and cancellations staying near 48.9%, so the backlog, already 52.65% of last year's fourth quarter closings, fails to refill. The first operating break is fewer closings in the first half of 2027, which cuts revenue against largely fixed overhead; the issuer's own history shows EBITDA falling 2.27 times as fast as revenues. Gross margin then compresses further through incentives and impairments, and a 28.29% fall in EBITDA would take cover on the issuer's measure to 1.00x, already breached on the stricter measure.
Basis: calculation C12; calculation C8; calculation C5; calculation C3

Cash transmission matters more than earnings: with fewer closings, the inventory release that produced USD 184.732 million of operating cash flow slows, the borrowing base, which is built on land and lots, can shrink, and availability of USD 378.3 million falls with it, one for one, because the USD 1100.000 million of senior notes and the revolver borrowings sit inside the same base. Management has levers: land spending has already been cut 83.49%, selling and administrative costs have been trimmed, deposits cap the cost of leaving land contracts, repurchases were restricted through 2026 before the amendment and property sales brought USD 36.954 million. On this evidence resilience looks sufficient to avoid liquidity distress through 2027 because nothing matures before 2028 and liquidity is USD 447.737 million, but covenant headroom is not established, the interest cover test was due to rise from 1.15x to 1.50x after 2026 against 1.33x on interest incurred unless the amendment changed it, and the 2028 and 2029 notes (USD 700.000 million) would have to be refinanced on weaker cover.
Basis: calculation C14; calculation C25; calculation C17; calculation C20; calculation C35; calculation C36; calculation C37; 10-K_2025-12-31_lgih-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 82; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lgih-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4

The primary downside path is a sustained order and cancellation slump that cuts closings, margin and the inventory cash release together, leaving thin cover and a heavy 2028 and 2029 refinancing.

### Overall credit view

Overall, LGI Homes presents an adequate business profile supported by liquidity, flexible costs and land commitments, no sponsor and a long lot position, but credit quality is constrained by thin interest cover on high leverage, falling orders and backlog and compressed margins. Cash-flow durability appears weak because recent cash generation comes from shrinking inventory rather than earnings. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on order recovery, the borrowing base and the covenant levels after the 2026 amendment, because USD 700.000 million of notes fall due in 2028 and 2029. Compared with typical leveraged issuers in the sector the profile is not established because no peer data is supplied. Further analysis would require the executed credit agreement and indentures, ownership data, sector comparables and fourth-quarter order data. Top risk: a sustained fall in orders that cuts closings and cash conversion before the 2028 maturities.
