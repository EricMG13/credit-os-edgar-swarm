## Findings

### Bottom line

Teleflex is an adequate credit that has improved through asset sales more than through earnings, and creditors now depend on how management splits the proceeds still to come between debt and shareholders. Net leverage was 2.82x at 2026-09-30 against 7.64x at year-end 2025 on a narrower earnings base. It is 2.92x after USD 41.4 million of later repurchases and 1.56x if the Acute Care and Interventional Urology sale closes at about USD 530 million, while the effect of fourth-quarter taxes on the OEM gain is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 54; calculation C12

The retained business turned 57.71% of last-twelve-months EBITDA into free cash flow and has no note maturity before 2028-06-01, but USD 50.3 million of one-off gains sits in last-twelve-months EBITDA, shareholder payments were 2.53 times free cash flow, and covenant headroom is not reported.

### Key findings

Leverage is moderate because debt was repaid, not because earnings grew, and the ratio depends on the earnings base. After the OEM sale closed Teleflex repaid USD 700.0 million of Term A-2 and USD 490.0 million of the revolver, yet the 2.82x reported net leverage becomes 4.01x on FY2025 EBITDA and 3.21x without USD 50.3 million of settlement and tariff-refund gains. On the reported basis the pro forma readings are 2.92x after USD 41.4 million of repurchases made after 2026-09-30 and 1.56x if the sale for about USD 530 million closes; the OEM tax effect is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 52; calculation C11; calculation C12

Earnings improved, but more than a tenth of last-twelve-months EBITDA is one-off. The EBITDA margin is 18.67% against 14.86% in FY2025, yet the USD 25.0 million patent settlement gain and USD 25.3 million of tariff refunds are 11.95% of EBITDA, and without them the margin is 16.44% and nine-month gross margin is 58.27% instead of the reported 59.8%. Further tariff refunds may arrive in the fourth quarter but no receivable is recorded, and quality remediation and excess inventory charges already weigh on gross margin.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 42–44; calculation C11

The retained core earns well, but Interventional is the swing factor for earnings. Vascular and Surgical earned segment margins of 38.12% and 45.10% and grew nine-month revenue 7.2% and 8.4% without acquisitions, while Interventional earned 10.37%, saw third-quarter revenue fall 2.6%, and has a goodwill cushion of about 15% that a 1% rise in the discount rate would cut below 5%. Its profit is 13.21% of segment profit but 32.77% of nine-month operating income after unallocated costs, so losing all of it would cut operating income by about a third, although segment profit would still be 4.11% above the nine-month 2025 total.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a more favourable view is what management did with the first proceeds and what the retained segments earn. It repaid USD 1,190.0 million of bank debt against USD 350.0 million of repurchases since 2026-07-01, pushed bank maturities to 2031-05-26, grew segment operating profit 20.0% in nine months, and today covers run-rate interest 5.04x. The strongest evidence for a weaker view is the fourth-quarter OEM tax payment, the unstated financial policy, and earnings that carry 11.95% of one-offs.

It would weaken if the revolver rises above USD 60.0 million at year-end to pay taxes while repurchases continue under the USD 400.0 million authorisation, if Interventional revenue keeps falling and an impairment follows, if the Acute Care sale fails or slips beyond 2027, or if the strategic review ends in a sale of the company.

### Company description

Teleflex is a Delaware company founded in 1943 that sells single-use critical care and surgical devices to hospitals through direct sales forces, independent representatives and distributors, and it reports three continuing segments. Vascular (Arrow catheters, EZ-IO intraosseous access, QuikClot hemostats) was 46.05% of FY2025 continuing net revenues, Interventional (coronary and peripheral catheters, GuideLiner, MANTA and, since 2025-06-30, BIOTRONIK drug-coated balloons and stents) was 32.51% and Surgical (ligating clips, fascial closure, a powered bariatric stapler) was 20.98%; by the nine months of 2026 Interventional had grown to 37.17% of segment net revenues. Substantially all revenue is single-use devices used in high-acuity procedures, so revenue recurs with procedure volumes, but most orders ship within days or weeks, backlog gives no visibility, and sales are highest in the fourth quarter. The credit consequence is a base of consumable demand with short visibility rather than contracted revenue.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 39; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C13

The company is much smaller than the one the debt was raised against. Continuing operations are 60.44% of FY2025 combined net revenues, because the OEM business was sold for net cash proceeds of USD 1.5 billion on 2026-08-03 and the Acute Care and Interventional Urology businesses are agreed to be sold for about USD 530 million but had not closed by 2026-11-05. The disposals replaced a plan announced on 2025-02-27 to separate those businesses into a new listed company, and the BIOTRONIK purchase of 2025-06-30 for USD 825.2 million added the growth segment. Portfolio changes of this size make earlier leverage and margin series poor guides, so the analysis uses the recast continuing figures only.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-K_2024-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12; calculation C13

Cyclicality is limited. Demand follows hospital procedure counts, with elective procedures and flu seasons moving the quarterly pattern, and the one disclosed structural demand loss is bariatric surgery, where the adoption of GLP-1 products reduced demand and led to a USD 100.0 million impairment of the Titan SGS asset group in 2025. Regulation is a barrier and a cost: products are mainly Class I and Class II devices cleared through the FDA 510(k) process, the European Union Medical Device Regulation certification is expected to complete in 2026, and the FDA quality system regulation was replaced on 2026-02-02. No customer, distributor or group purchasing concentration is disclosed, so concentration risk is neither found nor ruled out.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 63; 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 14–16; 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 46

### Ownership and group structure

Teleflex is publicly held and is its own reporting entity, so issuer and group are the same company. No controlling holder, sponsor or parent is named, 315 holders of record were reported at 2026-02-24, and the activist Irenic Capital Management called on 2026-03-27 for board changes and an evaluation of strategic alternatives without a stated stake. Financial policy is therefore set by a board and management that changed substantially in 2026: the chief executive left on 2026-01-08, an interim served until Jason Weidman started on 2026-06-08, and the independent Chair changed on 2026-01-08 and again on 2026-05-15. The credit consequence is that policy is neither anchored by an owner with a stated leverage tolerance nor yet tested by the new team.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 59; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 60; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Debt sits at the parent. Substantially all material domestic subsidiaries guarantee the Credit Agreement and both note series, each guarantor is directly or indirectly 100% owned, and the bank facilities are secured by a lien on substantially all assets of the company and each guarantor; the security of the notes is not stated and the guarantors are not listed in the filings. The share of assets and earnings outside the guarantor group is not established, and the receivables securitization vehicle is bankruptcy-remote, so its assets are not available to the parent's other creditors. Obligor group total assets at 2025-12-31 were restated twice, from USD 2,797.8 million to USD 2,831.2 million and then to USD 5,011.1 million, so guarantor-level comparisons across reports are not reliable.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 54–55; 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 80; 10-Q_2026-03-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 141; calculation C16

Financial policy is visible only in actions. The quarterly dividend is USD 0.34 per share, the board authorised USD 1.0 billion of repurchases on 2025-12-09 of which USD 400.0 million remains, and management said in the 2025 annual report and the first-quarter 2026 report that expected net after-tax proceeds of about USD 1.8 billion would be used primarily to return capital through repurchases and to pay down debt, a statement the later reports do not repeat. The Credit Agreement limits dividends and other restricted payments subject to thresholds, and the note indentures as summarised limit liens, mergers and sale-leasebacks without mentioning restricted payments, but neither document is supplied, so the capacity to return cash is not established. Creditor alignment is partial: repayment so far exceeds repurchases, but nothing binds the remaining proceeds.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 54; 10-Q_2026-03-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 30; 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 61; calculation C07

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue growth is mostly acquired and the organic base grows slowly. Of the USD 262.7 million rise in nine-month net revenues, USD 198.1 million came from the BIOTRONIK business, USD 37.7 million from higher volumes of existing products and the remaining USD 26.9 million from price, currency and new products; against a base of USD 1,423.7 million these are 13.91, 2.65 and 1.89 percentage points of the 18.45% rise. Existing-product volumes and selective price increases are the repeatable organic drivers, and volume growth of 2.65% for nine months cannot by itself change leverage quickly.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 44; calculation C01

The latest quarter shows the stall. Third-quarter net revenues rose 0.2% to USD 567.8 million, helped by USD 6.0 million of volume and by price increases and held back by a prior-year reserve release under the Italian payback measure of USD 9.035 million that did not recur; segment net revenues, which exclude that release, rose 1.8%. Vascular grew 4.7% on volume and price, Surgical grew 4.2% on volume, new products and price, and Interventional fell 2.6% on lower volume and price decreases, so pricing power is selective: it is positive in Vascular and Surgical and negative in the acquired Interventional business.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 44; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 48–50; calculation C02

Government and cost-containment pressure is the structural price risk. Most customer relationships outside the United States are with government entities, 42.91% of nine-month revenue was earned outside North America, and the annual report says cost containment, collective purchasing, competitive bidding and tenders, including China's volume-based procurement, may exert downward pressure on pricing. Customer concentration, contract duration, renewal rates and switching costs are not disclosed, and the company names only the Teleflex name and the Arrow brand as essential intellectual property, so durability rests on clinical use and regulatory barriers rather than on patents or contracts.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 28; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; calculation C13

Revenue is recurring, regulated and mostly non-discretionary, partly cyclical through elective procedures, exposed to government pricing and to one demonstrated substitution risk, and not structurally declining in the retained segments. The weaker link is growth, which was bought, so revenue durability supports base cash flow but gives little organic deleveraging.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C02

### Cost structure and margin resilience

The reported margin gain is partly one-off. Nine-month gross margin was 59.8% against 57.1%, helped by lower inventory step-up charges from the BIOTRONIK acquisition and by a USD 25.3 million benefit from refunds of tariffs that a court invalidated on 2026-02-20, and held back by quality remediation and excess and obsolete inventory charges; without the refund the gross margin is 58.27%. In the prior-year third quarter Interventional cost of goods sold was USD 123.1 million on USD 216.0 million of revenue against USD 83.4 million on USD 210.3 million in 2026, so part of the improvement is a base effect rather than efficiency.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 42–44; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 37; calculation C03; calculation C11

Operating expenses rose faster than revenue. Selling, general and administrative expense was 39.2% of nine-month revenue against 35.0%, mainly because a prior-year gain of USD 87.1 million on foreign exchange forwards hedging the BIOTRONIK price did not recur and the acquired business adds operating, integration and amortization costs; research and development rose to 7.9% from 6.7% on Interventional project spend. Operating leverage shows in the segments: Vascular profit rose 16.5% on revenue up 7.2% and Surgical profit rose 34.3% on revenue up 8.4%, while Interventional profit rose only 7.3% on revenue up 45.8%. The same leverage works in reverse, so a revenue decline in a high-margin segment would cut profit faster than revenue.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 44–45; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 48–50

Cost actions are large relative to the earnings they are meant to protect. Three restructuring plans carry aggregate charges of USD 79 million to USD 96 million and expected annual savings of USD 74 million to USD 86 million, the company expects most or all of the charges of the two larger plans to be cash, USD 15 million to USD 19 million of cash costs are expected in 2026 under the plan tied to the sales, and the plans run to 2027 and 2028 with some savings dependent on exit from transition agreements with the buyers. Until they are delivered, stranded costs left by the sold businesses are a drag whose size is not disclosed.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 46–47; calculation C15

Input-cost exposure is real but unquantified. The annual report says commodity price and freight cost volatility can have a significant impact on production cost and that alternative sources of materials and sterilization can be delayed by regulatory requirements, and the third-quarter report adds energy price and supply chain risk from the Middle East conflict. No pass-through mechanism, fixed-cost share or price indexation is disclosed, so the ability to defend margin under cost inflation is not established; the evidence of defended margin is the price increases in Vascular and Surgical in 2026.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 43; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 49–50

Margins can be defended under modest volume pressure because gross margin is high, but this is not demonstrated under input cost inflation or adverse mix. The 29.19% segment margin overstates group profitability: unallocated items, which include corporate costs and manufacturing variances, absorbed 56.28% of segment profit in nine months against 46.51% a year earlier, and income before interest and taxes was 11.77% of nine-month revenue, so margin resilience is better read on the group EBITDA margin of 18.67% over the last twelve months.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 38; calculation C03

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital intensity is low, which limits investment risk but leaves little to cut. Capital expenditure was USD 54.4 million in nine months, 3.23% of net revenues against 5.02% a year earlier and 3.47% over the last twelve months, while depreciation and amortization were 9.20% of nine-month revenue because of acquired intangibles. Capital spending is already a small cash call, so reducing it is a weak lever in a downturn.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 38; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C06

Conversion of EBITDA to free cash flow is volatile. It was 39.26% in FY2023, 79.73% in FY2024, 0.49% in FY2025 after a build-up of receivables and higher tax payments, and 57.71% over the last twelve months; nine-month free cash flow was USD 180.4 million against a negative USD 61.1 million a year earlier. Without the USD 50.3 million of settlement and refund receipts, last-twelve-months free cash flow would be USD 192.69 million before tax effects, or 51.98% of EBITDA without the one-offs.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 53; calculation C05

Cash uses compete with debt service. Cash interest paid was USD 119.337 million in FY2025, while accrued interest expense fell to USD 21.2 million in the third quarter from USD 31.8 million as debt was repaid and the average interest rate on debt fell to 3.5% from 4.1%. Financial foundation's last-twelve-months cover on accrued interest is 4.09x, below the 5.04x on run-rate coupon cost because it includes interest on debt repaid in August. Dividends were USD 58.774 million and repurchases USD 554.951 million over the last twelve months, together 2.53 times free cash flow, restructuring cash costs and the fourth-quarter OEM taxes are still to come, and income taxes payable were USD 194.638 million at 2026-09-30 against USD 18.787 million at year-end 2025. Noncurrent operating lease liabilities of USD 60.785 million are outside the debt figures.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense and shows first in Interventional. The annual report calls the industry highly competitive, with competitors from start-ups to larger and better-funded companies, and says Teleflex competes on clinical superiority, reliability, support and cost-effectiveness; no competitor is named and no share is given. The numbers show rivalry in Interventional price decreases and lower volumes in the third quarter while Vascular and Surgical raised prices, so the threat falls on the acquired segment's 10.37% margin and on the goodwill cushion that rests on it.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 49; calculation C03

The threat of new entrants is moderate. Regulatory clearance, European Union certification, quality system rules and hospital relationships limit entry, but the company says no single patent is essential beyond the Teleflex name and the Arrow brand, and its USD 25.0 million patent settlement in April 2026 shows both the value of its intellectual property and the need to defend it. Entry would reach creditors through price, so it is a margin risk.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 14–16; 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 42

The threat of substitutes is concentrated in bariatric surgery. The Titan SGS bariatric stapler faced reduced demand as GLP-1 products spread and was impaired by USD 100.0 million in 2025; the filings name no other substitution threat to the retained products, and Surgical revenue still grew 8.4% in nine months. The risk is bounded: further impairment would be non-cash, but a broader shift away from procedures would cut volume in a segment that earned a 45.10% margin.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 63; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 46; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 49–50; calculation C03

Buyer power is high in government-funded markets. Hospitals buy through direct sales forces, representatives and distributors, most customer relationships outside the United States are with government entities, and governments use price controls, collective purchasing, competitive bidding and tenders, with China's volume-based procurement named as an adverse influence on future pricing; concentration among buyers is not disclosed. The consequence is a cap on price increases in the 42.91% of revenue earned outside North America, which makes price a less reliable offset to cost inflation there.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18; 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 28; calculation C13

Supplier power is low to moderate. Materials, components and sterilization services come from many suppliers and no single supplier is substantial, yet the risk language names sole-source suppliers and sterilization providers, regulatory requirements can delay alternative sources, and a contract sterilizer whose ethylene oxide plants at two sites have faced legal proceedings has sterilized some vascular and surgical products. A suspension at those plants could interrupt supply of the affected products, which is a revenue and working-capital risk more than a margin risk.

### Macro and policy factors

PEST factors are a material credit driver here, because tariffs, foreign currency, government pricing and regulation each move margin, cash flow or refinancing cost, so the four categories are assessed.

Political and regulatory factors work through tariffs and pricing rules. A court decision on 2026-02-20 invalidated the tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, replacement tariffs were imposed under other authority, and Teleflex received refunds that gave a USD 25.3 million benefit in continuing operations and USD 13.9 million in discontinued operations; more refund requests are expected in the fourth quarter with no receivable recorded, and the scope and durability of the replacement tariffs are uncertain. Gross margin and cash flow therefore carry tariff risk in both directions. Regulatory cost is shifting rather than rising: European Union Medical Device Regulation costs fell in the third quarter, the FDA quality system rule was replaced on 2026-02-02, and European Union certification is expected to complete in 2026.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 42–43; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 45; 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 14–16

Economic factors work through currency, interest rates and energy. About 42.91% of nine-month revenue was earned outside North America, the principal currency exposures are the euro, Chinese renminbi, Mexican peso, Malaysian ringgit, Swiss franc and Canadian dollar, and Teleflex hedges with forward contracts of USD 547.3 million of notional and cross-currency swaps of USD 1.6 billion of notional at 2025-12-31, the swaps being designated as net investment hedges. A 10% rise in the dollar would raise the fair value of the hedge contracts by USD 179.5 million and a 10% fall would cut it by USD 182.2 million at that date. Floating-rate debt is 38.72% of principal, so a 1.0 percentage point rise in rates would add USD 6.32 million a year to interest cost, and the Middle East conflict is cited as a source of energy price and supply chain risk.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 92; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 24–25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 43; calculation C09; calculation C13

Social and technological factors are secondary. Social factors matter only through treatment shifts that change procedure demand, and the single disclosed case is the adoption of GLP-1 products, which reduced bariatric demand. Technology is not a primary credit driver on the evidence: research and development is 7.9% of nine-month revenue, the acquired business adds drug-coated balloons, stents and catheters, and no disruptive technology threat to the core single-use products is stated, although Interventional development spend must keep the acquired franchise competitive.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 63; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 45

### Downside scenario

The scenario is Interventional keeping the third-quarter pattern while the fourth-quarter taxes and further repurchases are drawn on the revolver. The initiating condition is that Interventional revenue, which fell 2.6% in the third quarter on lower volume and price decreases, stays weak at a 10.37% margin. The first operational break is that the acquired business fails to hold its revenue base; with a goodwill cushion of about 15% that a 1% rise in the discount rate would cut below 5%, the first financial signal would be an impairment test on goodwill and intangibles of USD 3,693.068 million, 58.18% of total assets and 105.71% of equity, a non-cash charge against equity that already has no tangible cushion, since equity less goodwill and intangibles is negative USD 199.352 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 49; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 41–42; calculation C03; calculation C04

The financial transmission is through earnings and cash. Interventional produces 32.77% of nine-month operating income, and the USD 50.3 million of one-off gains, 11.95% of EBITDA, would not recur, so leverage would move toward the without-one-offs reading of 3.21x before any operating loss; all leverage readings here use net debt at 2026-09-30, and the pro forma positions of 2.92x and 1.56x and the unstated OEM tax effect are set out in the financial profile above. The liquidity and refinancing consequence is limited in the next two years: cash and the undrawn revolver of up to USD 940 million cover the notes due 2028-06-01, of USD 500.0 million, 2.74 times, but the next refinancing would be priced off a weaker earnings record, and the covenant tests of 4.50x net leverage and 3.00x interest coverage cannot be checked against reported ratios.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-Q_2026-09-30_tfx.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21; calculation C03; calculation C10; calculation C11

Management has levers, and they are enough to avoid liquidity distress on the disclosed figures. It can suspend repurchases, since USD 400.0 million remains and the programme may be suspended at any time, hold the dividend, which costs about USD 53.94 million a year at the current share count and is 22.20% of last-twelve-months free cash flow, realise restructuring savings, and apply the Acute Care proceeds to the revolver. Free cash flow after dividends was USD 184.216 million over the last twelve months, so the realistic failure mode is slow leverage creep and loss of policy credibility, not default. The observable triggers are a revolver balance above USD 60.0 million at 2026-12-31, Interventional revenue and margin in the fourth quarter, any goodwill impairment charge, the pace of repurchases and the covenant disclosure in the next annual report.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Teleflex presents an adequate business profile supported by recurring single-use device demand, high Vascular and Surgical margins, low capital intensity and no note maturity before 2028-06-01, but credit quality is constrained by acquired rather than organic growth, a thin and impairment-sensitive Interventional segment, earnings that include USD 50.3 million of one-offs, shareholder payments of 2.53 times free cash flow and an unstated financial policy. Cash-flow durability appears adequate due to 57.71% conversion of EBITDA into free cash flow and 3.47% capital intensity, tempered by working-capital timing and receipts that may not repeat. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on holding leverage near the current range through the fourth-quarter taxes, on the Acute Care proceeds, and on market access at roughly the 5.875% coupon paid in June 2026 as the 2028 notes approach in 2027, with no agency rating to anchor that access. The primary downside path is Interventional weakness and fading one-offs while the revolver funds taxes and repurchases, lifting leverage against covenants that cannot be checked. Against typical leveraged-credit issuers in the sector the profile is stronger on margin and free of a sponsor, and weaker on stated policy; the filings give no peer data, so that comparison is a judgement. The financial profile trend is better on leverage and cash flow but is not comparable across periods because the perimeter changed. Further analysis would require the covenant compliance calculation, letters of credit, the OEM tax amount, the proxy statement, the executed agreements, a leverage target and the securitization and Term A-1 terms.
Analysis complete. Top risk: shareholder returns and tax payments absorbing the proceeds that reduced leverage.
