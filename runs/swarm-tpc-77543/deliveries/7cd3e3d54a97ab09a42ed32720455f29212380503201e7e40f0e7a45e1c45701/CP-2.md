## Findings

### Bottom line

Tutor Perini has an adequate and improving credit profile: gross debt is covered by cash and no debt of size falls due before 2033, so creditors are exposed mainly to fixed-price project execution and one court award, not to refinancing.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tpc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 14–18; calculation C23; calculation C14

At 2026-09-30 gross debt is 1.02x last-twelve-months EBITDA, USD 504,218 thousand of general-purpose cash plus the undrawn USD 350,000 thousand revolver gives USD 854,218 thousand of liquidity, and backlog of USD 20,353.3 million is 3.25x last-twelve-months revenue. The weak points are earnings that were a loss in FY2024 on legal charges, a USD 175 million Philadelphia hotel award under appeal that equals 34.71% of general-purpose cash, and customer advances of USD 2,044,207 thousand that fund the business and would reverse if awards stopped keeping pace with revenue. Awards were 0.96x revenue in the first nine months of 2026, so backlog replacement and claims collection are what to watch.

### Key findings

The business profile is adequate: large and scaled in work where few firms compete, but concentrated in a handful of big fixed-price public projects. Last-twelve-months revenue is USD 6,254,126 thousand, public agencies were 73.82% of nine-month 2026 revenue, and two customers with multiple projects made up 24.1% of it, at 13.3% and 10.8%; the third-quarter report names the second customer, so the earlier Business & governance figure of one customer at 10.8% understates concentration. Management says fewer firms now bid on the largest civil projects, which helps pricing but cannot be tested because no market-share figure is supplied. A funding delay or a dispute on one large project therefore moves earnings and cash more than the revenue base suggests, so scale is a strength and concentration is the matching weakness.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tpc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 44, 49–50; 10-K_2025-12-31_tpc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 124; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C7; calculation C8

Revenue is visible for about three years, but the pipeline that feeds it has stopped growing. Nine-month 2026 new awards of USD 4,540.3 million were 0.96x revenue recognised, against USD 7.0 billion in the same period of 2025, and backlog is down 5.77% on a year earlier although up from USD 19.9 billion at the end of the second quarter. Firm remaining performance obligations, which exclude unexercised options, are USD 17.4 billion, 2.78x revenue and down only 2.79% on a year earlier, so the firm base is steadier than the headline backlog. Management attributes the fall to an unusually strong 2025 and fewer large awards reaching decision in 2026, and points to a pipeline above USD 200 billion over three to four years and a multi-billion-dollar California healthcare project to be added within two years; these are expectations, not bookings. Backlog equal to 3.25 years of revenue protects earnings in the near term, but sustained awards below revenue would shrink it and the customer advances attached to it.

Margin recovery is real but comes from volume and lower stock-linked pay rather than better gross margins, so it is less secure than the headline suggests. Income from construction operations moved from a margin of -2.40% in FY2024 to 4.18% in FY2025 and 6.37% in the first nine months of 2026, yet gross margin was 12.37% against 12.38% a year earlier; of the USD 120,487 thousand rise in operating income, 72.69% came from higher gross profit on 17.62% more revenue and the rest from lower general and administrative cost as share-based pay fell by USD 43.5 million. Civil earned a 14.0% margin against 4.8% in Building and 2.1% in Specialty Contractors, so Civil execution carries the credit.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an adequate and improving view is that the improvement is recent and leans on items that may not repeat: FY2024 EBITDA was negative, 44.20% of FY2025 operating cash flow came from working-capital inflows, awards are below revenue, legal and settlement charges have recurred in both years, and the Philadelphia award equals 44.13% of last-twelve-months EBITDA. The strongest evidence for a better view than adequate is that consolidated net debt is -1.78x EBITDA, general-purpose cash alone exceeds gross debt, and a cash call of USD 337,900 thousand from the award, the window wall ruling and the share awards would still leave USD 516,318 thousand of liquidity.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tpc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 25–28, 49–50; 10-K_2025-12-31_tpc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 32–33, 71; calculation C11; calculation C6; calculation C18; calculation C17; calculation C23; calculation C19

The view would worsen if the appeal fails while awards stay below revenue and billings in excess of costs start to fall, if unapproved change orders are written down, or if repurchases accelerate while backlog shrinks. It would improve if awards exceed revenue for several quarters, the appeal cuts the award, claims on the California transit and other projects convert to cash, and the debt documents show tight limits on payouts.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next one to two years earnings should hold or improve while cash is drawn on by known items. The large projects now in execution have substantial scope remaining, backlog rose from USD 19.9 billion at the end of the second quarter to USD 20,353.3 million, annual cash interest is expected to fall by the issuer-stated USD 21.0 million, and management projects lower stock-linked pay in 2026 and a much larger fall in 2027. Against that, the Philadelphia appeal, the share award settlements and the repurchase authorisation of USD 170 million could all draw on the same USD 504,218 thousand of general-purpose cash, so liquidity should stay ample but shrink before it grows.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tpc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 25–28, 47–50, 61–62, 67; calculation C7; calculation C26; calculation C16; calculation C14

Over three to five years the outlook turns on funding that is not settled. The federal infrastructure law funding window closed on 2026-09-30 and surface-transportation authorisations were extended only to 2026-12-11 while a USD 580 billion reauthorisation bill is negotiated; management states that most major projects rely more on state and local funding, cites USD 41.4 billion of voter-approved transportation measures from November 2024, and points to defence funding in Guam and the Indo-Pacific. These are management statements and the filings give no probability or timing for reauthorisation, so a funding gap would first show as slower awards and shrinking customer advances rather than as a debt problem.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_tpc-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 52–53; 10-K_2025-12-31_tpc-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8

Competitive and cost forces matter to creditors mainly through bidding and margin. Management says few firms bid on the largest fixed-price civil projects, which supports awards and pricing, while customers are mostly public agencies that can end contracts for convenience and buy by competitive bid, so buyer power is high on smaller jobs and lower on the largest. Cost inflation and tariffs are managed by buying out materials, taking fixed-price quotes from subcontractors and pricing escalation into bids, which shifts much of the risk to suppliers but not all of it; about 4,000 employees are union members with wage escalation priced into bids. Technology and social factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver based on the provided materials.

### Downside scenario

The realistic downside is a loss on the Philadelphia appeal combined with awards that stay below revenue, which hits cash before it hits leverage. First, the court award of about USD 175 million and the USD 42 million window wall ruling are upheld and the share awards settle in cash, a cash call of USD 337,900 thousand that leaves USD 166,318 thousand of general-purpose cash and USD 516,318 thousand of liquidity with the revolver, and net debt on general-purpose cash of USD 237,323 thousand, or 0.60x last-twelve-months EBITDA. Second, awards below revenue stop the growth of customer advances; a return of the advances outside joint ventures to their end-2024 level would absorb a further USD 587,370 thousand, 68.76% of the original liquidity, which together with the first call, USD 925,270 thousand, would exceed the original USD 854,218 thousand of liquidity, but that needs several years of shrinking awards and is the tail, not the base case.
Revenue would not fall quickly because backlog covers about three years, and margins would be pressured by further adverse adjustments like the USD 16.4 million recorded in the first quarter of 2026 and by the loss of the recovery carried on the hotel project. Management levers are the undrawn revolver, the discretionary repurchase authorisation, dividends of USD 5.0 million declared in the third quarter, the timing of equipment purchases and distributions to joint venture partners. Resilience looks sufficient to avoid liquidity distress in the first case; distress needs the combined tail, and the covenants apply only to the revolver, so a breach would first affect revolver access, and whether it would reach the notes depends on cross-default terms that are not supplied. Each 10% of claims and unapproved change orders that is not collected would cost USD 68,792 thousand of earnings and cash. These figures are arithmetic on stated amounts, not a forecast.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Tutor Perini presents an adequate business profile supported by a USD 20,353.3 million backlog, public customers and a refinanced balance sheet, but credit quality is constrained by fixed-price execution risk, recurring dispute charges and customer-funded working capital. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because operating cash flow before working capital was USD 415,231 thousand in nine months, although it is flattered by customer advances that cannot be assumed to grow. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on earnings holding near current levels and on bond market access as the 2033 notes approach maturity, since nothing of size matures sooner. The primary downside path is a loss on the Philadelphia appeal combined with awards below revenue and shrinking customer advances. Further analysis would require the executed debt documents, the covenant definitions, the collection timetable for claims and unapproved change orders, the recovery carried on the hotel project and market prices for comparison. The top risk is backlog replacement failing while claims remain uncollected.
