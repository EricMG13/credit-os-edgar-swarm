## Findings

### Bottom line

Advanced Drainage Systems has a strong credit profile, with high margins, strong free cash flow and no bond maturity before 2030, but management is spending that strength on acquisitions and buybacks, so rising leverage, not liquidity, is the risk for creditors.
Basis: calculation F01; 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 121; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

Net debt was 1.60x trailing reported EBITDA at 2026-09-30, and 2.30x pro forma for the StormTrap purchase (agreement dated 2026-10-01) and the share repurchase after the quarter end (no day stated), which are 2.18x and 1.72x taken alone. Net sales excluding NDS fell 4.50% in the September 2026 quarter, so the main downside path is weaker volume meeting higher leverage while covenant terms are not supplied.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

The credit is strong today but weakening in direction, and the cause is financial policy more than trading. Total debt rose from 1.45x reported EBITDA at FY2025 to 1.77x over the twelve months to 2026-09-30 while earnings still covered cash interest 9.46x, and the StormTrap purchase and the repurchase after quarter end would lift net leverage from 1.60x to 2.30x pro forma.

A smaller buyback, or StormTrap earnings that prove large against its price of about USD 530 million, would move the view toward stable.

### Company description

Advanced Drainage Systems makes thermoplastic corrugated pipe and related water management products for stormwater and onsite wastewater, sold mainly through distributors into non-residential, residential, infrastructure and agriculture construction. It reports two segments, Stormwater at 78.59% and Wastewater at 21.41% of FY2026 external net sales, and runs 64 manufacturing plants and 41 distribution centers that give it a national delivery footprint, which supports cost and service against regional competitors.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 15; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Cash-flow visibility is moderate rather than high. Orders are short-cycle with many customers on no long-term arrangement, sales are higher in the April to September half (55.08% of FY2026 net sales fell in the first half), and no backlog is published; against that, the customer base is large and the products are specified into long-lived drainage and septic installations, so debt service rests on strong margins and cash conversion rather than on contracted revenue.
Basis: calculation F08; 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 20

### Ownership and group structure

The company is publicly held and no sponsor or parent is named: directors, officers and principal stockholders hold about 16% of the shares and about 22% of the votes counting the employee retirement plan, and charter provisions make a takeover harder. Creditors therefore face no sponsor-style recapitalisation risk and have no sponsor to turn to for support, and policy is set by a board whose independence and credit orientation are not established because the proxy statement is not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 42; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The borrower is the parent, and StormTech, Infiltrator Water Technologies and Orenco Systems guarantee the credit agreement; the joint ventures are small, with the issuer owning 51% of ADS Mexicana and 63% of EQ TruePointe One and guaranteeing at most USD 5.5 million of the South American joint venture's debt, which expires on 2026-12-31. The supplied pages disclose no holding company layer or unrestricted subsidiary designation, but the credit documents that would define them are not supplied and the ranking and security of the senior notes are not stated.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is cyclical, short-cycle and concentrated, with partly lagged pass-through pricing. Demand follows non-residential and residential construction, infrastructure funding and farm income, which the issuer links to credit availability and interest rates; the ten largest customers took about 50% of FY2026 net sales and the two largest distributors 25.8%, so volume risk sits with a few buyers who can switch suppliers on short notice. The annual report states directly what the Business & governance analysis recorded as not described, that many customers have no long-term arrangement.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 48; 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 29; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Pricing power is real but lagged. In the September 2026 quarter price and mix improved while volume fell in both pipe and allied products, the issuer says allied products are less sensitive to resin prices, and it argues that conversion from concrete, steel and PVC to thermoplastic pipe continues as regulatory approvals widen; that conversion is management's view, and it protects volume only while thermoplastic stays price-competitive with the alternatives.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_wms_wms-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 49

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Resin is the main variable input: the company buys more than 1.0 billion pounds a year from about 450 suppliers, had no fixed-price purchase commitments outstanding at 2026-09-30 and relies on selling-price increases to recover cost, which the filing says can lag. Plants carry substantial fixed costs, so margin falls faster than sales when volume drops: against the prior-year quarter the September 2026 gross margin was 1.11 percentage points lower and the Stormwater segment margin 1.48 percentage points lower, even with NDS added.
Basis: calculation F18; calculation F15; 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 50; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wms_wms-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 32

Wastewater held its margin at 44.89% against 44.91% a year earlier, which locates the pressure in Stormwater, where the NDS mix and the resin-linked pipe and allied business both sit. Margins can be defended against modest volume pressure through recycled resin, diesel hedging and price increases, but a combined volume decline and resin spike would outrun those defences, since the filing warns that sharp resin rises can reach levels that cannot be fully passed through because of competing-product pricing.
Basis: calculation F16; calculation F17; 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 50; 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 32

### Porter's five forces

Competitive rivalry is moderate. The stormwater industry is highly fragmented with many regional specialists, the company competes with concrete, corrugated steel and PVC producers as well as other HDPE makers, and claims the only national corrugated HDPE footprint; rivalry is mostly on local coverage, availability and price, which limits margin only when volume is weak.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 20

The threat of new entrants is low to moderate. Entry needs a plant network that serves a shipping radius of about 250 miles, regulatory approvals from municipalities and state transport departments, and recycled-resin processing know-how, which protects returns on the issuer's installed base without preventing regional entry.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 24

The threat of substitutes is moderate and price-linked. Concrete, steel and PVC remain the alternatives; thermoplastic pipe has been gaining share on weight, cost and installation, but the filing accepts that a sharp resin rise can leave the company unable to pass costs through because of competing-product pricing, so substitution risk peaks exactly when resin is expensive.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 49; 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 50

Buyer power is high. Two distributors took 25.8% of FY2026 net sales and the ten largest customers about 50%, many without long-term arrangements, and customer consolidation could move volume to competitors; the consequence for creditors is that a single customer decision can move sales and margin within a quarter.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 29; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Supplier power is moderate. Polypropylene resin suppliers are limited and high-density polyethylene suppliers are geographically concentrated, although the company buys from about 450 suppliers, holds multi-year supply agreements and processes its own recycled resin; supplier power therefore shows up as price volatility and a pass-through lag more than as a supply stoppage.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 50; 10-K_2026-03-31_wms_wms-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 14

Social and technological factors are not primary credit drivers on the supplied evidence.

### Qualitative downside scenario

The most plausible issuer-specific downside begins with a housing and non-residential slowdown in the seasonally weaker second half, coinciding with a resin spike that the company cannot pass on quickly. Pipe and allied volume falls further after the 7.59% drop in domestic pipe sales in the September 2026 quarter, price and mix cannot offset it, and fixed plant costs push gross margin and the Stormwater segment margin down faster than sales, as the 1.11 and 1.48 percentage point falls of the September 2026 quarter already show.

Free cash flow falls but stays positive because capital expenditures can be cut from about USD 200 million, working capital releases in a downturn, and the buyback, which with dividends exceeded first-half free cash flow by USD 43,939 thousand, can be stopped, while the dividend is small. The risk is sequencing: if StormTrap closes funded mainly from the revolver while payouts continue, revolver exposure could pass USD 262,500 thousand and bring the 4.25x covenant into force just as earnings fall, and at pro forma net leverage of 2.30x on reported EBITDA there is less room than at 1.60x. Management levers are enough to avoid liquidity distress in this scenario, because liquidity of USD 891.88 million and no bond maturity before 2030 give time, but they would have to be used. The primary downside path is a volume-led fall in margin colliding with leverage raised by acquisitions and buybacks.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Advanced Drainage Systems presents a strong business and financial profile supported by high margins, strong and flexible cash generation, ample liquidity and maturities that begin in 2030, but credit quality is constrained by customer concentration, volume and resin sensitivity, and a financial policy that has spent more than free cash flow on acquisitions and buybacks. Cash-flow durability appears adequate to strong, because underlying conversion is about half of Adjusted EBITDA and capital expenditure can flex. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on leverage at the time of the 2030 notes and 2031 revolver maturities, which is set by the next two years of payout and acquisition decisions. The primary downside path is a volume-led fall in margin meeting higher leverage while covenant terms are not supplied. Further analysis would require covenant and basket terms, StormTrap and NDS earnings and a stated leverage target. Fundamental credit analysis is complete. Top risk: releveraging through acquisitions and buybacks into a softer volume cycle.
Basis: calculation F23; calculation F24; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)
