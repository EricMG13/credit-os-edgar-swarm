## Findings

### Bottom line

NCL Corporation is a wholly owned subsidiary that the parent supported with equity in 2025 and has not drained, so governance risk is Medium and creditors' exposure rests on the issuer's own covenants and liquidity rather than on parent behaviour.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a benign reading is the revolver. The annual report says its maturity becomes 17 November 2026 if the 2027 exchangeable notes, USD 216,175 thousand at 30 September 2026, have not been repaid or refinanced beyond April 2030 and a liquidity test is not met; the amendment of 15 October 2026 may have changed this, but its text is not supplied, and the quarterly report says only that the issuer expects to repay the notes in cash or refinance them before maturity in February 2027. If the trigger survived and the test failed, a facility with about USD 2.8 billion of commitments after the October 2026 amendment, secured on vessels, would fall due 12 days after the as-of date. A second challenge is that a new chief executive, a recent activist agreement and the Havana Docks case against the parent, in which the Supreme Court on 21 May 2026 reversed the dismissal of a claim that had produced a judgment of USD 112.9 million, could push parent policy toward shareholder returns or asset sales. Governance risk would move to Low on the amendment text removing the trigger and holding distribution limits, and to High on any cash distribution to the parent, a release of collateral without repayment, or cash settlement of exchangeable notes while the parent issues shares to others.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

In October 2026 NCL Corporation Ltd. issued USD 950.0 million of senior unsecured notes due 15 October 2031 under an indenture dated 15 October 2026. The notes pay 8.75% a year, are redeemable at a make-whole price before 15 October 2028 and allow up to 40% to be redeemed from equity proceeds at 108.75% of principal. The proceeds and cash on hand redeemed all of the 6.125% senior notes due 2028, repaid USD 380.0 million of revolver borrowings and prepaid about USD 42.2 million of export-credit loans, together with accrued interest and any premiums, fees and expenses, none of which is stated. On the same date the third amendment to the revolving credit agreement raised commitments from about USD 2.5 billion to about USD 2.8 billion and modified financial covenant definitions and collateral; neither the amended terms nor the collateral changes are described. The 30 September 2026 balance sheet is not pro forma for these events, and issuer-level pro forma net debt, leverage and liquidity are not stated. The new notes pay a coupon 2.625 percentage points above the 6.125% notes redeemed, and the identified uses of USD 947.2 million are close to the USD 950.0 million raised on the assumption that the original USD 525.0 million of 2028 notes was still outstanding.
