## Findings

### Bottom line

Amentum's credit profile is adequate: federal backlog, cost-plus contracts and no named maturity before 2031 support debt service, but 3.32x net leverage, a thin margin and cash conversion helped by receivable sales leave little room for a funding or execution slip.
Basis: calculation C3; calculation C81; calculation C74; 10-Q_2026-07-03_amtm-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12

The primary downside is a repeat of the first-quarter cash squeeze, when operating cash flow was an outflow of USD 136 million, and the full-year free cash flow guidance now needs USD 312 million to USD 362 million in the fourth quarter against USD 261 million a year earlier.
Basis: calculation C90; calculation C39; calculation C40; EX-99.1_2026-08-10_exhibit991-august102026.htm.jsonl, page 4

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a better view is the trend and the issuer's own measure: net leverage fell from 3.53x to 3.32x, the issuer prints 3.0x on principal debt and Adjusted EBITDA, USD 847 million of debt has been repaid voluntarily since June 2025, book-to-bill was 1.1x in the third quarter and 1.3x over the last twelve months, and operating cash flow of USD 225 million and USD 146 million in the second and third quarters reversed the first-quarter outflow. The view would improve if the fourth quarter delivers at least the USD 312 million of free cash flow that the low end of guidance requires without further receivable sales, if funded backlog recovers above its fiscal 2025 year-end level of USD 5,585 million, or if the covenant level and a numeric leverage target are disclosed. It would weaken if fourth-quarter free cash flow falls short of that level, if funded backlog keeps falling, if another funding lapse occurs, if the sponsor group sells down or the board adopts buybacks now that the tax-agreement limits have lapsed, or if fixed-price estimates reverse.

### Business and ownership

Amentum is a federal services contractor with two segments, Digital Solutions and Global Engineering Solutions, formed by the September 2024 merger with the Jacobs business, which lifted fiscal 2025 revenue by 71.59% and makes fiscal 2025 the first comparable year. Control is shared: Jacobs shareholders and a sponsor group holding 38.0% by calculation own the shares, no single holder is shown to control the board, and the credit consequence is that a sponsor sell-down, not a sponsor demand, is the ownership event to watch.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 5–7; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 128; calculation C87

### Revenue durability

Revenue is predictable but not growing: federal customers supplied 81.09% of fiscal 2025 revenue under contracts that can be terminated for convenience, remaining performance obligations due within 12 months cover 49.76% of last twelve months revenue, and the book-to-bill of 1.1x in the third quarter and 1.3x over the last twelve months keeps adding backlog. Nine-month revenue fell 2.51% and the guided midpoint is 3.60% below fiscal 2025, so durability rests on awards replacing the revenue lost to joint venture transitions and divestitures, and the August cut to revenue guidance suggests that replacement is slower than planned.

### Margin resilience

Margins are defended by cost pass-through but not widened by it: cost of revenues is 89.90% of revenue, cost-plus-fee work is 55.88% of revenue, and selling, general and administrative expenses fell 17.95% on merger synergies, which is why the nine-month margin rose to 8.11% from 7.68% while revenue less cost of revenues slipped. Synergy savings do not repeat, so further margin gain has to come from awards and execution, and the fixed-price share raises the chance of reversals.
Basis: calculation C56; calculation C54; calculation C78; calculation C80; calculation C50; 10-Q_2026-07-03_amtm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-07-03_amtm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-Q_2026-07-03_amtm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 27

### Cash conversion

Earnings turn into cash slowly and unevenly: last twelve months free cash flow of USD 474 million is 42.02% of Adjusted EBITDA, cash interest takes 25.44% of Adjusted EBITDA, cash taxes paid were USD 26 million against USD 67 million in the nine months and capital needs are small, so the gap to cash is working capital and receivable sales. The first-quarter outflow reversed in the second and third quarters, and the annual report says federal year-end awards may favour the fiscal fourth quarter, which is consistent with the heavy fourth-quarter requirement in guidance but is not quantified.

### Competitive forces

Rivals, one buyer class and labour set the margin: the annual report names nine primary competitors and says awards are competed and may favour price, and protests held up two awards until the third quarter, so rivalry caps pricing; entry is limited by security-cleared staff, past performance and the need for teaming and contract vehicles; government insourcing is the substitute risk; buyer power is high because federal customers supplied 81.09% of revenue, may terminate for convenience, and 9.01% of revenue came through other prime contractors; and supplier power sits in labour, materials and subcontractors inside cost of revenues of 89.90% of revenue, which cost-plus pass-through limits. The credit consequence is a stable but price-disciplined margin near 7.67%.

### Outlook

The next 12 to 24 months depend on the fourth quarter and the next appropriation: the August update cut revenue guidance by USD 150 million at the low end and USD 350 million at the high end while raising the low end of Adjusted EBITDA guidance by USD 15 million, applying the issuer's May adjustment to fiscal 2025, the new midpoint implies underlying revenue growth of only 0.96% against about 3% stated in May, and Adjusted EBITDA growth of 5.18%. Fiscal 2025 had 14 weeks in its fourth quarter, so implied revenue per week in the fourth quarter ranges from a decline of 1.36% to an increase of 2.75% against that base if the 2026 quarter has 13 weeks. Over three to five years, nuclear and space awards and a book-to-bill of 1.3x over the last twelve months support growth, while federal budget risk stays the main headwind.

### Downside scenario

A funding lapse or continuing resolution like the one that ended on 12 November 2025 is the realistic downside: billing and collections slip, revenue falls, as it did by 2.51% in the nine months to 3 July 2026, and operating cash flow turns negative as in the first quarter, when the outflow was USD 136 million and free cash flow was an outflow of USD 142 million. Working capital then absorbs cash (USD 303 million in nine months), the receivable sale balance has only USD 100 million of headroom under its maximum, and liquidity of USD 1,376 million is 10.12x the first-quarter outflow, so liquidity distress is not the likely outcome. Leverage is the casualty: if reported EBITDA fell USD 100 million the ratio would be 3.69x against 3.32x, and with no dividend to cut, little capital spending to defer and voluntary prepayments as the main lever, recovery to current leverage would take longer rather than fail. Observable signals are funded backlog, the fourth-quarter cash result against USD 312 million, the receivable sold balance and any revolver draw.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Amentum presents an adequate business profile supported by federal backlog, cost-plus contracts and light capital needs, but credit quality is constrained by a thin margin, funded backlog of only 12.94% and cash conversion that leans on receivable sales. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because costs are largely passed through and liquidity is USD 1,376 million. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the amended facilities holding to 2031 and on leverage staying near 3.32x. The primary downside path is a federal funding lapse that delays billing and cash while fixed-price estimates weaken. Further analysis would require the executed credit agreement, the proxy statement, peer and rating evidence, and the fourth-quarter result. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: federal funding timing hitting cash conversion.
