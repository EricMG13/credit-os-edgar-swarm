## Findings

### Bottom line

Creditors face concentrated federal demand and thin margins rather than sponsor extraction: no distribution or creditor-adverse action is documented, but the restrictions on buybacks and asset sales were due to lapse in September 2026 and the sponsor group's current stake is not disclosed.
Basis: 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 64–66; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 72; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 93–94; calculation C56

Sponsor and governance risk is Medium on incomplete evidence, not on conduct. Federal agencies supplied 81.09% of fiscal 2025 revenue, adjusted EBITDA margin was 7.67%, and only 12.94% of the USD 48,237 million backlog is funded.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C18; calculation C45; 10-Q_2026-07-03_amtm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 28

### Key findings

The customer base is deep but concentrated: federal agencies supplied 81.09% of fiscal 2025 revenue (USD 11,672 million of USD 14,393 million), led by the Department of War and the intelligence community at 53.35%, while commercial and international customers rose to 18.91% from 9.55% after the merger. Federal contracts can be terminated for the government's convenience or curtailed at little notice, and the shutdown that ended on 12 November 2025 reduced nine-month revenue, so concentration shows up first as funding-timing risk and only later as customer loss.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C1; calculation C3; calculation C5; calculation C6; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 95; 10-Q_2026-07-03_amtm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 25

Backlog gives multi-year visibility but little funded certainty: total backlog of USD 48,237 million at 3 July 2026 equals 3.41 years of last twelve months revenue, yet only USD 6,240 million (12.94%) is funded, about 5.30 months of revenue. Funded backlog fell by 26.16% during fiscal 2025 while total backlog rose by 4.83%, so conversion to cash depends on annual appropriations rather than on the size of the book.
Basis: calculation C44; calculation C45; calculation C46; calculation C48; calculation C49; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 103; 10-Q_2026-07-03_amtm-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 27–28

Margin improvement is real but partly a shift to riskier contract types: the nine-month adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 8.11% from 7.68%, yet cost-plus-fee work fell from 64.15% to 55.88% of revenue while fixed-price work rose from 23.45% to 29.54%, and net favourable cumulative catch-up adjustments equal 10.02% of nine-month adjusted EBITDA against 4.98% a year earlier. The auditor's one critical audit matter concerns revenue recognition on certain fixed-price contracts, so the contract type gaining share is also the one that depends most on management estimates.
Basis: calculation C21; calculation C22; calculation C30; calculation C31; calculation C32; calculation C33; calculation C50; calculation C51; 10-Q_2026-07-03_amtm-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8; 10-Q_2026-07-03_amtm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 113

Ownership is dispersed but sponsor-influenced, and the sponsor's current stake is unknown: by calculation from merger-date percentages, Jacobs shareholders held 62.0% and the sponsor group 38.0% of the shares after the March 2025 escrow release, the sponsor group can nominate up to five of the 13 directors while it holds 25.1% or more, and most directors are independent. Because the proxy statement that would report current holdings is not supplied, creditors cannot size the sponsor's voting weight or the scale of a possible sell-down.
Basis: calculation C52; calculation C53; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 68; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 71; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 128; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 164–165

Documented sponsor behaviour is creditor-aligned or neutral: the sponsor group put USD 235 million of cash into the company before the merger, advisory fees of USD 4 million a year were not charged in fiscal 2025, no dividend or buyback is recorded, and voluntary term loan payments since June 2025 total USD 847 million (USD 722 million in fiscal 2025 and USD 125 million on 30 June 2026). The one adverse-leaning feature is a charter provision under which sponsor affiliates owe the company no duty to offer corporate opportunities.
Basis: calculation C59; calculation C60; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 69; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 93–94; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 153; 10-Q_2026-07-03_amtm-20260703.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4; 10-Q_2026-07-03_amtm-20260703.htm.jsonl, page 12

The main change for creditors is flexibility rather than conduct: for two years after the 27 September 2024 closing the tax matters agreement restricted buybacks, sales of 30% or more of the acquired assets and equity transactions that could change control, and the sponsor group was bound to vote for the Executive Chair; both periods ended on 27 September 2026 by calculation, 39 days before the as-of date. The annual report states a policy to retain earnings and repay debt, but the 2026 reports do not restate it and management refers to a target leverage profile that it does not quantify.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against a Medium rating is that the record is cleaner than that: no dividend, buyback or sponsor fee in fiscal 2025, USD 847 million of voluntary debt reduction, a majority-independent board and a clean audit all point to Low. The rating stays Medium because creditor protection is judged from summaries: the credit agreement, indenture and stockholders agreement are not supplied, the sponsor's holding is unknown, and the restrictions that held back buybacks and large disposals were due to end in September 2026. The view would move to Low if the proxy statement shows the sponsor group below 15% with no further consent rights and the credit agreement shows tight restricted-payment baskets; it would move to High if a dividend, a debt-funded buyback or acquisition, or a non-pro-rata amendment appeared, or if overruns on the fixed-price contracts now gaining share began to erode margins.
Basis: calculation C60; calculation C56; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 64–66; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 68; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 93–94; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, page 113; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 165–166

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The 2024 transaction merged the sponsor-owned legacy Amentum business with the Critical Mission Solutions business and part of the Divergent Solutions business of Jacobs, spun off in a tax-efficient reverse Morris Trust that closed on 27 September 2024. Before the spin-off the acquired business paid about USD 911 million in cash to Jacobs; Jacobs shareholders received 58.5% of the shares, the sponsor-owned equityholder 37.0% and 4.5% sat in escrow until March 2025, when 3.5% went to Jacobs shareholders and 1.0% to the sponsor group. Total consideration was USD 4,032 million, the legacy business was the accounting acquirer and goodwill of USD 3,004 million was recognised, so fiscal 2024 results exclude the acquired business and fiscal 2025 includes it for the full year.
Basis: 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 5–7; 10-K_2025-10-03_amtm-20251003.htm.jsonl, pages 128–131; calculation C62; calculation C52; calculation C53

Debt raised at closing was a USD 3,750 million seven-year term facility and a USD 850 million revolver, which replaced the earlier first and second lien agreements, together with USD 1,000 million of senior notes due 2032 sold in August 2024 into escrow. On 24 April 2026 an amendment replaced the term facility with a USD 1,400 million Term Loan A and a USD 1,591 million Term Loan B (USD 1,466 million after a voluntary payment of USD 125 million on 30 June 2026) and raised the revolver to USD 1,000 million; the revolver and Term Loan A mature on 24 April 2031, Term Loan B on 27 September 2031 and the notes on 1 August 2032. Voluntary term loan payments since June 2025 total USD 847 million, and the executed amendment is not supplied, so these terms rest on the filings' summaries.

Other transactions are small by comparison: the sale of the Rapid Solutions hardware business to Lockheed Martin for USD 360 million in cash on 26 June 2025, an undated sale of a New Zealand facilities maintenance business, and a USD 70 million working capital payment in the third quarter of fiscal 2025 that settled the merger price. No acquisition was paid for in the nine months to 3 July 2026, and the receivable purchase agreement maximum was raised to USD 400 million in December 2024 and amended again in March 2026.
