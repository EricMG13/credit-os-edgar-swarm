## Findings

### Bottom line

Charles River is a diversified, cash-generative research services group with an adequate but not strong credit profile: net leverage is 2.61x on EBITDA before impairments but 5.06x as reported, and revolver-funded acquisitions and buybacks leave creditors dependent on management's financial policy.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation F1

Demand has turned, with organic revenue growth of 4.7% in the third quarter, and free cash flow could repay the drawn revolver of USD 943,174 thousand in 2.36 years, but covenant headroom, ratings and market access are not established, so the view is adequate rather than comfortable. It would improve if free cash flow went to the revolver and weaken if buybacks resumed while the revolver stays drawn.
Basis: calculation F5; calculation P1; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Key findings

The recovery in demand is real and is led by the largest segment, so the operating trend now supports credit quality. Organic revenue growth moved from down 1.5% in the first quarter to up 0.1% in the second and up 4.7% in the third, discovery and safety assessment grew 5.9% organically in the third quarter on higher regulated study volume, its backlog rose to $2.0 billion from $1.9 billion at the year-end, and management raised its 2026 organic growth guidance by 150 basis points in August and by 100 basis points in November. Management attributes the third-quarter non-GAAP operating margin of 23.0%, against 19.7% a year earlier, to operating leverage on higher study volume and to the sale of the loss-making contract manufacturing business known as CDMO, so some of the gain is portfolio change and not demand. Reported revenue is still guided to fall 1.5% to 2.0% in 2026 because of the May divestitures, and the guidance and backlog are management measures.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an adequate view is on the financial side. Reported total debt to EBITDA of 5.51x over the last twelve months is above the 4.25x covenant ceiling, repeated impairments suggest that earlier acquisitions have not earned their cost, Manufacturing goodwill already carries $380 million of accumulated impairments, and the 2026 programme of acquisitions and buybacks was funded mostly by debt because free cash flow covered only 37.78% of it. If the revolver rises again, or a further impairment lands before the covenant definition is seen, reported leverage becomes the better anchor and the view should move from adequate to weak. The evidence for a stronger view is the demand recovery, a third-quarter margin 330 basis points above the prior year, the sale of a loss-making business, free cash flow of USD 399,634 thousand over the last twelve months and the absence of any material maturity before 2028.
Basis: calculation F1; calculation F6; calculation F11; calculation P6; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 25; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The view would strengthen if free cash flow went to the revolver and buybacks stayed paused until net leverage on EBITDA before impairments fell back to 2.26x, if the compliance certificates and the credit agreement showed ample headroom on the contract definition, or if a rating and market yields showed open access. It would weaken if buybacks resumed or a large acquisition were announced while the revolver is drawn, if organic growth turned negative again, if the backlog of discovery and safety assessment fell below $1.9 billion, or if the 2026 compliance certificates showed tight headroom on the contract definition.
Basis: calculation F1; calculation P1

### Business description and ownership

Charles River is a US-listed provider of outsourced non-clinical drug discovery, safety assessment, research models and quality-control testing, and the cash flow it generates comes from drug developers that must buy regulated studies and models. Discovery and safety assessment was 59.8% of FY2025 revenue, research models and services 21.1% and manufacturing solutions 19.1%, and the United States provided 53.34% of revenue by operating entity, so cash flow follows pharmaceutical and biotechnology research budgets with a euro, sterling and Canadian dollar overlay. No single client was above 4% of revenue, which limits single-client risk, and the company describes many relationships as long-term, but a services backlog can be cancelled and is not a reliable forecast. The business model supports cash-flow visibility through backlog and contracts that can run for several years and weakens it through fixed-price work and client budget swings, so debt service depends on keeping utilisation high.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 143; calculation P2

The company is publicly listed, has no controlling shareholder or sponsor, has paid no common dividend and is influenced by a large shareholder through a cooperation agreement of May 2025 that put a board committee in charge of a strategic review. The review set acquisitions, disposal of non-core assets, cost efficiency and balanced use of cash among acquisitions, buybacks and debt repayment as priorities, the share count fell as buybacks continued, and the company holds 90% of Noveprim with a put on the other 10% from December 2030. Ownership therefore adds buyback and acquisition pressure but no sponsor leakage, and creditors rely on board independence and the revolver's covenants for protection; the files supply no ownership percentages, standstill terms or guarantor structure, and no controlling shareholder is stated.

### Revenue durability, cost structure and cash conversion

Revenue is cyclical and discretionary, not recurring: study volumes follow client research budgets, which fell in 2024 and 2025 and are recovering in 2026. In FY2025 service revenue fell 1.6% and product revenue rose 2.6%, discovery and safety assessment revenue fell 2.0% on lower study volume while research models rose on large-model volume and small-model pricing, and the manufacturing segment lost CDMO customers. Remaining performance obligations on contracts of more than a year were $675.3 million with about 60% due within twelve months, up from about 50% at the year-end, so the multi-year contracted book is only a part of revenue and the larger backlog includes short contracts that the performance obligation figure excludes and that can be cancelled. Revenue durability is Average: diversification across clients is strong, but visibility is short.

### Competitive forces, external factors and credit-translated SWOT

Rivalry is intense, and the company states that it is the largest provider in a regulated field. Safety assessment has dozens of competitors with two main ones, discovery has hundreds with two main ones, research models has four and microbial testing has four, and clients can also use internal resources, so price and capacity compete with service quality, and fixed-price contracts transfer cost overruns to the company; this limits margin upside, and the files give no data on switching costs, client retention or entry barriers, so the durability of share is not established and any view that new entrants are limited is an analyst inference from regulation and capital intensity. The threat of substitutes is rising slowly: the FDA announced expanded use of new approach methodologies in April 2025 and the United Kingdom published a roadmap in November 2025, and the company is building such methods itself, so substitution would shift revenue from animal studies to other services rather than remove it, which is an analyst inference. Buyer power is high collectively because large clients seek lower research cost per drug candidate and many contracts allow termination without a fee, yet low single-client concentration limits any one buyer. Supplier power was high for primates, with a pricing environment the annual report calls extremely dynamic, and eased in 2026 as the company bought a Cambodian supplier and reported lower sourcing cost for large research models.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months the balance of evidence is positive for operations and neutral for leverage. Tailwinds are the discovery and safety assessment backlog of $2.0 billion, bookings and proposals that the company calls robust, the highest net book-to-bill in nearly four years reported for the second quarter, a guidance path that has risen in each of the last two releases, the sale of loss-making CDMO operations and cost savings that are meant to reach about $300 million annualised by the end of 2026. Headwinds are revenue lost to the divestitures, weaker research model demand with organic revenue down 0.6% in the third quarter, restructuring charges for the Create the Future programme, payments of deferred acquisition consideration, and cash taxes that were unusually low in 2026 and may rise. The credit consequence is better EBITDA and free cash flow, which helps deleveraging only if distributions are restrained.

Over three to five years, the main issues are the substitution of animal studies, the pace of the cost programme and the refinancing of USD 1,943,174 thousand of notes and revolver that fall due in fiscal 2028 and 2029. Management expects incremental savings of more than $300 million between 2027 and 2030 and the sale of non-core assets of about 7% of 2025 revenue, which together could raise margins and cut scale. The credit consequence is that refinancing is likely to be manageable if free cash flow is applied to the revolver before 2028, and harder if debt-funded acquisitions continue; the files provide no medium-term leverage target.

### Downside scenario

The most credible downside combines the client funding squeeze the company has already lived through with a continuing programme of revolver-funded acquisitions and buybacks. The initiating condition is renewed restraint in biopharma and biotechnology research budgets, such as the 2024 to 2025 period when discovery and safety assessment revenue fell 2.0% and the backlog slipped from $2.0 billion to $1.9 billion. The first operational break is lower study starts and cancellations in discovery and safety assessment, where cost behaves as semi-fixed and 37.10% of lost revenue reached segment operating income in FY2025 including restructuring charges. The financial transmission is lower EBITDA and free cash flow, with restructuring charges and fixed-price overruns adding cash costs; in the earlier downturn free cash flow still held at USD 501,610 thousand in FY2024 and USD 518,494 thousand in FY2025, so the cash base proved resilient. The liquidity and leverage consequence depends on policy: if buybacks and acquisitions continue while free cash flow falls, the revolver of USD 943,174 thousand would rise again, net leverage would exceed 2.61x on EBITDA before impairments, and reported leverage would stay above the 4.25x covenant ceiling with contract headroom unseen. Management levers are suspension of the $700.0 million buyback programme, lower capital expenditure, slower acquisitions and further cost cuts, and resilience appears sufficient to avoid a liquidity crisis because USD 1,257,207 thousand of liquidity is available and no maturity falls before 2028, provided the revolver can be drawn. The observable triggers are discovery and safety assessment backlog below $1.9 billion, organic growth turning negative again, revolver borrowing rising while organic growth is weak, and any change in the covenant disclosure.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 88; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, pages 101–102; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 27; calculation F1; calculation F8; calculation F13; Financial foundation (CP-1)

A second path is a supply or regulatory shock, such as a primate import restriction or an acceleration of animal-testing reform. The first break would be a halt to studies or a drop in volume, the transmission would be lower revenue with fixed laboratory cost, and the consequence would be a slower recovery than management guides; the files do not quantify this path and it is smaller in the near term than the funding path.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, pages 52–53; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, pages 58–59

### Overall credit view

Overall, Charles River presents an adequate business profile supported by client diversification, a recovering backlog, moderate capital intensity and a cost programme, but credit quality is constrained by revolver-funded acquisitions and buybacks, recurring impairments and an unresolved gap between reported leverage and the covenant definition. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow held at USD 501,610 thousand and USD 518,494 thousand through the 2024 to 2025 downturn and converted 58.61% and 60.82% of EBITDA before impairments in those years. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the use of free cash flow to reduce the revolver before the fiscal 2028 and fiscal 2029 maturities and on market access that no rating or yield in the files confirms. The primary downside path is renewed client funding restraint while distributions and acquisitions continue to be funded on the revolver. Further analysis would require the credit agreement and compliance certificates, a rating or market yields, management's leverage and cash flow targets and the revenue split between large pharmaceutical and biotechnology clients. Top risk: financial policy that lets net debt rise faster than earnings recover.
