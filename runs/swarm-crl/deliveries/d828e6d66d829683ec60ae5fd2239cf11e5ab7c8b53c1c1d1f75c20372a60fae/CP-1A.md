## Findings

### Bottom line

Charles River is a broad, client-diversified research services group whose 2026 portfolio reshuffle, driven by a board strategic review that followed a cooperation agreement with a large shareholder, makes its capital allocation the main governance question for creditors, although the files show no creditor-adverse conduct.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 51; calculation A1

Sponsor and governance risk is medium on the evidence read: no client is above 4% of revenue and regulatory inquiries closed in 2025, but a $1.0 billion repurchase programme, revolver-funded acquisitions and unquantified litigation sit beside loan documents and a proxy statement that were not supplied.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 83; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 91

### Key findings

The business is diversified by client and geography, and its demand risk is cyclical rather than concentrated. No client accounted for more than 4% of 2025 revenue and none for more than 8% of any segment, the discovery and safety assessment segment generated 59.8% of revenue, research models and services 21.1% and manufacturing 19.1%, and the United States provided 53.34% of revenue. The same segment lost revenue in 2025 as biopharma R&D spending stayed cautious, its backlog slipped to $1.9 billion at the year-end and has since recovered to $2.0 billion, so earnings depend on client funding rather than on any single customer.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 88; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, pages 142–143; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 35; calculation A1; calculation A3; calculation A5

The portfolio is being reshaped, and the reshaping is the largest 2026 change for creditors. In 2026 the company agreed a preliminary $507.3 million for a Cambodian primate supplier, took full control of PathoQuest for total consideration of $67.4 million, sold certain European Discovery Services businesses to IQVIA for $126.4 million and sold its CDMO and Cell Solutions businesses to GI Partners, paying the buyer net cash of $12.4 million and accepting a possible funding obligation of up to $45.0 million. The sales remove the CDMO and Cell Solutions asset groups that caused the fiscal 2025 intangible impairment of about $211.0 million, and the supplier purchase vertically integrates a constrained input, but the acquisitions were funded from cash and the revolver, so the net effect on creditors depends on how the revolver is repaid and on what is bought next.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, pages 13–14; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, pages 36–37; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 78; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 137; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 142

Shareholder influence is documented, but its financial consequence for creditors is not yet visible in a debt-funded distribution. A cooperation agreement with a large shareholder in May 2025 put a board committee in charge of a strategic review, which in November 2025 set priorities of acquisitions, partnerships, disposal of non-core assets equal to about 7% of 2025 revenue, cost efficiency and a balanced use of cash between acquisitions, buybacks and debt repayment. The company has paid no common dividends, repurchased $350.0 million of shares in 2025 and $300.0 million in the first nine months of 2026 with $700.0 million of a $1.0 billion authorisation left and no programme purchases in the third quarter, and the share count fell 2.66% between January and October 2026, so buybacks are the lever to watch.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, pages 51–52; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 78; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 85; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 54; calculation A6

Leadership has turned over in the roles that matter to lenders, and the files give no cause for concern beyond continuity risk. The chief executive and chair succession was set for 2026-05-05 and the quarterly reports from May 2026 are signed by a new chief executive, the board named an interim chief financial officer in October 2025, the 2025 annual report was signed by that interim officer and the 2026 quarterly reports by a permanent one, and nine of eleven directors are independent with fully independent audit, pay and governance committees. The date of the chief financial officer change is not stated in the files read.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 37; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 181; 10-Q_2026-03-28_crl-20260328.htm.jsonl, page 50; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 56

Regulatory risk has narrowed but litigation risk has not closed. The Department of Justice closed its primate import investigations in August 2025 and the SEC enforcement staff told the company on 2025-11-14 that it did not intend to recommend action, after a charge of $27 million in fiscal 2024 that wrote an inventory of imported primates down to zero, yet the securities class action was partly revived on appeal in August 2025, a second amended complaint was filed on 2026-09-15 and the company moved to dismiss on 2026-10-15, with the plaintiff's answer due 2026-11-16. The company cannot estimate a possible loss, so the claim is an unquantified contingent liability.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 83; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 91; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 32

Restructuring is a recurring cost, not a one-off, and it consumes cash while it lifts margins. The company expects about $300 million of cumulative annualised savings by the end of 2026, announced a further programme in September 2026 intended to save more than $300 million between 2027 and 2030, and booked restructuring charges of $99.8 million in fiscal 2025, $107.0 million in fiscal 2024 and $73.8 million in the first nine months of 2026. Repeated charges mean restructuring spend should be treated as part of running costs when judging free cash flow.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 89; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 30; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 36

Supply of primates is a single point of failure that the company is addressing by integration. The annual report lists dependence on a limited source of supply for its primate offerings as an industry risk, and the January 2026 acquisition of a Cambodian supplier brings part of that supply in house, which lowers the interruption risk but adds exposure to the same regulators and to deferred consideration, of which about $5 million was unpaid at the third quarter.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 13; calculation A7

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a medium risk view is the pace and funding of change: the two 2026 acquisitions cost a combined $574.7 million at preliminary prices, a shareholder-led review is still running, the unused repurchase authorisation of $700.0 million is large against cash, and the revolver peaked at the first quarter before divestiture proceeds arrived. If management were to complete a large acquisition or resume buybacks while the revolver is drawn, creditor-adverse risk would be the better reading. Evidence that points the other way is the repayment of the revolver after the sales, no dividends, closed regulatory inquiries and independent board committees.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, pages 13–14; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 27; calculation A8

The view would change with the credit agreement and indentures, which would show what distributions, investments and debt the documents permit, with the terms of the cooperation agreement and the 2026 proxy statement, which would show board seats, standstill terms and holdings, and with a statement of how the 2026 divestiture proceeds were used.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

No new notes or term debt were issued in fiscal 2025 or in the first nine months of 2026: the three series of senior notes are unchanged since 2021 and the revolving facility is the only material borrowing that moved. The revolver was drawn to fund the 2026 acquisitions of a Cambodian primate supplier and of PathoQuest, which together cost a preliminary $507.3 million and $67.4 million, and it has since been reduced from its first-quarter peak.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-27_crl-20251227.htm.jsonl, page 151; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, pages 13–14; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-Q_2026-03-28_crl-20260328.htm.jsonl, page 23; calculation A8

Two businesses were sold in May 2026: certain European Discovery Services businesses for $126.4 million in cash with up to $10.0 million of contingent payments, and the CDMO and Cell Solutions businesses to GI Partners, where the company paid the buyer net cash of $12.4 million, may fund up to $45.0 million of future losses and capital expenditure over four years and may receive up to $50.0 million of contingent payments valued at $15.7 million. The pre-tax loss on the CDMO and Cell Solutions sale was $181.7 million, recognised in other expense and so outside operating income and EBITDA.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, page 37

A March 2026 sale of Wilmington property for $60.1 million, with a long-term lease back of some buildings, produced a gain of about $38.5 million inside operating expenses; the lease back adds an operating lease claim that sits outside the debt figure.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-26_crl-20260926.htm.jsonl, pages 37–38
