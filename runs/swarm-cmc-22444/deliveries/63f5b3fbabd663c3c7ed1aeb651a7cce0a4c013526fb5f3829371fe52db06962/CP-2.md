## Findings

### Bottom line

CMC is an adequate but cyclical credit whose leverage rose sharply because debt paid for two precast acquisitions, so creditors now depend on acquired and legacy earnings holding up through the integration. Net leverage was 2.47x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA at 2026-05-31, the pro forma figure for the dividend declared on 2026-06-24 is not established, run-rate interest cover is 6.72x, and committed liquidity is USD 1,558,789 thousand.
Basis: calculation M1; calculation M3; calculation M5; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The main downside is a fall in tariff-supported steel margins while interest, capital spending and a USD 373,476 thousand litigation accrual stay fixed; on earnings at the fiscal 2025 level before the litigation charge, net leverage would be 3.54x, or 2.71x if precast reaches its expected USD 245 million a year.
Basis: calculation M7; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-05-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 3

### Key findings

- Leverage is the dominant constraint and it is new. Net leverage at 2026-05-31 was 2.47x and total debt 2.96x of reported EBITDA, after USD 2,000 million of senior unsecured notes and cash funded USD 2,516,122 thousand of acquisitions; at the 2025 year end net leverage was 0.71x, or 2.25x pro forma for the acquisition on 2025-12-01 and 4.90x pro forma for the acquisition on 2025-12-15.
Basis: calculation M1; calculation M2; Financial foundation (CP-1)

- Business quality is the strongest support. The company is the largest U.S. rebar manufacturer and fabricator on its own description, runs scrap recycling, mills and fabrication as one chain, and North America Steel Group earned 77.7% of segment adjusted EBITDA; the integrated chain and a fabrication backlog give more visibility than a pure mill, though products are commodity-priced.
Basis: calculation M9; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-08-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 11

- Cash generation is solid but heavily absorbed. Free cash flow of USD 405,038 thousand over the last twelve months is 0.35x of EBITDA after capital spending of USD 513,190 thousand, and about USD 550 million is planned for fiscal 2026 for the West Virginia mill, so deleveraging comes mainly from earnings growth rather than free cash flow.
Basis: calculation M4; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-05-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 48

- Liquidity is adequate near term but not a large surplus. Cash of USD 559,759 thousand and the undrawn USD 1,000 million revolver give USD 1,558,789 thousand committed, which is 1.10x the identified near-term claims of USD 1,413,168 thousand before any operating cash flow, with the revolver running to 2030-12-17.
Basis: calculation M5; calculation M6; 10-Q_2026-05-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, pages 47–48

- Interest cover reads better than the run-rate. Cover on cash interest paid is 13.46x, but the new notes carry an annual coupon of USD 117,500 thousand and interest payable over the next twelve months is USD 170.9 million, so run-rate cover is 6.72x.
Basis: calculation M3; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

- Financial policy is average: the board is independent and a leverage goal below 2x by mid-2027 is stated, but buybacks and a higher dividend continued while debt rose, and restricted payment capacity is not established. The litigation accrual of USD 373,476 thousand is 13.15% of net debt and sits outside debt.
Basis: calculation M12; calculation M13; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against the cautious view is earnings momentum and the issuer's path: adjusted EBITDA margin of 13.04% over the last twelve months against 5.65% in fiscal 2025, precast adjusted EBITDA of USD 86.5 million in nine months, and a stated aim of leverage below 2x. Business & governance reproduced the issuer's 2.1x as 2.12x from its own inputs, which supersedes the Financial foundation statement that it could not be reproduced. That view would win if fiscal 2026 results show reported net leverage moving toward that 2.12x, free cash flow applied to debt and no cash payment of the litigation accrual. The cautious view would hold or strengthen if steel margins narrow after any relaxation of tariffs, if capital spending stays high, or if the accrual is paid from cash.
Basis: calculation M8; calculation M10; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-05-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 37

### Limitations

- Covenant ratios at 2026-05-31 and the terms of the amended credit agreement are not stated, so the liquidity and leverage-tolerance assessments are not tested against covenant headroom; a ratio close to its limit would make liquidity less comfortable than shown.
- No agency ratings or market prices are supplied, so the refinancing capacity judgement rests on the successful issue of USD 2,000 million of notes in November 2025 and the enlarged revolver; weaker market access later would lower it.
- The latest statements end on 2026-05-31, so delivery of the fourth quarter outlook and any year-end leverage are not established; the downside scenario is built from fiscal 2025 results and the issuer's expectation for precast, and moves with both.
- Restricted payment and debt capacity are not established because the credit agreement and indentures are not supplied, so the view that capital returns are modest relative to cash flow cannot be set against what the documents permit.

Strength: scale and integration. CMC reports net sales of USD 7,798,480 thousand and says it is the largest U.S. rebar manufacturer and fabricator, with recycling, mills and fabrication in one chain that secures scrap and keeps its mills loaded; this lowers input and demand risk compared with a stand-alone mill and supports cash generation through the cycle.
Basis: calculation M9; 10-K_2025-08-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-K_2025-08-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 13

Strength: trade protection and demand visibility. A 50% tariff on most steel imports supports domestic prices, and fabrication backlog of USD 845.7 million of remaining obligations with about 70% due within twelve months gives visibility for North America fabrication; this supports margin and cash flow while the tariff stands.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-05-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 18; 10-Q_2026-05-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 37

Strength: liquidity and market access. Cash of USD 559,759 thousand, an undrawn revolver of USD 1,000 million to 2030-12-17 and a USD 2,000 million note issue in November 2025 show lenders and the bond market funded a large acquisition programme; this lowers near-term refinancing risk.
Basis: calculation M5; 10-Q_2026-05-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 22

Strength: earnings recovery and precast contribution. Adjusted EBITDA reached USD 1,154,161 thousand over the last twelve months, a 13.04% margin, and precast added USD 86.5 million in nine months; this lifts debt service capacity if it persists.
Basis: calculation M8; calculation M10; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Weakness: leverage after debt-funded acquisitions. Net leverage of 2.47x at 2026-05-31, against 0.71x at the 2025 year end, shrinks the cushion against a margin squeeze, and goodwill and intangibles are 81.9% of the allocated acquisition price, so little tangible value backs the new debt.
Basis: calculation M1; calculation M13; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

Weakness: interest and capital spending absorb cash. Run-rate interest of USD 170.9 million and capital spending of about USD 550 million this year leave free cash flow at 0.35x of EBITDA and make deleveraging depend on earnings.
Basis: calculation M3; calculation M4; 10-Q_2026-05-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 48; 10-Q_2026-05-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 50

### Business and ownership

CMC is a vertically integrated North American and Polish steel and construction-products group that recycles scrap, melts it in electric arc furnaces, rolls rebar, merchant bar and wire rod, and fabricates reinforcing steel; since December 2025 it also makes precast concrete and pipe products. Net sales were USD 7,798,480 thousand in fiscal 2025, 79.7% of them in the United States, and North America Steel Group supplied 78.0% of external net sales and 77.7% of segment adjusted EBITDA in the first nine months of fiscal 2026, so the credit rests mainly on U.S. rebar economics. The business is cyclical, seasonal and commodity-priced, with net sales higher in the third and fourth quarters, which makes interim leverage swing with the season.
Basis: calculation M9; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-08-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 11

The company is listed with no controlling holder identified, nine of ten directors independent and a separate chairman, so there is no sponsor to extract or support and creditor alignment rests on the board and stated policy. Dividends and buybacks continued after the acquisitions, the financial policy is average, and the proxy statement, credit agreement and indentures are not supplied, so ownership above five percent, restricted payment capacity and guarantor scope are not established.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation M12

### Revenue, margin and cash conversion

Revenue is driven by steel price and tons shipped. In nine months to 2026-05-31 North America steel products metal margin per ton rose 27% and tons shipped fell 3%, so earnings came from price spread rather than volume, which is more reversible; precast adds infrastructure-oriented products whose cyclicality is not yet shown in the sources, and fabrication backlog gives visibility only in North America. Customer concentration is not disclosed, so durability beyond that backlog is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-05-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 44; 10-Q_2026-05-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 18

Margins depend on the spread between selling prices and scrap, plus power and gas, which are partly hedged; the adjusted EBITDA margin was 12.22% in fiscal 2024, 5.65% in fiscal 2025 (10.30% before the litigation charge) and 13.04% over the last twelve months, so margin has moved by several percentage points within two years. Operating leverage is high because mills run at high fixed cost, so a volume or spread decline passes quickly to EBITDA and then to free cash flow.
Basis: calculation M8; 10-Q_2026-05-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 23

Cash conversion is moderate: capital spending is 5.80% of last-twelve-months net sales, free cash flow is 0.35x of EBITDA, and nine months of working capital absorbed USD 195,098 thousand as inventory costs and selling prices rose; capital spending is partly discretionary once the West Virginia mill is finished, which gives some flexibility over the maturity wall that begins with USD 300,000 thousand of notes due in January 2030.
Basis: calculation M4; calculation M11; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Competitive and external forces

Competitive rivalry is high and the main force on margin: the 2025 annual report describes global steelmaking capacity far above demand and import competition, so price takers lose margin first when imports return; buyer power is moderate because fabricated rebar is generally sold through competitive bids, often on fixed-price projects; supplier power is moderate because scrap and energy prices swing, partly offset by company-owned recycling; new entrants are limited by the capital needed for a mill and substitutes for rebar are few. The tariff and the European carbon border mechanism are the policy factors that matter to credit: the tariff supports U.S. margin and its removal is the largest external risk, while the European mechanism improved Polish volumes and carbon cost assistance of USD 36.0 million was received in nine months against USD 48.1 million a year earlier, so Europe Steel Group earnings partly depend on government payments.
Basis: 10-K_2025-08-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-K_2025-08-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-Q_2026-05-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 37; 10-Q_2026-05-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 46

### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale and market position are strong: net sales of USD 7,798,480 thousand and a self-described leading position in U.S. rebar give shock absorption. Competitive advantage is average: integration and cost position help, but products are commodity-priced and margin relies on tariffs. Business diversification is average: North America Steel Group is 75.1% of net sales in nine months and the United States 79.7% of fiscal 2025 sales, with precast and Europe adding limited offset. Cost and capex flexibility is average: capital spending is 5.80% of last-twelve-months net sales and part of it ends when the West Virginia mill is finished, but power-intensive mills carry high fixed cost. Margin stability is weak: adjusted EBITDA margin was 12.22%, 5.65% and 13.04% across fiscal 2024, fiscal 2025 and the last twelve months. Free cash flow stability is average: it was USD 575,437 thousand, USD 312,249 thousand and USD 405,038 thousand in the same periods, positive but variable. Ability to refinance is average: USD 2,000 million of notes were placed in November 2025 and the first large maturity is in January 2030, but ratings are not stated. Liquidity is average: committed liquidity of USD 1,558,789 thousand covers identified near-term claims 1.10x before operating cash flow. Financial policy and governance are average: independent board and a stated leverage goal, offset by continued buybacks and a higher dividend while leverage rose; these are analyst assessments, not ratings.
Basis: calculation M4; calculation M5; calculation M6; calculation M8; calculation M9; calculation M11; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Outlook

Over twelve to twenty-four months the issuer expects the fourth quarter core EBITDA to rise sequentially, a mill outage headwind of about USD 20 million to fall away and precast to add; leverage should fall if precast earns its expected USD 245 million a year, but this is management's view and is not confirmed by any later document. Over three to five years the West Virginia mill and precast platform lift capacity and diversification, while the company plans leverage below 2x by mid-2027, so the medium-term credit trend is improving if delivery follows, with the tariff and construction cycle as the swing factors.
Basis: calculation M10; EX99.1_2026-05-31_cmc_cmc-5312026xearningsreleas-htm.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-05-31_cmc.htm.jsonl, page 37

### Downside scenario

The downside begins with a relaxation of import protection or a construction slowdown that narrows steel prices against scrap, and mills first lose margin per ton; as legacy earnings fall back to the fiscal 2025 level before the litigation charge, EBITDA drops while interest of USD 170.9 million a year, capital spending and the litigation accrual do not. On net debt at 2026-05-31, net leverage would be 3.54x on that earnings level and 2.71x if precast reaches USD 245 million, so free cash flow falls toward zero, buybacks would have to stop and the company would draw first on cash of USD 559,759 thousand and then on the undrawn revolver. Resilience looks sufficient to avoid liquidity distress, because committed liquidity is 1.10x the identified near-term claims before operating cash flow, but covenant headroom is not established, and the observable triggers are metal margin per ton, tariff changes, free cash flow and the pace of buybacks.
Basis: calculation M6; calculation M7; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Overall credit view

Overall, CMC presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, integration and domestic leadership, but credit quality is constrained by leverage that rose to 2.47x after debt-funded acquisitions, run-rate interest cover of 6.72x and a litigation accrual outside debt. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow is positive but absorbed by capital spending, and refinancing capacity is likely to depend on delivery of precast earnings and on the tariff, with the first large maturity of USD 300,000 thousand in January 2030. The primary downside path is a margin squeeze in U.S. steel while interest, capital spending and litigation stay fixed. Further analysis would require covenant headroom at 2026-05-31, the credit agreement and indentures, agency ratings and fiscal 2026 results. Top risk: leverage after debt-funded acquisitions combined with margin cyclicality.
Basis: calculation M1; calculation M3; calculation M7; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)
