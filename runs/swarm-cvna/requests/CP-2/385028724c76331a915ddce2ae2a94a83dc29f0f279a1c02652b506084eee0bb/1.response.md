## Findings
### Bottom line
Fundamental credit quality is adequate and improving: low leverage and cash balances protect creditors, but earnings depend on loan-sale markets and funding lines that renew during 2027, so a funding shock rather than an earnings fall is the main path to deterioration. Total debt of USD 5,228 million is 1.90x last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA and net debt is 0.79x, and two illustrative shocks to loan-sale gains and vehicle profit lift leverage only to 2.58x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvna-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C01; calculation C06

The weakest dimension is diversification, because other sales and revenues, which are all gross profit, were 39.4% of nine-month gross profit and depend on investors buying the loans Carvana originates. Sponsor and governance risk is Medium, and the tax receivable agreement, related-party revenue and the 2028 expiry of the rate hedge are the secondary risks.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvna-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 60; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvna-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 70; calculation C02

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest evidence against treating funding as the weak point is the record of renewal and growth. Ally's commitment to buy loans rose from USD 4.0 billion in January 2025 to USD 6.0 billion in October 2025 and USD 8.0 billion in July 2026, fixed-pool commitments reached USD 12.5 billion, seven receivable facilities are undrawn, USD 13.9 billion of loans were sold in the nine months to 2026-09-30, and the August 2026 refinancing priced a seven-year loan at a lower cost than the notes it replaced. On that record the funding channel has widened as volume grew, and the shock modelled here would need both a market closure and a fall in gains at once.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cvna-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 129; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvna-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 18–19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvna-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 23–26; calculation C09; calculation C17

The assessment of market access would move from average to strong if the facilities due in 2027 were extended beyond 2027 and Ally's commitment were renewed three months ahead of its 2027-07-26 expiry, as the July 2026 amendment was, with gain per dollar sold holding at about 7.4% or better. It would move to weak if any loan-sale commitment were shortened, if the facility due 2027-02-14 were not extended, if gain per dollar fell below about 5.4%, or if gross profit per unit fell by another USD 365 to about USD 6,632, either of which would move the profile toward the modelled stress level.

### Strengths and weaknesses
Scale and growth are the first strength: retail units grew 39.4% in Q3 2026 while the industry was about flat, and Adjusted EBITDA reached USD 2,752 million over the last twelve months, which supports debt service and lets the company spend on capacity from earnings. Low leverage and large cash are the second: debt is 1.90x Adjusted EBITDA and unrestricted cash is USD 3,049 million, so earnings would have to fall far before debt service is threatened. Long-dated first-lien debt is the third: 97.3% of corporate debt falls due in 2031 or later and the Term Loan B ranks pari passu with the 2031 secured notes, so apart from USD 107 million of unsecured notes there is no corporate maturity wall before 2031. Liquidity is the fourth: USD 2,071 million of availability and USD 4,100 million of unused receivable lines against USD 71 million drawn. A supportive record on payouts is the fifth: no dividend and USD 126 million of sponsor equity in 2023.

### Company description
Carvana is an online used-car retailer that sources, reconditions, finances and delivers vehicles through its own network, and it earns profit from three lines. Retail vehicle sales were 75.4% of nine-month revenue but only 49.5% of gross profit, while other sales and revenues, which are loan-sale gains and commissions with no cost of sales, were 39.4% of gross profit. Cash flow therefore depends on retail volume and on investors buying the loans it originates, which makes visibility shorter than the revenue base suggests, and refinancing confidence rests on loan-sale access as much as on vehicle sales. Retail units grew 39.4% in Q3 2026 while the industry was about flat, according to management.

### Ownership and group structure
The Garcia Parties hold about 83% of the voting power through ten-vote Class B shares and own DriveTime, so control and a key counterparty sit with the same group. Carvana Co. owns about 65.1% of the operating group, and the holders of the other units have a tax receivable agreement claim of USD 2,230 million that accelerates on a change of control. Behaviour on record is creditor-supportive, including USD 126 million of equity committed in 2023 and no dividend in FY2025, but the legal capacity to change that is not established, so sponsor and governance risk is Medium.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power
Revenue is volume-driven and discretionary. Retail vehicle sales rose 53.8% in Q3 2026 on 39.4% more units and a higher revenue per unit of USD 28,263 against USD 25,625, a mix of share gains and used-car price appreciation that can reverse. There is no contract revenue: every sale is a one-off transaction with a seven-day return right, and competitors include CarMax, Amazon and DriveTime, which holds a cross-license and could compete. The growth is therefore share gain in a flat industry and not visible recurring revenue, and higher interest rates raise monthly payments and weaken demand.

### Cost structure and margin resilience
Margins are defended by scale, not by contract. The Adjusted EBITDA margin was 10.0% in Q3 2026 against 11.3% a year earlier, so growth of 39.4% in units did not stop it falling by 1.3 percentage points. SG&A was USD 805 million in the quarter, of which advertising was USD 152 million and compensation USD 255 million, both linked to volume, while fixed and variable cost shares are not disclosed. Management attributes USD 365 of lower gross profit per unit to loan pricing given back to customers and lower wholesale profit, so margin holds under volume pressure only if loan-sale spreads hold.

### Competitive forces
Rivalry is intense but fragmented: the ten largest used-car retailers held less than 10% of the market in 2024 and Carvana has about 2% of it, so share gains can continue, while price competition from CarMax, Amazon and franchise dealers limits margin. New entrants face high operating complexity in reconditioning and logistics, though technology changes such as artificial intelligence and autonomous vehicles are cited as risks to the model. Buyer power is high because switching costs are low and returns are allowed for seven days. Supplier power matters most in funding: vehicles come from customers, auctions and fleets, but Ally and the loan buyers decide how much Carvana can sell, and commercial suppliers review their disposition channels through requests for proposals.

### Macro and regulatory factors
Economic factors are a primary credit driver because benchmark rates and used-car prices set loan-sale pricing and affordability: management reports rapidly rising benchmark rates and fuel prices that cost about USD 200 per unit in Q3 2026. Regulators scrutinise advertising, financing and insurance in used-vehicle sales, which could narrow Carvana's differentiation, and the quarterly report says tariffs and the conflict involving Iran did not materially affect Q3 results. Technology and social factors are not quantified in the sources and are not treated as primary drivers.

### Financial profile and credit quality
Scale and market position is strong: retail units grew 39.4% in Q3 2026 and the business has about 2% of the used market, which leaves large room for share gains but shows it is far from dominant. Competitive advantage is average: management says its margins are about twice the industry average, but switching costs are low and rivals can copy the online model. Business diversification is weak: a single market, the United States, one online channel and one product category, with other sales and revenues tied to retail units and to loan buyers. Cost and capex flexibility is average: capex is only 0.7% of revenue and advertising can be cut, but the fixed cost share is not disclosed. Margin stability is average: the Adjusted EBITDA margin was 3.1% in FY2023, 10.1% in FY2024, 11.0% in FY2025 and 10.0% in Q3 2026, a short record that has not met a downturn. Free cash flow stability is average: positive in each period but only 40.4% of Adjusted EBITDA. Ability to refinance and access capital markets is average: USD 1,660 million was refinanced in August 2026 and USD 13.9 billion of loans were sold in nine months, but all eight short-term facilities mature in 2027. ### Downside scenario
The initiating condition is a rise in benchmark rates and wider asset-backed spreads at a time of lower used-car prices, the conditions management already names. The first operational break is the price investors pay for loans: gain per dollar of receivables sold falls further, as it did by about 2.6 percentage points over the past year. The financial transmission is direct because other sales and revenues are all gross profit: two percentage points less gain on the Q3 pace removes USD 412 million a year, and a 10% fall in vehicle gross profit removes USD 312 million more, leaving Adjusted EBITDA at USD 2,028 million, leverage at 2.58x and cash interest cover at 4.33x. Liquidity then depends on whether loan buyers keep buying: if every channel closed, cash plus unused receivable lines would fund about 4.2 months of loan production, and the 2027 facility renewals would fall due in the same period. Management levers are slowing unit growth and inventory purchases, which release working capital, and cutting advertising. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid distress under the earnings shocks alone, and insufficient to absorb a prolonged closure of the loan-sale channel. The observable triggers are gain per dollar sold, the Ally renewal and the 2027 facility extensions.

### Overall credit view
Overall, Carvana presents an adequate business profile supported by scale, growth, low leverage and large cash, but credit quality is constrained by dependence on loan-sale gains, thinning unit profit and concentrated control. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow is positive but converts only 40.4% of Adjusted EBITDA. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the renewal of the 2027 facilities, Ally's commitment and the price investors pay for Carvana's loans. The primary downside path is a loan-sale funding shock that cuts gains and tightens liquidity at the same time. Further analysis would require ratings, loan performance data, the indentures and a maturity ladder after the August 2026 refinancing. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: loan-sale funding stress.
