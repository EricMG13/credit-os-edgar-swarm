## Findings

### Bottom line
Leverage is moderate but earnings-dependent: total debt of USD 5,228 million is 1.96x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA and 1.90x Adjusted EBITDA, an improvement that came from earnings growth, not repayment.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvna-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvna-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 77; calculation C05; calculation C06

### Financial interpretation

Gross leverage on reported EBITDA fell from 4.35x at FY2024 to 2.37x at FY2025 and 1.96x on the last twelve months to 2026-09-30, and the issuer-adjusted measure shows the same path, from 4.09x to 2.29x to 1.90x. Debt did not fall: carrying debt was USD 5,632 million, USD 5,115 million and USD 5,228 million, so the improvement came from Adjusted EBITDA rising from USD 1,378 million to USD 2,237 million and USD 2,752 million on the last twelve months. Because debt edged up after FY2025 and the August 2026 refinancing left gross principal unchanged, creditors' protection depends on EBITDA holding at this level.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_cvna-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 100–101; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cvna-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C01; calculation C05; calculation C06

The strongest contrary evidence is cash: unrestricted cash rose from USD 1,716 million to USD 2,327 million and USD 3,049 million, so net leverage on reported EBITDA is 3.02x, 1.29x and 0.82x. That view wins only while the cash stays on the balance sheet; working capital can reabsorb it, as inventory growth did with USD 1,025 million in the nine months to 2026-09-30.
