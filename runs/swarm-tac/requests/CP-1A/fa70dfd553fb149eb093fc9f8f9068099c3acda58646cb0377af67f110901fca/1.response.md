## Findings

### Bottom line

Governance and ownership risk looks moderate, but the business carries Alberta merchant-price exposure and execution risk on the Centralia conversion and data-centre plans, so creditors rely on contracted and hedged cash flow and on growth that is only partly equity-funded.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 25

Alberta supplies 51.6% of nine-month adjusted EBITDA and 77% of Alberta capacity is exposed to the merchant market, while 53% of total capacity is contracted with a weighted average life of 9 years. Five senior roles changed in 2026, the cash price of the Colorado acquisition was paid with new equity, and the dividend rose 8% with no documented debt-funded distribution.
Basis: calculation C01; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 12; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 25; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-05-06_q12026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-07-31_q22026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 5

### Key findings

The profit pool is diversified by technology but concentrated in Alberta and gas. Gas earned CAD 374 million of the CAD 759 million nine-month adjusted EBITDA and Alberta CAD 392 million, so Alberta spot and ancillary prices drive results even though the fleet spans hydro, wind, solar and gas across Canada, the United States and Western Australia. A hedge position priced at $67 per MWh covers part of fourth-quarter 2026 Alberta production, which dampens but does not remove the exposure.
Basis: calculation C01; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, pages 24–25

Management continuity is orderly but thin on history. The chief executive retired on April 30, 2026 and was succeeded by the chief financial officer, a new chief financial officer and chief commercial officer followed within a week, and two further senior roles changed on June 8, 2026. Four independent directors, all audit committee financial experts, sit on the audit committee, and internal control was assessed as effective at Sept. 30, 2026 excluding the Far North acquisition.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-05-06_q12026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 3; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-07-31_q22026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 5; 40-F_2025-12-31_tac-20251231_d2.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, pages 66–67

Capital allocation has been acquisitive and partly equity-funded, not extractive. The June 2026 offering of about CAD 350 million funded the cash price of the Colorado acquisition, the Far North purchase of CAD 107 million used cash and credit facilities, the 49 per cent Oklahoma wind sale for US$305 million brings in cash, and no shares were repurchased in 2026 against CAD 24 million in the first nine months of 2025. The trade-off is project debt of US$750 million assumed with Colorado and an 8% higher dividend.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-07-31_q22026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 5; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 70; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 77; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 98

No sponsor controls the company on the evidence supplied, but holder percentages are not established. Brookfield holds CAD 750 million of exchangeable securities, made up of CAD 350 million of 7% debentures due 2039 and CAD 400 million of preferred shares; they are exchangeable into the Alberta Hydro Assets at its option, it cannot call for repayment, and it may nominate two directors. A related-party power sale to Brookfield was small and fell in the first quarter of 2026.

Execution and regulatory risk sit in three projects. The Centralia unit has had no generation in 2026 and runs under repeated 90-day federal orders whose costs are excluded from adjusted EBITDA and not yet recovered, the conversion needs about US$600 million of capital with a decision expected in early 2027, and the Alberta data-centre plans depend on approvals and definitive agreements while four thermal units are mothballed or notified for mothballing.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a lower-risk reading is financial discipline: the cash price of the main acquisition was equity-funded, no extraction is documented, Brookfield's capital cannot be called, and the committed credit lines were extended in July 2026. The moderate rating would move higher if the Colorado project debt proves non-recourse and the data-centre agreement becomes a binding long-term contract, and lower if a debt-funded distribution, a further large acquisition without equity, or a failed Centralia approval appeared. Ownership percentages and creditor documents would also change it.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 40; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 98; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-05-06_q12026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 4

### Limitations

- The annual information form is not supplied, so the finding that no sponsor controls the company could change if a large holder or control right exists outside the interim filings.
- No credit agreement or indenture is supplied, so the finding that no creditor-adverse behaviour is documented is not tested against restricted-payment, investment or asset-transfer capacity.
- The Colorado project debt terms are not stated, so whether the acquisition adds recourse debt, and how much it weakens the equity-funded-growth finding, is not established.
- Management and board history beyond the 2026 appointments is not supplied, so the continuity finding rests on appointment dates alone.
