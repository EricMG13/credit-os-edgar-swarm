## Findings

### Bottom line

TransAlta's fundamental credit profile is adequate but weakening: leverage on issuer-adjusted EBITDA rose to 4.19x gross and 3.85x net at the end of Q3 2026, while liquidity of CAD 1,867 million and most principal falling due after 2030 keep near-term refinancing risk low. Pro forma net debt and leverage after the Colorado acquisition and Oklahoma wind sale of October 22, 2026 are not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C06; calculation C07

The primary downside path is a further fall in Alberta merchant earnings: nine-month adjusted EBITDA already fell CAD 98 million, and the Colorado acquisition adds US$750 million of assumed project debt. S&P rates the company BB+ with a negative outlook, and the issuer's own leverage of 4.1x is above its 3.0x to 4.0x target.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C04; calculation C11; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 38; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, pages 40–41; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 98

### Key findings

The business profile is adequate: diversified by technology but dependent on Alberta. The fleet spans hydro, wind, solar and gas in three countries and 53% of capacity is contracted for a weighted average of 9 years, yet Alberta produced 51.6% of nine-month adjusted EBITDA and 77% of Alberta capacity is exposed to the merchant market, so Alberta prices set the earnings that service debt.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C13; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 25

The financial profile is weakening on leverage but stable on liquidity. Last-twelve-months issuer-adjusted EBITDA fell 8.9% to CAD 1,006 million while debt at carrying value fell only 2.8%, so gross leverage rose from 3.93x to 4.19x and net leverage from 3.75x to 3.85x; these figures exclude the October 22, 2026 Colorado acquisition and Oklahoma wind sale, and pro forma net debt after them is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C03; calculation C04; calculation C05

Interest cover is moderate rather than thin. Nine-month issuer-adjusted EBITDA was 3.80x cash interest paid, and free cash flow of CAD 338 million covered the CAD 100 million of common and preferred dividends paid 3.4 times, leaving CAD 238 million after dividends. A twelve-month cover is not available because full-year 2025 cash interest paid is not supplied.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C08; calculation C09; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, pages 70–71

Liquidity and refinancing risk are low in the near term. Liquidity of CAD 1,867 million is more than four times the CAD 392 million of principal due by the end of 2027, no cash is drawn on committed facilities, and the syndicated and bilateral facilities were extended in July 2026. Instrument-level terms and covenant levels are not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C06; calculation C07; calculation C12; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, pages 39–40

Governance and sponsor risk is moderate. The cash price of the Colorado acquisition was paid with new equity, the dividend rose 8% and no extraction is documented, but five senior roles changed in 2026 and ownership percentages and debt documents are not supplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-05-06_q12026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 4; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-07-31_q22026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 5

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence that the profile is better than adequate is cash generation and guidance: the company reaffirmed 2026 adjusted EBITDA of CAD 950 million to CAD 1,050 million, needs only CAD 191 million in the fourth quarter to reach the low end, and paid its dividends from free cash flow with room to spare. If fourth-quarter 2026 adjusted EBITDA exceeds the CAD 247 million of a year earlier, last-twelve-months EBITDA would rise above CAD 1,006 million, which would ease the 4.19x gross leverage if debt were unchanged. The view would weaken if Alberta prices fall further, if the Colorado project debt proves to be recourse, if the leverage ratio stays above the top of the issuer's target, or if S&P acts on its negative outlook.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C10; calculation C14; calculation C15; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, pages 23–24; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 38

### Limitations

- Debt terms are known only by category and maturity year, so the view that refinancing risk is low rests on maturity totals and could change if specific instruments carry calls, security or covenants not shown.
- Covenant levels are not supplied, so the liquidity view cannot be tested against headroom and could weaken if a financial covenant is closer than the compliance statement implies.
- The pro forma effect of the Colorado debt and the Oklahoma sale is not established, so the leverage trend could improve or worsen after October 22, 2026.
- Audited FY2025 statements are not supplied, so the weakening-leverage conclusion compares a twelve-month figure with a year-end headline figure rather than audited annual statements.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strength: liquidity and bank support. Cash of CAD 345 million and undrawn committed capacity of CAD 1,522 million give CAD 1,867 million of liquidity, no cash is drawn on committed facilities, and the July 2026 extensions to 2030 and 2028 remove near-term bank maturity risk, so a price shock would meet a large buffer.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C12; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, pages 39–40

Strength: a diversified, partly contracted fleet. Gas, wind and solar, hydro and marketing earn separately, 53% of capacity is contracted, and hedges are priced at $65 per MWh for 2027, which narrows the range of near-term cash flow.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 12; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 24

Strength: growth funded partly with equity. New equity paid the cash price of the Colorado acquisition and about 74% of principal falls due after 2030, although the Colorado project debt is not in that maturity ladder.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C07

Weakness: leverage above the issuer's target and rising. The issuer measures 4.1x against a 3.0x to 4.0x target, and the 4.19x gross and 3.85x net ratios on issuer-adjusted EBITDA rose in 2026 because EBITDA fell faster than debt, which leaves little room for a further earnings fall before ratings pressure builds.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C11; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, pages 40–41

Weakness: merchant earnings and execution risk. Alberta provides 51.6% of nine-month adjusted EBITDA with 77% of its capacity merchant, Centralia has no generation under repeated federal orders, and four thermal units are mothballed or notified for mothballing while data-centre demand is unproven.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C13; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 5; 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-11-03_q32026newsreleasecombined.htm.jsonl, page 12

Weakness: opaque debt terms. Covenant levels, instrument ranking, guarantors and the recourse of the US$750 million of Colorado project debt are not supplied, so creditor protection cannot be judged beyond the consolidated figures.
Basis: Source readiness (CP-0); Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)
