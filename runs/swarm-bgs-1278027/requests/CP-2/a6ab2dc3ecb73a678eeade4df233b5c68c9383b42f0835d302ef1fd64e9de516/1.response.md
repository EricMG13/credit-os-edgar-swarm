### Bottom line

Creditors should treat B&G Foods as a highly leveraged, thinly covered branded-food credit whose steady base business can service debt to 2028 only if liquidity is not drawn down further and the 2028 maturities are refinanced; refinancing, not operations, decides the outcome.

### Credit-relevant company description

B&G Foods sells branded shelf-stable and frozen food and household products, mostly in the United States, through four segments, and its cash flow rests on long-established brands in categories the issuer itself describes as often flat to modestly declining. The portfolio is being reshaped: four brand sales since May 2025 and one acquisition have lowered reported net sales while the issuer says they raise margin. Revenue visibility is moderate because the products are staples bought through large retailers, but concentration is high, with one customer at 31.0% of net sales and contract terms not disclosed. For creditors the business supports steady, low-growth cash flow that can service debt only while costs and retailer terms stay stable.

### Ownership and group structure

Ownership is dispersed and no sponsor or controlling holder is identified in the supplied filings, although the proxy statement that would show large holders was not supplied. The chief executive retired in August 2026 with no successor named in the supplied text. For creditors this means no sponsor support or sponsor extraction to weigh, a dividend that is a policy choice rather than a covenant-forced one, and an open leadership question.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Net sales fell 6.30% in fiscal 2024 and 5.37% in fiscal 2025, which the issuer states as 5.4% and attributes mainly to lower base business sales, and 2.9% in the first three quarters of 2026, mostly through divestitures. Base business net sales fell 4.0% in fiscal 2025 on volume down 3.5% and price and mix down 0.3%, and rose 1.8% in 2026 on volume up 1.4% and price and mix up 0.3%. Pricing power is limited: the issuer says price increases may lag costs and competition can limit quick increases, retailers push for lower prices and more promotion and favour private label, and part of the Spices & Flavor Solutions growth came from private label and foodservice. Revenue is therefore predictable as staple demand but structurally pressured by channel shifts and concentrated in one buyer, so creditors can lean on the 2026 volume turn only if it persists for several quarters.

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital expenditure is low, $30.6 million in fiscal 2025 and $17.8 million in the first three quarters of 2026, with $25.0 million or less planned for fiscal 2026, and the business is seasonal, buying produce from June to October and maple syrup from April to August. ### Downside scenario

The scenario replays the issuer's own fiscal 2025 experience. The initiating condition is a retailer or category setback that cuts net sales by 5.37%, the fall seen in fiscal 2025, from the twelve-month level of $1,791.549 million. The first operational break is lost volume with costs for oil, fuel and freight still rising, and in fiscal 2025 each dollar of lost net sales coincided with 22.37 cents of lost issuer-adjusted EBITDA. On that ratio net sales fall $96.20 million and issuer-adjusted EBITDA falls $21.52 million to $263.04 million, which takes net debt to 7.52x issuer-adjusted EBITDA from 6.95x and cover on run-rate interest to 1.55x from 1.68x. Free cash flow falls by about the lost earnings before tax while cash interest steps up to its run-rate of $169.74 million, so the seasonal build would rely on $55.1 million of revolver availability. Management levers are the remaining dividend of $30.8 million a year, capital expenditure of $25.0 million or less, inventory release and a sale of the Canadian business. Resilience looks sufficient to avoid a liquidity crisis within twelve months, because no bond matures before September 2028, but the covenant step-downs to 7.25x and 7.00x would be the first break and cannot be tested here, and a covenant breach would put the 2028 refinancing at risk. The observable triggers are base business net sales, Specialty adjusted EBITDA, revolver availability and the compliance statements in each quarterly report.

### Overall credit view

Overall, B&G Foods presents an adequate business profile supported by established brands with stabilising volumes, but credit quality is constrained by high leverage, thin interest cover and a narrow revolver cushion. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on base business growth, the largest segment's margin recovering, liquidity being rebuilt and covenant room being shown before the 2028 maturities become current.
