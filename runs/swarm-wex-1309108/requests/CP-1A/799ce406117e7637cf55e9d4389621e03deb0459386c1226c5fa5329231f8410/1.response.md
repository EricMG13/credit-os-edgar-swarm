## Findings

### Bottom line

WEX is a listed, bank-funded payments group with medium governance risk for creditors: the board has returned large amounts of capital through buybacks, partly funded with new debt, while earnings swing with fuel prices and depend on a regulated bank subsidiary. Buybacks of USD 2,016.1 million from 2023 to September 2026 cut shareholders' equity to USD 1,152.1 million.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 171; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 19; calculation C4

No controlling shareholder or sponsor is named in the supplied filings, and the shareholder register and the terms of the May 2026 cooperation agreement are not established, so the willingness to curb buybacks if earnings fall cannot be tested.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 103; 10-Q_2026-06-30_wex-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 61; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

The business is a three-segment payments platform in which Mobility supplies more than half of revenue and moves with the fuel price. Mobility was 54.46% of revenue in the nine months to September 2026 against 52.09% in FY2025, and the company attributes USD 119.0 million of the USD 161.3 million rise in Mobility revenue to higher fuel prices, which is 73.78% of that growth while gallons processed slipped. Revenue, receivables and the funding they need therefore rise and fall with the fuel price, which is a source of earnings volatility rather than of contracted cash flow.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 40–41; calculation C1; calculation C3

The bank subsidiary is the credit-relevant feature of the structure. WEX Bank funds receivables with deposits, FHLB advances and federal funds and holds the HSA deposits behind the Benefits segment: short-term deposits were USD 6,180.5 million at 2026-09-30, of which USD 4,758.0 million were HSA deposits, and restricted bank net assets were about 38% of consolidated net assets at 2025-12-31. A large part of group assets therefore sits behind regulatory dividend limits, so parent creditors rely on dividends from WEX Bank and on cash outside the bank. The FDIC consent order on compliance management, issued on 2023-09-20, ended on 2026-10-15, which removes a constraint on the bank.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 200; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 20, 58

Capital allocation favours shareholders over deleveraging and has been partly debt-funded. The 2025 tender offer paid USD 750.0 million for shares and was financed mainly by USD 1,000.0 million of new debt, a USD 450.0 million Term B-3 and USD 550.0 million of senior notes; buybacks of USD 801.6 million in FY2025 exceeded adjusted free cash flow of USD 638.0 million, while nine-month 2026 buybacks of USD 261.8 million were covered 1.92x. A new USD 1,000.0 million program announced on 2026-05-14 had USD 740.0 million left at 2026-09-30. Equity fell from USD 1,820.6 million at the end of FY2023 to USD 1,152.1 million, leaving less loss-absorbing capital beneath the debt.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 119, 171, 187–188; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 19, 56, 61; calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C6

Ownership gives creditors little to work with. There is no sponsor, and the one holder the filings describe as having a board nomination right, Warburg Pincus, sold all its shares and had its USD 310.0 million of convertible notes bought back at 119% of par in 2023, a USD 70.1 million loss on extinguishment. Part III information on current holders and the board is incorporated by reference and not supplied. In 2026 an activist nominated four directors and a proxy contest ended in the second quarter, with a cooperation agreement whose terms are not supplied, so shareholder pressure is a live driver of capital return without a visible counterweight in creditor terms.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 64, 188, 211; 10-Q_2026-06-30_wex-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 46, 61

Reporting is good enough to monitor but not to establish creditor rights. Both annual reports carry audit opinions that the statements present fairly, controls over disclosure were effective at 2025-12-31, and the quarterly report states covenant compliance with a leverage ratio of 2.7x against a 4.75x maximum. Covenant definitions, guarantors and collateral are only summarised, so lender control, the capacity for restricted payments and the springing maturity rules cannot be confirmed from the filings.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 137, 212; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 55–56

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a medium risk reading is that no dividend has been declared since 2005, covenant leverage was 2.7x against a 4.75x maximum, the bank's regulatory order has ended, and the supplied filings show no covenant waiver. Nine-month 2026 buybacks were covered 1.92x by adjusted free cash flow, against 1.72x in FY2023, and no supplied filing shows an amendment that tightened creditor terms. Event risk from a leveraged takeover is also limited, because acquiring 25% of the voting stock confers regulatory control of the bank and needs prior approval, and the senior notes can be put at 101% on a change of control. The view would improve if the executed Credit Agreement shows restricted-payment limits that bind before corporate leverage rises, or if buybacks stay within adjusted free cash flow. It would worsen if a further tender offer is financed with new debt as in 2025, when buybacks covered only 0.80x of adjusted free cash flow, or if the cooperation agreement commits the company to larger returns.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 96, 119, 199; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 55, 56, 58, 59; calculation C6

### Limitations

- The shareholder register, board composition and the terms of the May 2026 cooperation agreement are not supplied, so the medium governance risk level rests on company policy and behaviour rather than on holder concentration or board control, and it could move either way once the proxy statement and the agreement are read.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 103; 10-Q_2026-06-30_wex-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 61
- The executed Credit Agreement and Indenture are not supplied, so the restricted-payment capacity quoted from the annual report cannot be tested against the definitions, and the separation of capacity from willingness to return capital stays open.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 199; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 55–56
- WEX states no sponsor, fund vintage or related-party fees, and the only related party the filings describe has exited, so no sponsor behaviour conclusion is drawn and the risk level rests on listed-company behaviour alone.
  Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 211

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

WEX issued new debt in March 2025 to fund shareholder returns, not an acquisition: the Seventh Amendment on 2025-03-06 added a USD 450.0 million Term B-3 due 2032-03-06 and the company placed USD 550.0 million of senior unsecured notes at 6.5% due 2033-03-15, with proceeds used primarily to fund the USD 750.0 million tender offer that closed on 2025-03-31 and to repay revolver borrowings. Earlier amendments in 2024 repriced the term loans, raised revolver commitments to USD 1.6 billion and extended maturities, and in 2026 no new debt issue appears in the supplied filings. The one operating transaction is the bp co-branded fleet card program, an asset acquisition for which USD 58.6 million was advanced in 2025 and USD 23.0 million for receivables was paid in the second quarter of 2026. Creditors should read the 2025 financing as raising corporate debt by USD 1,000.0 million for a distribution, partly offset by longer maturities.
