## Findings

### Bottom line

WEX has an adequate but leverage-tolerant credit profile: three fee-earning segments and a committed revolver support the debt, while buyback-driven borrowing, fuel-price swings and dependence on a regulated bank leave creditors with stable operations but little deleveraging. Consolidated debt is 4.85x calculated EBITDA for the last twelve months and 3.20x on corporate debt alone, against a reported covenant ratio of 2.7x and a 4.75x maximum.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 55–56; calculation C1

The view is bounded by two gaps: WEX states no EBITDA, so the ratios rest on a calculated figure, and the executed debt documents are not supplied, so covenant headroom cannot be tested.
Basis: Source readiness (CP-0); Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Key findings

Earnings are large and fee-based but move with the fuel price. Calculated EBITDA was USD 995.0 million in FY2025 on revenue of USD 2,660.8 million, a 37.39% margin, and USD 1,097.8 million over the last twelve months to 2026-09-30. Mobility is 54.46% of nine-month 2026 revenue, and USD 119.0 million of its USD 161.3 million revenue growth came from higher fuel prices, so recent growth is price rather than volume and could reverse.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 40–41; calculation C2

Leverage is high on the consolidated measure and moderate on the corporate cut, and the consolidated figure has risen since FY2023. Total debt to EBITDA was 4.19x at the end of FY2023, 4.88x at the end of FY2025 and 4.85x at 2026-09-30, with net debt to EBITDA of 4.31x; excluding the bank and securitization funding of receivables, corporate debt was 3.20x. Creditors of the credit agreement and notes should anchor on the corporate cut while recognising that EBITDA still includes bank earnings.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1

Debt service cover is the thinnest metric and has fallen. EBITDA covered cash interest paid 2.98x in FY2025 against 3.72x in FY2023, and covered accrued financing interest 4.14x in FY2025; the cash-paid measure is conservative because it appears to include interest on bank funding that EBITDA already deducts. Interim cash interest is not printed, so no cover is stated at 2026-09-30.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1

Liquidity is adequate on paper but rests on counterparties. The revolver had USD 1,160.5 million undrawn at 2026-09-30 and cash was USD 593.2 million, of which USD 463.0 million sat in WEX Bank, whose FHLB capacity was only USD 14.3 million and whose federal funds lines are uncommitted. The revolver is available only while the covenants are met, so a covenant breach would remove the main committed buffer.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21–23, 53

Refinancing is a 2028 event, inside 500 days of the cutoff. The Term B-2 Loans of USD 1,364.0 million fall due on 2028-04-01, 500 days from the cutoff, and the Term A-1 Loans and revolver fall due on 2028-01-01, 409 days away, if the early-maturity rule in the credit agreement note applies, or on 2029-05-10 on the annual report's schedule. WEX placed new notes and term loans in March 2025, so market access is demonstrated, but the maturity outcome depends on leverage then.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 190; calculation C3

Financial policy is the main constraint. Buybacks of USD 2,016.1 million since 2023 include USD 801.6 million in FY2025, which was 0.80x of adjusted free cash flow of USD 638.0 million and was funded in part by new debt, while nine-month 2026 buybacks were covered 1.92x. USD 740.0 million of authority remains, and equity of USD 1,152.1 million was 7.11% of total assets. Financial foundation shows corporate debt rose by USD 452.5 million over FY2025; Business & governance describes the March 2025 debt as raising corporate debt by USD 1,000.0 million, which is the gross issue, and the net rise was smaller because the same proceeds repaid revolver borrowings.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 119, 171, 187–188; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 19, 56, 61; calculation C4; calculation C5

The bank is both the funding advantage and the structural risk. Short-term deposits were USD 6,180.5 million, restricted bank net assets were about 38% of consolidated net assets, and WEX Bank's total capital ratio of 13.01% sat above the 10.00% well-capitalized level. Parent creditors depend on dividends from the bank and on cash outside it, and any loss of deposit or FHLB funding would first hit receivable growth and then the revolver.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 20, 37; 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 200; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against an adequate but leverage-tolerant view is the covenant ratio the company reports: 2.7x against a 4.75x maximum, with adjusted free cash flow of USD 502.8 million in nine months 2026 covering buybacks 1.92x, corporate net debt of 3.10x EBITDA and a bank that is well capitalized with its regulatory order ended. If the executed credit agreement defines covenant EBITDA and permitted debt in a way that leaves wide headroom and binds restricted payments, the profile deserves a stronger view. It would weaken if a second tender offer were financed with debt as in 2025, if fuel prices fell and removed the roughly 3 percentage points of Mobility margin attributed to them, or if cash interest cover fell below its FY2025 level.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 19, 42, 55–56; calculation C1; calculation C6

### Company description and credit linkage

WEX is a payments group whose cash flows are transaction-driven, not contracted, so debt service rests on volume, price and credit performance. Mobility processes fleet fuel and related payments, Benefits administers health savings accounts and invests the deposits, and Corporate Payments earns interchange and network incentives on virtual card payments; the group funds the receivables it creates through WEX Bank. That model gives visibility through many small customers with none above 10 percent of revenue, but it ties revenue and working capital to fuel prices and to funding markets.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 11–24, 208; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 34, 40–45; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Ownership and group structure

WEX is a listed company with no sponsor, 12 holders of record and no controlling holder named in the supplied text, and with a wholly owned Utah industrial bank that holds the deposits. Debt sits at three levels: the credit agreement and notes at the parent, securitization vehicles with no recourse to the parent, and bank-level funding. Parent creditors face structural subordination to bank depositors for the bank's assets, and about 38% of consolidated net assets are restricted by bank dividend rules.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 103, 200; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 22; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is predictable in structure but cyclical in amount. Payment processing revenue follows dollars of fuel purchased and the net processing rate fell to 1.20% in nine months 2026 from 1.32% as fuel prices rose, because part of the fee is fixed; late fees and factoring add finance fee revenue that grows with receivables. Benefits revenue is more recurring, with SaaS accounts and custodial investment income, and Corporate Payments depends on network incentive pricing agreed in a 2025 contract amendment. A fall in fuel prices would cut Mobility revenue and receivables together.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 40–45; 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 21

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Costs are partly variable with volume and partly fixed, which is why margin moves with the fuel price. Mobility segment adjusted operating margin was 42.52% in nine months 2026 against 39.60% a year earlier and the company attributes about 3 percentage points to fuel prices, while provision for credit losses and operating interest rise with receivables. Stock-based compensation also rose because performance awards track the fuel-driven results, so part of the margin gain is paid out to employees.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 42–43; calculation C2

### Capital intensity and cash flow conversion

Capital intensity is low and conversion from EBITDA to adjusted free cash flow is high on the company's own definition. Purchases of property, equipment and software were USD 140.6 million in FY2025, and adjusted free cash flow was USD 638.0 million against calculated EBITDA of USD 995.0 million, although adjusted free cash flow includes net funding activity from deposits and borrowings and is not cash after debt service. Reported operating cash flow is not a guide, because rising receivables funded by deposits and borrowings drove nine-month 2026 operating cash flow to negative USD 419.6 million.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 119, 141; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 56–59

### Porter's Five Forces

Rivalry and buyer power limit pricing while the bank funding advantage and network coverage defend it. Competitors named in fleet payments include Corpay, U.S. Bank Voyager, Radius, DKV and Edenred, and WEX's closed-loop network covers more than 90 percent of U.S. fuel sites; fuel retailers and partners hold bargaining power over interchange and program terms, and the company's cost of funds through WEX Bank is its stated edge. For creditors the force that matters most is partner programs such as bp, whose economic terms are not disclosed.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 19; 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17

### PEST analysis

Regulatory and economic factors are credit drivers here. Bank regulation matters because the industrial bank exemption, FDIC supervision and dividend limits govern funding and cash movement, and a loss of the exemption could force a divestiture or create a credit agreement default; the economic driver is the fuel price. Social and technological factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver on the filings.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 28–29, 70, 200

### SWOT

Strengths: scale in fleet payments, a captive bank and fee diversification across three segments. Weaknesses: leverage tolerance, fuel-price dependence and reliance on short-term bank funding. Opportunities that could improve credit quality: sustained buybacks within free cash flow, extension of the 2028 maturities and a covenant definition with wide headroom. Threats: a fall in fuel prices, loss of deposit or FHLB funding, regulatory change to the industrial bank exemption and a further debt-funded distribution.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 70

### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale and diversification are average to strong because three segments each earn fees and none exceeds 55% of nine-month revenue, margin stability is average because Mobility margin includes a fuel price benefit, free cash flow stability is average because adjusted free cash flow includes bank funding movements, liquidity is average because it rests on a conditional revolver and uncommitted bank lines, access to capital markets is average to strong on the 2025 issuance of notes and term loans, and financial policy is weak to average because buybacks were partly debt-funded. The trend is stable earnings with rising leverage, and the key missing datapoints are covenant definitions, a market view and interim cash interest.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over twelve to twenty-four months the tailwinds are higher fuel prices raising Mobility revenue, growth in average HSA custodial cash assets of 11.39% year on year and the end of the FDIC order, and the headwinds are the reversal risk on fuel prices, continued buybacks and the approach of the 2028 maturities. Over three to five years the question is whether WEX holds corporate leverage near the level of FY2023 to FY2024 while returning capital, and the covenant ratio and refinancing terms in 2027 will show it.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 40–44, 58; 10-K_2025-12-31_wex-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 190; calculation C7

### Qualitative downside scenario

A fuel price decline is the initiating condition. It cuts Mobility payment processing and late fee revenue first and removes the fuel price benefit of about 3 percentage points to Mobility segment adjusted operating margin, then lowers EBITDA while cash interest stays fixed; leverage rises and cover falls from 2.98x. Receivables shrink and release working capital, which softens the cash effect, but a smaller receivables book also reduces deposit and FHLB funding need and bank earnings on the portfolio. Management can slow buybacks, which are discretionary, and has USD 1,160.5 million of revolver capacity, so resilience looks sufficient to avoid distress unless the covenant fails; the observable triggers are the average fuel price, the reported leverage ratio and renewals of the bank funding lines.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_wex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21–23, 42, 55–57; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Overall credit view

Overall, WEX presents an adequate business profile supported by fee-based scale, a funding bank and a committed revolver, but credit quality is constrained by debt-funded shareholder returns, fuel-price dependence and reliance on a regulated subsidiary. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because adjusted free cash flow exceeded buybacks in nine months 2026. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on corporate leverage when the 2028 loans come due. The primary downside path is a fuel price fall that cuts EBITDA while buybacks and bank funding costs continue. Further analysis would require the executed debt documents, covenant EBITDA, a market view and interim cash interest. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: debt-funded shareholder returns.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)

## Gaps

- WEX prints no EBITDA, and the covenant Consolidated EBITDA and consolidated interest charges are defined only in the executed Credit Agreement, which is not supplied, so every leverage and cover figure uses calculated EBITDA and covenant headroom cannot be tested. Severity High.
- No credit rating, market quote, valuation or peer input is supplied, so the comparison with typical leveraged issuers in the sector is a judgement from the filings and no relative-value or rating view is offered. Severity Medium.
- The shareholder register, board composition and the terms of the 2026 cooperation agreement are not supplied, so the strength of shareholder influence over capital return is not established. Severity Medium.
- No maturity table is printed for 2026-09-30 and no interim cash interest or taxes paid are printed, so the maturity ladder is at year-end 2025 and the cover at 2026-09-30 is not stated. Severity Medium.
- The funding source of 2026 buybacks, the size of seasonal swings and the split of capital spending between maintenance and growth are not stated. Severity Low.
- Bank-only statements are not supplied, so the split between corporate and bank-funded leverage is an analyst split of debt lines with EBITDA that still includes bank earnings. Severity Medium.
