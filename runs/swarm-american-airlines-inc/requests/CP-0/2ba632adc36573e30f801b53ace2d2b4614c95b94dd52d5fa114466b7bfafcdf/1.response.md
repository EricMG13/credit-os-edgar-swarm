## Findings

### Bottom line

The seven supplied documents are enough to start a credit snapshot of American Airlines, Inc. on its own accounts, because every annual and quarterly report prints the subsidiary's statements apart from its parent's, so creditors can be judged at the borrower's own perimeter. The parent's group figures and all three earnings presentations describe American Airlines Group Inc. only, so they serve as context and never as the issuer's own numbers.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-03-31_aal-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-Q_2026-06-30_aal-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26; EX-99.2_2026-10-22_earningspresentation.htm.jsonl, page 1

What the documents cannot supply is a rating anchor, executed debt documents, market prices, a peer set and director and ownership detail, which sits in a proxy statement that was not supplied. Covenant, recovery and relative-value conclusions therefore cannot be drawn from these documents, and the credit view must rest on the filings' own summaries of debt terms. The latest document is dated 2026-10-22, inside the 2026-11-05 cutoff.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 264; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 71–73; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 73
