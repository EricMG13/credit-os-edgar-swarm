## Findings

### Bottom line

American is a scaled network airline whose creditors face fuel, labour and parent-funding risk rather than sponsor risk, because it is wholly owned by a widely held parent that paid no dividends and bought back no shares in 2025 or 2024. The main governance exposure is that American has lent USD 12,512 million net to that parent, with repayment terms not established.
Basis: calculation K06; calculation K07; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 102; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 103; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40

The 2026 refinancings pushed the main revolving facilities out to March 2031 and the 2014 term loan to May 2033, but they also placed junior debt on aircraft that were already financed. Director, executive and ownership detail and the executed debt documents are not in the sources, so legal capacity to pay dividends or move assets is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 264; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

- The franchise is a hub network carrier whose cyclical passenger core is cushioned by loyalty income. Passenger travel is 83.49% of FY2025 revenue and loyalty and co-brand card revenue 13.82%, and cash from card and other partners was USD 6.2 billion in 2025, so a stream that does not move with fares or fuel sits beside revenue that does.
Basis: calculation K01; calculation K03; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 211
- The most defensive revenue stream is also the most encumbered. The AAdvantage notes and term loans of USD 5,985 million are secured by a first-priority interest in the program agreements and payments and are guaranteed by the parent and special-purpose subsidiaries, so loyalty cash is first available to those lenders and only then to other creditors.
Basis: calculation K04; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 227
- There is no sponsor or controlling shareholder in the sources, so extraction risk rests on financial policy, which has been conservative on payouts. The parent paid no cash dividends and made no share repurchases in 2025 or 2024, foreign ownership is capped at 24.9% of votes and 49.0% of equity, and a tax benefit plan penalises any holder of 4.9% or more; the parent is widely held rather than sponsor owned.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 102; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 103; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 104
- The parent relationship is the principal governance exposure. American's net related-party receivable of USD 10,430 million is larger than its stockholder's equity of USD 8,895 million, and the annual report ties its growth to American providing cash for the parent's financing, including the 2025 settlement of the parent's convertible notes; repayment terms, security and interest are not established in the sources.
Basis: calculation K06; calculation K07; calculation K08; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 257
- The 2026 financing extended maturities and lowered margin but added encumbrance. The main revolving facilities now mature in March 2031, the 2014 term loan was refinanced to May 2033, and the margin on the 2025 AAdvantage term loan fell by 0.50 percentage points; against that, USD 870 million was borrowed junior to existing equipment notes on aircraft already financed, and new aircraft and engine certificates added further secured debt.
Basis: calculation K14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 35
- Fuel and labour dominate the cost base and neither is hedged or fixed. Fuel expense rose to USD 12,444 million in the first nine months of 2026 from USD 8,017 million with no fuel hedges, the group estimates USD 45 million of annual fuel cost for each one cent per gallon, and most employees are unionised under agreements several of which become amendable in 2027.
Basis: calculation K09; calculation K10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 45; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 69; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 253

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a moderate governance view is the size of the parent exposure: the net receivable from related parties exceeds American's own equity, and the parent's consolidated accounts show a stockholders' deficit of USD 4,224 million, so American's positive equity depends on a claim on a group that does not itself carry book equity. A second challenge is litigation, where the Northeast Alliance antitrust class action is at the certification stage and the American Eagle accident actions were still being filed when the annual report was written, both without an estimate of amount in the sources; the issuer reports industry-standard insurance for the accident.
Basis: calculation K07; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 42; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 251; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 113

What would change the view is evidence on the receivable: repayment terms, security, subordination or any restriction on upstream cash in the debt documents would move the assessment toward lower risk, while a deferral, a write-down or a further large advance would move it toward higher risk. An adverse class certification ruling, a dividend or buyback restart before leverage falls, or a sponsor or large holder emerging in the proxy statement would also change it.

### Limitations

- Director, executive, compensation and ownership detail is incorporated from a proxy statement that was not supplied, so the conclusion that there is no controlling shareholder rests on the absence of a disclosed holder and would change if a large holder were disclosed.
- No executed debt documents were supplied, so the view on parent support and extraction rests on behaviour and not on legal capacity, and a restricted-payments or intercompany-transfer limit in those documents could change it.
- The business description in the annual report covers American and its parent together, so network, passenger and regional fleet figures describe the combined operation and may overstate American's own scale in the business conclusion.
- Capacity, unit revenue and unit cost are printed for the group only, so the conclusion on fuel and cost sensitivity uses group measures as a proxy for American.
- Passenger revenue by region is incomplete for the nine months of 2026 in the text supplied, so the revenue mix conclusion uses FY2025 and the third quarter of 2026 only.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

No acquisition, sponsor buyout, recapitalisation or change of control involving American is stated in the sources. The transactions in the period are financings: amendments and extensions of existing facilities, refinancings, and new secured borrowings against aircraft and engines, set against prepayments of older debt.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 257; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; Source readiness (CP-0)

In February 2026 the AAdvantage issuers amended the term loan agreement and cut the margin on the 2025 AAdvantage term loan. In March 2026 American extended its three main revolving facilities to March 2031, raising aggregate commitments from USD 3.0 billion to USD 3.1 billion, and extended a smaller USD 350 million revolver to March 2028. In May 2026 it refinanced the 2014 term loan of about USD 1.1 billion, moved its maturity from January 2027 to May 2033, added USD 703 million and priced the loans at 3.00% over SOFR, with annual repayment of 1.00% from May 2027.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 257; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 46

New secured borrowing in the first nine months of 2026 came mainly from aircraft and engine financings, which the group reports at about USD 4.1 billion of proceeds. They were a USD 870 million junior borrowing in March on aircraft already financed, aircraft certificates of about USD 1.1 billion in May, engine certificates of USD 567 million in June that refinanced USD 228 million of maturing 2019 certificates, aircraft certificates of about USD 1.3 billion in August of which about USD 633 million was held in escrow at the quarter end and is not reported as debt, and USD 705 million of equipment loans at variable rates averaging 5.87%.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 46

Against these, American repaid USD 629 million of a senior short-term term loan, prepaid USD 1.0 billion of the 8.50% senior secured notes and prepaid USD 310 million of equipment notes. The creditor consequence is mixed: maturities are longer and margins lower, but more aircraft and engines are pledged and some new debt ranks behind existing equipment notes, which reduces the unencumbered asset base available to later or unsecured creditors.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 46; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 34
