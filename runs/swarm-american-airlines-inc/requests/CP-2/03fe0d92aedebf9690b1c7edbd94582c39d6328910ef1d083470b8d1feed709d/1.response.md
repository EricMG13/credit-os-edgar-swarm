## Findings

### Bottom line

American's credit rests on liquidity and long-dated secured funding rather than earnings, because fuel has cut operating margin to 1.14% and lifted net leverage to 6.04x, leaving creditors exposed to a further fuel shock and to the parent's ability to repay USD 10,430 million.
Basis: calculation N06; calculation N01; calculation N13; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

Liquidity of USD 11,763 million with nothing drawn on the revolving facilities, extended maturities and no shareholder payouts give time, but USD 7,861 million of principal falls due in 2028 and trailing free cash flow is a deficit of USD 132 million, so deleveraging depends on fuel prices easing before the 2028 maturities.
Basis: calculation N08; calculation N10; calculation N11; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 65

### Key findings

- Earnings, not the debt balance, are the weak link. Net debt fell by USD 1,820 million between the 2025 year-end and 2026-09-30, but trailing EBITDA fell by USD 479 million, so net leverage rose to 6.04x from 5.73x and cover of interest paid fell to 2.06x from 2.19x. The cause is fuel, which rose by USD 4,427 million in nine months against revenue growth of 15.63%, so operating margin fell to 1.14% from 2.56%.
Basis: calculation N01; calculation N02; calculation N03; calculation N04; calculation N05; calculation N06; Financial foundation (CP-1)
- Liquidity is the main support and it is long-dated. USD 11,763 million is available, including USD 3,510 million undrawn, and the three main revolving facilities now run to March 2031; that is 1.03x the USD 11,411 million of principal due in 2027 and 2028, although the same cash also funds operations and the customer-funded float.
Basis: calculation N08; calculation N09; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 32
- Refinancing is manageable but concentrated in 2028. USD 7,861 million, or 30.74% of principal, falls due in 2028, and all of American's principal is secured, so refinancing depends on continued access to the aircraft certificate market that American used repeatedly in 2026.

- Cash conversion is weak. Operating cash flow of USD 4,141 million over the trailing twelve months was less than capital expenditure of USD 4,273 million, a free cash flow deficit of USD 132 million; the USD 1,780 million surplus for nine months reflects seasonal liabilities and the absence of a 2025 outflow to the parent, and aircraft commitments continue.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against this cautious view is that the earnings squeeze is a fuel shock on a franchise that is growing: revenue rose 15.63% in nine months, management reports premium revenue up 22.9% and loyalty enrolments up 27.4% at group level, and it guides to fourth-quarter group revenue growth of 19.5% to 22.5% on a fuel price of about USD 4.70 per gallon. Liquidity is higher than at either earlier year-end, the loss is concentrated in one cost line, and federal tax losses of about USD 11.7 billion shield cash taxes, so a retreat in fuel prices would restore margin quickly.
Basis: EX-99.2_2026-10-22_earningspresentation.htm.jsonl, page 5; EX-99.2_2026-10-22_earningspresentation.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

What would change the view upward is trailing EBITDA returning above the FY2025 level of USD 3,395 million with net leverage back below 5.73x, evidence that the net receivable from the parent is secured or repayable on set terms, and the 2028 maturities being refinanced early. What would change it downward is fuel staying near the outlook price into 2027 while growth slows as management expects, a card processor holdback, a restart of dividends or buybacks before leverage falls, or a further advance to the parent; each ten cents per gallon adds about USD 450 million to group annual fuel cost.
Basis: calculation N15; calculation N01; EX-99.2_2026-10-22_earningspresentation.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 253; Financial foundation (CP-1)

### Limitations

- Reported EBITDA is derived from statement lines and omits depreciation booked in other lines, so the leverage and cover conclusions overstate leverage and understate cover, by an amount that the sources allow to be sized only for FY2025, where total leverage would be about 0.59x lower.
Basis: calculation N18; Financial foundation (CP-1)
- No credit ratings, market prices or peer figures were supplied, so the conclusion that credit quality is constrained is not placed against the sector or the market and could move either way once those are known.
- No executed debt documents were supplied, so the liquidity and refinancing conclusions rest on the issuer's summaries and no covenant headroom, ranking or legal capacity is assessed; a tighter covenant than summarised would weaken the liquidity conclusion.
- The 2026 interim cash flow statements are year-to-date and omit working capital lines, so the cash conversion conclusion for 2026 is qualitative about what drove operating cash flow.
- Nothing after 2026-10-22 is in the sources, so the fourth-quarter fuel and demand outcome is unknown and the downside path is a scenario built on management's stated assumptions, not a forecast.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. The first is liquidity: USD 11,763 million is available with no revolver drawing and facility maturities extended to 2031, which gives time to absorb a fuel or demand shock. The second is loyalty and co-brand income at 13.82% of FY2025 revenue, a stream less tied to fares than ticket sales, although it is pledged to AAdvantage lenders. The third is policy: no dividends or buybacks in 2025 or 2024 and no sponsor. The fourth is maturity management in 2026, including the 2014 term loan moved to May 2033, which removed a January 2027 maturity. The fifth is scale: a mainline fleet of which 54.00% is owned and a hub network that management says is carrying premium and corporate growth.
Basis: calculation N08; calculation N16; calculation N17; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 100

Weaknesses. The first is leverage of 6.04x net on derived EBITDA with cover of 2.06x, which leaves little room for a further earnings decline. The second is unhedged fuel, which took operating margin to 1.14%. The third is the receivable from the parent of USD 10,430 million, above American's equity. The fourth is encumbrance: all principal is secured and USD 870 million was added junior on financed aircraft. The fifth is cash conversion, with a trailing free cash flow deficit of USD 132 million while fleet commitments continue.
Basis: calculation N01; calculation N02; calculation N06; calculation N13; calculation N19; calculation N11; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Business, ownership and revenue drivers

American is a major network carrier that sells scheduled passenger and cargo transportation through nine hubs, buys regional capacity from wholly-owned and third-party carriers at fixed fees, and earns a loyalty and co-brand income stream alongside ticket revenue. The credit link is mixed: passenger travel is 83.49% of FY2025 revenue and is discretionary and cyclical, so cash-flow visibility comes mainly from the 13.82% loyalty share and from advance ticket sales, and refinancing confidence rests on a secured funding market that American used repeatedly in 2026.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 211

Ownership is simple: American is wholly owned by a widely held parent with no sponsor, foreign ownership caps and a tax plan that deters concentrated holders. The credit implications are low extraction risk and no sponsor support to count on; the exposure runs the other way, through the net receivable from the parent, and legal capacity to move cash cannot be read without the executed debt documents.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 102; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 104; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 40

Revenue is driven by volume and fare on a domestic base of 70.91% of FY2025 passenger revenue, with growth in Atlantic and Pacific flying and in premium products. Pricing power is limited because fares are set against competing network, low-cost and ultra-low-cost carriers that match price cuts quickly, which means higher fuel cost cannot be assumed to pass through to fares. The loyalty stream is the least cyclical part of revenue, and the 10-year Mastercard agreement and the Citibank exclusivity from 2026 add contractual visibility.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 211

### Cost structure, margin and cash conversion

Costs are largely fixed or outside management control in the short run. Fuel was 26.49% of revenue in nine months of 2026 against 19.73% a year earlier, salaries and benefits are the largest line with 87% of employees under collective agreements, and regional capacity is bought at predetermined fees with fuel and airport costs reimbursed. Operating leverage is therefore high: margin fell to 1.14% from 2.56% on a 15.63% revenue rise, and it cannot be defended against a fuel shock without capacity or fare action.
Basis: calculation N06; calculation N04; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 253

Capital intensity is high and only partly flexible. Capital expenditure and aircraft purchase deposits were USD 2,660 million in nine months and definitive orders extend beyond 2029, but management reduced its 2026 delivery plan from 55 to 49 aircraft and capital expenditure to about USD 4 billion at group level, which shows some flexibility over the maturity wall. Free cash flow conversion is poor on a trailing basis, and the seasonal float and federal tax losses of about USD 11.7 billion flatter cash flow, because ticket and mileage cash reverses and the tax shield is not permanent.
Basis: calculation N11; calculation N14; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 64; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; EX-99.2_2026-04-23_earningspresentation.htm.jsonl, page 6

### Competitive and macro forces

Rivalry is intense and sets pricing, because carriers match fare cuts quickly and ultra-low-cost carriers press fares in many markets, which limits margin recovery when costs rise. The threat of new entrants is moderated by hub, slot and gate constraints that the annual report describes and by aircraft supply, and substitutes matter mainly on short segments where ground and rail compete. Buyer power sits with corporate accounts and online travel agents that control distribution and its cost, and supplier power is high because a limited number of manufacturers supply aircraft and engines, delaying deliveries or raising fleet cost, while unions and fuel suppliers set the two largest costs.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 19; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 65

Macro and policy factors are material here, unlike in many sectors. Conflict in the Middle East and Ukraine has contributed to oil price volatility, which feeds straight into fuel cost, and a prolonged government shutdown, regulation of routes and slots, and antitrust litigation over the Northeast Alliance can each change earnings or cash. These matter to credit because fuel is the main cause of the margin squeeze and because litigation cash calls compete with debt service.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 43; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 42

In credit terms the strengths are liquidity, loyalty income, payout policy, extended maturities and an owned fleet; the weaknesses are leverage, unhedged fuel, the parent receivable, pledged assets and weak cash conversion. The opportunities are fuel prices easing, premium and loyalty growth that management reports, and fleet renewal that lowers unit cost; the threats are a sustained fuel shock, labour contract resets in 2027, litigation and a stressed parent.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.2_2026-10-22_earningspresentation.htm.jsonl, page 5

### Financial profile and outlook

The financial profile reads as follows, dimension by dimension. Scale and market position are strong, with a mainline fleet of more than 1,000 aircraft and revenue of USD 54,626 million in FY2025. Competitive advantage and business diversification are average, because hubs and loyalty income are offset by fare competition and a domestic base. Cost and capex flexibility, margin stability and free cash flow stability are weak, for the reasons above. Ability to refinance is average: secured access is proven but the issuer carries a non-investment grade rating, and USD 7,861 million falls due in 2028. Liquidity is strong at USD 11,763 million. Financial policy and governance are average, because payouts are conservative while the parent receivable is large and legal capacity is unread.
Basis: calculation N08; calculation N10; calculation N06; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 33

Over the next 12 to 24 months management guides at group level to fourth-quarter revenue growth of 19.5% to 22.5% and a full-year 2026 adjusted loss per share of USD 1.00 to USD 1.60, on a fuel price of about USD 4.70 per gallon and a fuel cost headwind of about USD 7 billion for the year, and says growth will slow in 2027 amid elevated fuel prices. The headwinds are fuel, labour resets and fleet spending; the tailwinds are premium, corporate and loyalty growth and the secured financing market. Medium-term outlook beyond 2027 is not established in the sources.

### Downside scenario

The scenario is a prolonged fuel shock rather than a recession. The initiating condition is fuel staying near the outlook price of about USD 4.70 per gallon through 2027 while growth slows, as management expects. The first operational break is that unit revenue gains fade but fuel, labour and fixed regional fees do not, so operating margin falls below 1.14% and EBITDA below the trailing USD 2,916 million; each ten cents per gallon adds about USD 450 million to group annual fuel cost. Financial transmission follows: net leverage rises above 6.04x, cover falls below 2.06x, and free cash flow stays negative while aircraft deliveries continue and the customer-funded float of USD 21,861 million reverses in a slower season.
Basis: calculation N15; calculation N01; calculation N14; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 69; EX-99.2_2026-10-22_earningspresentation.htm.jsonl, page 6

The liquidity consequence is that USD 11,763 million of liquidity covers the USD 11,411 million of principal due in 2027 and 2028 only 1.03x, with cash also needed for operations, so a card processor holdback would matter. Management levers are real: capital expenditure and deliveries can be cut, as they were in 2026, aircraft certificates have repeatedly funded deliveries, sale-leasebacks have yielded USD 77 million in nine months, and repayment of part of the USD 10,430 million receivable by the parent is a possible lever although its terms are not established. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress through 2027 on stated liquidity, but the 2028 maturities of USD 7,861 million would need earnings recovery or continued secured market access, and an observable trigger is the refinancing of the 2028 notes and loans before leverage rises further. No default is assumed.
Basis: calculation N09; calculation N10; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_aal-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 26; EX-99.2_2026-04-23_earningspresentation.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-12-31_aal-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 253

### Overall credit view

Overall, American presents an adequate business profile supported by liquidity, loyalty income, extended maturities and conservative payouts, but credit quality is constrained by fuel-driven margin compression, net leverage of 6.04x on derived EBITDA, a USD 10,430 million receivable from its parent and a largely pledged asset base. Cash-flow durability appears weak because trailing free cash flow is a deficit of USD 132 million. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on fuel prices and continued secured market access ahead of USD 7,861 million due in 2028. The primary downside path is a prolonged fuel shock on fixed labour, regional and fleet costs. Further analysis would require ratings, executed debt documents, the proxy statement and the intercompany terms. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: sustained fuel cost without margin recovery.
