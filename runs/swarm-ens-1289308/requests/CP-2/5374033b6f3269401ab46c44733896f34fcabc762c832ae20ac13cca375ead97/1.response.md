EnerSys has a strong fundamental credit profile on leverage and liquidity, so creditors are well covered today: net debt was 0.89x twelve-month reported EBITDA at 2026-10-04, or 0.95x pro forma for about USD 40 million of buybacks between 2026-10-05 and 2026-11-06.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against treating cash use and credit dependence as the main risks is the half-year record: operating cash flow and cash on hand repaid USD 294,563 thousand of revolver principal while also funding buybacks, reported EBITDA excluding the credit still rose 58.33%, and even if the credit's share of twelve-month EBITDA matched the six-month 28.83% (the fourth-quarter guidance implies 26.83% to 27.65%), net debt over EBITDA would be about 1.25x with interest cover of 9.46x on a proxy basis. The view would turn more negative if net debt over reported EBITDA returned to the 1.38x of fiscal 2025 after Ultralife and further buybacks, if the Ultralife purchase or buybacks were funded by drawing the revolver, or if the production credits were cut before the 2030 phase-out the annual report describes. It would turn more positive if the notes due in 2027 were repaid or refinanced without drawing the revolver and the fiscal 2026 results confirmed the half-year trend.

The next 12 to 24 months are supportive for revenue and neutral for cash: management guides the quarter to 2027-01-03 to net sales of USD 935 million to USD 975 million, which is 1.73% to 6.08% above the prior-year third quarter, expects material handling to start recovering, and excludes Ultralife and tariff refunds from its outlook; against that, Industrial Mobility Solutions margin is expected to come under pressure as transportation outgrows material handling, and the notes due in 2027 fall due on 2027-12-15.
Basis: EX99.1_2026-10-04_ens_ex991-earningsx2qfy27-htm.htm.jsonl, page 5; EX99.1_2026-10-04_ens_ex991-earningsx2qfy27-htm.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-Q_2025-12-28_ens.htm.jsonl, page 3; calculation C19

Over 3 to 5 years the plant and the tax credits dominate: construction in Greenville is expected to begin in the first half of fiscal 2028 with full production about three years later, net investment is about USD 500 million, the revolver matures on 2030-09-30, and the annual report says the production credit begins to phase out in 2030, so the plant's cash needs overlap with the period when the credit support starts to fade.

Tailwinds are data center and defense demand and a communications recovery, which are less sensitive to tariff policy; headwinds are forklift and Class 8 truck demand, lead and freight costs, currency and tariff uncertainty.

### Downside scenario

The primary downside path starts with forklift and truck demand staying weak while the production credit is cut or phased down earlier than expected and lead prices rise: Industrial Mobility Solutions, 42.7% of net sales, would shrink further, losing the credit would raise cost of sales by an amount equal to 28.83% of six-month reported EBITDA, and a 10% rise in lead cost would add about USD 32 million per six months before prices catch up six to nine months later.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 36; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 55; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 37; calculation C8; calculation C15

Financial transmission is cushioned: if the credit's share of twelve-month EBITDA matched the six-month share, EBITDA would be about USD 433.1 million and net debt would be about 1.25x it, with interest cover of 9.46x on the proxy basis, and without the tariff refund of about USD 29 million net debt would be 0.93x EBITDA, so leverage rises but stays far below the 4.00x maximum last stated on the issuer's definition, which the credit agreement lets rise temporarily to 4.50x for four quarters after an acquisition larger than USD 250 million, a size the Ultralife enterprise value of about USD 320 million exceeds although whether it qualifies is not established.
Basis: EX99.1_2026-10-04_ens_ex991-earningsx2qfy27-htm.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-Q_2025-12-28_ens.htm.jsonl, page 27; calculation C3; calculation C9; calculation C17

The liquidity and refinancing effect is limited unless cash leaves at the same time: cash and committed lines of USD 1,073.83 million cover the USD 300.0 million notes due in 2027 3.58 times, but cash alone covers them 0.92 times, the Ultralife purchase and buybacks draw on the same cash, and revolver availability depends on covenants that are not established, so distress needs both a sharp earnings fall and heavy cash outflows.

Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress on the supplied evidence.

### Overall credit view

Overall, EnerSys presents an adequate business profile supported by a diversified customer base, defense and data center growth and no sponsor, but credit quality is constrained by cash use and by earnings that lean on production credits and a tariff refund. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow conversion ranged from 24.93% to 109.54% and was 73.18% in the latest half year without the tax refund. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the cash left after buybacks, Ultralife and the plant, since cash and committed lines cover the notes due in 2027 3.58 times. The primary downside path is a cut in the credits and weak forklift demand while cash leaves through buybacks and acquisitions. Further analysis would require the fiscal 2026 annual report, the credit agreement, the Ultralife terms and peer data, because no peer or rating evidence is supplied to compare EnerSys with typical leveraged-credit issuers in its sector. Top risk: cash leaving the company through buybacks and acquisitions while earnings rely on production credits.
