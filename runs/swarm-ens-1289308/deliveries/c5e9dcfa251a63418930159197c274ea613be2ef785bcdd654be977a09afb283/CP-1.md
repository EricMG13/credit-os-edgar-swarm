## Findings

### Bottom line

EnerSys has low leverage and ample liquidity, so creditors are well covered: net debt was 0.89x twelve-month reported EBITDA at 2026-10-04, down from 1.38x at 2025-12-28, and USD 1,073.83 million of cash and committed lines is 3.58 times the notes due in 2027.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 22; EX99.1_2026-10-04_ens_ex991-earningsx2qfy27-htm.htm.jsonl, page 16; EX99.1_2025-12-28_ens_ex991-earningsx3qfy26-htm.htm.jsonl, page 15; calculation N6; calculation S5

The risk is how cash is used, not leverage today: the company spent USD 180.0 million on buybacks in the half year and about USD 40 million more since quarter end, has agreed to buy Ultralife at an enterprise value of about USD 320 million to be funded from cash on hand, and gets 28.83% of half-year reported EBITDA from the IRC 45X credit. Cash interest cover was 11.44x in fiscal 2025 but is not supplied since, and covenant terms after fiscal 2025 are only partly established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 123; calculation S1; calculation S8

### Leverage and coverage

Net debt at carrying value rose from 1.15x reported EBITDA at 2024-03-31 to 1.38x at 2025-03-31 and was still 1.38x at 2025-12-28, because the fiscal 2025 acquisitions, share repurchases and a build in prepaid taxes were funded in part on the revolver, which had net borrowing of USD 280.0 million in the year. It fell to 0.87x at 2026-07-05 and 0.89x at 2026-10-04 as twelve-month reported EBITDA rose to USD 608.6 million and gross debt fell by 30.90% from 2025-12-28.
Basis: 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 97; 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 119; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 2; EX99.1_2026-10-04_ens_ex991-earningsx2qfy27-htm.htm.jsonl, page 16; calculation S2; calculation S4; calculation N1; calculation N2; calculation N3; calculation N5; calculation N6

Reported EBITDA covered cash interest paid 9.03x in fiscal 2024 and 11.44x in fiscal 2025. Cash interest paid is stated for fiscal years only, so later cover rests on a proxy on twelve-month interest expense, 13.29x at 2026-10-04, which is a different measure and is labelled as one. The issuer's own net leverage of 0.8x against a 4.00x maximum shows wide headroom on its definitions, but covenant levels after fiscal 2025 other than the 4.00x leverage level are not established, so headroom under the covenants cannot be calculated here.
Basis: 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 82; 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 123; EX99.1_2026-10-04_ens_ex991-earningsx2qfy27-htm.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2025-12-28_ens.htm.jsonl, page 27

Net debt flatters domestic leverage, because a substantial majority of cash is held by foreign subsidiaries and is considered indefinitely reinvested while the senior notes are obligations of the parent company. Leverage could also rise again if the cash-funded Ultralife purchase and the buybacks continue, because the half year's operating cash flow included USD 115.5 million of one-off tax refunds.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 31; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 4

### Cash flow, liquidity and maturities

Operating cash flow fell to USD 260.3 million in fiscal 2025 from USD 457.0 million because the production credits were booked as prepaid taxes, which rose by USD 192.1 million, before being collected; the half year to 2026-10-04 then produced USD 372.8 million, of which an IRS refund of prior-year taxes supplied USD 115.5 million, after USD 137.0 million in the prior-year half. Operating cash flow therefore follows the credit-collection cycle and is not a clean run rate. Capital expenditure was 1.32% of net sales in the half year against 3.35% in fiscal 2025, while the company's own outlook is about USD 70 million for the year, so free cash flow would be lower in the second half if the outlook holds.
Basis: 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 62; 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 77; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 51; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 52; EX99.1_2026-10-04_ens_ex991-earningsx2qfy27-htm.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation S7; calculation S11; calculation S12; calculation S13

Cash of USD 275.5 million plus undrawn committed facilities of USD 798.33 million gives USD 1,073.83 million, 3.58 times the USD 300.0 million 4.375% notes that mature on 2027-12-15 and will be current debt from the quarter ending 2027-01-03. The revolver, USD 193.0 million drawn of a USD 1,000 million commitment, matures on 2030-09-30 and the 2032 notes on 2032-01-15, so no other contractual maturity falls before fiscal 2031; the availability of the revolver is subject to the credit agreement financial covenants.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 38; 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 120; calculation L6; calculation S5

The company also funds itself through a receivables purchase program: it sold USD 682.504 million of receivables in the half year and USD 775.236 million in fiscal 2025, all collected by the dates reported, and receivables of USD 372.887 million held by EnerSys Finance would likely not be available to other creditors in an insolvency until the purchasers are repaid. A lapse of the program, which matures on 2028-12-15, would tie up cash in receivables.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 117; 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 118

### Data quality and definitions

Reported EBITDA is the primary denominator and adjusted EBITDA per credit agreement is a separate row; the two adjusted measures the issuer publishes, its company adjusted EBITDA and the credit-agreement figure, are never mixed, and the first-quarter fiscal 2027 company figure differs between two releases. Carrying-value net debt differs from the issuer's credit-agreement net debt by the unamortized issuance costs and standby letters of credit, with a small residual, as the definition register sets out.
Basis: EX99.1_2026-07-05_ens_ex991-earningsx1qfy27-htm.htm.jsonl, page 2; EX99.1_2026-10-04_ens_ex991-earningsx2qfy27-htm.htm.jsonl, page 2; EX99.1_2026-10-04_ens_ex991-earningsx2qfy27-htm.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 22

The segment structure changed on 2026-05-28, so fiscal 2024 and 2025 segment sales are on the four-segment basis and are not joined to the three-segment half-year figures.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-10-04_ens.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-K_2025-03-31_ens.htm.jsonl, page 149; calculation S9

Events after 2026-10-04 are kept out of the period figures: share repurchases of about USD 40 million, which would lift net leverage to 0.95x, the dividend payable on 2026-12-31 and the pending Ultralife acquisition, whose effect on net debt is not stated.
