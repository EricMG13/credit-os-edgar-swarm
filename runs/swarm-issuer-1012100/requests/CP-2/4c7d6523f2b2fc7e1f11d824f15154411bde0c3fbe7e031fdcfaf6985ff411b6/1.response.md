## Findings

### Bottom line

Sealed Air was an adequate and deleveraging packaging credit at the end of 2025, but the April 2026 CD&R buyout makes that picture historical, so creditors cannot size post-closing leverage, ranking or reporting from the filings.
Basis: STATUS_15-12G_2026-04-20_see.htm.jsonl, page 1; Source readiness (CP-0)

The support is cash generation: debt fell by USD 394.2 million in 2025 (USD 344.2 million like-for-like), free cash flow after dividends was USD 339.3 million and the covenant leverage ratio of 2.95x sat inside its 4.50x maximum. The constraint is that reported EBITDA of USD 939.0 million was barely above the USD 926.1 million of 2023, USD 600 million of secured notes fell due in October 2026, and the buyer's USD 10.3 billion price is 9.08x FY2025 adjusted EBITDA with its financing undisclosed.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious reading is that the filings show a credit that was improving and conservatively run. Debt fell by USD 394.2 million in 2025, the covenant ratio of 2.95x was well inside its 4.50x limit, the revolver was undrawn, bank maturities had been moved to October 2030, and the senior secured debt was rated Baa2 and BBB-, investment grade at both agencies. Protective adjusted EBITDA also rose 21% in the fourth quarter, to USD 80 million from USD 67 million, which suggests the erosion may be bottoming, and management states that U.S. cash and committed facilities available to U.S. borrowers are sufficient for at least the next 12 months. A buyout can also leave existing creditors protected if sponsor equity is large, or end their exposure if the notes were repaid at closing. The view would improve if post-closing statements show leverage on the issuer's own measure no higher than its year-end level, the notes and credit agreement left in place and creditor reporting continuing. It would worsen if the financing adds net debt on a scale where each USD 1,000.0 million adds 0.88x, if the secured layer grows ahead of the unsecured notes, or if Protective volume resumes falling.

### Limitations

- The overall assessment of an adequate credit describes the company on December 31, 2025, and it would move up or down once the buyout financing, its security and its guarantees are known, because none of that is in the filings.
- EBITDA here is derived from the statements and sits USD 195.3 million below the issuer's adjusted figure, so the leverage and cover conclusions read weaker than the issuer's own leverage measure, and they soften if the issuer's add-backs are accepted.
  Basis: calculation C5; calculation C11
- The committed liquidity of USD 1,055.3 million is this module's reconciliation of three notes that the issuer does not itemize, and the securitization part depends on eligible receivables and ratings, so the liquidity conclusion is lower if availability sits below the program limits.
  Basis: calculation C28; calculation C29
- The like-for-like free cash flow range uses the year-end derecognized balance as the size of the 2025 benefit, so the cash conversion conclusion carries an uncertainty of up to USD 50.0 million.
  Basis: calculation C17
- Segment gross profit excludes corporate cost and the segments' adjusted EBITDA is the issuer's own measure, so the conclusion that Protective is eroding while Food carries the credit could be overstated or understated if costs moved between segments.
- No peer set, market prices or agency commentary were supplied, so the comparison with other leveraged packaging issuers is a judgement and the view carries no market cross-check.
- The interest and EBITDA sensitivities are mechanical illustrations that assume refinancing at the coupon of the 2032 senior notes and hold everything else constant, so they show the scale of the effect on cover and not a forecast.

### Company description and ownership

Sealed Air Corporation, a Delaware company, is the registrant of a consolidated group that sells packaging materials, automation equipment and services to food processors (Food, 67.1% of 2025 net sales) and to e-commerce, logistics, industrial and medical customers (Protective, 32.9%). The business model builds cash-flow visibility through long customer relationships, equipment placed with customers and brands, but not through contracts: remaining performance obligations were USD 17.9 million against net sales of USD 5,359.8 million, and the filing says it generally imposes no annual minimum purchase volumes on distributors, who handle about 60% of Protective sales. Debt service therefore rests on repeat purchasing and the spread of customers rather than contracted backlog, which gives refinancing lenders little revenue visibility beyond the current order flow.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sealed-air-corp.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 8, 14, 132, 133; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C48; calculation C50

Control passed on April 9, 2026 from a widely held public shareholder base, where the largest holders had 10.9%, 10.1% and 9.2%, to Sword Purchaser, LLC, which is affiliated with CD&R. A holding-company structure matters for ranking: the filing says a substantial part of operations runs through subsidiaries that are not all guarantors, so note holders and lenders depend on guarantor subsidiaries and on distributions, and receivables held in securitization vehicles or sold under factoring programs are not available to them. The registrant's reporting ended with the Form 15, and the sponsor's own funds, fees and distribution policy are not described, so creditor alignment under sponsor ownership is not established.

### Revenue, cost and cash conversion

Revenue is stable rather than growing and its quality differs by segment. Net sales fell 0.61% in 2025 and 2.35% between 2023 and 2025; Food price rose by USD 21.5 million in 2025 on contract-formula pricing while its volume fell by USD 25.4 million, and Protective lost USD 40.0 million of volume from earlier customer churn in its fulfillment portfolio and USD 21.6 million of price. The credit implication is that Food's formula pricing keeps its margin more predictable, while Protective's price and volume are exposed to the e-commerce and industrial cycle and to distributor purchasing that carries no minimum commitments.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sealed-air-corp.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 66, 67; calculation C18

Margins can be defended against modest volume pressure but not against a repeat of the 2024 Protective shock without further cost action. Gross margin was 29.8% in 2025, cost of sales is 70.2% of net sales, and in 2024 an 8.08% fall in Protective net sales cut its adjusted EBITDA by 13.35%, or USD 48.3 million, even with savings flowing in. The plant network and workforce are largely fixed cost, so volume losses weigh heavily on margin, and the finished savings program removes the offset that held 2025 earnings flat.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sealed-air-corp.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 65, 68, 77, 79; calculation C25

Cash conversion is moderate and flattered at the margin. Free cash flow was 48.83% of reported EBITDA in 2025, dividends took 18.98% of operating cash flow, and capital expenditure of 3.16% of net sales gives some flexibility, since it fell USD 74.7 million between 2023 and 2025, but 2026 spending is planned higher. The issuer expects USD 201 million of interest and about USD 155 million of tax payments in 2026, plus pension contributions of about USD 10 million and benefit payments of about USD 9 million, so interest and taxes together, USD 356 million, call on more cash than the USD 225 million of planned capital expenditure.

### Competitive forces and external factors

Competitive rivalry is the force that matters most for credit and it is uneven. The filing describes global and regional competitors competing on performance, automation, sustainability, service and price, with competitors and customers consolidating, and Protective has lost both price and volume since 2023, which is evidence that rivalry caps its pricing; Food, with equipment-integrated materials sold directly to food processors, shows rising margins. For creditors this means Protective earnings are the first to give way and Food holds the cash flow.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sealed-air-corp.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 27, 56, 57, 66; calculation C23; calculation C24

The threat of new entrants is moderate, because brands, equipment integration, product registration requirements and plant scale are barriers but the filing says the business does not depend on any single patent or trademark. The threat of substitutes comes from paper, molded pulp and corrugated alternatives in protective packaging and from regulation of plastics and PFAS, which could cut demand or force reformulation; Food's shelf-life and food-safety function makes switching harder, although competing food packaging also uses plastic, metals and other materials. Buyer power is limited by customer spread, with no customer above 10%, but is real in Protective, where distributors carry no minimum purchases, and supplier power is limited because most resins are available from several sources, although sole-source suppliers exist in some regions and a resin spike reaches cost of sales ahead of prices.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sealed-air-corp.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 13, 14, 15, 16, 25, 36, 37; calculation C50

Regulation, trade policy and currency are material to credit and are treated here as the political, economic, social and technological factors that change cash flow. Plastic-waste legislation, PFAS rules and tariffs imposed during 2025 raise compliance cost, can lower demand for plastic packaging and complicate sourcing; 49% of net sales are earned outside the United States, so currency moves translate into reported earnings and a USD 15.1 million loss from highly inflationary economies, which the filing ties to Argentina, was treated as a special item in 2025; about USD 30 million of cash sits in Russia and Ukraine; sustainability preferences push customers toward recyclable materials; and labor agreements covering 31% of the roughly 5,200 covered employees expire in 2026. Automation demand from labor scarcity is a management claim that supports Food and Protective growth but is not quantified.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

The next 12 to 24 months are set by the buyout financing more than by operations. The operating tailwinds are the annualized savings already delivered, a Protective segment whose fourth-quarter volume rose by USD 6 million and whose adjusted EBITDA rose 21%, and Food contract-formula pricing; the headwinds are volume weakness in Food's industrial portfolio, raw-material deflation that lowers price, a USD 55.5 million step-up in capital expenditure, labor negotiations and the October 2026 notes. These support a stable operating outlook but cannot offset an unknown capital structure.

### Downside scenario

The realistic downside combines a second Protective shock with leverage added by the buyout. The initiating condition is a repeat of 2024, when Protective volume fell USD 101.8 million and price USD 49.3 million, now joined by the weakness already seen in Food's industrial portfolio. The first operational break is distributor and fulfillment volume, which carries no minimum commitments, with price concessions on top; the financial transmission is that a repeat of the 13.35% fall in Protective adjusted EBITDA would cost USD 41.44 million, 3.65% of consolidated adjusted EBITDA, and lower EBITDA cover of cash interest from 3.50x to 3.34x, so the operating shock alone is survivable.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sealed-air-corp.htm.jsonl, pages 66, 67, 77; calculation C13; calculation C25; calculation C45

What would turn it into a liquidity event is the capital structure behind the shock. Each USD 100.0 million of lost reported EBITDA lowers cover of cash interest to 3.12x, each USD 1,000.0 million of acquisition debt adds 0.88x to leverage on adjusted EBITDA, and replacing the October 2026 notes at the 2032 notes' coupon adds USD 29.56 million of interest, while securitization availability shrinks if ratings fall and the revolver is the only large committed source. Management levers are capital expenditure, which fell by USD 74.7 million between 2023 and 2025, the dividend, whose post-closing policy is unknown, and working-capital programs that are already large. Before the buyout resilience was sufficient to avoid liquidity distress, with cover of current debt 1.93x; after it, resilience is not established. The observable triggers are Protective volume, revolver drawings and any amendment or waiver of the credit agreement.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_sealed-air-corp.htm.jsonl, pages 82, 83, 84, 143, 149, 150; calculation C31; calculation C35; calculation C37; calculation C41; calculation C44

### Overall credit view

Overall, Sealed Air presented an adequate business profile at the end of 2025, supported by a USD 5,359.8 million sales base with no customer above 10%, a larger and higher-margin Food segment and conservative pre-sale financial policy, but credit quality is constrained by a Protective segment that lost 17.00% of gross profit in two years, finished cost savings and the loss of visibility after the CD&R buyout. Cash-flow durability appears adequate, because free cash flow was USD 458.5 million and USD 339.3 million after dividends, although up to USD 50.0 million of 2025 operating cash flow came from selling receivables that the old program had carried as debt. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on how the acquisition was financed and whether the October 2026 notes and the bank debt were repaid, amended or waived. The primary downside path is acquisition debt that raises leverage and interest cost while Protective volume stays weak. Further analysis would require the financing documents, a post-closing debt schedule, 2026 interim statements and creditor reporting terms. Fundamental credit assessment completed. Top risk: undisclosed acquisition financing and its effect on leverage, ranking and liquidity.
