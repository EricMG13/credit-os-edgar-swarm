## Findings

### Bottom line

Herc's fundamental credit profile is adequate: a scaled, diversified fleet business whose net leverage of 4.12x on reported last-twelve-months EBITDA is elevated and falls only through earnings growth, leaving creditors reliant on cash flow, an asset-backed revolver and disposals rather than sponsor support.
Basis: calculation C1; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The main support is spread: no customer exceeds 3% of equipment rental revenue, liquidity is USD 2,100 million with USD 2,034 million available under a revolver due 2030, and last-twelve-months EBITDA rose 16.53% from March to September 2026. The main constraint is cash generation: issuer free cash flow of USD 133 million includes USD 345 million of disposal proceeds, and the fully drawn USD 550 million AR Facility matures on August 31, 2027.
Basis: calculation C5; calculation C8; calculation C9; Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_hri-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18

The primary downside path is a construction slowdown that cuts utilization while rent, insurance and overhead stay fixed, which would raise leverage on flat debt; the monitor is net leverage on reported EBITDA moving above 4.12x.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_hri-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 13; calculation C1

### Key findings

- Scale and customer spread are the main credit support. No customer exceeds 3% of equipment rental revenue, national accounts are 49%, and equipment rental is the only segment, so weakness in one end market is diluted across contractors, industrial, infrastructure and commercial facilities customers.
  Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_hri-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11
- Leverage is the main constraint and is falling through earnings, not repayment. Net debt is 4.12x reported last-twelve-months EBITDA and total debt 4.16x at September 30, 2026, against 3.90x reported by the issuer on adjusted pro forma earnings; the ratio was 4.79x at March 31 and 4.27x at June 30 while carrying net debt at September 30 was only USD 23 million above its March 31 level.
  Basis: calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C6; calculation C4; Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-10-27_herc2026q3-pressrelease.htm.jsonl, page 4
- Interest absorbs much of the earnings gain. EBITDA covers cash interest 3.65x over the last twelve months, cash interest paid rose 54.95% in nine months 2026 as the acquisition financing ran for a full period, and the floating-rate revolver and term loan keep it sensitive to rates.
  Basis: calculation C3; calculation C12; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_hri-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18
- Free cash flow is thin and disposal-dependent. Issuer free cash flow of USD 133 million for nine months 2026 includes USD 345 million of disposal proceeds; before them operating cash flow less capital expenditure is negative USD 212 million, and dividends of USD 71 million take 53.38% of the issuer figure.
  Basis: calculation C9; calculation C10; calculation C11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_hri-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 6
- Liquidity is adequate for now and the nearest refinancing is the AR Facility. Cash plus revolver availability is USD 2,100 million, 3.96x last-twelve-months cash interest; the AR Facility of USD 550 million is fully drawn and due on August 31, 2027, and the largest principal bucket, 45.82% of the December 31, 2025 ladder, falls in 2030.
  Basis: calculation C8; calculation C14; calculation C13; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_hri-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 18
- Governance and financial policy are mixed rather than adverse. No sponsor or controlling holder is disclosed, the dividend of USD 0.70 per share continues and the acquisition was debt-funded; capacity under the debt agreements is unread, so willingness and capacity to extract value are both unevidenced.
  Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_hri-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 53
- Margins face cost pressure that the issuer ties to the acquisition, fuel and delivery. Direct operating expense was 44.63% of equipment rental revenue in nine months 2026 against 42.95%, while the issuer's adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 41.5% from 41.0%.
  Basis: calculation C16; calculation C18; 10-Q_2026-09-30_hri-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 4; EX-99.1_2026-10-27_herc2026q3-pressrelease.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3
- Integration is reported complete but cannot be verified. Management says synergies are tracking to plan, and no integration cost or synergy amount is disclosed, so the earnings gain cannot yet be split between synergies, organic demand and acquired revenue.
  Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); EX-99.1_2026-07-28_herc2026q2-pressrelease.htm.jsonl, page 1

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is the earnings trajectory: last-twelve-months EBITDA rose 16.53% in six months, the third-quarter adjusted EBITDA margin reached 44.2%, the issuer raised 2026 guidance for the second time this year and replaced its 2027 notes at par. If net debt stays flat and EBITDA keeps growing, reported leverage would keep falling toward the issuer's 3.90x. The view would improve if free cash flow before disposals turned positive and synergy amounts were disclosed; it would worsen if utilization or rental rates fell, if disposal proceeds weakened, or if the AR Facility were not extended well ahead of August 31, 2027.
Basis: calculation C5; calculation C18; EX-99.1_2026-10-27_herc2026q3-pressrelease.htm.jsonl, pages 1–4; 10-K_2025-12-31_hri-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 104

### Limitations

- Reported leverage is built from filed figures; an issuer-adjusted trailing EBITDA, which is not supplied, would lower the ratio and could move the assessment of leverage tolerance from constrained toward adequate.
  Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); EX-99.1_2026-10-27_herc2026q3-pressrelease.htm.jsonl, page 4
- No rating, market price or peer data is supplied, so the judgement that leverage is elevated is absolute, not relative to comparable issuers, and the access-to-markets view rests on past issuance alone.
  Basis: Source readiness (CP-0)
- The refinancing view uses a maturity ladder dated December 31, 2025; a ladder at September 30, 2026 could change the size and timing of near-term maturities.
  Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-K_2025-12-31_hri-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 103–104
- The downside scenario is qualitative because no management case, sensitivity or executed covenant terms are supplied, so liquidity runway under stress is not quantified.
  Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_hri-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths are scale, spread and access. Scale and market position are average, with an estimated 4% market share in a fragmented industry; business diversification is strong because no customer exceeds 3% of equipment rental revenue and national accounts are 49%; the ability to refinance is average, supported by the redemption of the 2027 notes at par with new 2031 and 2034 notes and by USD 2.8 billion of 2030 and 2033 notes issued in 2025, but untested by rating or price evidence; liquidity is strong at USD 2,100 million, with the revolver due in 2030.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_hri-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-12-31_hri-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 104; calculation C8

Weaknesses are cash generation and cost. Free cash flow stability is weak because issuer free cash flow depends on USD 345 million of disposal proceeds; margin stability and cost flexibility are average, since direct operating expense rose to 44.63% of equipment rental revenue while rent, insurance and overhead are fixed; competitive advantage is average, resting on scale and national accounts rather than contracts, as virtually all rentals can be cancelled within a day; financial policy and governance are average, with a debt-funded acquisition, a continuing dividend and no sponsor to backstop equity.
Basis: calculation C9; calculation C16; 10-Q_2026-09-30_hri-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 11; Business & governance (CP-1A)
