## Findings

### Bottom line

Creditors now rest on a larger and more leveraged industrial business whose credit case depends on integrating Kito Crosby, a USD 2,811.91 million all-cash purchase financed by new capital that was 76.12% secured debt and 23.88% CD&R senior preferred.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_cmco-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 86–87; calculation C01; calculation C02

The business sells products that management calls safety-critical through distributors, and Kito Crosby already supplies 56.50% of first-half sales, but the issuer's own leverage measure still adds back USD 132.39 million of annualised earnings and synergies. Sponsor and governance risk is medium: CD&R supplied equity and shows no documented extraction, yet the investment agreement, board terms and proxy statement were not supplied.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_cmco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10; EX-99.1_2026-11-03_exhibit9911132026.htm.jsonl, page 14; calculation C07; calculation C10

### Key findings

A material part of the earnings behind the leverage measure is annualised, not yet earned. Kito Crosby supplied 15.76% of FY2026 net sales but 56.50% of H1 FY2027 net sales, and the issuer's Credit Agreement measure adds USD 132.39 million, or 28.30% of its USD 467.80 million twelve-month earnings figure, for a full year of Kito Crosby, the divestiture and expected synergies. That add-back was 59.94% of the measure at March 2026, when it included USD 70.64 million of expected synergies. Creditors should read the 4.9x ratio as management-normalised, not measured.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_cmco-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 87; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cmco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 10; EX-99.1_2026-06-04_exhibit9913312026.htm.jsonl, page 15; EX-99.1_2026-11-03_exhibit9911132026.htm.jsonl, page 14; calculation C07; calculation C10

The capital structure is debt-heavy with a growing senior equity layer rather than common equity beneath the debt. New secured debt of USD 2,550.00 million and USD 800.00 million of 7% preferred raised USD 538.09 million more than the purchase price, a sum the filings do not break down between refinancing, fees and cash. The preferred accreted USD 37.43 million through September 2026 and the issuer guides to paying it in kind during FY2027; paid in cash it would cost about USD 58.62 million a year on a simple calculation, against USD 4.04 million of common dividends in H1 FY2027. Debt holders gain from the cash retained, but the preferred claim grows ahead of common holders and would compete for cash if the election changed.
Basis: 10-K_2026-03-31_cmco-20260331.htm.jsonl, pages 130–131; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cmco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cmco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 56; EX-99.1_2026-06-04_exhibit9913312026.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation C02; calculation C04; calculation C05; calculation C20

CD&R holds influence and a senior claim, but the documents read show no extraction, so governance risk is medium rather than high or low. The investor owns all of the Series A preferred, may name up to three directors, has preemptive rights to keep its pro rata stake and would hold about 43.45% as converted at September 2026 on a calculation that ignores unvested awards, up from 42.48% at issue because the dividend accretes. The annual report itself warns that the investor's interests may differ from other holders. No fees, related-party dealings or creditor-adverse transactions appear in the filings read, but the agreements that would show consent rights and fees were not supplied, so capacity and willingness cannot be separated.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is trading momentum and cash retention. Adjusted EBITDA margin was 20.6% in Q2 FY2027 against 15.4% a year earlier, orders rose 122% largely through the acquisition, and the issuer raised its FY2027 net sales and Adjusted EBITDA guidance twice, to USD 2,130 million to USD 2,170 million and USD 425 million to USD 440 million. Liquidity of USD 531.98 million is far above the USD 17.98 million of principal due in FY2027 and the USD 69.90 million due in FY2029, the sponsor's USD 800.00 million is equity behind the debt and is being paid in kind, and the 50 bp margin cut lowers interest cost. If these hold, the medium governance rating and the doubts about the annualised leverage measure would both ease.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-06-04_exhibit9913312026.htm.jsonl, page 4; EX-99.1_2026-11-03_exhibit9911132026.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; 10-K_2026-03-31_cmco-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 115; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cmco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cmco-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 55; calculation C15; calculation C19; calculation C22

The view would improve if the investment agreement and proxy statement show only customary investor consent rights and no fees, if FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA lands within guidance with synergies realised in cash, and if the planned USD 150.00 million paydown is made while preferred dividends stay in kind. It would worsen if the issuer elects cash preferred dividends, funds further sponsor-linked acquisitions with debt, repeats the AR facility waiver, or lets revolver borrowings reach the 30% level at which the first lien leverage covenant is tested.
