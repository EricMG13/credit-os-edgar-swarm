## Findings

### Bottom line

CoreWeave has very strong contracted demand but a weak financial profile: debt is high and rising, free cash flow is deeply negative and the build depends on markets staying open, so creditors are exposed to funding conditions more than to demand.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C3; calculation C6

Net debt was USD 29,544 million at June 30, 2026, or 7.86x reported last-twelve-month EBITDA, and the pro forma effect of the August 2026 borrowings on net debt is not established. Reported EBITDA covers cash interest 2.86x, and the USD 10,597 million of principal due through 2027 is about twice the USD 5,524 million of cash. Against that, contracted obligations of USD 103.7 billion and secured, contract-backed loans give a long runway, which the primary downside path, a delay in delivering capacity or a closed funding window, would test.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C6; 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 15; 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 56

### Key findings

- Leverage is the weakest dimension and is still rising. Total debt reached 9.33x reported EBITDA and net debt 7.86x at June 30, 2026, and net leverage is 6.71x on the issuer's adjusted EBITDA, so leverage is high on either definition, while the August 2026 effect on net debt is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C2; calculation C3
- Free cash flow is deeply negative by design, so the credit is a funding story. Operating cash flow over the last twelve months fell USD 13,655 million short of capital spending, and that gap was met with new debt and equity, which makes market access the first thing to fail in a downturn.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C5
- Coverage is thin and flattered by capitalised interest. Reported EBITDA covers cash interest paid 2.86x and contractual interest expense 2.08x, and most of the credit facilities carry floating rates that are largely unhedged, so a rise in SOFR would reduce cover directly.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C4; 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 136
- Near-term maturities exceed cash but not total liquidity. Principal of USD 10,597 million falls due through 2027 against USD 5,524 million of cash, and total liquidity of USD 15,553 million includes USD 10,014 million of facility availability that can be drawn only against eligible assets, so the shortfall is covered only if the issuer keeps delivering financeable capacity.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C6; calculation C7
- Contracted revenue visibility is the main support. Obligations of USD 103.7 billion are about three times total debt, committed contracts were 98% of revenue and the weighted-average duration is about five years, so cash flow is contractually underpinned if capacity is delivered on schedule.
Basis: calculation C9; 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15; 10-K_2025-12-31_crwv-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 8
- Customer concentration persists although it is falling. The largest customer fell from 67% of FY2025 revenue to 40% of first-half 2026 revenue, but the three largest customers are 72% of second-quarter 2026 revenue, so one counterparty can move both revenue and the security behind the loans.
Basis: calculation C8; Business & governance (CP-1A)
- Customers and vendors fund part of the build. Deferred revenue of USD 9,692 million is 1.75 times cash, and supplier credit sits outside debt, so a stop in new prepayments or vendor terms would take cash out of a business that already has negative free cash flow.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C10
- Governance adds risk. Founder voting control, material weaknesses in internal control and open litigation reduce the reliability of reported ratios and give creditors no equity-owner counterweight, which lowers confidence in every figure above.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 59; 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 141

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a more positive view is that debt is mostly asset-level and contract-backed: the issuer says its delayed draw loans are collateralised by the contracts and cash flows of mostly investment-grade counterparties, contracted obligations are about three times total debt, revenue grew 112.08% and adjusted EBITDA 96.10% year on year in the first half of 2026, and large equity investors continue to fund the build, so trailing ratios may overstate risk from capacity that is financed but not yet earning. This view would win if the third-quarter 2026 report showed adjusted EBITDA growing faster than debt, capital spending converging toward operating cash flow, the delayed draw facilities amortising on schedule, and the control weaknesses remediated. The negative view would win if a large customer delayed payment or capacity, if debt or equity markets closed, or if availability under the delayed draw facilities stopped growing.
Basis: calculation C9; calculation C11; calculation C13; 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 56; 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 72

### Limitations

- No credit agreement or indenture is supplied, so covenant levels, guarantor scope and ranking are taken from note descriptions, which limits the debt structure assessment to what the issuer chose to disclose.
- No management guidance or customer contract terms are supplied, so the outlook and downside rely on reported backlog and risk-factor text, which limits how precisely the downside can be sized.
- No rating, market price or peer evidence is supplied, so the view is not benchmarked against peers or market-implied risk.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The main strengths are contracted revenue visibility, a financing model that pledges specific customer contracts to lenders, large and diversified funding sources, improving customer diversification and strategic equity backing from a key supplier; each lowers the chance that demand, rather than funding, causes distress. The main weaknesses are high and rising leverage, deeply negative free cash flow, a maturity profile that exceeds cash, concentration in customers and in one chip supplier, and founder control with an ineffective control environment; each raises the chance that a funding or delivery shock turns into a liquidity problem. On balance the strengths protect against a demand shock and the weaknesses expose creditors to a funding shock.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C5; calculation C6; calculation C8; calculation C9

### Credit analysis

#### Company description

CoreWeave provides an AI-focused cloud platform of proprietary software and GPU infrastructure, sold mainly on multi-year take-or-pay contracts to AI labs, technology companies and enterprises, and it had 1.5 GW of active power at June 30, 2026. Committed contracts were 98% of revenue, and revenue grew from USD 2,194 million in the first half of 2025 to USD 4,653 million in the first half of 2026, so revenue depends on delivering contracted capacity rather than on selling on demand. That supports cash-flow visibility and debt service only while delivery and customer credit hold.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C11; calculation C16; 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 14

#### Ownership and group structure

CoreWeave is a public company with no sponsor, controlled in voting terms by its three Co-Founders through ten-vote Class B shares that convert no later than May 31, 2032 under current terms. USD 13,643 million of secured delayed draw loans sit in special-purpose subsidiaries and are mostly guaranteed by the parent, apart from the non-recourse DDTL 4.0 facility, while USD 16,617 million of unsecured notes are obligations of the parent, so lenders to the subsidiaries have first claim on the pledged contracts and equipment and noteholders rely on the parent's remaining assets. Creditors therefore rely on subsidiary collateral and parent guarantees, not on a sponsor's support.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C15; 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 26–31

#### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is driven by contracted capacity delivered, with pricing set in long contracts, and customer concentration is high: the three largest customers are 72% of second-quarter 2026 revenue. Risk factors state that new commitments from OpenAI and Meta are expected to make them significant customers, so concentration may fall but remains high. Customers have contractually specified NVIDIA hardware, which ties the issuer to one supplier, and integrated software may add switching costs, but the filings give no retention or churn data, so pricing leverage over very large buyers is not established.
Basis: calculation C8; 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 64–65; 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 72

#### Cost structure and margin resilience

The largest cost is the equipment fleet: technology and infrastructure expense, which includes depreciation, was USD 2,780 million of USD 4,846 million operating expenses in the first half of 2026, and depreciation and amortisation rose to USD 2,540 million. Reported EBITDA of USD 3,760 million over the last twelve months gives a margin of 49.54%, against 57.97% on the issuer's adjusted measure, whose gap is mainly stock-based compensation of USD 619 million. Margins hold while contracts price power and equipment cost in, but power prices are volatile and hedged only in part, so operating leverage cuts both ways.
Basis: calculation C12; Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 48; 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 63

#### Capital intensity and free cash flow conversion

Capital spending was USD 20,566 million over the last twelve months against revenue of USD 7,590 million, or 2.71 times revenue, and operating cash flow of USD 6,911 million covered only 34% of it. Unpaid equipment purchases and vendor financing sit outside cash capital spending, so true investment is higher than the cash figure and free cash flow stays negative until the contracted capacity under construction starts to earn. Slowing capital spending would mostly mean deferring contracted deliveries, which would delay revenue that the lenders' security depends on.
Basis: calculation C5; calculation C12; Financial foundation (CP-1)

#### Porter's five forces

Buyer power is high because a few hyperscale and AI-lab customers contract very large blocks, which limits pricing and concentrates credit risk. Supplier power is very high because all GPUs are NVIDIA parts that customers have specified, so allocation or price changes pass straight to delivery timing and cost. Rivalry is intense with hyperscalers that are also customers, and substitutes such as customers' own capacity or alternative accelerators are a long-term threat to utilisation. Each force raises the importance of the contracted backlog, not the margin, as the credit support.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 64–65

#### PEST analysis

Regulatory and political factors are material: a state moratorium on large data centres announced in July 2026, evolving AI rules, export controls and tariffs on imported equipment could delay or raise the cost of capacity, which would postpone the revenue that services the delayed draw loans. Economic factors matter through interest rates because most credit-facility debt is floating and mostly unhedged. Social and technological factors are not primary credit drivers on the materials provided.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 123; 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 136; 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 67

#### SWOT

Strengths are contracted visibility, contract-backed financing and strategic equity support. Weaknesses are leverage, negative free cash flow, concentration and control weaknesses. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are the conversion of backlog into revenue as new capacity commences and diversification into enterprise customers. Threats that could weaken it are a customer or supplier shock, a closed funding market and construction delays in the first self-developed campuses.
Basis: calculation C8; calculation C9; Business & governance (CP-1A)

#### Financial profile and credit quality

Scale is strong and competitive position is average with high concentration; business diversification is weak; cost and capex flexibility is weak because contracted deliveries drive spending; margin stability is average; free cash flow stability is weak; ability to access capital markets has been strong so far but is the critical dependency; liquidity is average because cash is small against near-term principal while facility availability is conditional; and financial policy and governance is weak because of founder control and ineffective controls. The trend is rising scale and rising leverage together, and the key missing data are covenant levels, customer contract terms and anything after August 11, 2026.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C6; calculation C7; Business & governance (CP-1A)

#### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over 12 to 24 months the tailwind is conversion of obligations into revenue as active power rises from 1.5 GW toward about 3.7 GW contracted, which would lift EBITDA and cover if delivered on time. The headwinds are principal due through 2027, amortisation that begins on several delayed draw loans in 2026 and 2027, floating-rate interest and capital that must be raised repeatedly. Over three to five years the issue is whether EBITDA from contracted capacity grows faster than the debt raised to build it, which cannot be established from the materials supplied.
Basis: calculation C6; calculation C16; 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 27–30; EX-99.1_2026-08-11_coreweave2q26earningspress.htm.jsonl, page 2

#### Qualitative downside scenario

The scenario starts with a delay in delivering capacity at a large site, caused by power, equipment or construction problems, which the issuer lists among its risks. The first operational break is that contract revenue cannot start on schedule at that site while the delayed draw loans for it begin monthly or quarterly amortisation on fixed dates, so cash leaves before revenue arrives. Financially, operating cash flow weakens against capital spending that is already committed, cash of USD 5,524 million is drawn down against principal of USD 10,597 million due through 2027, and availability that depends on eligible assets and project conditions shrinks, which raises leverage and forces new debt on worse terms. The issuer's levers are slowing capital spending, drawing the revolving facility, which was drawn for USD 1,200 million in August 2026, and raising equity; resilience appears sufficient only while markets stay open. Observable triggers are slippage in delivery milestones, lower facility availability and wider pricing on new issues.
Basis: calculation C5; calculation C6; calculation C7; calculation C17; 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 29–31; 10-Q_2026-06-30_crwv-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 63

#### Overall credit view

Overall, CoreWeave presents a strong contracted business profile supported by USD 103.7 billion of obligations and secured, contract-backed financing, but credit quality is constrained by leverage of 9.33x reported EBITDA, deeply negative free cash flow and a control environment that is not effective. Cash-flow durability appears adequate for contracted capacity but not fully assessable for capacity under construction. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on continued access to debt and equity markets and on timely delivery of contracted capacity. The primary downside path is a delivery delay or closed funding window that leaves amortisation and maturities to be paid from cash and conditional availability. Further analysis would require executed financing documents, customer contract terms, guidance and third-quarter 2026 results. Top Risk: dependence on continued capital-markets access to fund a build that does not yet generate free cash flow.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C5; calculation C6; calculation C9
