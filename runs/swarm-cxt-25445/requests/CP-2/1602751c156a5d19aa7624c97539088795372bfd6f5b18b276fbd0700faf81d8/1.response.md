## Findings

### Bottom line
Crane NXT’s positive cash generation and stated long-dated facility maturities support debt service, but acquisition-funded leverage and weak acquired-segment profitability leave creditors exposed to integration and refinancing execution.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxt.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21

### Key findings
Reported revenue growth overstates underlying momentum: acquisitions, including Antares Vision, supplied most of the latest reported increase while DTT organic sales declined. DTT’s Q3 margin is below the former CPI-only perimeter, but segment reorganization and acquired-intangible amortization make this comparison non-like-for-like. Integration remains a near-term cash-flow test rather than a proven source of deleveraging.
Basis: EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxt_exhibit991-pressreleasexq3-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxt.htm.jsonl, pages 13–15

Positive free cash flow supports debt service, but does not yet demonstrate a reduction in creditor risk: carrying-value net debt increased and reported LTM leverage is higher than at FY2025. The issuer’s lower adjusted leverage uses a different debt and EBITDA definition, so the two ratios should not be blended.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C2-03; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxt.htm.jsonl, pages 3–5; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxt_exhibit991-pressreleasexq3-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10

Customer concentration is material within SAT: one unnamed customer accounted for a substantial share of sales, leaving that segment exposed to customer order, renewal and product decisions that cannot be monitored through named-customer disclosures.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_cxt.htm.jsonl, page 91; calculation C2-05

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view
The strongest counterweight is cash generation alongside higher reported order backlog. The latest release also gives FY2026 sales-growth and adjusted EBITDA margin guidance, though this remains management’s forward-looking view rather than a realized result. Those indicators could support faster deleveraging if backlog converts, acquired businesses move toward operating profitability and cash is retained for debt reduction. The view would improve with successive periods of organic growth, DTT margin recovery and lower carrying net debt; it would weaken if acquired sales mask continued organic contraction, Antares operating losses persist or distributions and further acquisitions consume cash generation.
Basis: EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxt_exhibit991-pressreleasexq3-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxt.htm.jsonl, pages 13–15; calculation C2-04

### Credit view
Crane NXT combines authentication, payment-verification, product-security and traceability activities, with cash generation that has covered capital spending and cash interest in the latest reported period. The creditor case is constrained by a sharp increase in carrying debt following acquisitions, carrying-value leverage above the issuer-adjusted presentation, SAT customer concentration and the acquired Antares business’s operating loss. A larger backlog and positive free cash flow provide support, but backlog is not cash and the segment reorganization limits annual comparability. The primary downside path is delayed integration or customer/order weakness reducing margins and cash conversion, slowing debt reduction and increasing dependence on future refinancing. Stated loan maturities are several years away, although principal allocations by maturity are incomplete.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxt.htm.jsonl, pages 13–15, 20–21; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxt_exhibit991-pressreleasexq3-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 6, 9

### Operating model and business risk synthesis
Crane NXT sells equipment, software and services used to verify currency and payments, protect physical products, and authenticate or trace products through supply chains. SAT’s banknote and product-security activity provides a current profit offset to DTT; DTT combines the former CPI business with Antares Vision. This addition broadens the product and end-market set but creates integration risk and makes pre- and post-acquisition segment comparisons non-like-for-like. The filings identify a persistent individually significant SAT customer, but do not name it or disclose its contract duration. Those facts constrain conclusions about customer switching costs and renewal resilience.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_cxt.htm.jsonl, pages 7–8, 89–91; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxt.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 14–15

The source evidence supports a limited competitive assessment: proprietary authentication solutions and customer use in banknotes, consumer goods and government tax stamps create differentiated product applications, while the significant SAT customer and lower DTT organic sales demonstrate concentrated demand and exposure to order timing. The documents do not quantify market share, switching costs, supplier dependence or competitor pricing, so those factors do not support a stronger competitive-position conclusion. The annual report identifies macroeconomic, trade-policy, inflation, foreign-exchange and geopolitical risks that could weaken customer demand, raise sourcing costs or make refinancing less favorable. It also reports FY2025 interest-rate and currency sensitivities, but neither those disclosures nor the subsequent interim filing quantify current demand, input-cost or refinancing sensitivities. In credit-translated SWOT terms, backlog and SAT profitability are supports; acquired-business losses, customer concentration, rising debt and non-comparable segment history are constraints; Antares’s life-science and food traceability applications are a potential growth avenue, while delayed integration or order conversion is the principal threat.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_cxt.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 13, 47; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxt.htm.jsonl, pages 13–15, 34; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxt_exhibit991-pressreleasexq3-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 6

### Cost structure, cash conversion and financing
Operating cash flow of USD 184.5 million and free cash flow of USD 139.5 million in 9M 2026 exceed the prior-year period. The improvement supports debt service, but cash flow was not fully available for principal reduction: acquisition payments and dividends competed with deleveraging, and the Antares contribution has not been separately reconciled to post-close EBITDA and cash generation. Reported LTM cash-interest cover remains positive, but carrying-value net debt and leverage increased since year-end. The company’s 2.5x figure uses adjusted EBITDA and principal before financing costs; it is not directly comparable with the 3.03x carrying-value net leverage calculation.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxt.htm.jsonl, pages 3–5, 20–21; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxt_exhibit991-pressreleasexq3-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; calculation C2-03; calculation C2-04

The Credit Agreement facilities have stated maturities several years beyond the latest reporting date. The latest filing does not allocate all current loan principal by maturity year, so the stated dates identify refinancing windows but not a complete maturity wall. A revolving commitment is disclosed, but current letter-of-credit use is not reported; the FY2025 filing reported USD 97.5 million of standby letters, so current undrawn liquidity cannot be inferred from that older balance. This leaves the near-term liquidity conclusion supported by positive cash and operating cash flow, while exact committed availability and facility headroom remain unestablished.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxt.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21; 10-K_2025-12-31_cxt.htm.jsonl, pages 105–109

### Outlook and downside
Over the next year, the main upside is conversion of the reported backlog and realization of management’s stated FY2026 sales-growth and adjusted-margin outlook. Over a longer horizon, Antares may expand traceability exposure in life sciences and food and beverage, but the filings do not establish standalone acquired-business returns. The principal downside is a delayed integration or weaker customer and order demand: DTT’s acquired contribution would fail to cover its lower organic sales, group operating profit would weaken, and working-capital absorption could reduce free cash flow. Lower cash generation would slow debt reduction and increase reliance on refinancing, although stated facility maturities are not immediate. Cost alignment and cash retention are management levers, but the supplied evidence does not quantify savings, fixed-cost flexibility or downside runway.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxt.htm.jsonl, pages 13–15, 21–22; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxt_exhibit991-pressreleasexq3-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 6


The latest management outlook is forward-looking and should not be treated as achieved performance.

### Strengths and weaknesses
The principal strengths are demonstrated post-capex cash generation, a larger backlog, SAT’s current profit contribution and debt facilities with stated maturities several years beyond the latest reporting date. Each provides time or cash-flow capacity to service debt if backlog converts and acquired activities improve.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxt_exhibit991-pressreleasexq3-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxt.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21

The principal weaknesses are the increase in carrying-value net debt, the acquisition-related operating loss and lower DTT margin, persistent SAT customer concentration, and incomplete loan maturity allocation. Together they reduce visibility into the pace of deleveraging, concentration resilience and the amount of debt requiring refinancing at each stated date.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_cxt.htm.jsonl, page 91; 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxt.htm.jsonl, pages 13–15, 20–21


### Overall credit view
Crane NXT’s disclosed product applications, backlog and positive free cash flow support debt service, while acquisition-funded leverage, Antares losses and customer concentration constrain the credit profile. The business cannot be ranked against typical leveraged-credit issuers from these issuer-only materials. Financial capacity weakened on a carrying-value basis since FY2025, although positive cash flow moderates the pressure. Refinancing depends on organic demand, acquired-margin repair, cash retention and execution ahead of stated facility dates; the principal downside is weaker orders or delayed integration reducing margins and cash conversion. A more definitive view needs a current loan-principal schedule by maturity and an integrated cash forecast that tests customer, margin and working-capital shocks.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0); 10-Q_2026-09-30_cxt.htm.jsonl, pages 13–15, 20–21; EX99.1_2026-09-30_cxt_exhibit991-pressreleasexq3-htm.jsonl, page 6
