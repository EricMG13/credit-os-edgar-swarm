## Findings

### Bottom line

KBR is an adequate credit before separation, with moderate leverage and little capital intensity, but creditors' position after the planned January 2027 spin-off of Mission Technology Solutions depends on a debt reduction that the filings do not quantify. Net debt is USD 2,102 million, 2.35x trailing reported EBITDA and 2.07x on the issuer's adjusted measure; no pro forma figure for the planned cash distribution is established. Excluding the add-back introduced in 2026, adjusted leverage is 2.16x against 2.17x at fiscal 2025 year-end, so it has barely moved. The business that stays, after all corporate costs, supplied 43.2% of nine-month Adjusted EBITDA, so on an illustrative basis its leverage would be 4.69x without a distribution.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against caution is that the separation is built to cut debt and the arithmetic is not demanding: holding today's 2.07x needs a distribution of about USD 1,175 million, only 2.00x the spun business's annualised Adjusted EBITDA before any corporate costs it takes over, lenders consented to the reorganisation steps in Amendment No. 14, and KBR states it intends to use the distribution to reduce debt. The business that stays holds a record backlog of USD 5,828 million, up 35.2%, with book-to-bill of 1.3x. Part of the 2026 cash flow fall is a high 2025 comparison, because without the USD 128 million change order the earlier nine months would be USD 378 million against USD 371 million adjusted in 2026. Nine-month capital spending is 0.61% of revenue, and management reaffirmed fiscal 2026 guidance at each quarterly release, with adjusted operating cash flow of USD 560 million to USD 600 million implying USD 189 million to USD 229 million in the fourth quarter.

### Limitations

- The illustrations of the business that stays use annualised nine-month Adjusted EBITDA, keep all corporate costs with it and leave all existing debt with it, so they size the distribution needed but cannot say where debt will sit; a different allocation in the registration statement would change both the 4.69x and the USD 1,175 million.
- Covenant headroom is not calculable because the covenant definitions and ratios are not printed, so the liquidity conclusion rests on facility availability and the issuer's statement of compliance, and a tighter definition would lower it.
- The LNG project is not named and how much estimate-change benefit remains is not stated, so the earnings-quality conclusion and the 2.80x sensitivity are directional; the benefit could end sooner or later than assumed.
- Pricing power cannot be tested because rate cards, win rates and contract-level margins are not printed, so the view that margins rest on cost-reimbursable terms and mix rather than on pricing is inferred from contract mix alone.
- No ratings, market prices or peer data are supplied, so access to capital markets and competitive standing are judged from the issuer's filings and fair-value notes, and the refinancing conclusion would change with a price for the notes or a rating action.
- The outlook rests on guidance for the whole group before separation, which management says it will replace with separate outlooks, so the fourth-quarter ranges implied by guidance test the current perimeter only.

### Company description and business model

KBR, Inc. is a Delaware corporation headquartered in Houston that sells engineering, technology and logistics services through two segments. Mission Technology Solutions serves defence, intelligence, space and aviation programmes, mainly for U.S., U.K. and Australian agencies, and includes the Aspire Defence concession, a 35-year U.K. contract whose publicly held bonds are non-recourse to KBR. Sustainable Technology Solutions licenses more than 85 proprietary process technologies in ammonia, chemicals, refining and circular-economy processes and adds engineering, advisory and digital services for commercial and international government clients. Revenue was USD 7,786 million in fiscal 2025, up 1.0% after 10.8% in fiscal 2024, the year of the LinQuest acquisition, and USD 5,935 million in the nine months to 2026-10-02, up 0.6%.

For creditors, cost-reimbursable government work gives multi-year, cost-protected cash flows, with 36% of USD 13.6 billion of remaining performance obligations due within a year and 41% in years two to five, while technology licensing and engineering are project-based and lumpier. The model is capital-light and working-capital heavy: receivables were USD 1,300 million against customer advances of USD 429 million, so liquidity depends on collections and advances more than on capital spending. Debt service capacity is good at the current perimeter, and refinancing confidence depends on the group staying whole through the separation.

### Ownership and group structure

KBR is a widely held public company: 52 shareholders of record are reported, no controlling holder or sponsor is named in the filings read, and board composition, beneficial ownership and executive pay sit in a proxy statement that is not supplied.
