## Findings

### Bottom line
EverCommerce's single secured term loan is serviceable but less lightly levered than the issuer's adjusted figures suggest: gross debt is 4.22x last-twelve-month reported EBITDA at June 30, 2026, against 2.96x on the adjusted measure, and the balloon is not due until 2031.
Basis: calculation C04; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 18–21

Net debt is 3.14x reported EBITDA and reported EBITDA covers cash interest 3.73x, so servicing is comfortable on cash figures the issuer itself reports. The adjusted ratios rest on USD 52.28 million of add-backs, mostly stock compensation and one-off costs, and adjusted EBITDA has slipped below the fiscal 2025 level as management guides to the low end of its range. The main contrary point is an undrawn revolver and no balloon before 2031, which limits refinancing pressure. The view would worsen if adjusted EBITDA keeps falling or if buybacks again exceed free cash flow.
Basis: calculation C03; calculation C04; calculation C07; calculation C10; EX-99.1_2026-08-05_evcmq226earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 54

### Financial interpretation

Leverage is moderate and its direction is flat to slightly worse. Gross debt at carrying value fell by only USD 2.449 million between December 31, 2025 and June 30, 2026 while last-twelve-month reported EBITDA fell below the fiscal 2025 figure, because sales and marketing and product development spending rose by more than the whole increase in revenue in the first half of 2026. For creditors this means deleveraging is coming from debt paydown that is too small to matter, not from earnings growth, so the reported ratio has edged up from 4.14x to 4.22x even though the issuer's adjusted ratio looks lower.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C03; calculation C05; calculation C13; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 6

The gap between reported and adjusted EBITDA is entirely three issuer add-backs. Stock-based compensation recurs in every period and is a real economic cost, because the company issues shares to staff and spends cash on repurchases, which adjusted EBITDA ignores. The transaction-related and other non-recurring line has been present in every year shown, which means its non-recurring character is not established, and the issuer gives no component split. A creditor should therefore anchor on the reported ratio and treat the adjusted ratio as an upper bound on earnings quality, not as a covenant calculation.
Basis: calculation C07; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 131; EX-99.1_2026-08-05_evcmq226earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 11

The strongest offset is cash generation and a small, simple capital structure. Free cash flow after cash interest and capital spending was positive in every period shown, and a single term loan with scheduled amortization of USD 5.500 million a year leaves no refinancing event before the 2031 balloon. The offset is weakened by capital allocation: fiscal 2025 repurchases exceeded free cash flow, and cash fell even with disposal proceeds. First-half 2026 repurchases were covered by free cash flow, so the observable test is whether that holds as earnings soften. Cash flow figures are consolidated and still include the sold marketing business, so they overstate continuing cash generation to an extent the filings do not quantify.
Basis: calculation C10; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 160; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 18–21

Liquidity is adequate and not the near-term constraint. Cash at June 30, 2026 plus the undrawn revolver, before letters of credit, is the only liquidity the filings establish; USD 30.0 million of the commitments expired on July 6, 2026, a dated later event that is not blended into the period figure. The revolver carries a springing first lien leverage test that applies only if more than 35% of commitments are drawn, nothing is drawn, and the reported ratio is not stated, so covenant headroom cannot be calculated from these tables. Interest rate protection is a second watch item: swaps cover most of the term loan only until October 31, 2027, after which the whole loan floats, while the issuer's own sensitivity today is small.
Basis: calculation C09; calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 51–52; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 156

Customer deposits and deferred revenue are a non-debt funding source and not ordinary payables. Together they stand at about USD 35.152 million and have grown slowly from December 2024, so customers prepay for services the company must still deliver. A drop in billings would remove this float before revenue falls, which makes these two lines an early indicator to monitor alongside net revenue retention.
Basis: calculation C12; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 158

A supported downside sequence starts with weaker retention: the annualized net revenue retention rate was about 94% in the second quarter of 2026 against about 97% a year earlier, and pro forma revenue growth in that quarter was 2.0%. Slower growth against rising sales and product spending would first compress EBITDA, which raises leverage on a debt balance that barely amortizes; cash and the undrawn revolver would then absorb any shortfall, with repurchases the discretionary outflow. Observable triggers are net revenue retention, the guidance range for adjusted EBITDA, buybacks against free cash flow and any revolver draw. No numerical stress or runway is established here.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 33; EX-99.1_2026-08-05_evcmq226earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; calculation C13; calculation C10
