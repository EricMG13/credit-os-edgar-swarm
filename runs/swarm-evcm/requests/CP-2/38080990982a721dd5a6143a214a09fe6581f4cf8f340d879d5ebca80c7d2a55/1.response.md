Scope is the integrated credit view built on the annual report, three quarterly reports and three earnings releases and on the accepted source readiness, financial foundation and business and governance analyses. ## Findings

### Bottom line
EverCommerce is an adequate credit, not a strong one: recurring, widely spread revenue and a single term loan not due until 2031 support debt service, while slowing growth, add-back-heavy earnings and sponsor control leave little room for error. Gross debt is 4.22x reported EBITDA and 2.96x the issuer's adjusted measure, and reported EBITDA covers cash interest 3.73x, so servicing is comfortable today.
Basis: calculation C01; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A)

The main downside path is weaker customer retention and spending that grows faster than revenue eroding earnings while share repurchases continue. Full-year guidance also needs a fourth quarter the company has not yet reported, which tests management's forecasting more than debt service.
Basis: calculation C02; calculation C04; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 33; EX-99.1_2026-08-05_evcmq226earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3

### Key findings

Revenue durability is average: the base is broad and mostly recurring, but customers can leave quickly. About 93% of customers each contributed less than USD 2 thousand of revenue in fiscal 2025 and about 96% of revenue was recurring or re-occurring, so no single customer failure moves cash flow, yet most contracts are open-ended and can be ended on 30 days' notice. Annualized net revenue retention slipped to about 94% in the second quarter of 2026 from about 97% a year earlier, so the installed base is shrinking before new sales and price increases, and growth now depends on selling more and charging more.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 53; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 120; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 33; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Margins hold at the gross line but erode below it. Gross margin before depreciation and amortization was 78.23% in the first half of 2026 against 77.75% a year earlier, yet sales and marketing together with product development grew 10.67% against revenue growth of 3.17%, and Adjusted EBITDA fell 5.22% to USD 85.21 million. Reported EBITDA is 20.66% of last-twelve-month revenue and Adjusted EBITDA 29.40%; the gap is USD 52.28 million of add-backs, 29.73% of the adjusted figure, so the adjusted ratios flatter the cushion.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C02; calculation C03; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 45–46; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Cash flow covers mandatory debt service many times but not shareholder returns on top of it. Free cash flow after cash interest, taxes and capital spending was USD 71.69 million over the last twelve months against scheduled amortization of USD 5.50 million, which is 13.03x cover, but cash share repurchases of USD 82.11 million exceeded free cash flow after amortization by USD 15.92 million and were 3.32x the stock compensation expense. Only USD 19.2 million of the authorization remains, though the board has raised it five times, so the buyback is a discretionary draw on liquidity that the board, under sponsor control, can renew.
Basis: calculation C05; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 160; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 54; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Leverage is moderate but not falling, and the term loan is one long bullet. Reported leverage was 4.22x at June 30, 2026 against 4.14x at the end of 2025 and 4.94x at the end of 2024, because last-twelve-month EBITDA slipped while debt fell by only USD 2.45 million in the half. About 95.28% of the principal falls due in July 2031, so repayment rests on refinancing a business whose growth is slowing, helped by lenders having agreed three amendments in 2024 and 2025 that cut margins and pushed maturities out.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C06; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 18–21; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 186–187

Liquidity is adequate and refinancing is a 2030 to 2031 question, not a near-term one. Cash of USD 133.50 million plus the undrawn revolver gives USD 258.50 million after USD 30.0 million of commitments expired on July 6, 2026, before any letters of credit, which the filings cap at a USD 20.0 million sub-limit. The only financial test described is a first lien leverage ratio of 7.50x on the revolver, applied only if more than 35% is drawn, so the filings describe no financial maintenance test for the term loan.
Basis: calculation C06; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 51–52; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 54; Source readiness (CP-0); Financial foundation (CP-1)

Interest-rate protection is partial and temporary. Swaps fix the floating rate on USD 425.0 million, 81.1% of the principal, only until October 31, 2027; each 100 bp rise costs about USD 1.0 million a year now and about USD 5.16 million a year once the swaps expire, which would lower reported cash interest cover from 3.73x to 3.23x on last-twelve-month figures.

Full-year guidance looks back-end loaded, which questions management's forecasting more than the debt. Governance is the weakest dimension, and the weakness is about information and control rather than documented creditor harm. Two sponsors hold about 85.7% of the shares and must consent to a change of control, to deals above USD 500.0 million and to chief executive changes while together at 30% or more; the revenue-accounting control weakness has been reported in three annual reports and disclosure controls were not effective at June 30, 2026; and the latest release refers to a new chief executive without detail. No dividend has ever been paid and no sponsor extraction is documented, but the credit agreement that would show what the sponsors may do with cash was not supplied.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is conduct and cash. The 2026 quarterly ranges were met or beaten: first-quarter Adjusted EBITDA of USD 40.67 million sat inside a range of USD 39.0 million to USD 41.0 million and second-quarter USD 44.54 million was above a range of USD 41.0 million to USD 43.0 million. First-half repurchases of USD 28.57 million were covered by free cash flow of USD 36.08 million, free cash flow has been positive in every period reported, no dividend has ever been paid, and adjusted leverage of 2.96x leaves room for earnings to fall before servicing is stressed. Lenders also agreed three amendments that lowered margins and extended maturities, which shows market access.
Basis: calculation C05; calculation C09; EX-99.1_2026-03-12_evcm4q25earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 3; EX-99.1_2026-05-07_evcmq126earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 2; EX-99.1_2026-08-05_evcmq226earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 186–187; Financial foundation (CP-1)

This view would win if retention stabilizes near 97%, discretionary spending growth fades toward revenue growth so that EBITDA stops falling, and the fourth quarter delivers the step-up the guidance requires; the credit would then rate better than adequate. It would worsen if third-quarter Adjusted EBITDA falls below USD 44.0 million, if the revolver is drawn, if the repurchase authorization is raised again while repurchases exceed free cash flow, if the sponsors sell below 30% or the chief executive change proves disorderly, or if the control weakness is still unremediated in the next annual report.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 33; EX-99.1_2026-08-05_evcmq226earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 204–206; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Limitations

- No creditor-protection document was supplied, so the conclusion that servicing is comfortable rests on cash flow and not on contractual rights: what the term lenders can enforce, how much the sponsors may distribute and how large any letters of credit are remain unknown, the covenant's own ratio and EBITDA definition are not stated so the leverage ratios here are not covenant calculations, and the liquidity figure stays gross of letters of credit.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 51–52; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 208–209
- No peer or sector benchmark was supplied, so "adequate" and "moderate" compare the issuer with its own history and with the 7.50x revolver test, not with other leveraged software issuers, and the business profile against typical leveraged-credit issuers in the sector is not established.
Basis: Source readiness (CP-0); 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 52
- The back-end loading finding is the analyst's arithmetic on the issuer's published ranges; the issuer gives no quantified bridge to the fourth quarter beyond a May statement that growth should accelerate, so an unannounced price increase, acquisition or timing item could make the ranges reachable and the finding would then fall away.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-08-05_evcmq226earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 3; EX-99.1_2026-05-07_evcmq126earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation C02
- The filings do not split revenue growth between price and volume or give retention by customer group beyond one rate, so how much of the weaker retention is controllable cannot be established and the cost-flexibility estimate carries that uncertainty.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Dispersed, mostly recurring revenue is the first strength. About 93% of customers each contributed under USD 2 thousand in fiscal 2025 and about 96% of revenue was recurring or re-occurring, so a customer loss is small and renewals repeat; the consequence for creditors is steadier cash flow at moderate leverage, tempered by the issuer's unsized warning of key customers.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 53; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 120

A stable gross margin and some pricing power are the second strength. Gross margin was 78.23% in the first half of 2026 and business management revenue rose USD 9.5 million mainly on higher revenue per customer, so price and mix are offsetting weaker retention for now; if retention keeps slipping, price increases become the main lever and carry churn risk.
Basis: calculation C03; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 45

A simple, long-dated capital structure is the third strength. One term loan with scheduled amortization of USD 5.50 million a year and an undrawn revolver to July 2030 leave no maturity wall before 2030, so refinancing depends on earnings in 2029 and 2030, not on the next two years.
Basis: calculation C06; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 18–21

Free cash flow that covers mandatory debt service is the fourth strength. Free cash flow after cash interest was USD 71.69 million over the last twelve months, 13.03x scheduled amortization, and capital spending including capitalized software was 5.41% of fiscal 2025 revenue, so cash generation does not depend on heavy reinvestment.
Basis: calculation C05; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 160

Liquidity with no revolver draw is the fifth strength. Cash of USD 133.50 million and USD 125.0 million of undrawn revolver commitments give USD 258.50 million, and customer deposits and deferred revenue of USD 35.15 million are a non-debt source of funding that would shrink if billings fell; the revolver commitment expired in part on July 6, 2026 and is shown after that date.
Basis: calculation C06; calculation C10; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 20

Governance and reporting reliability are the first weakness. An unremediated revenue-accounting control weakness in three annual reports, controls that were not effective at June 30, 2026 and an unexplained chief executive transition reduce confidence in the figures creditors rely on and in management continuity, while sponsor consent rights let two holders steer capital policy.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 204–206; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 56; EX-99.1_2026-08-05_evcmq226earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 95–98

Earnings quality is the second weakness. Add-backs of USD 52.28 million are 29.73% of Adjusted EBITDA and include stock compensation of USD 24.75 million and transaction costs that have appeared every year, so reported leverage of 4.22x is the safer anchor and the 2.96x adjusted figure an upper bound on the cushion.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C03; Financial foundation (CP-1)

Slipping retention and rising spending are the third weakness. Retention of about 94% and spending growth of 10.67% against revenue growth of 3.17% mean EBITDA is falling before any economic shock, leaving less room to absorb one.
Basis: calculation C03; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 45–46

Shareholder returns above free cash flow are the fourth weakness. Repurchases of USD 82.11 million over the last twelve months exceed free cash flow after amortization by USD 15.92 million, and the board, under sponsor control, can renew the authorization, so cash that could repay debt returns to equity instead.
Basis: calculation C05; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 54; Business & governance (CP-1A)

A bullet maturity with a floating rate is the fifth weakness. 95.28% of principal is due in July 2031 and the swaps that fix 81.1% of the loan end in October 2027, so both repayment and interest cost depend on conditions in 2027 to 2031 that no document fixes.
Basis: calculation C06; calculation C07; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 18–21

### Financial profile

The nine-dimension view is as follows. Scale and market position is average: last-twelve-month revenue of USD 598.10 million in fragmented markets with low barriers to entry. Competitive advantage is average: integrated vertical workflows raise switching costs, but most contracts end on 30 days' notice and retention is slipping. Business diversification is strong: customers are dispersed across home, health and wellness services and 88.79% of first-half 2026 revenue is from the United States, which limits currency exposure. Cost and capex flexibility is average: sales, marketing and product development are 36.53% of first-half revenue and can be slowed, but rent, salaries and debt service are fixed or semi-fixed. Margin stability is average: gross margin is steady and the EBITDA margin is falling. Free cash flow stability is average: positive in every period but on a perimeter that still includes the sold business. Ability to refinance is average: three amendments show access, but 95.28% of the principal is one 2031 bullet in a sponsor-controlled company. Liquidity is strong on stated figures at USD 258.50 million against USD 33.11 million of last-twelve-month cash interest, before letters of credit. Financial policy and governance is weak: concentrated control, a persistent control weakness and buybacks above free cash flow after amortization, offset by no dividend ever paid.

### Business model and ownership

EverCommerce sells vertically tailored software to service small and medium businesses in home, health and wellness services, bundling business management, billing and payments and customer-experience tools in one segment. Revenue visibility comes from subscription and transaction fees, 96.27% of fiscal 2025 revenue, and the cash-generation mechanism is simple: recurring fees on a dispersed base funded from a low-capital-intensity model. Ownership is concentrated in two sponsors that hold about 85.7% of the shares, so the credit depends on their financial policy rather than on public-market discipline; the filings name no parent, and the creditors' claim sits on the operating group through subsidiary guarantees and pledged equity under the credit facilities.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9–11; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 95; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 51; calculation C08; Business & governance (CP-1A)

The roll-up history matters for creditors only through cash and earnings. The company has bought 54 businesses since inception, paid about USD 36.0 million in cash for ZyraTalk in September 2025 with no new debt, and sold its marketing technology business for about USD 45.0 million in cash in October 2025, so acquisitions are now funded from cash and the disposal shrank revenue; ZyraTalk added USD 1.8 million of first-half revenue against USD 5.3 million of operating expense, an early drag on EBITDA.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 12–13; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 45–47; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Revenue, cost and cash conversion

Revenue is predictable and recurring but not contractually locked. Business management software drove a USD 9.5 million rise in first-half subscription and transaction revenue, mainly through higher revenue per customer, billing and payment solutions added USD 0.8 million on volume and a slightly higher take rate, and rebates from group purchasing fell USD 1.4 million. Payments are about 20% of revenue and depend on card networks and processors, so a pricing-led increase combined with falling retention makes revenue more sensitive to churn and to small-business spending than the recurring label suggests.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 45; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 35–38; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 53

Margins can be defended against volume pressure only if discretionary spending is slowed. Cost of revenues is steady, but sales and marketing rose 12.49% and product development 7.94% in the first half, and the increase over the prior-year spending ratio is worth about USD 7.42 million per half-year, or 8.44% of last-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA when annualized. That is a real management lever, but pulling it trades near-term EBITDA for slower growth in a business where retention is already weakening.
Basis: calculation C03; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 45–46

Cash conversion is good before shareholder returns. Free cash flow before cash interest was 84.82% of last-twelve-month reported EBITDA and 59.60% of Adjusted EBITDA; the gap to the adjusted figure reflects capital spending, taxes and the transaction-related costs that the issuer adds back but pays in cash, which makes the adjusted measure a poor guide to cash. The weakness is the use of that cash, since capitalized software has risen faster than expense and buybacks absorb more than the cash left after amortization.
Basis: calculation C05; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 160; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 8

### Claims outside the debt figure

The claims outside the debt figure are small against cash generation. Operating lease liabilities of USD 12.01 million, USD 13.24 million undiscounted with a weighted remaining term of 3.89 years, sit outside debt and their cost is already inside EBITDA; non-cancelable purchase obligations for software services, mail, fulfilment and hosting total USD 31.97 million to 2029; and up to USD 6.5 million of ZyraTalk contingent consideration may be paid over three years. Together the leases and purchase obligations are USD 43.98 million, less than one year of free cash flow. The company has no finance leases and the sources read show no pensions, retiree medical, factoring or supply-chain finance, and the Delaware class action carries no accrual because a loss is not probable or estimable.

### Competitive and external forces

Competitive rivalry is high and caps pricing. The issuer competes with manual processes, vertical specialists and horizontal vendors including Salesforce, Intuit and Square, and describes barriers to entry as generally low, so spending to defend retention is structural and margin gains are hard to hold. New entrants and substitutes work through the same mechanism: the filings expect more entrants and name partners that may become competitors, and the analyst view is that AI-native tools are a further substitute the filings do not size, so each raises the sales and product spending needed to keep revenue per customer rising. Buyer power is individually weak but collectively strong, because small customers can leave on 30 days' notice and are price-sensitive in a downturn. Supplier power is moderate: card networks and payment processors sit between the company and about 20% of its revenue and can fine or terminate it for non-compliance.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 33; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 34–35; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 53

Economic and regulatory factors are material to credit; currency and politics are not. A weaker economy could close small businesses at higher rates, shrink card volumes and slow spending on software, and the company says fixed and semi-fixed costs including debt service cannot be cut quickly; interest-rate rises matter through the floating-rate term loan. Payment-network rules, data-privacy law and AI regulation raise compliance cost but are not quantified in the filings, and international revenue is 11.21% of the first half, so currency is minor.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 58; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 24–25; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 35; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 84; calculation C08

The credit-translated SWOT is as follows. Strengths are dispersed recurring revenue, a stable gross margin, a long simple capital structure and positive free cash flow. Weaknesses are slipping retention, add-back-heavy earnings, a persistent control weakness and repurchases above free cash flow after amortization. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are payments cross-selling and AI products raising revenue per customer, remediation of the control weakness and holding repurchases inside free cash flow. Threats that could weaken it are a small-business downturn, price competition from low-barrier entrants, rising rates after the swaps expire and a sponsor sell-down or disorderly chief executive change.

### Outlook

Over 12 to 24 months the issuer's own guidance and the swap expiry set the picture. Management expects results toward the lower end of ranges that were unchanged since March, quarterly results have so far met or beaten their ranges, and no maturity falls due beyond USD 5.50 million of amortization a year; headwinds are retention near 94%, spending that outruns revenue, the dilutive ZyraTalk purchase and the October 2027 swap expiry. A hearing on the sponsors' chief executive approval right was scheduled for October 19, 2026 and its outcome is not in the sources.
Basis: calculation C02; calculation C09; EX-99.1_2026-08-05_evcmq226earningsrelease.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 20–21

Over three to five years the question is refinancing. The revolver matures in July 2030 and the term loan in July 2031, so the 2029 and 2030 results decide the terms of a refinancing of about USD 523.88 million of principal; sector tailwinds are small-business demand for vertical software and digital payments, and sector headwinds are small-business failures, competitive entry and consolidation of customers, which can bring fee pressure.

### Downside scenario

The realistic downside begins with the customer base, not a lender event. A soft economy closes small businesses and cuts card volumes while retention, already near 94%, slips further and contracts end on 30 days' notice, so revenue growth stalls; because spending is rising 10.67% and rent, salaries and debt service are semi-fixed, EBITDA falls faster than revenue. Each USD 10.0 million of lost last-twelve-month reported EBITDA lifts gross leverage from 4.22x to 4.59x and lowers reported cash interest cover from 3.73x to 3.43x, and the pressure compounds if floating-rate cost rises after October 2027.
Basis: calculation C01; calculation C03; calculation C07; calculation C11; 10-K_2025-12-31_evcm-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 58

Liquidity would absorb the shock before covenants or maturities matter. With free cash flow of USD 71.69 million and cash of USD 133.50 million, only a revolver draw above 35% of commitments, which is USD 43.75 million, would start the first lien leverage test of 7.50x; with zero operating cash flow, cash alone would meet cash interest and amortization for 3.46 years and cash plus the full revolver, if it stayed available, for 6.70 years. The levers are suspending buybacks of USD 82.11 million a year, slowing spending by about USD 7.42 million per half-year and capitalized software of USD 15.58 million per half-year, and resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress within two years; the exposure is the 2030 to 2031 refinancing if EBITDA is lower by then.
Basis: calculation C05; calculation C06; calculation C11; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-06-30_evcm-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 51–52

### Overall credit view

Overall, EverCommerce presents an adequate business profile supported by dispersed recurring revenue and a stable gross margin, but credit quality is constrained by slowing growth, add-back-heavy earnings and sponsor-controlled governance with a weak reporting record. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow covers scheduled amortization 13.03x, although it does not cover buybacks on top. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on EBITDA stabilizing before the revolver matures in July 2030 and the term loan in July 2031, and on lenders' continued appetite. The primary downside path is weaker retention and spending that outruns revenue pressing EBITDA and leverage while repurchases continue. The profile against typical leveraged-credit issuers in the sector is not established because no peer set was supplied. Further analysis would require the credit agreement, the proxy statement, peer benchmarks and the filings for the quarter ended September 30, 2026. Top risk: retention-led earnings erosion ahead of a 2031 bullet refinancing.
