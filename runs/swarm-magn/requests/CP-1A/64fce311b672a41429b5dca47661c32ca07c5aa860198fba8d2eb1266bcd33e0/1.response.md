## Findings

### Bottom line

Magnera's creditor risk is a debt-funded separation and weak controls, not a sponsor: no controlling holder is disclosed, but USD 1,111 million went to the former parent at closing and internal controls are not effective.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 43; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 20

The business is diversified by region and end market but concentrated by customer, with the top ten customers about 42% of net sales, and its thin segment margins depend on passing resin and pulp costs through to customers with a lag; the latest year shows self-help gains but also USD 61 million of restructuring cost in nine months.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 7

### Key findings

- Ownership and control are undisclosed rather than benign. The 10-K says the pre-merger Treasure shareholders received 90% of the combined company and the Glatfelter shareholders kept 10%, but names no holder, board seat, consent right or fee, because those items sit in a proxy statement that was not supplied. There is no sponsor in the run settings, so the creditor question is whether any holder can steer financial policy, and the documents read cannot answer it.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 44; Source readiness (CP-0)
- The capital structure was built for the separation, not for the operating business. The new term loan and the 7.25% secured notes raised USD 1,556 million of cash proceeds, USD 1,111 million of which went to the former parent as a cash distribution (71.40% of the proceeds), and the filing says the remainder helped retire part of Glatfelter's debt. Creditors therefore carry interest on borrowing whose main use was a payment to a holder that no longer controls the company.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 34; calculation C02
- Customer concentration and the lag in cost pass-through are the main business risks. The top customer is about 14% and the top ten customers about 42% of net sales, many customers have no long-term arrangement, and resin and pulp costs are passed on only over time. In fiscal 2025 organic volume fell by 2% and the price-cost spread cost the Americas segment USD 14 million of Adjusted EBITDA, so a raw-material spike or a customer switch hits earnings before prices catch up.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 16; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 27
- Margins are improving through self-help that costs cash. Restructuring and other activities charges were USD 89 million in fiscal 2025 and USD 61 million in the first nine months of fiscal 2026, equal to 25.14% and 21.63% of Adjusted EBITDA, while the third-quarter price-cost spread added USD 11 million. The synergies the issuer cites are management estimates, so creditors should treat them as unrealised until cash flow shows them.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 7; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_ef20079553_ex99-1.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation C05
- Governance is the clearest creditor-adverse area. Internal control over financial reporting was not effective at the fiscal 2025 year end, the auditor gave an adverse opinion on it, disclosure controls were still not effective at June 27, 2026, and legacy U.S. systems run under a transition services agreement with the former parent. Management reports no material error, so the effect is lower confidence in the numbers and slower detection of problems, not a known misstatement.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 10; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 22; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 43; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 20
- Capital allocation since closing has favoured creditors, but the stated appetite for acquisitions could reverse that. The cash flow statements show debt repayments of USD 65 million in nine months and no dividend or buyback line, while the third-quarter report says the company intends to pursue further acquisitions. A debt-funded purchase would raise leverage that is only slowly falling.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 14
- Sponsor and governance risk is Medium. Documented behaviour is mixed, the control environment is weak and ownership is not disclosed, but there is no recorded dividend recapitalisation, creditor-adverse amendment or exchange since closing.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 34; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 43

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is that behaviour since closing has been creditor-friendly: the company repaid USD 65 million of debt in nine months, shows no distribution after the closing payment, described the third quarter as a record with Adjusted EBITDA of USD 99 million and kept its free cash flow outlook. The auditor's opinion on the financial statements themselves was unqualified and the largest customer is only about 14% of sales. The view would turn more positive if remediation removed the control weaknesses and the proxy statement showed dispersed holders with no fee arrangements. It would turn more negative if a holder with consent rights emerged, if an acquisition were funded with debt, or if debt repayment stopped while restructuring spend continued.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 4; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_ef20079553_ex99-1.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 27

### Limitations

- The proxy statement was not supplied, so the finding that no controlling holder is disclosed could change if a holder with board or consent rights exists; sponsor-style risks would then apply.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 44; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 45
- No indenture or credit agreement was supplied, so restricted-payment, investment and acquisition capacity is not assessed and the capital-allocation view rests on behaviour alone; a permissive document would weaken the favourable reading.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, pages 46–47
- The evidence ends with the June 2026 quarter, so the repayment trend, which slowed to USD 2 million in the third quarter, may have moved further; a stop in repayment would move the capital-allocation finding from favourable to mixed.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-Q_2026-03-28_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 4
- Synergy and cost-saving figures are management estimates and customer names and contract terms are not disclosed, so the concentration and self-help findings are stated as shares and charges, not as durable earnings.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-08-06_ef20079553_ex99-1.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 27

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

On November 4, 2024 Treasure Holdco, Inc., a wholly owned subsidiary of Berry Global Group, Inc., merged with Glatfelter Corporation, which was renamed Magnera Corporation. Treasure shareholders received 90% of the combined company and Glatfelter shareholders kept 10%, and Treasure was the accounting acquirer, so the fiscal 2024 figures are carve-out statements of the business Berry contributed. Total consideration was the USD 74 million fair value of Glatfelter common stock, and Glatfelter debt with a fair value of USD 869 million was assumed.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 26; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 32

To fund the closing the company put in place a USD 785 million term loan due 2031, a USD 800 million issue of 7.25% First Priority Senior Secured Notes due 2031 and a USD 350 million asset-based revolver due 2029, and it kept Glatfelter's 4.75% notes due 2029. The statement of cash flows shows proceeds from long-term borrowings of USD 1,556 million, a cash distribution to Berry of USD 1,111 million and repayments of USD 484 million; the filing says the term loan and 7.25% note proceeds retired part of Glatfelter's debt and funded the distribution. The face amounts of USD 785 million and USD 800 million add to USD 1,585 million, USD 29 million more than the cash proceeds shown, and no source explains the difference.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 32; 10-K_2025-09-27_form10k.htm.jsonl, page 34; calculation C01

The fiscal 2026 cash flow statements show no proceeds from borrowings. Outstanding principal has been reduced by prepayments, to USD 706 million on the term loan and USD 760 million on the 7.25% notes at June 27, 2026, while the revolver is undrawn. No acquisition, sponsor transaction or refinancing after the merger is described, and the third-quarter report says only that the company intends to pursue acquisition targets.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-Q_2026-06-27_form10q.htm.jsonl, page 13
