## Findings

### Bottom line

Thor is a listed company with no sponsor or controlling shareholder identified in the filings supplied, so creditors face the RV cycle and shareholder payouts rather than sponsor extraction, though ownership and board detail sits in a proxy statement not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 85; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 86

Payouts are the main governance signal: dividends and share repurchases of USD 223,891 thousand in fiscal 2026 were 132.61% of operating cash flow after capital spending, against 34.90% a year earlier, while the quarterly dividend was still raised to $0.52. Dealer concentration is the main business risk, with one dealer at 13.0% of net sales and standby repurchase obligations of USD 3,315,731 thousand on dealer inventory financing.

### Key findings

No sponsor or controlling shareholder is identified, which removes the extraction and exit-pressure risks of a sponsor-owned credit, but the evidence is the annual report's silence rather than a disclosure of holders. The common stock trades on the New York Stock Exchange, the annual report counts only a small number of holders of record, and ownership, directors and related-party dealings are incorporated from a proxy statement that is not supplied, so a holder of 10% or more cannot be ruled out. Certain business combinations that 75% of the directors have not approved need the vote of 75% of the shares, which slows any change of control.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 44; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 85; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 86

Scale and diversification support the business risk, but the earnings base is cyclical and weakening in North America. Thor is the largest recreational vehicle maker in North America by units and revenue, and fiscal 2026 net sales split 33.1% North American towable, 25.6% North American motorized and 34.3% European, with a supply business at 10.2% before eliminations. North American towable net sales fell 16.1% and its pre-tax income fell 40.4%, while Europe grew 9.0%, or 3.1% at constant currency, and North American motorized 12.8%, so group net sales rose only 0.3% and pre-tax income fell 19.4% as gross margin narrowed from 14.0% to 12.6%.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 6; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 65; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 66

Dealer concentration and repurchase obligations are the largest structural exposure outside debt. One dealer, FreedomRoads, accounted for 13.0% of net sales in fiscal 2026 and 14.0% a year earlier, and for 13.0% of trade receivables, while standby repurchase obligations on dealer inventory financing were USD 3,315,731 thousand, 3.79x total contractual debt principal, and two floor-plan lenders held about 50% of the floored dollars for Thor products. Losses on settled repurchases were not material in fiscal 2026 and fiscal 2025 and USD 7,107 thousand in fiscal 2024, so the obligation is contingent, but a floor-plan default by a large dealer in a downturn would become a cash call that the filings do not size by dealer.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 30; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 47; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 76; calculation A07

Financial policy is shareholder-oriented but has so far been funded without adding debt. The dividend rose to $0.52 a quarter in October 2025, repurchases rose to USD 115,126 thousand in fiscal 2026 from USD 52,647 thousand, and USD 264,174 thousand of the USD 400,000 thousand authorization remains until July 31, 2027, yet term-loan repayments of USD 205,000 thousand in fiscal 2025 and USD 56,264 thousand in fiscal 2026 reduced debt. Dividends are conditioned on a minimum level of adjusted excess cash availability and a fixed charge coverage test, which the filings do not quantify.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 75; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 128; calculation A09

Fiscal 2026 was the first of the three years shown in which payouts exceeded free cash flow. Dividends and repurchases of USD 223,891 thousand compared with USD 168,836 thousand of operating cash flow after capital spending, 132.61% against 34.90% in fiscal 2025 and 42.01% in fiscal 2024, so cash fell by USD 104,608 thousand as operating cash flow dropped to USD 321,223 thousand from USD 577,923 thousand. Of the USD 481,988 thousand of cash at year end, USD 263,889 thousand or 54.75% sat in Europe, so cash within easy reach of the United States parent is smaller than the headline.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 73; calculation A01; calculation A02; calculation A03; calculation A04; calculation A14

Acquisition behaviour is small and not debt-funded, but goodwill is large relative to equity. Business acquisitions cost USD 9,555 thousand net of cash in fiscal 2026, nothing in fiscal 2025 and USD 7,314 thousand in fiscal 2024, and the July 1, 2026 step to full ownership of the Roadpass Digital holding company carried USD 93,987 thousand of net fair value including the previously held interest, with immaterial consideration. Goodwill of USD 1,916,330 thousand plus amortizable intangibles of USD 684,494 thousand is 37.39% of total assets and 61.05% of equity, and about 80% of the goodwill sits in reporting units whose fair value exceeds carrying value by less than 25%, with the valuation of the USD 464 million allocated to Airxcel a critical audit matter. A modest fall in forecasts could therefore force an impairment that reduces equity and earnings without a cash cost, although whether any covenant definition adds back impairments is not established.

Restructuring charges rose to USD 58,889 thousand in fiscal 2026 from USD 42,123 thousand across North American towable, motorized, Europe and corporate, and gains on fixed asset sales of USD 36,942 thousand in North American towable partly offset them. Medium reflects documented payouts above free cash flow in one year and missing capacity evidence, set against no documented extraction, no described liability management and steady debt reduction.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against the benign view is cash use. In fiscal 2026 payouts exceeded operating cash flow after capital spending by USD 55,055 thousand, cash fell by USD 104,608 thousand, and USD 264,174 thousand of repurchase authority remains while North American towable earnings are falling. If repurchases continued at that pace with operating cash flow near USD 321,223 thousand, the debt reduction that supports creditors would stall, and the unquantified dividend conditions could begin to bind. Against that, management itself lists reducing or ceasing dividends among its responses to a cash shortfall, so payouts are a lever, not a commitment.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 47; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 128; calculation A02; calculation A03; calculation A04

A proxy statement showing a holder of 10% or more, or a controlling group, would change the ownership conclusion, and executed credit agreements with wide restricted-payment or investment baskets, or a guarantor group narrower than the operating group, would raise the risk level. Payouts held below free cash flow in fiscal 2027 while the order backlog of USD 3,298,760 thousand converts to sales would lower it, as would continued term-loan repayment; a repurchase claim from a floor-plan lender would raise it.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 2; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 12; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 86; 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 118

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Thor has no sponsor buyout, recapitalisation or acquisition financing in the evidence: it is a listed company and the four filings state no purchase price, entry multiple, equity contribution or sources and uses to report.
Basis: 10-K_2026-07-31_tho.htm.jsonl, page 52

Its senior debt was last raised on October 14, 2021, when it issued USD 500,000 thousand of senior unsecured notes due October 15, 2029 and used the proceeds with cash to repay USD 500,000 thousand drawn on the asset-based facility. The notes rank equally with other senior debt and behind secured debt, and the guarantors named in the indenture are not identified in the supplied text. Of USD 875,768 thousand of debt principal, 98.09% falls due in fiscal 2030 and fiscal 2031, so no maturity pressure is scheduled before then, and the loan documents contain restrictions that can limit dividends and new debt.
