## Findings

### Bottom line

Skeena is a pre-revenue, single-project developer whose senior secured notes rest on construction funding and on Eskay Creek reaching production on management's 2027 timetable, so creditors are underwriting completion risk that prefunding mitigates but does not remove. The profile is weak on today's fundamentals: there is no revenue, derived EBITDA is negative in every period, and leverage cannot rank the credit.

Prefunding is the main support: cash of CAD 134,808 thousand and restricted cash of CAD 500,853 thousand at June 30, 2026 exceed obligations due within a year of CAD 316,513 thousand, and a reserve funds the first three coupons. These balances already include the April 10, 2026 notes issuance; a pro forma position for earlier dates is not established. The main downside is a delay or overrun that outlasts the funds before first cash flow.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a weak reading is that the financing was sized for construction: total cash is 2.01 times the obligations due within a year, a reserve funds the first three coupons, the project is described as fully permitted, feasibility studies from 2021 to 2023 are referred to in the statements and the annual report lists consents from eight independent engineering and environmental firms. The equity value indicated by option pricing is far above the notes' carrying value, the notes carry a fair value above carrying value, and a gold price near the USD 4,017 per ounce used in the valuations would favour project economics if costs are as management describes. If construction stays on schedule these facts make a funding failure before commissioning less likely than the absence of revenue suggests; what they cannot show is operating cost, which decides whether mine cash flow covers the coupon.

### Revenue, cost and capital intensity

There is no revenue to test for durability. After commissioning revenue will come from gold and silver sold as concentrate, less the stream and royalty deductions, and its predictability will turn on throughput, metal prices and smelter terms, none of which the filings let a reader test. The May 2026 release lists concentrate sales risks, including smelter terms that are market dependent, possible export restrictions on critical minerals that limit the choice of smelters, and geopolitical risk in contracting with customers in regions such as China, so revenue visibility after commissioning rests on contracts the filings do not describe and a policy change could cut the buyers available.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 months to 24 months the outcome turns on construction and commissioning. Tailwinds are a permitted project, funded construction, a gold price near USD 4,017 per ounce in the valuation inputs and an equity market that has accepted the company's shares. Headwinds are execution risk on a single project, spending at a pace that leaves little slack beyond the first-production window, and a calendar that crowds together the stream completion test on September 30, 2027, the end of the reserve cover around eighteen months after issue and the first date of fixed redemption prices, April 1, 2028. The fourth semi-annual coupon, about two years after issue, is the first that must be paid from operating cash or other funds, so first production needs to arrive well before it.

Over three to five years the notes are the only debt maturity, due in full on April 1, 2031, with redemption prices starting at 104.25% from April 1, 2028 and falling to par from 2030, and a right to redeem up to 40% with equity proceeds at 108.5% before April 1, 2028. A producing mine would allow refinancing or equity-funded paydown on those terms, while a delayed one would leave the single maturity to be met from a smaller cash cushion. ### Downside scenario

The realistic downside is a construction or commissioning delay on the single project. Management's own risk list names delays in grid energization, commissioning or ramp-up and cost overruns, so the scenario starts with a delay of a quarter or more to initial production. The first operational break is that ore mining and stockpiling slip, so the mill is not fed on time and no concentrate is sold. The financial transmission is that spending continues at CAD 41,916.67 thousand to CAD 51,676.33 thousand a month with no inflow, so cash outside the interest reserve of CAD 505,433 thousand is used up between late April and early July 2027, and the statements say additional financing would then be needed.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.1_2026-08-13_ske-20260813xex99d1.htm.jsonl, pages 2–3; 6-K-EX-99.2_2026-08-13_ske-20260813xex99d2.htm.jsonl, pages 3–4; calculation C4; calculation C7; calculation C8

The consequences follow in order. If the completion test is not met by September 30, 2027 the stream percentage steps up, by 0.05 percentage points if completion falls in the first quarter after that date and by 0.28 percentage points if it falls after the third, and the fourth coupon, about two years after issue, may fall due before operating cash arrives. New money would be raised during construction, probably as equity given the indicated equity cushion, or as debt or a stream whose cost the statements do not establish, since the stream is valued at a 15.77% credit spread. Management's levers are limited: committed spending is CAD 138,109 thousand within a year, marketable securities are CAD 14,240 thousand and equity is the main source. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress if first production arrives in the second quarter of 2027 and spending slows as construction ends, and insufficient for a delay beyond about a quarter without new money, so default risk before commissioning is driven by funding access rather than by operations.
Basis: 6-K-EX-99.2_2026-08-13_ske-20260813xex99d2.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 8, 10–11, 14; calculation C7; calculation C8; calculation C10; calculation C11

### Overall credit view

Overall, Skeena presents a weak business profile for a leveraged credit today, with no revenue and one project, supported by prefunded construction, a permitted project and open capital markets. Against typical leveraged gold issuers, which earn operating cash flow, its profile is weaker by construction, although no peer figures are supplied. Cash-flow durability cannot yet be established because there is no operating history to measure and no cost data to size margins. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on commissioning on schedule, the gold price and the cost of any new money. The primary downside path is a construction delay that outlasts restricted funds before first cash flow. Further analysis would require operating cost and production data, the construction budget, the indenture and stream agreement and statements after June 30, 2026.
