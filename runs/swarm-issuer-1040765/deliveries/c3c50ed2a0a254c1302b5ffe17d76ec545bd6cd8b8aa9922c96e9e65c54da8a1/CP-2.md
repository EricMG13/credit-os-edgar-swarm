The strongest evidence against an average view is asset value. The Park Avenue contribution was valued at USD 900.00 million against a USD 400.00 million loan and is expected to yield a net gain of about USD 485.00 million, the Alexander's stake has a market value of USD 407.73 million against a carrying amount of USD 84.27 million, book debt is 0.48x total assets, and the third-party units and common shares were worth about USD 6,895.62 million at the quarter-end price implied by the units' redemption value, which puts debt at 52.06% of debt plus equity at market value, an analyst proxy and not the covenant measure. ### Limitations

- The look-through ratios of 8.88x and 10.28x add debt of consolidated joint ventures at 100% to NOI after the minority share, so they overstate attributable leverage by a total the filings do not state; the 30% minority share of the USD 1,200.00 million San Francisco mortgage alone is USD 360.00 million and would lower the first ratio to 8.57x, which narrows but does not remove the finding that look-through leverage exceeds the consolidated ratios.
- The 2027 and 2028 mortgage amounts update the only printed ladder for documented 2026 refinancings and reconcile to mortgage principal, and the split of the 2027 amount between the satisfied USD 400.00 million loan and a USD 480.00 million remainder rests on loan sizes and a January 2027 maturity; an unreported prepayment or extension would lower the USD 2,780.00 million wall.
- No agency rating, bond price or spread is supplied, so the view that capital-market access is adequate rests on one USD 500.00 million unsecured issue in January 2026 and cannot be tested against market pricing; a wide spread would move access to weak.
- Covenant levels and the definition of market capitalization are not printed, so the finding that liquidity and unsecured maturities are comfortable cannot be tested against covenant headroom, and the 52.06% debt to debt plus equity proxy drawn from the units' redemption value is not the covenant measure.
- The unencumbered real estate of USD 4,155.48 million is the difference between book real estate and a pledged figure printed rounded to the nearest tenth of a billion, so the 1.83x cover of unsecured debt is approximate and rests on book rather than market values.
- No management guidance or forecast is supplied, so the outlook, the downside illustration and the monitoring signals are analyst judgement built from reported rent spreads, expirations, hedge dates and sensitivities, not management's plan.

The credit's strengths are an office franchise that is leasing at higher rents on rising occupancy, a tenant base with no tenant above 10% of revenues and ten largest tenants at 26.9% of rent, an unsecured maturity profile with nothing due before April 2029 if extensions are used, and asset values that appear to sit above book. Each links to cash flow or recovery: higher rents raise NOI, a diversified rent roll limits single-tenant loss, distant unsecured maturities remove a near-term refinancing event for the partnership's bank and note investors, and values above book support sales that repay debt.

### Company description

Vornado Realty L.P. is the operating partnership through which Vornado Realty Trust holds substantially all of its assets; the trust is sole general partner and owned about 91.2% of the common limited partnership interest at 2026-09-30. Creditor figures here use the partnership's own statements; the trust's per-share and funds-from-operations measures are context only. The business is a Manhattan-weighted office landlord with street retail, residential, THE MART in Chicago and a controlling interest in an office complex in San Francisco; the New York metropolitan area supplies 88% of NOI at share and office about 78% in FY2025. The Park Avenue development venture named in the findings is the venture on 350 Park Avenue formed on 2026-10-20, which is separate from the 49.0% interest in the Park Avenue Plaza office building bought in June 2026.

Rental revenues were 87.39% of nine-month revenues, from leases whose third-quarter New York office signings ran 9.5 years on average, so the revenue model is contractual rent with escalations and tenant reimbursements, plus smaller fee income from cleaning, management and leasing. That gives good cash-flow visibility and supports debt service, but the concentration in one market and one property type means a Manhattan office downturn would hit nearly all cash flow at once, and the partnership's ability to refinance depends on lenders' views of that market.

### Ownership and group structure

The trust is the sole general partner with exclusive control of day-to-day management, third-party Class A unitholders hold about 8.8% of common units with a right to tender for cash, and the trust has 48.79 million preferred shares outstanding whose distributions the partnership funds. Chairman and chief executive Steven Roth also leads Alexander's, and Interstate Properties and its partners held about 7.1% of the trust's shares and 26.0% of Alexander's at 2025-12-31, which the annual report calls substantial influence. For creditors the structure means the partnership's unsecured claims rank alongside other partnership-level liabilities but behind the creditors of operating subsidiaries and consolidated joint ventures, and cash leaves the partnership first to preferred distributions and then to the trust's common dividend; guarantors, a restricted group and the legal rank of each instrument are not established because the governing documents are not supplied.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is predictable and largely recurring but cyclical in its re-leasing. Leases are long (9.5 years on third-quarter New York office signings), expirations are spread (6.1%, 6.2% and 6.3% of New York office square feet in 2026, 2027 and 2028), and weighted average escalated office rent rose from USD 80.01 per square foot in 2021 to USD 90.56 in 2025 while occupancy moved between 88.8% and 92.2%. Pricing power is real in prime office, where second-generation rents rose 23.5% on a GAAP basis over nine months, and absent in retail, where cash rents fell 7.1%. The mechanism is that rolling 2026 expiring office rents of USD 79.82 per square foot to the USD 85 to 95 management expects on re-leasing raises NOI, while tenants' hybrid-work and space-reduction choices set the vacancy risk, so the credit implication is steady organic growth that must still outrun a higher interest bill.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Operating costs are largely fixed in the short run: mortgage payments, real estate taxes and maintenance do not fall when rents fall, the building-services workforce is unionised, and nine-month consolidated operating expenses rose 4.37% against revenue growth of 2.38%, taking the consolidated NOI margin to 48.47% from 49.45%. Same-store operations did better, with NOI at share up 8.4%, so the margin pressure came from acquisitions, development and non-reimbursable costs rather than the stabilised portfolio. Margins can be defended against modest volume pressure through reimbursements and escalations but not against a deep occupancy fall or a sharp rise in wage and energy costs, so operating leverage cuts both ways for interest cover. Multiemployer pension contributions of USD 9.65 million and health plan contributions of USD 34.88 million in FY2025 sit inside operating expenses, and withdrawal liability is not quantified.

### Porter's five forces

Competitive rivalry is the force that matters: the partnership competes with many owners and developers, some willing to accept lower returns, on rents, concessions, location and building quality, and tenants have moved to modern amenitised buildings; the mechanism is higher concessions and capital to hold Class A status, and the implication is that leasing capital stays near 12% to 13% of initial rent. New entrants take the form of newer, more amenitised competing buildings in the same market, which the annual report names as a risk to leasing and effective rents, and the threat of substitutes is real through hybrid work, co-working, sublease space and artificial-intelligence-driven space reduction, which could cut demand for office space in the largest tenant industries, financial services at 23% and technology at 13% of rents. Buyer power is moderate: no tenant exceeds 10% of revenues and the ten largest are 26.9% of rent, but large tenants negotiate free rent and allowances. Supplier power is moderate: building-services labour is unionised and construction and renovation costs are named as a factor in results, and the annual report says it is not assured that increases in operating costs can be passed through to tenants in full.

### PEST

### Overall credit view

Overall, Vornado Realty L.P. presents a strong business profile supported by a leasing-led Manhattan office franchise at 93.0% occupancy, a tenant base with none above 10% of revenues and liquidity of USD 1,768.78 million, but credit quality is constrained by leverage of 8.08x trailing EBITDA and 8.88x look-through NOI at share and by free cash flow that is near zero after capital spending and preferred distributions.
