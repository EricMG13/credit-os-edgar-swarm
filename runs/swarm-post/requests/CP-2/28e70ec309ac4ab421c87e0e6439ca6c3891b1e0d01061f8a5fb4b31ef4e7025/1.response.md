## Findings

### Bottom line

Post presents an adequate business profile with a weak financial policy: steady cash flow services high leverage, but cash repurchases ran at 2.28x free cash flow in nine months, so creditors depend on management restraining shareholder returns.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_post-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2025-09-30_post-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 96; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C14

Net debt was USD 7,367.0 million at June 30, 2026, or USD 7,406.3 million pro forma for USD 39.3 million of repurchases between July 1 and August 5, 2026, which is 5.33x reported EBITDA, or 5.36x pro forma. Liquidity of USD 1,242.4 million and no maturity before fiscal 2027 give a runway of at least a year, so the main risk is leverage rising on lower earnings, not a liquidity gap.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_post-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 17, 21; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_ex99-1q32026er.htm.jsonl, page 6; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C17

### Key findings

The business is large and spread widely enough to give reasonable cash-flow visibility, but reported growth overstates underlying demand. Net sales were USD 8,412.5 million over the last twelve months across four segments, and no customer exceeds 17.4% of sales, yet pet food supplied USD 890.6 million, or 76.3%, of the USD 1,167.1 million rise in sales between fiscal 2023 and fiscal 2025, and in nine months to June 2026 Post Consumer Brands sales fell 8.6% without 8th Avenue while pet food sales fell 15.0%. Creditors are relying on acquired earnings that are already shrinking organically.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_post-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 157; 10-Q_2026-06-30_post-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 33; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C1; calculation C21; calculation C22; calculation C23

Pricing power is limited and buyers hold most of the leverage. The largest customers are 29.9% of Post Consumer Brands, 33.0% of Weetabix, 42.0% of Foodservice and 34.9% of Refrigerated Retail, management expects the ready-to-eat cereal category to stay weak, and pet food prices fell on mix and promotion. Eggs are the exception only because avian influenza outbreaks lifted prices: that pricing raised Foodservice profit by USD 91.6 million, or 29.7%, in fiscal 2025 and is now lapping, so third-quarter Foodservice sales fell 6.5% and its profit fell 18.6%.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_post-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 76, 155; 10-Q_2026-06-30_post-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 26, 33–34; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_ex99-1q32026er.htm.jsonl, page 4; calculation C24; calculation C25

Margins have held at group level, but the mix beneath them is moving and the main cost pressures are external. Reported EBITDA margin was 16.0% in fiscal 2024, 16.3% in fiscal 2025 and 16.4% over the last twelve months, helped in the nine months to June 2026 by USD 198.0 million of lower raw material costs in Foodservice, mainly eggs. Tariffs and the conflict in Iran have raised input, energy and freight costs, and management plans USD 80 million to USD 90 million of cage-free and precooked egg expansion, so margins depend on items management does not control.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_post-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-06-30_post-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 29, 34; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_ex99-1q32026er.htm.jsonl, page 7; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C15

Cash conversion is moderate and flattered in the latest period because capital spending fell. Free cash flow was USD 553.1 million over the last twelve months, 40.0% of reported EBITDA against 36.7% in fiscal 2025, after USD 439.5 million of capital expenditure and USD 370.1 million of cash interest in fiscal 2025. Nine-month free cash flow rose to USD 401.5 million from USD 336.5 million because additions to property fell, not because operating cash flow rose, and the split of capital spending between maintenance and growth is not stated.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_post-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 96, 128; 10-Q_2026-06-30_post-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 4; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C12; calculation C13; calculation C14

Leverage is high and barely falling, and cover is thinner than the issuer's own ratios suggest. Net debt of USD 7,367.0 million at June 30, 2026 is 5.33x reported EBITDA, or USD 7,406.3 million and 5.36x pro forma for USD 39.3 million of repurchases between July 1 and August 5, 2026, against 5.45x at September 30, 2025. The issuer reports 4.6x net leverage and 3.8x interest cover on credit-agreement Adjusted EBITDA, but add-backs are 16.84% of reported EBITDA, reported EBITDA covered interest expense 3.30x over the last twelve months, and principal debt rose USD 223.8 million in nine months.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_post-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 17; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_ex99-1q32026er.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 22; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C5; calculation C6; calculation C9; calculation C7; calculation C8; calculation C11

Liquidity and the near maturity profile are the main supports, and the longer wall is the refinancing risk. Cash and undrawn revolver capacity were USD 1,242.4 million at June 30, 2026 before the later repurchases, 2.16x the USD 576.3 million falling due in fiscal 2027, of which USD 575.0 million is convertible notes due in August 2027 with a conversion price about 19.5% above the average price Post paid for shares after the quarter end. USD 4,566.0 million, or 59.5% of principal, falls due in fiscal 2030 to fiscal 2033, and refinancing the two lowest-coupon issues at the 6.50% coupon of the December 2025 notes would add about USD 45.6 million of interest a year.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_post-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 17–18, 20–21; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_ex99-1q32026er.htm.jsonl, page 6; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C16; calculation C17; calculation C18; calculation C19; calculation C20

Financial policy is the weakest dimension. Cash repurchases ran at 2.28x free cash flow in nine months to June 2026 and 1.45x in fiscal 2025, the board has approved four authorizations since August 2025, no leverage target is stated, and no sponsor or controlling holder is disclosed to support or constrain the issuer. Business & governance rates sponsor and governance risk as Medium, and the one financial covenant tests secured net leverage only when revolver usage passes 30% of commitments, so it is untested at current usage.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_post-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 23; 10-K_2025-09-30_post-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 96; Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C14; calculation C32

The realistic downside is leverage drifting up on lower egg earnings, not a liquidity event. If Foodservice earnings fell back to the USD 500 million run rate management describes and about USD 20 million of divested earnings left, Adjusted EBITDA would settle near management's comparable fiscal 2027 base of about USD 1.48 billion, USD 135.9 million or 8.4% below the last twelve months. With pro forma net debt of USD 7,406.3 million unchanged, reported-basis net leverage would rise from 5.36x to 5.94x, and free cash flow would fall to about USD 417.2 million, so using the USD 490.7 million of authorization left would exceed it by USD 73.5 million. Liquidity still covers the 2027 convertible notes, and management can stop repurchases, trim capital spending toward the USD 370 million to USD 390 million guided for fiscal 2026, or sell facilities, so distress is not the expected outcome.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-08-06_ex99-1q32026er.htm.jsonl, pages 6–7; 10-Q_2026-06-30_post-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 21; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C29; calculation C30; calculation C31; calculation C17; calculation C4

Over 12 months to 24 months the outlook is flat earnings, with the convertible notes due in August 2027 the one dated test. Management narrowed fiscal 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance to USD 1,560 million to USD 1,570 million, which implies a fourth quarter of USD 369.5 million to USD 379.5 million against USD 425.4 million a year earlier, and describes fiscal 2027 as generally flat on a comparable base. Tailwinds are lower egg costs, Weetabix margin gains and lower capital spending; headwinds are pet food distribution losses, a weak cereal category, higher coupons on refinanced debt and the refinancing wall in fiscal 2030 to fiscal 2033. Over 3 years to 5 years the open questions are whether cereal and pet food volumes stabilise and whether that wall is refinanced at coupons above those it replaces.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-08-06_ex99-1q32026er.htm.jsonl, pages 4–7; 10-Q_2026-06-30_post-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 34; calculation C28; calculation C29; calculation C18; calculation C19

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against caution is cash and time: USD 1,242.4 million of liquidity, no revolver drawings, no maturity before fiscal 2027 and free cash flow of USD 553.1 million, with segment profit up 9.1% over nine months and repurchases that the board can stop. The view would improve if repurchases fell to the level of free cash flow and net debt began to fall, if Foodservice held earnings above the USD 500 million run rate, or if the 2027 convertible notes were settled from cash without new borrowing. It would worsen if fourth-quarter Adjusted EBITDA came in at the low end of the USD 369.5 million to USD 379.5 million implied range while repurchases stayed above free cash flow, or if revolver usage rose toward the 30% level at which the leverage covenant applies.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_post-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 21, 23, 26–27; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_ex99-1q32026er.htm.jsonl, pages 6–7; calculation C17; calculation C12; calculation C27; calculation C28; calculation C32

### Strengths and weaknesses

Liquidity covers the nearest maturity twice over. Cash and undrawn revolver capacity of USD 1,242.4 million compare with USD 576.3 million due in fiscal 2027, so a cash-settled convertible does not depend on market access and no refinancing is forced in the next twelve months.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_post-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 21; calculation C17

Scale and customer spread cushion a single shock. Net sales of USD 8,412.5 million come from four segments and the largest customer is 17.4% of sales, so the loss of one account or one product line is partly absorbed by the others.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_post-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 16; calculation C1

Cash generation is steady. Free cash flow of USD 553.1 million, 40.0% of reported EBITDA, covers interest and capital spending with a surplus that would repay debt if repurchases stopped.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C12; calculation C13

Shareholder returns outrun cash generation. Cash repurchases were 2.28x free cash flow in nine months while principal debt rose USD 223.8 million, so each further quarter at that pace adds to net debt and delays deleveraging.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_post-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 17; calculation C14; calculation C11

Leverage is high and the fall from 5.45x to 5.33x is small. Net debt of USD 7,367.0 million, USD 7,406.3 million pro forma, leaves little room for an earnings decline: a fall of USD 135.9 million in EBITDA would lift reported-basis net leverage to 5.94x.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C5; calculation C6; calculation C4; calculation C29; calculation C30

Organic sales are shrinking in the largest segment. Post Consumer Brands sales fell 8.6% in nine months without 8th Avenue and pet food sales fell 15.0%, so acquired earnings erode and revenue visibility is lower than the headline suggests.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_post-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 33; calculation C22; calculation C23

Earnings lean on an egg price cycle. Foodservice produced 38.5% of nine-month segment profit and management puts about USD 60 million of its fiscal 2026 earnings above a USD 500 million run rate, so a reversal lowers EBITDA without any change in debt.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_post-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 26; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_ex99-1q32026er.htm.jsonl, page 7; calculation C26; calculation C29

Refinancing will cost more. The 2030 and 2031 senior notes carry lower coupons than the 6.50% paid in December 2025, and refinancing them at that coupon would add about USD 45.6 million of interest a year against interest that already takes 30.3% of reported EBITDA.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-30_post-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 19; calculation C19; calculation C10
