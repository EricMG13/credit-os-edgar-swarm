## Findings

### Bottom line

Lithia's credit profile is adequate but weakening at the margin: leverage on the company's own measure is 3.07x and reported EBITDA covers cash interest paid only 2.79x, while buybacks funded by borrowing are the main threat to creditors.

Aftersales and finance income give earnings ballast, asset-matched funding limits the risk in the largest debt lines, and funding maturities now run to 2028 and beyond, but covenants, ratings and note guarantors are not established, so this is a limited view that rests on the company's own non-GAAP measures.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against a weakening view is that the company's own leverage is flat since March 2026 at 3.07x, that the high point of 3.17x in June 2026 has already eased, that third-quarter Adjusted EBITDA rose 1.26% on the year, that net credit losses on managed receivables fell to USD 47.8 million in the nine months from USD 54.1 million, and that 95.24% of the added receivables and most floor plan debt is matched by assets. That case would win if trailing Adjusted EBITDA turned up toward the USD 1,701.9 million of a year ago while repurchases slowed to within operating cash flow. It would lose if used-vehicle values or borrowing-base availability fell, because the matched debt would then be repaid faster than the assets convert, or if the executed credit agreement shows distribution limits that are already close to binding. On governance, the company kept a lead independent director when the chief executive became chairman, which is a partial check on the combined role.

### Limitations

- No covenant levels, restricted-payments baskets, guarantors or ratings were supplied, which limits the view on distribution capacity and on how much of the USD 1,256.5 million of available credit is usable; the assessment of financial policy is therefore a view of behaviour, not of legal capacity.
- Leverage on the company's measure uses the company's non-GAAP net debt and Adjusted EBITDA, which exclude inventory-related and financing-operations debt and deduct floor plan and financing interest, so the 3.07x and 6.00x figures describe the corporate layer only and are not comparable with the full-perimeter ratios.
- The downside case applies the reported fall of 4.4% in trailing Adjusted EBITDA once more to unchanged net debt and is an anchored assumption, not a forecast, so the 3.21x outcome shows sensitivity and not a probable level.
- Interim statements are unaudited, and year-end 2025 trailing Adjusted EBITDA is not printed, so the path of leverage between December 2025 and March 2026 on the company's measure is not established.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The first strength is a profit base weighted to service and finance. Aftersales and finance and insurance supplied 67.52% of gross profit in the nine months to September 2026, so a fall in new-vehicle margin reduces earnings less than the vehicle mix alone would imply, which supports cash flow visibility and debt service through a vehicle-price cycle.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_lad-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 29

The second strength is funding that is matched and extended. Inventory of USD 6,624.3 million stands against floor plan notes of USD 6,566.8 million and net finance receivables of USD 5,578.6 million against USD 4,509.2 million of financing-operations debt, and facility maturities were pushed to 2031, 2029 and 2028 in 2026, so near-term refinancing pressure on the large lines is low.

The third strength is clean claims outside debt: pension plans were in surplus by USD 30.1 million at the end of 2025 and no off-balance-sheet arrangement is reported, so there is no hidden senior claim from pensions in the supplied evidence.

The first weakness is distributions that exceed operating cash flow. Buybacks of USD 960.9 million in 2025 and USD 624.5 million in the nine months to September 2026 were paid while operating cash flow was USD 356.7 million and an outflow of USD 204.9 million, which raises net debt and reduces headroom for the corporate layer's creditors.

The second weakness is margin and growth. Operating margin fell to 4.15% and selling, general and administrative expense reached 69.95% of gross profit, while same-store revenue was 1.5% lower, so the earnings cushion over interest is narrowing and growth depends on acquisitions that add floor plan debt.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_lad-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 3; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lad-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 29; calculation CALC-19

The third weakness is that protections cannot be verified. Covenants, guarantors, collateral by instrument, ratings and board holdings are not in the supplied documents, and the chief executive is also chairman from 2026-10-19, so the checks that normally limit debt-funded distributions cannot be tested.
