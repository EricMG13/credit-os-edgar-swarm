## Findings

### Bottom line

Lithia's creditor risk lies in cash allocation more than in its franchised retail model: share repurchases of USD 960.9 million in 2025 and USD 624.5 million in the nine months to September 2026 both exceeded operating cash flow.

Aftersales and finance and insurance supply about two thirds of gross profit, which steadies earnings while new-vehicle margins fall, but the filings do not identify the controlling holders, the board's composition or the note guarantors, so protections around distributions cannot be tested.

### Key findings

Lithia sells new and used vehicles through franchised stores in the United States, the United Kingdom and Canada, and earns most of its profit from service, parts and finance and insurance. New vehicles are 49.70% of 2025 revenue but 20.39% of gross profit, while aftersales and finance and insurance together are 66.82% of gross profit, and the nine months to September 2026 show the same shape at 67.52%. That mix means vehicle margin compression, which cut new-vehicle gross profit per unit from USD 2,943 to USD 2,699 in the nine months, is cushioned by a service and finance base that is less tied to new-vehicle cycles.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lad-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 44; 10-K_2025-12-31_lad-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 75; 10-Q_2026-09-30_lad-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 29; calculation CALC-1; calculation CALC-2; calculation CALC-3

The company is concentrated by country and by franchise relationship. The United States is 78.53% of 2025 revenue, the United Kingdom 18.37% and Canada 3.10%, five manufacturers account for 25% of sales, and each store operates under a manufacturer franchise that the manufacturer may end on a change of management or ownership without its consent. Mechanically, a change in who controls the company could therefore put franchises, and the cash flow behind the debt, at risk at the same time as a change-in-control trigger in the credit agreement and note indentures.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_lad-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 118; 10-K_2025-12-31_lad-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 20; 10-K_2025-12-31_lad-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-K_2025-12-31_lad-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 39; calculation CALC-4

Growth has come through purchases of stores funded by floor plan debt. The company bought a large group of stores in 2024 for USD 1.1 billion net of floor plan debt, 17 in 2025 for USD 751.0 million and nine businesses in the first nine months of 2026 for USD 264.8 million, with USD 1.17 billion of annualized revenue acquired in 2026 to date including an October purchase of four stores. Acquired inventory is financed with new floor plan borrowing, so debt rises by more than the cash paid, and the stores acquired in 2026 had added USD 277.9 million of revenue and USD 11.2 million of operating income by September.

Shareholder returns are the main financial-policy risk. Repurchases and dividends were 45.09% of cash deployed in 2025 and 56.28% in the nine months to September 2026, against the company's stated target of 40% to 50%, and in both periods repurchases exceeded operating cash flow, which was USD 356.7 million in 2025 and an outflow of USD 204.9 million in the nine months. The share count fell 17.80% between the end of 2024 and September 2026 while book equity was almost unchanged, so the capital returned was financed by borrowing, because reported operating cash flow did not cover it. The board added USD 500 million of repurchase authority in May 2026, leaving USD 535.4 million available.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case against the allocation concern is that 2025 shareholder returns were 45.09% of cash deployed, inside the 40% to 50% target, that the share count fell 17.80% as a result, and that the company says it has funding for the next twelve months. It would lose if the nine-month figure of 56.28% became the pattern while operating cash flow stays negative, because each dollar of buyback would then add a dollar of floor plan, warehouse or revolver debt; a board decision to slow repurchases, or a restricted-payments limit in the executed credit agreement that is already close to binding, would change conviction in either direction, and neither document is supplied.
