## Findings

### Bottom line

Governance and control weaknesses, not operating scale, are the main creditor concern, although fee cuts, debt-repaying asset sales and current reporting reduce the risk.

The business is a diversified owner of branded consumer and industrial companies, with 63.1% of nine-month 2026 revenue from branded consumer lines. Parent liquidity depends on cash that the eight subsidiaries upstream through intercompany loans, so subsidiary covenants matter to creditors. Board, executive and ownership detail is not established because the proxy statement is not in the sources.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, pages 48–51; 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, pages 98–99; 10-K_2025-12-31_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, page 209; calculation C2; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

- The structure puts creditors above a holding company that owns primary stakes of 85.0% to 99.4% in its subsidiaries, with fully diluted stakes lower because management holds subsidiary options. Cash reaches the parent through intercompany loans of USD 654.94 million, so subsidiary covenant compliance is a live transmission channel: the Altor subsidiary needed waivers at 2026-09-30 and others had waivers in 2025 and 2026.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, pages 36–37; 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, page 98; calculation C7
- Revenue is concentrated in a few names. The tactical apparel and gear subsidiary generated 30.2% of nine-month 2026 revenue, Altor 15.3% and Rimports 11.3%; Altor's three largest customers were about 16% of its 2025 net sales and Velocity Outdoor's three largest about 32% of its 2025 gross sales. Shifts at one of these names move group cash flow more than the average subsidiary.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, page 56; 10-K_2025-12-31_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, page 65; 10-K_2025-12-31_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, page 73; calculation C1
- The 2025 restatement stemmed from the Lugano investigation, in which a subsidiary's former chief executive misrepresented transactions. The 2025 annual report carries an adverse opinion on internal control, and management said at 2026-09-30 that the control weaknesses were not fully remediated. For creditors this means reported figures carry elevated risk until remediation is shown, and the securities class action is reasonably possible with no loss range estimated.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, page 221; 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, page 112
- The Manager is paid before shareholders, and the restatement showed that fees were overpaid by USD 50.4 million. The overpayment was recouped by reducing later fees, leaving a net USD 1.3 million receivable at 2026-09-30, and the ninth agreement caps the 2027 base fee at USD 30.0 million. Fees are lower, with cash paid in nine months 2026 of USD 23.3 million against USD 49.8 million a year earlier, which supports creditors but depends on the Manager's cooperation.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, pages 48–50; 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, page 94; calculation C8; calculation C9; calculation C5
- Shareholder payouts are limited to preferred shares. Common distributions have been suspended since 2025-05-27, and preferred distributions of USD 9.72 million a quarter continue on USD 477.83 million of preferred equity, which ranks below the debt; the preferred holders are therefore a cash call senior to common but junior to creditors.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, pages 35–36; 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, page 7; calculation C6; calculation C11
- Disposals have been used to repay debt: the Sterno food service sale at an enterprise value of USD 292.5 million produced about USD 282 million of proceeds, which repaid term loan debt, and the Lugano bankruptcy settlement returned USD 29.8 million in cash plus an interest valued at USD 9.5 million. The proceeds figure differs by USD 2 million between documents, which is a minor conflict. No new notes or term loans were issued in 2026, and revolver drawings of USD 94.00 million were repaid within the period.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, pages 16–19; EX99.1_2026-03-31_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc_ex991sternoclosingpressrel-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1; calculation C13; calculation C12

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a negative governance view is that creditors now hold stronger protections: the credit facility requires disposal proceeds to repay debt, caps manager fees, and lenders waived earlier defaults, while reporting is current and the management agreement is cheaper from 2027. A clean internal-control opinion for 2026, evidence that overpaid fees are fully recouped and board detail from the proxy statement would move the view toward a lower governance risk, while a new restatement, a covenant breach at the holding company or fee payments ahead of debt reduction would move it the other way.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, pages 27–28; 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, pages 48–50; 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, page 112
