## Findings

### Bottom line

The credit is deleveraging but still stretched: leverage sits above the covenant level due from late 2027, control weaknesses are unrepaired and most debt matures within 29 months, so creditors depend on further disposals.

The strongest support is progress already made: disposals repaid debt, manager fees are falling and liquidity covers scheduled amortisation. The weakest dimension is financial policy and governance, and the primary downside path is a miss on the 2026 earnings outlook that leaves the term loan unrepaid and the covenant steps out of reach.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C6; calculation C9; calculation C8

### Key findings

- Leverage is high but falling. Net debt was USD 1,434.11 million at 2026-09-30, which is 5.61x last-twelve-months reported EBITDA and 5.11x adjusted EBITDA, down from 8.48x adjusted EBITDA at 2025-12-31. Pro forma for the preferred distributions payable on 2026-10-30, net debt is USD 1,443.82 million and net leverage on reported EBITDA is 5.65x; the October 2026 term loan prepayment leaves net debt unchanged because it used cash already held, and the pro forma figure on adjusted EBITDA is not established.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C2
- The covenant path is the binding constraint. The issuer reports a covenant total leverage ratio of 4.53x against limits that step down from 5.75x to 5.25x on 2026-12-31, to 5.00x on 2027-06-30 and to 4.50x after 2027-09-30, so the reported gap falls from 1.22x to below zero. This is arithmetic on reported ratios and not a measure of capacity, because the covenant definitions were not supplied.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, pages 27–30; calculation C6; calculation C12
- The headline earnings measure flatters leverage. Management's 2026 outlook of USD 330.0 million to USD 355.0 million is Subsidiary Adjusted EBITDA, which excludes corporate cost; corporate cost was USD 77.83 million in nine months 2026, so consolidated Adjusted EBITDA of USD 189.83 million is well below the subsidiary sum of USD 267.66 million. A multiple built on the subsidiary measure understates leverage, because corporate cost is 29.1% of that measure.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, page 102; EX99.1_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc_exhibit991earningsreleaseq-htm.htm.jsonl, page 2; calculation C10; calculation C11
- Cash cover after fixed claims is thin. Last-twelve-months free cash flow of USD 124.06 million, less preferred distributions of USD 38.86 million a year and 2027 term loan amortisation of USD 45.00 million, leaves USD 40.20 million, and only USD 6.97 million once the nine-month working capital inflow of USD 33.23 million is removed, so deleveraging still depends on disposal proceeds.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, pages 35–36; calculation C4
- Refinancing is back-ended but concentrated. The secured facility matures in 13.8 months and the 2029 notes of USD 1,029.37 million are 65.5% of principal, with both note series 85.1%, so a failed market window in 2028 would hit the whole structure. The term loan of USD 233.50 million must be repaid by 2026-12-31 to avoid a USD 4.00 million milestone fee, and liquidity of USD 180.17 million, of which cash is USD 127.17 million, falls USD 53.33 million short of that, so the fee is likely unless another disposal closes. Pro forma cash after the preferred distribution is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, pages 27–30; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C3; calculation C8; calculation C9; calculation C7
- The business is diversified but uneven. Branded consumer lines were 63.1% of nine-month 2026 revenue and their adjusted earnings rose 27.5% in the third quarter, while industrial adjusted earnings fell 19.6% and one industrial subsidiary needed covenant waivers. Industrial weakness is the main operating risk to the earnings outlook.
Basis: EX99.1_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc_exhibit991earningsreleaseq-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C11; calculation C13
- Governance is the weakest dimension. The restatement stemmed from the Lugano irregularities and the control weaknesses were open at 2026-09-30, but manager fees are falling, with the 2027 base fee capped at USD 30.0 million, and an earlier overpayment of USD 50.4 million is being recouped, leaving USD 1.3 million due from the Manager. This keeps reported figures at elevated risk without changing the direction of travel.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0); 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, pages 48–50; 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, page 112; calculation C13

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a more positive view is the speed of deleveraging: the issuer's own covenant ratio fell from 5.3x to 4.5x in two quarters, branded consumer earnings are growing and the interest burden has fallen with debt. Another disposal at a good multiple that repays the term loan before 2026-12-31, a clean internal-control opinion for 2026 and a refinancing of the facility ahead of its 2028 maturity would move the view up, while an industrial earnings miss, a further subsidiary covenant breach or a new restatement would move it down.
Basis: EX99.1_2026-06-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc_exhibit991earningsreleaseq-htm.htm.jsonl, page 2; EX99.1_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc_exhibit991earningsreleaseq-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2; calculation C12; calculation C11

### Company description and credit linkage

The issuer is the operating company of a listed trust that owns controlling stakes in eight middle-market subsidiaries, five branded consumer and three industrial. Cash flow is the sum of eight separate businesses upstreamed through intercompany loans, which diversifies revenue but makes parent debt service depend on subsidiary covenants and on a Manager that is paid before shareholders.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C13

### Ownership and group structure

Control is held through the Trust and the LLC, with a profit-allocation holder and a Manager sitting alongside shareholders. The senior notes are unsecured and not guaranteed by subsidiaries, while the credit facility is secured on substantially all assets including subsidiary equity, so noteholders are structurally and effectively behind the secured lenders and the subsidiaries' own creditors.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, page 28

### Revenue durability, margin resilience and cash conversion

Revenue is spread across brands and industrial products, but two subsidiaries disclose that a few customers matter, and the industrial group is the weaker earner. Margins improved with the exit of Lugano, yet reported EBITDA is distorted by disposal gains, so cash conversion is better judged on operating cash flow, which was helped in 2026 by a working capital release that may not repeat.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C4

### Market frameworks

Competitive and buyer power matter most in the consumer lines, where retail channels and tariff policy affect pricing, and supplier power matters at the subsidiary that buys moulded parts from a related shareholder. Macro and policy factors beyond tariffs do not appear to be a primary credit driver based on the provided materials.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months the tailwinds are the growth in branded consumer earnings, lower interest cost as debt falls and a cheaper management agreement from 2027. The headwinds are industrial weakness, the 2028 facility maturity, the tightening covenant steps and the loss of earnings from disposed businesses. Over three to five years the 2029 and 2032 notes are the dominant refinancing exposure.
Basis: EX99.1_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc_exhibit991earningsreleaseq-htm.htm.jsonl, pages 1–2; 10-Q_2026-09-30_compass-group-diversified-holdings-llc.htm.jsonl, pages 27–30; calculation C11; calculation C8

### Downside scenario

An industrial earnings miss, with one large customer reducing volumes at a subsidiary that already needed waivers, would cut group earnings below the outlook. With the term loan unrepaid at year-end the milestone fee falls due, free cash flow after fixed claims is already thin, and leverage stays above the lower covenant steps. Management could slow capex and pursue further disposals, which would repay debt but remove earnings, so resilience looks adequate to avoid a liquidity crisis in 2027 but not to restore headroom without asset sales.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C4; calculation C6; calculation C7

### Overall credit view

Overall, the issuer presents an adequate but uneven business profile supported by diversification and a visible deleveraging record, but credit quality is constrained by high leverage, weak governance and control, and concentrated 2028 and 2029 maturities. Cash-flow durability appears adequate, since conversion is helped by working capital that may not repeat. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on further disposal proceeds and on the covenant and earnings path through 2027. The primary downside path is an industrial earnings miss that leaves leverage above the covenant steps and the term loan unrepaid. Further analysis would require the credit agreement, the indentures and the proxy statement.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); Source readiness (CP-0)

### Strengths and weaknesses

The main strengths are a diversified portfolio with branded consumer lines growing, a record of repaying debt from disposals that cut net debt by USD 375.20 million in nine months, liquidity of USD 180.17 million, a cheaper management agreement and the suspension of common distributions. The main weaknesses are leverage above the final covenant step, control weaknesses that are not yet repaired, maturities that concentrate in 2028 and 2029, an industrial group that is losing earnings, and a headline earnings measure that excludes corporate cost.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C1; calculation C3
