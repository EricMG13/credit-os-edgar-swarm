## Findings

### Bottom line

Qnity's leverage is moderate and falling: total debt was 2.72x and net debt 1.96x last-twelve-month reported EBITDA at 2026-09-30, against 2.87x and 2.22x at 2025-12-31, and scheduled maturities are only USD 24 million a year through 2030. Cash interest paid is not stated in any filing, so cash interest cover is not established; reported EBITDA covered nine-month interest expense of USD 186 million 6.15x.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_q-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 120; 10-Q_2026-09-30_q-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 6, 29, 42; calculation F16; calculation F15; calculation F22

Liquidity is large against near-term needs: cash of USD 1,124 million and an undrawn revolver net of letters of credit of USD 1,238 million give USD 2,362 million, against USD 23 million of principal due in the next twelve months. Free cash flow, operating cash flow less capital expenditures, fell to USD 357 million for the nine months from USD 568 million a year earlier, as interest payments began and working capital absorbed cash.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_q-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 6, 20, 42, 43; calculation F17; calculation F21

### Leverage trajectory and drivers

Leverage fell between 2025-12-31 and 2026-09-30 because earnings grew while debt barely moved. Carrying-value debt was USD 4,027 million at 2025-12-31 and USD 4,019 million at 2026-09-30, after USD 18 million of term loan installments in nine months, so total debt to reported EBITDA fell from 2.87x to 2.72x almost wholly because last-twelve-month reported EBITDA rose from USD 1,403 million to USD 1,479 million. Net debt to reported EBITDA fell further, from 2.22x to 1.96x, because cash rose from USD 915 million to USD 1,124 million. For creditors this means the improvement so far is earnings-led rather than repayment-led, so it holds only while EBITDA holds.
