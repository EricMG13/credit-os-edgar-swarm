## Findings

### Bottom line

Ingevity's fundamental credit profile is adequate: one high-margin automotive carbon business supports moderate leverage and liquidity, but concentrated earnings, an unfinished portfolio reshaping and shareholder returns above free cash flow make the USD 550.0 million 2028 notes the pivot. Net debt is 2.75x LTM Adjusted EBITDA of USD 384.8 million, cash interest is covered 6.66x and liquidity is USD 398.2 million, yet Performance Materials segment EBITDA of USD 339.5 million equals 88.2% of that Adjusted EBITDA.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation B1; calculation B2

The primary downside is weaker automotive volume in Asia and Europe while buybacks, which exceeded free cash flow in 2026, compete with debt repayment: an illustrative 20% fall in that segment's EBITDA would lift net debt to Adjusted EBITDA from 2.75x to 3.35x.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ngvt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 42; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ngvt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 5; calculation B4

### Key findings

The strongest support is pricing power in a high-margin automotive carbon franchise. Performance Materials earned a margin of 53.8% in FY2025 and 55.6% in nine months of 2026, and price and mix added USD 14.3 million to nine-month sales against a USD 1.3 million volume loss, so earnings held while volumes stalled. Price now carries earnings, and the ten largest customers buy about 90% of sales in this business on purchase orders, so customer acceptance of price is the channel through which pressure would arrive.

Earnings concentration is the weakest dimension. Performance Materials segment EBITDA of USD 339.5 million over the last twelve months is 88.2% of group Adjusted EBITDA of USD 384.8 million, while Pavement Technologies earned USD 44.4 million and Advanced Polymer Technologies USD 36.0 million, which after USD 35.1 million of corporate and indirect costs leave USD 45.3 million, or 11.8% of Adjusted EBITDA. Vehicle build, emissions rules and substitutes such as sealed fuel tanks and electric vehicles therefore act on almost all group cash flow.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is that the balance sheet is already shrinking and the pivot can be handled early. Debt including finance leases fell USD 255.5 million from FY2024 to USD 1,145.5 million, revolver borrowings fell USD 270.0 million, liquidity is USD 398.2 million and the issuer reports covenant ratios of 2.4x and 7.0x. The cautious view would lose if the 2028 notes are refinanced or cut below USD 250 million before 2028-08-02, if sale proceeds above USD 99.2 million repay debt, and if buybacks slow from the USD 38.9 million quarterly pace.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ngvt-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 16–18; calculation B6; calculation B12; calculation B15

The strongest evidence against treating concentration as the primary risk is stability: Performance Materials segment EBITDA rose 9.9% in FY2024 and fell only 2.1% in FY2025, while the other two segments fell 17.7% to 24.5% in single years, and management describes North American demand as resilient on hybrid vehicles. The concentration view would strengthen if volume declines widen beyond the 4% seen in the third quarter, if price and mix gains reverse or if a top customer is lost, since an illustrative 20% fall in Performance Materials EBITDA alone lifts net debt to Adjusted EBITDA to 3.35x.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ngvt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 145; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ngvt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 42; calculation B4; calculation B17

### Company description

Ingevity is a specialty chemicals and materials group with three reportable segments: Performance Materials, which sells hardwood-based activated carbon for gasoline vapor control in combustion and hybrid vehicles and for filtration, was 52.0% of FY2025 net sales; Pavement Technologies, named Performance Chemicals until the second quarter of 2026, sells asphalt additives, emulsifiers and agrochemical dispersants and was 34.3% including road markings; and Advanced Polymer Technologies, which sells caprolactone polymers, was 13.7%. Sales go to about 65 automotive customers, about 600 pavement customers and about 200 polymer customers, 43% of FY2025 sales were outside the United States, and the group sold the industrial specialties business on 2026-01-01 and road markings on 2026-04-15.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ngvt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 8; 10-K_2025-12-31_ngvt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 15–17; 10-K_2025-12-31_ngvt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20; 10-K_2025-12-31_ngvt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 24; calculation B18; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Cash-flow visibility is moderate: sales to the largest customers rest mainly on purchase orders, pavement revenue is seasonal and government-funded, and the company is smaller and simpler than a year ago, so debt service depends on pricing power in carbon holding while the portfolio settles. Customer stickiness is higher in automotive carbon, where the annual report cites a decades-long record of meeting life-of-vehicle emission standards, a company characterization, than in pavement, where competitors are named and demand follows paving budgets.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ngvt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_ngvt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 26–28; 10-K_2025-12-31_ngvt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9

The 2025 annual report takes board, ownership and related-party information from a proxy statement that is not supplied, so ownership, independence and insider holdings are not established.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is cyclical, concentrated and price-led. Performance Materials sales follow gasoline and hybrid vehicle production, with about half of sales in North America and about 40% in Asia Pacific, and in the third quarter volume fell USD 6.7 million, or four percent, on softness in China and Europe while price and mix added USD 5.4 million. Because the ten largest customers take about 90% of sales and buy on purchase orders, a price concession or a lost customer would move earnings quickly, and the annual report names sealed fuel tanks and electric vehicles as substitutes that would cut demand over time.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ngvt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 16–17; 10-K_2025-12-31_ngvt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 27–28; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ngvt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 42; calculation B3

Pavement Technologies depends on government infrastructure spending through about 600 customers, earns 70% to 75% of revenue between April and September and reported that higher asphalt costs limited sales growth, so its sales excluding road markings rose only 1% in the third quarter. Advanced Polymer Technologies raised sales USD 14.9 million in nine months mainly through price and mix of USD 9.9 million including surcharges, which recovered input costs with little margin gain, and it faces new entrants and competing materials.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margins can be defended in Performance Materials but are squeezed elsewhere. Group gross margin was 44.3% in nine months of 2026 against 40.9%, a rise of 3.3 percentage points that the quarterly report attributes to price and mix of USD 29.3 million, partly offset by volume and manufacturing costs. Manufacturing costs rose USD 8.6 million in Pavement Technologies and USD 8.0 million in Advanced Polymer Technologies, so those segments cannot yet pass costs through at a gain, whereas Performance Materials cut manufacturing cost by USD 8.3 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ngvt-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 38–39; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ngvt-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 42–44; calculation B8

The cost base is energy- and raw-material-intensive: natural gas powers carbon and chemical plants and is partly hedged, lignin and tall oil fatty acid are in limited supply, hydrogen peroxide comes from one named supplier, and several products are made at one plant, so operating leverage is high if volume falls. Management expects about USD 20 million of stranded costs from the two disposals to fall by 75% by the end of 2026, with 65% already removed on an annualised basis, which would support the guided margins if delivered.

### Porter's five forces

Rivalry is moderate where the company has differentiation and higher where it has not. In activated carbon the annual report names Norit, Kuraray, domestic producers and Chinese manufacturers, in pavement Nouryon, Arkema and Zydex, and in caprolactone Daicel, Hunan Juren and BASF, so Performance Materials margin of 55.6% implies protection that the other two segments, at 17.0% and 22.0%, do not show. Rivalry therefore threatens margin mainly in the segments that earn 11.8% of group Adjusted EBITDA after corporate costs.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ngvt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_ngvt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 19–20; calculation B2; calculation B3

The threat of new entrants is low in automotive carbon on the company's account of decades of meeting life-of-vehicle standards, but real in caprolactone, where new market entrants are cited, and Chinese manufacturers compete in carbon. The threat of substitutes is the largest long-term force: sealed fuel tanks reduce honeycomb use and electric and hydrogen vehicles reduce carbon demand, and competing monomers, thermoplastics and polyols challenge caprolactone, so substitution would hit the 88.2% earnings concentration directly.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ngvt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_ngvt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 27; calculation B2

Buyer power is high in automotive carbon, with about 65 customers, ten taking about 90% of sales and purchase-order terms, and low in pavement, with about 600 customers and the ten largest at about 38% of the line. ### PEST factors

Regulatory and political factors are material. Emission standards set carbon demand and the annual report names the risk that standards are not tightened, retaliatory tariffs by China could lower margin on carbon and chemicals exported from the United States, and pavement demand follows government infrastructure budgets. Economic factors are also material: crude-oil-driven asphalt costs limited pavement growth, 43% of FY2025 sales were outside the United States with exposure to the euro, yen, pound, real and renminbi, and a hypothetical 10 percent adverse move in three currencies would cut nine-month net sales by USD 11.1 million. Technological change is the most important: electric and hydrogen vehicles and sealed tanks threaten automotive carbon demand over time, and social factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver.

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

In the next 12 to 24 months management guides net sales of about USD 1.1 billion and Adjusted EBITDA of USD 385 million to USD 395 million for 2026, with Performance Materials margins in the mid-50s percent, pavement in the mid-teens and Advanced Polymer Technologies near 20%, which implies fourth-quarter Adjusted EBITDA of USD 70.5 million to USD 80.5 million. Tailwinds are price and mix gains, the stranded cost removal, lower cash interest and the January 2027 finance lease being funded from a restricted trust; headwinds are weaker automotive volume in China and Europe, asphalt costs, tariffs and a possible Advanced Polymer Technologies sale that removes earnings.

### Downside scenario

The downside path starts with weaker gasoline and hybrid vehicle build in China and Europe, already visible as a USD 6.7 million third-quarter volume loss, combined with tariff retaliation that can lower margin on carbon exported from the United States to China. The first operational break is lower carbon volume that price and mix can no longer offset, because buyers on purchase orders can resist increases; an illustrative 20% fall in Performance Materials EBITDA removes USD 67.9 million from a base of USD 339.5 million.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ngvt-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 42; 10-K_2025-12-31_ngvt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 25; 10-K_2025-12-31_ngvt-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 28; calculation B4

The financial transmission is Adjusted EBITDA of USD 316.9 million, net debt to Adjusted EBITDA of 3.35x on unchanged net debt and cash interest cover of 5.48x, with last-twelve-month free cash flow of about USD 54.7 million before any tax effect against USD 125.3 million of repurchases in nine months. Liquidity of USD 398.2 million still covers near-term maturities 5.21x, so a liquidity crisis is not implied, but the stress lands on the 2028 refinancing: leverage near 3.35x with an unfinished sale and no rating or market price makes refinancing the USD 550.0 million notes by 2028-08-02 harder.

Management has levers: suspending repurchases would conserve about USD 155.6 million a year at the third-quarter pace, capital expenditure can fall toward maintenance of USD 34.8 million, and an Advanced Polymer Technologies sale above USD 99.2 million applied to debt offsets lost earnings. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress in this scenario if the levers are used, and the observable triggers are a fall in Performance Materials volumes beyond four percent, a quarterly Adjusted EBITDA run rate below the guided range, repurchases continuing while free cash flow is below them, and no refinancing plan for the 2028 notes by the 2027 annual report.
Basis: calculation B16; 10-K_2025-12-31_ngvt-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 67–68; calculation B6; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ngvt-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 46–47

### Overall credit view

Overall, Ingevity presents an adequate business profile supported by a high-margin automotive carbon franchise with pricing power, moderate leverage of 2.75x and liquidity of USD 398.2 million, but credit quality is constrained by earnings concentration, shareholder returns above free cash flow and an unfinished portfolio reshaping. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because price and mix, a flexible capital expenditure budget and a shrinking debt balance offset volume softness, although free cash flow swung between 14% and 73% of Adjusted EBITDA. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on whether an Advanced Polymer Technologies sale repays debt, on the pace of repurchases, and on handling the USD 550.0 million 2028 notes before the revolver test on 2028-08-02. Primary downside path: weaker automotive volume in Asia and Europe lowering Performance Materials EBITDA, which carries 88.2% of Adjusted EBITDA, while repurchases and an unfinished sale leave leverage higher into the 2028 refinancing. Further analysis would require the financing agreements, the proxy statement, the use of disposal proceeds, a rating or bond price and any sale agreement for Advanced Polymer Technologies. Top risk: concentration of earnings in automotive carbon combined with the 2028 refinancing.
