## Findings

### Bottom line

Open Text has no sponsor and a mostly independent board, but it returned 91.35% of fiscal 2026 free cash flow to shareholders while a new chief executive and an open strategic review leave its direction unsettled, so governance risk is Medium.

Documented behaviour is mixed rather than adverse: USD 613.0 million of term loan prepayments from divestiture proceeds and cash, and an October 2026 refinancing that extended maturities, sit beside a portfolio being sold down and a share count cut by 4.97% in a year. No document shows priming, an uptier or another creditor-adverse step, but the legal capacity to distribute is not established because the executed agreements are not supplied.
Basis: calculation C3; calculation C4; 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 214; 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 126; 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 219

### Key findings

- Control sits with public shareholders, not a sponsor, and no holder is large enough to dominate the board. The four holders above 5% own 33.13% between them, the largest 11.17%, and directors and executive officers as a group 2.42%. Eleven of thirteen directors are independent; the chief executive and the chair are not. Creditors therefore face no sponsor extraction or exit-timing risk, but there is also no controlling holder whose support could be called on in stress.
  Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 214; 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 147; 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 219; calculation C5
- Shareholder distributions, not debt-funded transactions, are the main governance risk to creditors. In fiscal 2026 dividends, share repurchases and treasury stock purchases took USD 737.67 million, which is 91.35% of free cash flow of USD 807.52 million, and the share count fell 4.97%. The quarterly dividend of USD 0.28 per share continues and a renewed repurchase plan allows purchases of up to 10% of the public float. Because these payments take nearly all internally generated cash, debt reduction relies on asset sales and cash on hand, not retained free cash flow.
  Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 236–237; 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 136; 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 117; 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 127; 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 310; 10-Q_2026-09-30_otex-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 43; calculation C3; calculation C4

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for lower risk is behaviour: the company prepaid USD 613.0 million of its term loan in fiscal 2026, extended its nearest maturities in October 2026, has no history in these documents of exchange offers, priming or waivers, and says its focus is cash generation, debt reduction and capital allocation. That argues for Low. Evidence for High would be debt-funded distributions or acquisitions after the strategic review, use of the universal shelf registration filed in December 2025, or a repurchase programme that outruns free cash flow.
Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 126; 10-Q_2026-09-30_otex-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 43–44; EX-99.1_2026-08-06_q4-26pressreleaseexhibit991.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 126

The view moves to Low if the investor day sets a leverage target and ranks debt reduction ahead of repurchases, and to High if the next year shows buybacks or acquisitions financed by new debt. The legal documents, which would show how much value can leave, are the missing evidence.
Basis: EX-99.1_2026-11-05_q1-27pressreleaseexhibit991.htm.jsonl, page 4; 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 126–127

### Limitations

- The legal capacity to pay dividends, repurchase shares, add debt or release guarantees is not established because no executed agreement is supplied, so the Medium level rests on documented behaviour and would move if baskets prove wide or tight.
  Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 118–123
- The documents show conduct only from fiscal 2023, so the absence of exchange offers, priming or waivers describes these documents, not the company's full history; an earlier episode would raise the risk level.
  Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 19

  Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 214
- The sums invested in the strategic funds in which the company holds up to about 19.9% limited partner interests are not stated, so related-party leakage can be described but not sized, and the governance conclusion on conflicts stays qualitative.
  Basis: 10-K_2026-06-30_otex-20260630.htm.jsonl, page 218

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The latest large acquisition was Micro Focus, completed on 31 January 2023 for USD 6.2 billion, financed in part with the Acquisition Term Loan, which allowed commitments of up to USD 3.585 billion, and USD 1.0 billion of secured notes due 2027. Since then the group has sold businesses and used the proceeds to repay debt: the Application Modernization and Connectivity business for USD 2.275 billion in May 2024, eDOCS for USD 163.0 million in January 2026 and Vertica for USD 150.0 million in May 2026. In fiscal 2026 term loan prepayments reached USD 613.0 million, including USD 300.0 million from cash.
