## Findings

### Bottom line

Open Text is an adequate credit: recurring software revenue and no note maturity before 2028 protect liquidity, but a shrinking earnings base and payouts taking 91.35% of free cash flow leave creditors relying on asset sales, not retained cash, to cut debt.

Net debt was 2.90x reported EBITDA at 30 September 2026 against 2.85x at the 2026 year-end, so deleveraging has paused, and pro forma net debt after the 1 and 2 October 2026 events is not established. The outlook lowers the adjusted EBITDA margin to 32% to 33% from 36.3% and free cash flow to USD 625 million to USD 725 million from USD 807.52 million, which is the main reason leverage rises unless debt falls.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is operating momentum and execution on debt. Core revenue grew 2.4% and core cloud revenue 11.2% in the latest quarter, enterprise cloud bookings rose 22.55%, free cash flow rose in the latest quarter, and the company cut debt by USD 607.55 million in fiscal 2026 and completed a refinancing within a day of the quarter-end. The guided margin step-down is also hard to reconcile with a margin of 35.8% in the quarter just reported: the outlook implies about 30.80% to 32.13% for the remaining quarters, so if the company simply repeats the latest quarter, adjusted EBITDA ends well above the outlook and leverage falls. The view would improve if the next two quarters hold a margin close to the latest quarter's 35.8%, if cloud net renewal returns to 96% from 94%, or if the investor day names a leverage target and ranks debt reduction ahead of repurchases.

### Company and operating model

Open Text supplies data management software to large and regulated organisations, so its credit case rests on how sticky that revenue is. Cloud services and customer support, which renew, made up 80.93% of fiscal 2026 revenue, enterprise clients contribute about 90% of revenue, no customer exceeded a tenth of receivables, and the United States is 49.89% of revenue with most of the rest in Europe, the Middle East and Africa. That mix supports cash-flow visibility and debt service, but growth now has to come from moving support clients to cloud, which carries a lower gross margin.

The group has changed shape since the Micro Focus acquisition for USD 6.2 billion in January 2023: it sold the Application Modernization and Connectivity business for USD 2,275.0 million in May 2024 and two more businesses for USD 313.0 million in fiscal 2026, and used the proceeds to repay the term loan. The earnings base is smaller and rests on the four core categories, 76.79% of revenue, while the lines that are shrinking or being sold are 23.21%; further disposals are possible after the strategic review, which would shrink the base again.

### Revenue durability and pricing power

Revenue is mostly recurring and well covered for twelve months, but the base is not growing. Recurring revenue is 80.93% of the total, support agreements run for twelve months and renew at the client's option, and contracted revenue of USD 4.6 billion has 57% falling due within a year. The support net renewal rate rose to 93% from 91%, while the cloud net renewal rate fell to 94% from 96%, and both exclude small-business and consumer clients and migrations to cloud. Because net renewal is below full retention, some recurring revenue has to be replaced every year; enterprise cloud bookings of USD 946.7 million against USD 772.5 million, up 22.55%, are the main source of replacement, so durability beyond a year depends on bookings continuing to outrun support attrition.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Margin has been defended by cost cuts, not by pricing, and the room left is limited. Gross margin was 73.74%, with 90% on customer support and 64% on cloud, so each dollar that moves from support to cloud lowers gross margin by about 26 percentage points before any cost saving. Research and development and sales and marketing together fell 1.75% in fiscal 2026 as the Business Optimization Plan removed labour cost, and the adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 36.3% from 34.1% in fiscal 2024. The plan's remaining savings are USD 147.00 million to USD 165.00 million and its remaining cost is USD 23.80 million, after the expected total cost was raised to USD 270.0 million from USD 260.0 million; special charges were USD 33.29 million in the latest quarter against USD 20.14 million.

### Competitive forces

Competitive rivalry is intense and widening. The company names IBM, Microsoft, Oracle and ServiceNow among large rivals, competes with Microsoft while also partnering with it, and says AI has extended competition to platform providers and specialist knowledge platforms, so pricing and renewal terms are contested in every category. New entrants matter mainly through AI: they target the same unstructured-data workloads, which pressures the cloud net renewal rate that already slipped to 94%. Substitutes are the sharpest threat, because the company itself warns that clients may build internal AI alternatives, which would hit licence and support revenue first.
