## Findings

### Bottom line

Dorman is a public aftermarket parts supplier with no sponsor, parent or dividend, so creditors face business concentration and discretionary buyback risk rather than owner extraction, which supports a medium governance risk level.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 35, 48, 95; 10-Q_2026-09-26_dorm.htm.jsonl, page 28

Two customers took about 40% of 2025 net sales, all within Light Duty, which supplied 79.43% of sales and 91.57% of segment profit. Repurchases reached USD 113.8 million in nine months 2026, yet the executed financing documents that would show how much the company may distribute were not supplied. All three segment heads and the finance chief changed between February 2025 and May 2026, and the leases with related parties expire in 2027.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 43, 47, 95; 10-Q_2026-09-26_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 28; calculation N1; calculation N2; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

Ownership risk is low because there is no sponsor, parent or controlling holder, but the Chairman's family stake is influential and shrinking. The family beneficially owned about 13% of the common stock in February 2026, against about 15% a year earlier and about 16% at the end of 2023, and the company itself says the stake lets the family influence board elections and delay a change in control. A sale plan adopted in September 2026 covers 0.66% of shares outstanding, so the consequence for creditors is the absence of a fund exit clock, not a sign of extraction.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 35, 95; 10-K_2024-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 40, 99; 10-Q_2026-09-26_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 1, 31; calculation N15

Shareholder returns are the main discretionary drain on creditor cash, and the company's capacity to make them cannot be tested. No dividend is paid, but repurchases cost USD 113.8 million in nine months 2026, which is 48.28% of operating cash flow less capital spending and 83.66% once USD 99.7 million of tariff refunds and related interest, which came from one court ruling, is removed; USD 348.3 million of the USD 500.0 million authorisation remains to the end of 2027. Willingness to return cash is documented, while the limits that the new credit agreement and indenture place on it are not, so creditors cannot say how much of the remaining authorisation is permitted.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, page 100; 10-Q_2026-09-26_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 4, 5, 12, 28, 30; calculation N12; calculation N13; calculation N16; Source readiness (CP-0)

Business risk is concentrated in one segment and two customers. Light Duty supplied 79.43% of 2025 sales and 91.57% of segment profit, and the two customers that each exceeded 10% of sales took about 40% of the company total, about half of Light Duty sales. Their contracts typically carry no purchase commitment, the company records that large customers press for lower prices, longer payment terms and higher rebates, and customer credit provisions were USD 404.2 million in 2025, 18.97% of net sales. The four largest customers also owed 78% of receivables at the end of 2025. A loss or repricing at either large account would hit the segment that earns almost all segment profit.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 21, 22, 34, 79, 80, 86, 95, 111; calculation N1; calculation N2; calculation N5; calculation N17

Heavy Duty and Specialty Vehicle give little earnings cushion, and the acquisition that built Specialty Vehicle has so far returned little against its price. Heavy Duty made USD 5.11 million of segment profit on USD 232.59 million of 2025 sales, wrote off all USD 56.71 million of its goodwill in the fourth quarter of 2025 and reported a small segment loss in the third quarter of 2026. Specialty Vehicle, bought in 2022 for USD 509.8 million, earned USD 26.85 million of segment profit in 2025, equal to 5.27% of that price and 15.08% below 2023, and the earn-out of up to USD 100.0 million carried no liability at the end of 2023 or 2024.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 59, 83, 86–87; 10-K_2024-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 85, 95; 10-Q_2026-09-26_dorm.htm.jsonl, page 8; calculation N2; calculation N7; calculation N8; calculation N9

Related-party leakage is small in cash, and the continuity risks are the 2027 lease expiries and the turnover in senior roles. Rent and services paid to entities tied to the Chairman and to the former head of Specialty Vehicle were USD 3.59 million in 2025, 0.17% of net sales. The leases for the Madison and Shreveport sites that house specialty vehicle manufacturing and warehousing, and the Chairman-related Lewisberry lease, all expire in 2027 with renewal terms not stated. The three segment heads and the finance chief changed between February 2025 and May 2026, and the Chairman title moved to the chief executive, so board independence, which the missing proxy statement leaves unestablished, becomes a monitoring point.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 42, 43, 47, 65, 88; 10-K_2024-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, page 49; 10-Q_2026-03-28_dorm.htm.jsonl, page 26; EX99.1_2026-03-28_dorm_dorm-20260328xexx991-htm.htm.jsonl, page 1; 10-Q_2026-09-26_dorm.htm.jsonl, page 31; calculation N10

Financing is now long-dated and plain, but ranking and distribution capacity rest on documents that were not supplied. The June 2026 refinancing replaced a USD 600.0 million revolver and a term loan due October 2027 with an undrawn USD 800.0 million secured revolver due June 2031 and USD 450.0 million of 6.25% senior notes due June 2034, guaranteed by the same subsidiaries that guarantee the credit agreement; whether the notes are secured is not stated. Management calls acquisitions a key part of strategy, and the secured net leverage covenant of 3.50x rises to 4.00x for four quarters after certain acquisitions, so a large debt-funded purchase is the event to watch.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-26_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 16, 26; 10-K_2025-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, page 85; 10-Q_2026-06-27_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 32; Source readiness (CP-0)

Reporting quality supports monitoring of operations. The auditor has served since 2002 and gave unqualified opinions on the statements and on internal control, and its one critical audit matter was the accrual for customer credits, USD 197.4 million at the end of 2025, the reserve most sensitive to customer behaviour.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 69–70; calculation N17

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest case for a lower risk level is that nothing in the eight documents shows extraction or creditor-adverse conduct. There is no dividend, no management, monitoring or advisory fee to a shareholder, and no waiver, forbearance or exchange offer; the refinancing lengthened maturities, and the revolver was undrawn with USD 798.9 million available at the end of September 2026. Repurchases were paid from operating cash flow, since cash rose by USD 117.6 million over the nine months after USD 113.8 million of buybacks, and the share count fell by 2.69% between the end of 2025 and the end of September 2026.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-26_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 4, 5, 26, 28; calculation N20; calculation N21; calculation N24

Evidence for a higher level is the pace and the dependence: repurchases absorbed most of free cash flow once the refund effect is removed, two customers hold about 40% of sales, and the financial institutions that run the customer-sponsored receivable programs can modify or terminate them. The view would move to low if the executed documents show binding limits on restricted payments and the proxy statement shows an independent majority board. It would move to high if repurchases keep running above underlying free cash flow after the refunds end, if the revolver is drawn to fund them, or if either large customer cuts volume or a receivable program is withdrawn.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 33, 95; 10-Q_2026-09-26_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 25, 26; calculation N13; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

In June 2026 Dorman refinanced its whole debt stack. Amendment No. 3 to the credit agreement, dated 16 June 2026, replaced the USD 600.0 million revolver with a five-year USD 800.0 million revolver due 16 June 2031, guaranteed by the material wholly owned domestic subsidiaries and secured on substantially all their personal property and assets. The company also issued USD 450.0 million of 6.25% senior notes due 16 June 2034 under an indenture of the same date, with interest paid each June and December from December 2026. The proceeds repaid the USD 431.3 million term loan balance with accrued interest, paid advisory fees and went to general corporate purposes. The notes are guaranteed by each existing and future wholly owned subsidiary that guarantees the credit agreement; whether they are secured, who the guarantors are and the revolver margin are not stated. At 26 September 2026 the revolver was undrawn, letters of credit were USD 1.1 million and USD 798.9 million was available. The maintenance covenants are secured net leverage of 3.50x, rising to 4.00x for four quarters after certain acquisitions, and interest coverage of 2.00x.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-06-27_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 32; 10-Q_2026-09-26_dorm.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 26; 10-K_2025-12-31_dorm.htm.jsonl, page 85

The last large transaction was the 4 October 2022 purchase of SuperATV, a powersports aftermarket supplier, for USD 509.8 million net of USD 6.8 million of cash acquired, with an earn-out of up to USD 100.0 million tied to 2023 and 2024 revenue and gross margin targets. It was funded in cash through a refinancing of the existing credit facility, which brought a USD 500.0 million term loan and a USD 600.0 million revolver. The earn-out carried no liability at the end of 2023 or 2024, and the maximum remaining contingent payment across acquisitions fell from USD 102.0 million in the 2024 annual report to USD 2.0 million in the 2025 report. Purchase accounting assigned USD 247.5 million to goodwill and USD 157.5 million to identifiable intangibles. In 2026 the company paid USD 4.5 million for a business in the third quarter and reported a USD 1.5 million bargain purchase gain, and it sold an investment in the second quarter for USD 7.2 million; the target and the investment are not named.
