## Findings

### Bottom line

Group 1 is a franchised car retailer with no disclosed controlling owner, so creditors depend on management's capital allocation and on manufacturer approvals, and the USD 1.3 billion Hennessy purchase, funded by new unsecured notes, is the largest test of that discretion. Parts, service and finance and insurance earned 70.55% of gross profit in the first nine months of 2026, which steadies earnings against the vehicle cycle, but 2025 shareholder payouts of USD 580.4 million exceeded net income of USD 325.2 million.
Basis: calculation C2; calculation C7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 29

Governance and ownership risk is medium. The Conifer stockholder agreement caps that holder at 19%, one percentage point under the 20% change-of-control level that the company cites for some manufacturer agreements, so the pace of buybacks is tied to franchise approvals, while ownership percentages, director independence and related-party dealings are not established because the proxy statement was not supplied.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 29; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 64; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 66; Source readiness (CP-0)

### Key findings

- Aftersales and finance and insurance, not vehicle sales, carry the profit, so earnings are steadier than the revenue mix implies. New vehicles were 48.39% of revenues but 19.90% of gross profit in the first nine months of 2026, while parts and service and finance and insurance together were 70.55% of gross profit, against 69.58% in 2025 and 67.77% in 2024. The cushion is wearing at the edges: gross profit fell 5.6% as used retail units fell 8.7% and new retail units fell 4.8%, finance and insurance gross profit fell 8.0%, and selling, general and administrative expenses rose to 71.0% of gross profit from 69.8%.
Basis: calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C3; calculation C20; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 35–36
- No sponsor or controlling shareholder is disclosed, which removes sponsor-extraction risk but also any sponsor backstop. The largest named holder, Conifer, has agreed to a 19% ceiling, a standstill through at least the 2029 annual meeting and to vote with the board during a support period, and it can designate one director while it holds at least 5%; its actual percentage is not disclosed, and when the support period ends the standstill lapses and its voting commitment narrows to holdings above 20%. More than half of issued shares, 52.04%, are already held in treasury, so each further buyback raises every remaining holder's percentage, Conifer's included.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 28–29; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 66; calculation C9
- Shareholder payouts, not operations, are the discretionary credit risk, and they fell sharply in 2026. In 2025 repurchases of USD 554.8 million and dividends of USD 25.6 million came to 178.47% of net income and 136.73% of free cash flow, in a year when acquisitions absorbed a further USD 546.8 million. In the first nine months of 2026 repurchases fell to USD 72.4 million from USD 249.8 million a year earlier, all in the first quarter; none followed in the second or third quarter and the filings give no reason. The dividend rate was raised 10% to USD 2.20 a share, and USD 306.3 million of authorization remains with no expiry.

- The Hennessy purchase raises acquisition, integration and concentration risk more than it adds diversification. At approximately USD 1.3 billion it is 2.51x the Inchcape consideration, it adds 10 dealerships representing 12 franchises in one metropolitan market, and purchase accounting was incomplete when the report was issued. It was funded by USD 1,250.0 million of unsecured senior notes issued at par on 2026-09-22 plus cash on hand, which add about USD 80 million of annual interest expense; a USD 1.25 billion bridge commitment was terminated unused, and a seller indemnity escrow of USD 80.0 million, released in three stages over 18 months, cushions post-closing claims. Disposals are recycling capital: seven U.S. dealerships are held for sale and three were sold after quarter-end for about USD 235 million, but the filings do not say how the proceeds were used.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a medium risk judgement is behavioural: nothing in the filings shows value moving to a controlling holder, repurchases stopped after the first quarter of 2026, the dividend rose only 10%, Hennessy was funded with fixed-rate notes and cash with the bridge left undrawn, disposals brought USD 481.8 million of proceeds in nine months, and the largest named holder accepted a standstill. Against a comfortable view, payouts exceeded earnings in 2025, the purchase concentrates integration risk in one market before any acquired earnings are reported, and goodwill and franchise rights equal 94.78% of equity before Hennessy's goodwill is added.
Basis: calculation C7; calculation C10; calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-03-31_gpi-20260331.htm.jsonl, page 41; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 7; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 61; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 68

The view would worsen if repurchases resumed while Hennessy is being integrated, if a manufacturer invoked a change-of-control clause or refused a franchise transfer, if the proxy statement showed related-party dealings or a board without independent majority, or if the acquired dealerships needed an early impairment. It would improve if disposal proceeds were shown to repay debt, if Conifer's holding stayed clear of the 19% cap, and if post-closing accounts showed the acquired earnings carrying the added interest.
Basis: calculation C12; calculation C14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 64; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 66–67

### Limitations

- Ownership percentages, director independence and related-party dealings are not established because the proxy statement was not supplied; this limits the medium governance judgement, which would move to high if leakage to insiders or a conflicted board were disclosed and to low if the proxy showed none.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gpi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 82; Source readiness (CP-0)
- The Hennessy dealerships are not in any supplied balance sheet or income statement, so the acquisition-risk finding rests on the company's description and not on acquired earnings, goodwill or cash paid from balance sheet cash.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 67
- Manufacturer mix of new vehicle sales appears only as a chart that did not carry into the text, so the manufacturer dependency finding names Toyota and Lexus as the largest share but cannot size it or show how far Toyota's reduction of dealer support reaches.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_gpi-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 29
- The 19% cap, the 20% threshold and the buyback link come from the company's own summary of the stockholder agreement and the manufacturer agreements, which were not supplied, so the exact triggers and waiver rights could change how binding the buyback constraint is.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

On 2026-07-30 Group 1 agreed to buy the assets of 10 automobile dealerships and one collision center in Greater Atlanta, Georgia, with related real estate and leaseholds, and completed the purchase on 2026-10-19 for approximately USD 1.3 billion, subject to post-closing adjustments. USD 80.0 million of the price sits in an indemnity escrow, with 25% released three months after closing, a further 25% after nine months and the remainder after 18 months, subject to pending claims. The purchase is accounted for as a business combination, but the allocation of the price, the goodwill and the pro forma information were incomplete when the report was issued, and manufacturers may impose conditions on the transfer of the 12 franchises.
Basis: calculation C13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 67

The purchase was financed with the net proceeds of two private placements on 2026-09-22, each of USD 625.0 million issued at par: notes due February 2032 and notes due February 2035, together with cash on hand. The notes are unsecured, guaranteed by substantially all U.S. subsidiaries, subordinated to senior secured debt and to the liabilities of non-guarantor subsidiaries, and may have to be repurchased on certain asset sales or a change of control; issuance costs were USD 15.9 million and the company expects about USD 80 million of added annual interest expense. A 364-day senior unsecured bridge commitment of up to USD 1.25 billion from JPMorgan, obtained on 2026-07-30, was terminated unused at closing, and the part of the price paid from cash on hand is not stated.
Basis: calculation C12; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 13; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 22–24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_gpi-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 67
