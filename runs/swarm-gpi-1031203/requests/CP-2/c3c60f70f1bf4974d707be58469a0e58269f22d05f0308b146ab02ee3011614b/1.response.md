### Company description

Group 1 sells new and used vehicles, arranges finance and insurance, services vehicles and sells parts through franchised dealerships, with 145 U.S. and 104 U.K. dealerships at 2026-09-30 and 10 more in Greater Atlanta from 2026-10-19. In the first nine months of 2026 revenue was USD 16,188.8 million, of which new vehicles were 48.39%, but new vehicles produced only 19.90% of gross profit; parts and service produced 45.54% and finance and insurance 25.01%. The business model therefore turns a low-margin, cyclical vehicle flow into a steadier annuity of service and finance income, which is what supports cash-flow visibility and debt service, while the vehicle flow, funded by floorplan debt, is what moves with consumer affordability and manufacturer supply.

### Ownership and group structure

Group 1 is a listed company with no disclosed sponsor or controlling holder, which removes sponsor extraction as a risk but also any sponsor backstop. Conifer, one of its largest holders, signed a stockholder agreement on 2026-09-21 that caps its holding at 19%, binds it to vote with the board during a support period and gives it one board seat, scheduled from 2026-11-01, while it holds at least 5%; some manufacturer framework agreements treat ownership above thresholds as low as 20% as a change of control. The notes are issued by the parent and guaranteed by substantially all U.S. subsidiaries, the U.K. subsidiaries are not stated as guarantors, and the Bank of America mortgage loans are borrowed by a separate real estate subsidiary. The implication is that financial policy rests with management and the board, that buybacks are tied to a ceiling and to manufacturer approvals, and that creditors of non-guarantor subsidiaries, which may include U.K. lenders, rank ahead of the notes on those subsidiaries' assets.

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is driven by unit volumes and by price and mix, and in 2026 volumes are the weak side: in the first nine months retail new units fell 4.8% and retail used units 8.7%, while new vehicle prices rose 2.6% and used prices 4.2%, so revenue fell 4.7% and gross profit fell 5.61%. Management and the filing attribute the fall to affordability pressure, inventory limits on some brands and, in the U.S., a strong comparison period before tariffs; in the U.S. same-store parts and service gross profit was flat while same-store finance and insurance gross profit fell 9.6% on fewer units and lower penetration. Pricing power over new vehicles is limited because competitors buy from the same manufacturers on the same terms, so the earnings that can be defended are aftersales and finance income, and these depend on the number of vehicles sold and serviced and on warranty work billed to manufacturers. The credit implication is that revenue is cyclical and discretionary at the vehicle line and recurring at the service line, which supports a durable cash-flow floor but not growth.

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Selling, general and administrative expenses are the largest operating cost below gross profit and absorbed 71.04% of gross profit in nine months of 2026 against 69.82% a year earlier and 67.24% in 2024, so costs have fallen more slowly than gross profit; the U.S. same-store ratio rose to 69.1% from 65.9%. The company cut nearly 700 U.S. full-time positions in April 2026 and says it exceeded a target of USD 50 million of annual savings, and part of pay is commission that falls with gross profit, which gives some cost flexibility, but the quarter still ended at 72.32%. Floorplan interest of USD 66.3 million in nine months was almost fully offset by manufacturer assistance of USD 64.9 million, 97.89% of the cost, and one large manufacturer has said it will reduce such assistance, which would move straight into cost of sales. Margins can be defended against modest volume pressure, but not against a combined fall in volumes, assistance and finance income without a further cost programme.

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is high and local: franchised competitors have access to the same vehicles on the same terms and Group 1 has no purchasing cost advantage, so competition falls on location, service and price, and the weak pricing power in new vehicles shows in new vehicle gross profit per unit, which fell 5.6%. Entry by manufacturers selling directly, and the agency model in the U.K. under which the dealer earns a fee and records no vehicle sale, inventory or floorplan interest, threaten the vehicle line and would shrink both revenue and floorplan debt, so the service line would carry more of the profit. Buyers are price-aware and affordability-constrained, which reduces units. Manufacturers hold the strongest position among the forces because they allocate vehicles, set franchise terms and fund floorplan assistance, so supplier power over margins and over the right to operate is high. The credit implication is that margin durability rests on aftersales and not on the vehicle line, and that manufacturer decisions are a likely external trigger for a step change in earnings.

### Qualitative downside scenario

The initiating condition is a further fall in vehicle demand from affordability pressure, higher rates and tariff-driven prices, with a manufacturer reducing dealer assistance. The first operational break is gross profit: vehicle gross profit and finance income fall with units, as new units fell 3.5% and used units 10.5% in the third quarter, while costs stay sticky, with selling, general and administrative expenses at 72.32% of gross profit in the quarter. The financial transmission is lower EBITDA and lower operating cash flow, with payables unwinding as in 2026, and more floorplan interest cost as assistance falls. On pro forma net debt of USD 4,304.7 million, EBITDA at the 2025 level of USD 855.1 million gives 5.03x, and a further 5.61% fall in last-twelve-months gross profit with costs unchanged gives EBITDA of USD 715.9 million and 6.01x. The consequences are tighter covenant availability on the Acquisition Line, little free cash flow to repay the purchase and a 2028 refinancing at higher coupons. Management levers are the unused buyback authorization of USD 306.3 million, the remaining held-for-sale dealerships, deferral of discretionary capital spending of USD 244.9 million a year and further cost cuts. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress, since the price is 52.48% of reported liquidity, but both scenarios leave leverage above the pro forma 4.73x. The observable triggers are the quarterly gross profit and cost ratio, floorplan assistance, used units and covenant ratios.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Group 1 presents an adequate business profile supported by aftersales and finance income, scale and owned property, but credit quality is constrained by leverage of about 4.73x pro forma, falling gross profit and thin free cash flow. Cash-flow durability appears weak for the group, due to free cash flow of only 24.86% of EBITDA in the last twelve months, although the service line is steadier than the vehicle line. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on disposals reducing debt, the earnings of the acquired dealerships and the pricing of the 2028 refinancing, with USD 750.0 million due in about 21 months. The primary downside path is a further fall in vehicle gross profit with sticky costs and reduced manufacturer assistance after the purchase has consumed liquidity. Further analysis would require the post-closing balance sheet, acquired earnings, covenant definitions, a rating and peer financials. Top risk: leverage after the Hennessy purchase.
