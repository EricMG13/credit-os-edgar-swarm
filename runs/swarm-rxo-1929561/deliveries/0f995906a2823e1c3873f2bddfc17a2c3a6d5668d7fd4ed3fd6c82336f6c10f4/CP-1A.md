## Findings

### Bottom line

RXO is an asset-light freight broker with moderate customer concentration and no dividend history, but its pending sale to competitor C.H. Robinson makes control, not operations, the main creditor question, and the filings do not say how the debt is treated.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 31; 10-K_2025-12-31_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 45

The business earns a spread between what shippers pay and what carriers are paid, so the pressure point is margin: in the third quarter of 2026 the cost of transportation rose to 82.2% of revenue from 80.0% as buy rates outran contract sell rates. The largest customer was 11.4% of 2025 revenue and the top five 23%, so concentration is moderate. Governance evidence is thin because board independence, ownership and related-party detail sit in a proxy statement that was not supplied.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, page 21; 10-K_2025-12-31_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 103

### Key findings

- Operating risk sits in the spread between buy rates and sell rates, not in assets. Truck brokerage was 73.6% of 2025 revenue and 76.5% of nine-month 2026 revenue, its purchased transportation is a variable cost, and when capacity tightened the cost of transportation climbed to 82.2% of third-quarter revenue from 80.0%; margin, not volume, is what protects debt service.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rxo.htm.jsonl, page 85; 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 21; calculation C01
- Customer concentration is moderate and end markets are spread. The largest customer was 11.4% of 2025 revenue, the top five 23% and the top 20 37%, and retail and e-commerce, the largest vertical, was 37.4%, so losing one customer would not remove most of the revenue base.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 85; calculation C02
- Governance looks conventional but is under-evidenced. RXO has never paid a cash dividend, funded the 2024 Coyote acquisition with new equity rather than debt, and reports effective internal controls with a clean audit opinion; against that, the chair and chief executive roles are combined in the 2025 report where the 2024 report named a non-executive chairman, and the filings do not explain the change.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 45, 53, 61, 102, 107; 10-K_2024-12-31_rxo.htm.jsonl, page 114
- The pending sale is the dominant event risk. C.H. Robinson would acquire RXO for cash and shares at an implied USD 5.8 billion, expected to close in the first half of 2027; a holder of about 19% has agreed to vote in favour, RXO owes a USD 175 million termination fee in specified cases, and its freedom to act outside the ordinary course is restricted until closing. Nothing supplied says how the notes or the ABL Facility are treated on closing.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 29–31; calculation C03
- Contingent liabilities sit in last mile. Claims that delivery contractors should be treated as employees are unquantified, largely outside insurance and indemnified to the former parent; settlements to date are accrued and immaterial, so the exposure is a tail risk rather than a current charge.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 14–15; 10-K_2025-12-31_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 100–101
- The acquirer is itself a named primary competitor, so regulatory clearance is part of closing risk, and RXO's customers and carriers may react during the wait.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rxo.htm.jsonl, page 11; 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 28–30

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against concern is that part of the price is paid in cash and that RXO's own record is conservative: an equity-funded acquisition, no dividends, no distressed exchange or covenant waiver described, and a 2026 refinancing that moved maturities to 2031. If the merger agreement shows the notes and the ABL Facility left outstanding with the acquirer's support, or repaid at par or above (no filing states the acquirer's credit standing), the event risk would turn into a credit positive. If it shows RXO bearing acquisition debt, tighter restrictions or a regulatory remedy that sells business lines, the view would worsen. A further rise in the cost of transportation from sustained buy-rate pressure would weaken the view whatever happens to the merger.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 10–12, 16, 29–31; 10-K_2025-12-31_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 45, 53

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

C.H. Robinson Worldwide, Inc. agreed on 2026-10-04 to acquire RXO in a two-step merger that leaves RXO as a wholly owned subsidiary, for USD 17.25 in cash plus 0.0856 C.H. Robinson shares per RXO share, an implied USD 30.25 per share and about USD 5.8 billion in total. Holders may elect cash, shares or the mix, subject to proration, and closing needs approval by holders of a majority of RXO shares, regulatory approvals and customary conditions; the expected close is the first half of 2027. RXO would owe a termination fee of USD 175 million in specified circumstances, may not solicit competing offers beyond limited exceptions, needs C.H. Robinson's consent for actions outside the ordinary course, and has a holder of about 19% committed to vote in favour. The announced USD 5.8 billion is not defined in the filing: the per-share price on the basic share count gives about USD 4,990 million and on diluted weighted shares about USD 5,203 million, so the headline probably includes debt, but that is not stated.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 18–19, 29–32; calculation C03; calculation C04

RXO changed its debt in 2026 before the merger was agreed. On 2026-02-05 it replaced its unsecured revolving credit facility with a USD 450 million asset-based facility secured by a first priority interest in all assets of the credit parties other than real property, and on 2026-02-20 it sold USD 400 million of unsecured notes due 2031 and redeemed all of its notes due 2027 at 101.875% of principal. In the second quarter it added a small non-U.S. revolving credit facility. For creditors the secured facility ranks ahead of the unsecured notes over those assets, while the maturity wall moved from 2027 to 2031.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 10–12; 10-Q_2026-06-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, page 11; Source readiness (CP-0)

The filings state nothing on how the merger affects either instrument: there is no change-of-control term for the notes, no statement of whether C.H. Robinson will refinance, assume or guarantee the debt, and no financing commitment for the cash portion of the price. These terms sit in the merger agreement and the note indenture, which were not supplied, so the effect of the transaction on creditors is not established.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 16, 32; Source readiness (CP-0)
