## Findings

### Bottom line

RXO is an adequate business with a weak financial profile: debt of USD 496 million is 4.28x adjusted but 8.86x reported EBITDA and free cash flow is negative, so USD 360 million of liquidity and a 2031 maturity wall, not earnings, protect creditors.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 9–11; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01; calculation C04; calculation C05

The pending sale to C.H. Robinson is the swing factor, because the filings do not say whether the notes and the secured facility are repaid, assumed or left outstanding; this view is standalone and would change with the merger terms. Earnings are improving, with third-quarter adjusted EBITDA of USD 51 million against USD 30 million a year earlier, but through operating leverage on rate-inflated revenue rather than wider margins.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 16, 21; calculation C12

Revenue rose 18.7% in the nine months to 2026-09-30, but the issuer attributes the third-quarter rise of 31.7% to revenue per load up 48% against 2% more loads, and the only firm obligations it counts beyond a year, fixed-consideration contracts of USD 51 million, are 0.78% of last-twelve-month revenue of USD 6,539 million. Because freight rates and fuel pass through to revenue, debt service rests on gross profit, which rose only 2.77% in the nine months.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 9, 21, 23; calculation C07; calculation C12; calculation C13

Gross profit margin fell from 24.45% to 17.53% and adjusted EBITDA margin from 3.41% to 1.77%. Last-twelve-month adjusted EBITDA of USD 116 million is below the USD 134 million of 2023 on revenue 66.51% higher, so growth has not added earnings.

### Company description

RXO is a brokered transportation platform: it places shippers' freight with independent carriers and earns the spread between what the shipper pays and what the carrier is paid, so the company owns few assets and its cash flow depends on that spread more than on volume. Truck brokerage was 73.6% of 2025 revenue and 76.5% of nine-month 2026 revenue, last mile deliveries of heavy goods (where the issuer describes itself as the largest outsourced provider in the United States) were 20.8% and 18.6%, and managed transportation was 9.6% and 8.5%; about 8% of 2026 revenue arises outside the United States. Pricing is on a contract or spot basis, carriers are signed on non-exclusive one-year agreements and the firm fixed-consideration obligations the issuer counts beyond a year are only USD 51 million.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 85; 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9, 18; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C13; calculation C14

For creditors this means weak cash-flow visibility, because revenue is short-dated and moves with freight rates, and a cost of transportation that moves with carrier rates, so the spread, not revenue, is what carries the debt. It supports liquidity preservation, because capital needs are small and discretionary, but it makes debt service depend on gross profit dollars and on management's ability to cut overhead, not on contracted income. Refinancing confidence rests on liquidity and the 2031 maturity profile rather than on revenue durability.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 24; calculation C13; calculation C15

### Ownership and group structure

RXO is a listed company with no controlling holder: it was spun off from XPO in November 2022, had about 94 registered holders in February 2026 and one named holder of about 19%, MFN Partners LP, which has agreed to vote for the sale. It has never paid a cash dividend, funded the 2024 Coyote acquisition with about USD 1.1 billion of new equity rather than debt, and holds an unused repurchase authorization of USD 123 million, which points to a conservative leverage tolerance in the past. The notes are guaranteed by wholly owned domestic subsidiaries other than excluded subsidiaries, and the secured facility's credit agreement is between three named group companies, guarantors and lenders, with a first-priority security interest in the assets of the credit parties other than real property; the excluded subsidiaries and non-guarantor financials are not supplied, so structural priority cannot be tested.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 45, 53, 106; 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 11–12, 15, 31–32; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C14

The credit implication is that financial policy has been creditor-friendly, but the sale replaces it with the acquirer's policy, which the filings do not describe, and board independence cannot be judged because the proxy statement was not supplied. Creditor alignment under the standalone company is therefore reasonable on record and unproven on governance.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 18, 103; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is cyclical, discretionary, rate-dependent and moderately concentrated, and it is regulated through carrier licensing but not structurally declining. In the third quarter of 2026 revenue rose by USD 450 million, of which truck brokerage supplied USD 394 million, mainly through a 48% rise in revenue per load driven by freight rates and fuel prices, while load volume rose 2%; in the nine months revenue per load rose 34% and the issuer's text on volume contradicts itself. Fuel passes through to revenue over time without a matching rise in gross profit, so higher revenue overstates real growth: gross profit rose 17.25% in the third quarter and 2.77% in the nine months against revenue growth of 31.7% and 18.7%. The consequence for credit is that revenue and receivables swell when rates rise and debt service must be judged on gross profit dollars, not on revenue.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 21, 23; calculation C07; calculation C12; calculation C13

Pricing power is limited in both directions: contract sell rates lag carrier buy rates in a tight market, shippers soliciting bids from several providers can depress freight rates, and the pricing environment becomes more competitive in downturns. Customer concentration is moderate (largest customer 11.4%, top five 23%), retail and e-commerce are 37.4% of revenue, and the logistics and transportation vertical fell to USD 78 million in the third quarter from USD 123 million; the filings give no renewal or churn data.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 11, 21–22, 85; 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9; calculation C14

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Most cost is variable: cost of transportation was 82.47% of nine-month 2026 revenue, so margin is set by the gap between what carriers charge and what customers pay, and the issuer states that capacity leaving the market after regulatory changes and enforcement made buy rates rise faster than contract sell rates in 2025 and 2026, while in 2024 the squeeze came from the other side, when lower freight rates were not fully offset by cheaper purchased transportation. Overhead is 15.62% of nine-month revenue and includes variable compensation, which rose in the third quarter with profit, and restructuring savings with operating leverage took SG&A to 11.9% of third-quarter revenue from 14.6%. Margins cannot be defended against a carrier-rate shock by pricing, as the three-year decline shows, and the defence so far has been overhead reduction, which is finite; against volume pressure the defence is better because carrier cost and variable pay fall with volume, but overhead of USD 1,039 million over the last twelve months does not.
Basis: 10-K_2024-12-31_rxo.htm.jsonl, page 57; 10-K_2025-12-31_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 24, 49–50; 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 21–23; calculation C08; calculation C09; calculation C10

The credit implication is operating leverage on gross profit: overhead absorbs 89.96% of gross profit, and a 1 pp rise in cost of transportation removes USD 65.39 million, or 56.37% of adjusted EBITDA of USD 116 million. Fuel is a pass-through over time and fuel surcharges may not fully capture increases, and no wage inflation or input-cost data beyond that is given.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rxo.htm.jsonl, page 24; 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation C10; calculation C21

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital spending is low and flexible: USD 58 million over the last twelve months is 0.89% of revenue and 50.00% of adjusted EBITDA, and the issuer says it has significant discretion over amount and timing and is bound by no schedule. Cash interest was USD 29 million and cash taxes USD 7 million over the last twelve months, so adjusted EBITDA of USD 116 million leaves only USD 22 million before working capital and restructuring cash, and the issuer paid USD 25 million of restructuring cash in nine months with a USD 25 million reserve still open; operating lease liabilities of USD 231 million sit outside debt. Free cash flow was negative USD 73 million over the last twelve months, which is minus 62.93% of adjusted EBITDA, and negative in 2024 and 2025 as well.

### Porter's five forces

Rivalry is intense and sets the ceiling on pricing: the industry is fragmented with thousands of competitors, some with larger customer bases and more resources, and shippers solicit bids from several brokers, which presses rates and margins in weak markets and keeps contract sell rates from rising as fast as buy rates in tight ones. Threat of new entrants is not assessed in the filings beyond the fragmentation of the industry and management's claim of a technology advantage, which is not visible in margins that have fallen for three years. Substitutes matter through shippers building internal transportation capability, which the issuer names as a driver of results. Buyer power is moderate because the largest customer is 11.4% and the top five 23% of revenue, and the filings give no renewal terms. Carrier power is the force that shows in the numbers: one-year non-exclusive agreements and capacity exits raised buy rates faster than sell rates, and each 1 pp of revenue that shifts to carriers is worth USD 65.39 million of gross profit.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 11, 21, 85; 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, page 21; calculation C08; calculation C14; calculation C21

### PEST factors

Only the regulatory and economic factors materially alter the credit. Regulatory change and enforcement pushed capacity out of the truck market and lifted buy rates, which moved cost of transportation to 82.5% of revenue, and misclassification claims against last mile contractors are an unquantified tail risk; economic conditions drive volume, fuel and freight rates, and the issuer notes that recessions depress activity and that inflation hurts unless pricing follows. Social and technological factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver based on the provided materials.

### Credit-translated SWOT

Strengths are liquidity of USD 360 million, no large maturity before 2031, low capital intensity at 0.89% of revenue, moderate customer concentration and a cost base that has been cut. Weaknesses are an adjusted EBITDA margin of 1.77%, negative free cash flow, add-backs of 51.72% of adjusted EBITDA, tangible equity of negative USD 17 million and unknown covenant levels. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are contract sell rates catching up with carrier buy rates, which would widen the spread (an analyst inference; the 2024 report shows the spread narrowing when freight rates fell), continued overhead restraint, and a sale that places the debt under a stronger owner or repays it. Threats that could weaken it are further capacity exits that raise buy rates, a freight recession that cuts gross profit against fixed overhead, a failed or conditioned sale with a USD 175 million fee, and contractor misclassification claims.

### Financial profile and credit quality

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next 12 to 24 months the issuer's own text says the market remained tight in the third quarter with capacity still exiting, so, as analyst judgement, cost of transportation is more likely to stay high than to fall and revenue will stay inflated by rates; the offset is continued overhead restraint, since transaction and integration costs fell to USD 7 million from USD 18 million in the nine months and restructuring reserves are being used. Cash interest on the 2031 notes is scheduled to start on 2026-11-15 (payment is not confirmed by the supplied filings), and run-rate cash interest of about USD 28.9 million is close to the USD 29 million paid over the last twelve months. A sale closing in the first half of 2027 would end the standalone period, so the outlook for creditors beyond that date depends on terms not supplied.

Over three to five years the debt matures in 2031, the notes become callable from 2028-05-15 and the main sector tailwinds named by the issuer (broker penetration, digital adoption and e-commerce demand) are management statements that the margin history does not yet support, while the main headwinds are carrier pricing power, cyclical freight demand and legal change on contractor status.

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is a continuation of the pattern the issuer already reports, not a generic recession: capacity keeps leaving the market, carrier buy rates rise faster than contract sell rates, and cost of transportation rises a further 1 pp of revenue to 83.5% from 82.5%, a smaller step than the 2.2 pp rise between the third quarters of 2025 and 2026. The first operational break is gross profit, which falls by USD 65.39 million on last-twelve-month revenue while overhead of USD 1,039 million does not move quickly; adjusted EBITDA falls to USD 50.61 million, net debt over adjusted EBITDA rises to 9.31x from 4.06x and adjusted cover on cash interest falls to 1.75x. Free cash flow worsens from negative USD 73 million to about negative USD 138.39 million before any working-capital release, which USD 360 million of liquidity would cover for about 2.60 years.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 3, 10, 21–23; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C01; calculation C04; calculation C08; calculation C21

The observable triggers are cost of transportation above 82.5% of revenue with gross profit falling, a rise in borrowings on the secured facility, and any covenant waiver; the sale adds a second path, in which the deal fails or is conditioned, a USD 175 million fee is paid and customers and carriers drift during the wait. Management levers are variable compensation and further overhead reduction, capital spending that is discretionary, leaving the buyback authorization unused, repricing contracts as they renew (although the issuer says pricing turns more competitive in downturns), and a working-capital release if receivables shrink faster than payables. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress over the next twelve months on stated availability, but not demonstrated against the fixed charge coverage and minimum availability tests whose levels are not stated, and this scenario is an analyst sensitivity, not a forecast.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_rxo.htm.jsonl, pages 10–11, 16, 21, 24, 29–32; calculation C20; calculation C21

### Overall credit view

Overall, RXO presents an adequate business profile supported by an asset-light model, moderate customer concentration and USD 6,539 million of revenue, but credit quality is constrained by an adjusted EBITDA margin of 1.77%, add-backs of 51.72% of adjusted EBITDA and negative free cash flow. Cash-flow durability appears weak because carrier buy rates have outrun sell rates for three years and earnings have been held up by operating leverage and overhead restraint, not margin. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on liquidity of USD 360 million, the 2031 maturity profile and the merger terms for the debt. The primary downside path is a further rise in cost of transportation against fixed overhead, with the sale leaving the debt terms open. Further analysis would require the credit agreement and note indenture, the merger agreement, the proxy statement and contract and margin-per-load data. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: carrier buy rates rising faster than sell rates while overhead absorbs most gross profit.
