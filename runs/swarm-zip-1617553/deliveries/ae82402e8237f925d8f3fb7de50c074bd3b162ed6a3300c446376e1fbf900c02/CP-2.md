## Findings

### Bottom line

ZipRecruiter's credit is adequate but fragile: USD 174.7 million of cash backs the remaining USD 250.7 million of 2030 notes, but earnings are thin and cyclical and buybacks compete for that cash, so creditors rely on cash and cost cuts, not earnings.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_zip-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6 and 21; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation K1

Trailing reported EBITDA is USD 29.1 million against USD 61.3 million on the issuer's adjusted measure, so leverage is 8.53x or 4.06x, and the run-rate coupon of about USD 12.5 million is covered 2.33x or 4.89x. The primary downside is another hiring downturn, because in FY2025 about 81.64% of lost revenue fell through to reported EBITDA.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation K3; calculation K7

### Limitations

- No quantitative management guidance or forecast is supplied, so the view of 2027 earnings rests on the 2026 run rate, and a reversal of the hiring recovery would lower the assessment of debt service capacity.
- Covenant levels, call and make-whole terms and the amount of debt the indenture permits are not established, so refinancing capacity is judged on cash and cash flow alone and could be stronger or weaker with those terms.
- The 2026 statements are unaudited and the trailing figures are constructed from them, so the leverage conclusions could move after the FY2026 audit.
- Cash interest paid for the trailing year is not disclosed, so the debt service view uses the contractual coupon, which would be understated if other cash interest items exist.
- No rating or market quote is supplied; the notes' fair value is the company's own estimate in less active markets, so the market-access judgement is indicative only.

A second support is a business that converts to cash when costs are managed: free cash flow was USD 27.4 million over the last twelve months after interest, capital spending is light at about USD 7.5 million a year, and marketing and advertising of about 29.79% of revenue can be cut quickly. That matters because it gives management a lever to protect cash before debt service is touched.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_zip-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7 and 14; calculation K10

### Company description and credit linkage

ZipRecruiter runs a two-sided online marketplace in which employers pay subscription or per-click fees to post and distribute jobs and job seekers use it free. The revenue model is short-cycle: subscription plans run from a day to a year and are recognised ratably, performance-based campaigns last one to three months, and the company reports no material remaining performance obligations, so revenue visibility for debt service is measured in months, not years. Offsetting that, no customer is 10% or more of revenue, revenue outside the United States is under 2%, and 76.88% of FY2025 revenue was subscription, which is non-refundable and cancelable only at the end of its term.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_zip-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 16 and 17; 10-K_2025-12-31_zip-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 10, 97, 98 and 123; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation K10

### Ownership and group structure

The company is founder-led and listed, with no sponsor or parent: the chief executive officer holds 78.9% of the votes with 15.7% of the shares through Class B stock, the board is classified, and the notes are the obligation of the listed company, with guarantees mentioned but no guarantor named. Creditors therefore have no sponsor to support or extract value, and the financial policy that matters is the board's under one holder's control; structural priority and guarantor coverage cannot be assessed until the indenture is supplied.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_zip-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 90 and 93; 10-K_2025-12-31_zip-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 128

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue follows the number of paying employers and what each spends. Quarterly Paid Employers were 73,929 in Q3 2026 against 66,959 a year earlier, while revenue per paid employer was USD 1,683 against USD 1,717, so growth is volume-led and pricing power is not evident. Demand is cyclical and discretionary: employers cut hiring budgets in 2023 to 2025, and traffic depends in part on search and generative AI engines and Job Distribution Partners that the company does not control, so a change in those channels could raise acquisition cost.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_zip-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 28, 53 and 58; 10-K_2025-12-31_zip-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 73–75; calculation K10

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Gross margin is 89.25%, so almost all lost revenue reaches operating profit unless costs are cut, which is why the fall-through in FY2025 was 81.64%. Costs flex in two layers: marketing and advertising of USD 104.3 million for nine months 2026 can be scaled quickly, while research and development and general expenses moved only with headcount, and research and development fell 19.15% for nine months 2026 as staff were cut. Stock-based compensation fell 40.56% to USD 22.8 million for nine months, which shrinks the gap between adjusted and reported EBITDA but not the cash cost of tax withholding on equity awards.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_zip-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 98, 99 and 10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_zip-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 10, 14 and 37; calculation K7; calculation K10

### Capital intensity and free cash flow conversion

Capital needs are light: purchases of property and capitalised software were about USD 7.5 million in FY2025, and the business has no maintenance and growth split, no pension or finance lease debt and only USD 11.1 million of operating lease liabilities. Free cash flow was USD 3.5 million in FY2025 and USD 27.4 million over the last twelve months, so conversion of adjusted EBITDA to free cash flow was 44.78% on the trailing figures; the main leaks are buybacks, tax withholding on equity awards and, once the loss carry-forwards used against the 2026 gain are gone, cash taxes.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); 10-Q_2026-09-30_zip-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6 and 42; calculation K10; calculation K14

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense in a fragmented market that includes larger platforms such as Indeed, LinkedIn and Google, which limits pricing power and keeps marketing intensity high. Entry and substitution risk comes from generative AI and direct employer channels, buyer power is moderate because employers can switch freely but none is 10% or more of revenue, and supplier power sits with search engines, Job Distribution Partners and the cloud host, for which USD 76.5 million of hosting commitments run through 2030 and beyond.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_zip-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 10 and 130; 10-Q_2026-09-30_zip-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 14, 53 and 58

### Macro, regulatory and technology factors

The labour market is the dominant macro driver: the company calls it subdued and ties revenue to hiring demand, with the fourth quarter seasonally weaker. Privacy, data protection and artificial intelligence rules apply in the United States, Canada, the European Union, the United Kingdom and Israel and could raise costs, and generative AI changes how job seekers find jobs, which bears on traffic; neither factor is quantified in the filings, so both stay watch items rather than drivers.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_zip-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 13 and 75; 10-Q_2026-09-30_zip-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 31 and 53

### Credit-translated SWOT

Strengths are a smaller debt claim, cash of USD 174.7 million, light capital needs and flexible marketing spend; weaknesses are thin reported earnings, short revenue visibility, no committed facility and founder control with a history of large buybacks. The only opportunities that would lift credit quality are a sustained recovery in employer count and spend and the repurchase of more notes below par; the threats are another hiring downturn, a loss of search or AI traffic and a return of large distributions.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_zip-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6 and 28

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months the supports are rising paid employer counts, the halved coupon and a cost base cut by headcount reductions; the headwinds are a labour market the company still calls subdued, seasonally weaker fourth quarters, falling revenue per employer and the unknown pace of buybacks. Over three to five years the 2030 maturity dominates: the company must bridge USD 76.0 million between cash and principal from free cash flow or a refinancing, and the call price falls to par on 2027-01-15, which makes a later refinancing cheaper to execute.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_zip-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21, 28 and 31; 10-K_2025-12-31_zip-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 75 and 129; calculation K6

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is another fall in employer hiring demand, or a loss of search or generative AI traffic, that cuts Quarterly Paid Employers by more than the normal fourth-quarter dip of 11.73%. The first operational break is a fall in paid employers while marketing spend per employer rises, which compresses revenue; with gross margin of 89.25% and a fall-through of 81.64% in FY2025, a repeat of that year's 5.28% revenue fall would remove about USD 19.9 million of trailing reported EBITDA, leaving about USD 9.2 million. Free cash flow would shrink toward the FY2025 trough, but at the lower coupon that trough would be about USD 19.2 million rather than USD 3.5 million, so cash of USD 174.7 million would not be at risk from debt service; the real exposure is the combination of that earnings fall with buybacks drawing on the USD 107.3 million of authorisation and a refinancing in 2030 that needs a market window. Management levers are marketing cuts, headcount, stopping buybacks and further discounted note purchases. The observable triggers are paid employers, adjusted EBITDA margin back below 8.04%, cash below half the principal and any return of FY2025-scale buybacks.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_zip-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 28 and 96; 10-K_2025-12-31_zip-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 98; calculation K4; calculation K7; calculation K8; calculation K9; calculation K11

### Overall credit view

Overall, ZipRecruiter presents an adequate but fragile business profile supported by a debt claim cut by more than half, cash of USD 174.7 million and light capital needs, but credit quality is constrained by thin and cyclical earnings, founder control and a record of large buybacks. Cash-flow durability appears adequate rather than strong because free cash flow of USD 27.4 million over the last twelve months came after a year of only USD 3.5 million. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on free cash flow of about USD 23.07 million a year to 2030 and on market access that the notes' 83.49% fair value ratio does not yet confirm. The primary downside path is another hiring downturn with high fall-through to EBITDA while buybacks continue. Further analysis would require the indenture, the proxy statement, management guidance and market quotes. Fundamental credit analysis completed. Top risk: a hiring downturn that cuts earnings while cash is still being distributed to shareholders.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_zip-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 6, 19 and 21; calculation K1; calculation K2; calculation K6; calculation K10
