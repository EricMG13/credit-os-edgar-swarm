## Findings

### Bottom line

Newmark's fundamental credit profile is average: moderate leverage, strong liquidity and fee revenue spread across service lines support debt service, but capital returns above free cash flow, a lowered outlook and controlling-holder influence leave little room above the lowest investment-grade ratings.

Net debt is 1.38x reported EBITDA at 2026-09-30, or 1.41x pro forma for the dividend declared on 2026-10-28, cash interest cover is 8.17x, and undrawn revolver capacity is USD 620 million with no corporate maturity before 2029. Against that, shareholder returns were 1.39x free cash flow excluding loan activity in the nine months, capital markets revenue fell 4.36% in the third quarter, and management lowered its 2026 outlook.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better view is momentum, modest leverage and improving ratings: nine-month revenue grew 18.36%, third-quarter revenue grew 12.71%, last-twelve-month reported EBITDA is 10.84% above FY2025, net leverage on the issuer's own adjusted measure is 1.00x, S&P raised the rating to BBB- on 2026-09-16, and management still expects its best-ever full year. The profile would improve if shareholder returns fell back inside free cash flow and the revolver were repaid, if a chief executive successor were named with an orderly handover, and if the executed credit agreement showed ample covenant headroom. It would weaken if capital markets revenue fell again year on year in the fourth quarter, if net leverage stayed above the FY2024 level of 1.40x after the dividend, if revolver availability fell toward the USD 365 million of 2026-03-31, or if a downgrade triggered the rate increase on the notes.

### Business model, ownership and revenue durability

Newmark is a commercial real estate adviser and service provider with three revenue lines: management services, servicing fees and other; leasing and other commissions; and capital markets. About two-fifths of nine-month revenue is recurring fee income and the top ten clients were 9.1% of FY2025 revenue, so debt service does not rest on one client or one line. The cost of that breadth is dependence on the transaction lines, which follow deal volumes and interest rates, so cash-flow visibility is good for the fee half of the business and weak for the commission half.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_nmrk-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 35; calculation C23; calculation C38; calculation C11; calculation C10; calculation C9

One holder controls the vote: Mr. Brandon Lutnick controls 58.6% of it through Cantor and CFGM, Cantor holds 20.83% of the senior notes, and related-party services, lending and note placement run through the same group. Control is stable rather than contestable, which lowers takeover risk but means borrowing, distributions and board makeup follow one group's preferences, and creditor protection rests on Audit Committee approval of related-party matters. No sponsor transaction, merger or change of control is stated in the documents.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C37; calculation C24

Revenue is cyclical in its transaction half and sticky in its fee half. Leasing grew 26.49% and management services and servicing grew 18.30% in the third quarter, while capital markets fell 4.36% although it is still 14.55% higher over the nine months, so the commission lines now pull in different directions. Management states a goal of more than USD 2 billion of annual management and servicing revenue by 2029; that is management language, not a forecast, and it would raise the recurring share only if met.

### Cost structure and cash conversion

Cost flexibility is good where it matters. Compensation and employee benefits are 59.12% of FY2025 revenue and follow commission-based revenue, as the third-quarter release attributes the cost increase primarily to higher commission-based revenue, acquired companies and growth investment, so a drop in deal fees is partly met by lower pay. Depreciation, lease costs and cash interest do not flex, and the last-twelve-month reported EBITDA margin of 12.39% is close to the 12.32% of FY2024 even though the adjusted margin is 17.25%, because the reported measure carries equity-based compensation as a cost.

### Market forces and external factors

Competitive rivalry is the force that matters most to margins and market position. The annual report describes an industry that is fragmented and competitive, names larger rivals such as CBRE, Jones Lang LaSalle and Cushman & Wakefield, and says some competitors are substantially larger than Newmark; the third-quarter report says Newmark believes it gained share in investment sales, yet its U.S. debt volumes fell about 37% while industry originations rose, which the issuer ties to a strong prior-year comparison. Buyer power is limited by client spread, with the top ten clients at 9.1% of FY2025 revenue and clients increasingly awarding work to full-service firms. Supplier power sits with producers, who earn a substantial portion or all of their pay from production and are retained with advances, so producer departures are a margin and revenue risk. The documents do not discuss new entrants or substitutes, so those forces are not established.

### Downside scenario

The initiating condition is long-term rates that stay near 5.3% through the year-end selling season, so closings slip and the fourth quarter lands at the low end of the revised outlook, which implies fourth-quarter revenue growth of -1.40%. The first operational break is in the transaction lines, leasing and capital markets, while servicing and management fees keep growing. Compensation flexes with commissions, but depreciation, lease costs and cash interest do not, so reported EBITDA falls faster than revenue. As a severity marker chosen by the analyst, not a forecast, take reported EBITDA back at its FY2024 level: net debt at 2026-09-30 would then be 1.89x EBITDA, or 1.92x pro forma for the dividend declared on 2026-10-28, and interest cover would be 5.98x.

Liquidity holds in this case. USD 620 million of the revolver is undrawn and no corporate debt matures before 2029, so no refinancing event arises within the scenario. Resilience appears sufficient to avoid liquidity distress; the first constraint would be leverage at 1.89x to 1.92x and the investment-grade ratings, where a downgrade lifts the notes rate, and not cash. The scenario would break this view only if repurchases continued at the nine-month pace while earnings fell.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Newmark presents an adequate business profile supported by revenue spread across three service lines, low capital intensity and investment-grade ratings at Fitch and S&P, but credit quality is constrained by capital returns above free cash flow, controlling-holder influence and an earnings base that depends on deal closings in the fourth quarter. Cash-flow durability appears adequate because free cash flow excluding loan activity converted 72.52% of last-twelve-month reported EBITDA and about two-fifths of revenue is recurring. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on keeping the BBB- ratings and on the notes due 2029 being refinanced before they become current. The primary downside path is a weak fourth quarter and a downgrade while the revolver funds distributions. Further analysis would require the executed credit agreement and indenture, the proxy statement, and peer and market data. Fundamental credit analysis completed.
