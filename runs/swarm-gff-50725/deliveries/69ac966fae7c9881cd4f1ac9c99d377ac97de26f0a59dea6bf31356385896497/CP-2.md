## Findings

### Bottom line

Griffon is a cash-generative building-products credit of adequate quality, and the main risk to creditors is discretionary shareholder returns rather than operations.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 54, 73; calculation C3; calculation C10

Net debt of USD 946.66 million (USD 963.55 million pro forma for share repurchases to 2026-11-16) is 2.12x reported EBITDA (2.15x pro forma), free cash flow is USD 266.95 million and no material principal falls due before fiscal 2031. If the whole USD 544.80 million buyback authority were debt-funded, net leverage would reach about 3.37x.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 54, 56, 73, 103–105; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C3

### Key findings

Cash generation is the core credit strength, and it is stable rather than peaking. Operating cash flow from continuing operations was USD 307.37 million, USD 307.79 million and USD 304.87 million in the last three years, and capital expenditure of USD 37.91 million is only 2.05% of revenue. Free cash flow of USD 266.95 million is therefore 58.08% of adjusted EBITDA, is already struck after cash interest of USD 81.08 million and cash taxes of USD 96.04 million, and equals 25.54% of year-end debt, so debt service is not the constraint.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 72–74; calculation C1; calculation C12

Leverage is moderate but was bought with asset sales, and further deleveraging depends on the cash left after shareholder returns. Net debt of USD 946.66 million (USD 963.55 million pro forma for share repurchases to 2026-11-16) is 2.12x reported EBITDA (2.15x pro forma) and 2.06x issuer-adjusted EBITDA, against 2.0x on the issuer's own credit agreement definition. Adjusted EBITDA fell 0.48% in FY2026, so the fall in debt came mainly from USD 304.38 million of sale proceeds and not from earnings growth. After dividends and buybacks of USD 191.71 million, free cash flow leaves only USD 75.24 million, which would take 12.58 years to repay net debt. Pro forma net debt for the dividend declared on 2026-11-16 is not established because its total payout is not stated.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 53–54, 65–66, 73; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C2

Financial policy is the weakest dimension, because the board's buyback authority is larger than the liquidity that would fund it. Authority of USD 544.80 million is 1.68x year-end liquidity of USD 324.23 million and 2.04x annual free cash flow, so it could not be met from cash on hand. Returns absorbed 96.23% of three-year free cash flow, exceeded it in FY2024 at USD 345.72 million against USD 257.09 million, and no leverage target is stated. The senior notes have limited covenants and the revolver's restricted payment limits are not supplied, so a debt-funded authority would lift net leverage from 2.15x pro forma to about 3.37x.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 54, 56, 73; Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C3; calculation C10

Earnings are stable but their quality is eroding, because price is carrying weaker volume. FY2026 revenue rose 2.95% to USD 1,848.38 million on price and mix of 6% against volume down 3%, yet gross margin slipped to 46.36% from 47.24% and adjusted EBITDA margin to 24.86% from 25.99% in FY2024. Only 16.75% of the added revenue turned into gross profit, because lower volume absorbed less overhead and material costs rose, and raw material costs reach prices with a lag. The final quarter was better: derived adjusted EBITDA rose 4.06% against a 2.13% fall over the first nine months, at a quarterly margin of 26.04%.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21, 45–46, 65, 72; 10-Q_2026-06-30_gff-20260630.htm.jsonl, pages 4–5, 58; calculation C4; calculation C5

Demand is discretionary and concentrated, so volume is the variable that matters most, and the fan business is the weaker leg. Residential repair and remodel was 53.34% of revenue and commercial 39.29%. Home Depot and Menards take garage doors on an exclusive basis, hold 8% and 11% of net receivables, and the filing says losing either would be materially adverse. The fan business competes with retailer house brands, is the most exposed to tariffs, and a goodwill and trademark impairment of USD 243.61 million in FY2025 left the trademark with minimal headroom in the auditor's words.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_gff-20250930.htm.jsonl, pages 38, 52; 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 10, 19–20, 23, 70, 122–123; calculation C8

Liquidity is adequate and maturities are distant, but most of the liquidity is a revolver whose availability depends on covenant tests that are not supplied. Year-end liquidity of USD 324.23 million includes USD 225.60 million of undrawn revolver, 69.58%, and USD 52.30 million of the cash is held outside the United States; USD 16.89 million of repurchases have followed. Nothing material falls due before the revolver matures in fiscal 2031 and the USD 800.00 million of 6.25% notes in 2034. Run-rate interest on the notes and the drawn revolver is about USD 64.69 million against USD 81.08 million paid in FY2026, which lifts cover to about 6.92x from 5.52x.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 51, 55–57, 74, 103–105; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C6; calculation C9

Asset cover beneath the operating business is thin, so any recovery would rest on enterprise value rather than tangible assets. Goodwill and intangibles of USD 534.86 million exceed shareholders' equity of USD 306.78 million, leaving tangible equity below zero by USD 228.09 million, and USD 355.08 million of joint venture notes and equity, 115.75% of equity, pays no cash interest. The revolver is secured on substantially all domestic assets and USD 260.00 million is drawn, 24.53% of principal debt, ahead of the unsecured notes.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 55–56, 71–72, 93–95, 103–105; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C8

Ownership helps and event risk offsets it. The company is listed with no sponsor identified, and its board is independent apart from the chief executive, who is also chairman, which removes sponsor extraction risk. It has nevertheless reshaped its portfolio repeatedly since 2018, with the sale or closure of AMES in FY2026 the latest step, and a debt-funded acquisition is the other route to higher leverage that the notes' limited covenants may not restrict.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_gff-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 15, 27; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious reading is that the cash flows are stable and the balance sheet has been repaired. Free cash flow was USD 257.09 million, USD 266.10 million and USD 266.95 million over three years, net leverage of 2.12x (2.15x pro forma for repurchases to 2026-11-16) is moderate in absolute terms, run-rate interest cover is about 6.92x, and no material principal is due before fiscal 2031. Returns also fell each year, from USD 345.72 million in FY2024 to USD 191.71 million in FY2026, and were covered by free cash flow in FY2025 and FY2026.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 53–54, 73–74; Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation C1; calculation C6; calculation C10

The view would soften if the authority is used only from free cash flow so that net debt keeps falling, if the executed documents show tight restricted payment limits, and if adjusted EBITDA margin returns above 25.72%, its FY2025 level. It would harden if repurchases are funded by revolver draws, if volume falls without a price offset, or if the revolver tests sit close to the issuer's own leverage of 2.0x. A further USD 100.00 million of debt-funded buybacks adds about 0.22x to net leverage on reported EBITDA.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 54, 56, 66; calculation C3

### Strengths and weaknesses

The first credit strength is stable, capital-light cash generation. Operating cash flow of USD 304.87 million less capital expenditure of USD 37.91 million left free cash flow of USD 266.95 million after interest and taxes, so EBITDA reliably becomes cash and debt service is well supported.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 72–74; calculation C1

The second credit strength is the repaired debt structure. The term loan was repaid, the 5.75% notes due 2028 were replaced by 6.25% notes due 2034 and the revolver was extended to 2031, so no material principal is due before fiscal 2031 and refinancing pressure is low for several years.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 53, 55–57, 105; Financial foundation (CP-1)

The third credit strength is a falling interest burden. Interest on the new notes and the drawn revolver runs at about USD 64.69 million a year against USD 81.08 million paid in FY2026, which lifts reported EBITDA cover to about 6.92x if earnings hold.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 55–57, 74; calculation C6

The fourth credit strength is a leading position in garage doors held through exclusive supply to the two largest home-improvement retailers and an installing dealer network. That supports volume and price realisation, although the same relationships create the concentration weakness below.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9, 20

The first credit weakness is financial policy. The board's repurchase authority of USD 544.80 million is 1.68x year-end liquidity, returns took 96.23% of three-year free cash flow, and the senior notes carry limited covenants, so debt-funded returns could lift net leverage to about 3.37x without any earnings fall.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 54, 73; calculation C3; calculation C10

The second credit weakness is customer concentration and discretionary demand. Residential repair and remodel is 53.34% of revenue, two retailers hold exclusive garage door supply and 8% and 11% of net receivables, and the filing calls the loss of either materially adverse, so a change in volume or price terms reaches EBITDA quickly.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 20, 122–123

The third credit weakness is margin erosion under price-led growth. Gross margin slipped to 46.36% from 47.24% and only 16.75% of the added revenue turned into gross profit, which shows cost increases and lower overhead absorption taking the benefit of price.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 45, 72; calculation C4

The fourth credit weakness is thin tangible asset cover. Goodwill and intangibles exceed shareholders' equity and USD 355.08 million of joint venture notes and equity pays no cash interest, so recovery rests on enterprise value and on partners Griffon does not control.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 71–72, 93–95; calculation C8

### Company description and credit linkage

Griffon is a listed manufacturer of residential and commercial garage doors, rolling steel doors and ceiling fans, reported as one operating segment, Building Products, since the AMES tools and garden businesses were sold or closed in FY2026. Revenue was USD 1,848.38 million, of which residential was USD 1,122.21 million and commercial USD 726.17 million, and about 95% is in the United States. Products reach customers through two home-improvement retailers, independent installing dealers and direct-to-consumer fan sales, orders are short cycle, and no backlog is disclosed. Demand is seasonal, with doors strongest in the first and fourth fiscal quarters and fans in the second and third.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5–7, 9–12, 45, 92, 121–123

For creditors this means cash flow visibility is limited to an order cycle of under a year, so quarterly cash flow and revolver use swing with the season, but the business is capital-light and the exit from the lower-margin AMES businesses left a higher-margin core. That supports debt service and refinancing confidence, at the price of a narrower business that depends on housing activity and two customers.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 12, 45, 72–74; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Ownership and group structure

Griffon is a widely held, publicly listed parent with no sponsor or controlling holder identified, employees owning about 8% through the employee stock ownership plan, and no leverage target stated. Its board is independent apart from the chief executive, who has been chairman since 2018. In 2024 it repurchased stock for USD 98.25 million from funds affiliated with Voss Capital, whose chief investment officer had been a director, and the 2025 annual report records an activist campaign begun in 2021. The holder structure beyond that is not established because the proxy statement is not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 14–16, 41, 117, 120; 10-K_2025-09-30_gff-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 44; Business & governance (CP-1A)

Creditors hold claims on the operating subsidiaries: six wholly owned domestic subsidiaries guarantee the notes, material domestic subsidiaries guarantee the revolver, and the revolver is secured on substantially all domestic assets, while the notes are unsecured. The two equity-method joint ventures, 43% of Veritage Brands and 49% of Griffon ANZ, are not consolidated, so their own debt is not Griffon's, but Griffon is a creditor of both through paid-in-kind notes. The credit implication is that financial policy is set by a public board that has returned most free cash flow to shareholders, and that sponsor-style extraction is absent while policy discretion is wide.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 55, 58, 93–95, 103–105; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Revenue drivers and pricing power

Revenue is cyclical, discretionary and concentrated rather than recurring. Sales into remodeling follow the aging of housing stock and existing home sales, commercial sales follow commercial construction and repair, and purchases of many products are discretionary for consumers. Revenue fell 3.27% in FY2025 and rose 2.95% in FY2026, so it is stable around USD 1.8 billion, but FY2026 growth was price and mix of 6% against volume down 3%. The filing says price increases are hard to pass through quickly when raw material costs rise, and contract duration, renewal rates and switching costs are not disclosed.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 17, 21, 45, 72

The credit implication is that revenue is predictable in level but not in composition: a price-led year can hide weak volume, and the exclusive retailer relationships give volume visibility only as long as both retailers keep the supply arrangement. No customer reached 10% of FY2026 revenue, which limits single-name revenue risk, while the two largest hold 8% and 11% of net receivables.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 11, 20

### Cost structure and margin resilience

Cost of goods was 53.64% of FY2026 revenue and selling, general and administrative expense 23.65%, so gross profit funds a large overhead layer. Galvanized steel is the principal raw material, sourced finished goods were about 10% of revenue and sourced components about 13% of cost of goods, and the fixed and variable split of costs is not printed. Selling, general and administrative expense rose 1.5%, with higher stock compensation and distribution costs, while gross profit rose only 1%.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 21, 45–46, 72; calculation C11

Margins can be defended under mild volume pressure only if price keeps rising, because lower volume absorbs less overhead and raw material costs pass through with a lag. Only 16.75% of the added FY2026 revenue turned into gross profit, against an average margin of 46.36%, which shows how much of the price increase was absorbed. A further volume fall without price would therefore reduce EBITDA faster than revenue, which is the operating leverage that drives the downside case below.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 21, 45–46; calculation C4

### Capital intensity and cash conversion

Capital expenditure of USD 37.91 million was 2.05% of revenue and 0.94x depreciation and amortization, and no split between maintenance and growth spending is printed. Starting from adjusted EBITDA of USD 459.59 million, cash interest of USD 81.08 million, cash taxes of USD 96.04 million and capital expenditure leave USD 244.56 million, against free cash flow of USD 266.95 million; the USD 22.40 million difference is working capital, non-cash stock compensation of USD 30.54 million and other items not analysed separately.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 65, 72–74; calculation C12

Working capital is seasonal, with receivables of USD 191.17 million and inventories of USD 183.58 million against payables of USD 56.17 million, firm purchase obligations of USD 99.31 million due in fiscal 2027 and operating lease liabilities of USD 67.92 million that sit outside the debt figure. EBITDA converts to durable free cash flow at 58.08% of adjusted EBITDA, which is available for debt service, deleveraging and liquidity, although the board currently sends most of it to shareholders. Pension and retiree medical claims outside debt are small: the Hunter Fan pension plan was terminated and expected fiscal 2027 contributions are USD 1.74 million for supplemental benefits.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 57–58, 71–73, 107–109, 118, 126; calculation C1

### Competitive forces

Rivalry is intense, as the filing states. The sectional garage door and commercial rolling steel door industry has several large national manufacturers and many regional ones, and Griffon competes on service, quality, brand, design and price; the fan industry is fragmented and includes retailer house brands such as Hampton Bay and Harbor Breeze, which is why the fan business carries more margin risk and why the FY2025 impairment fell there.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10, 19

New entrants and substitutes pose a limited threat to doors, where brand recognition and the distribution network are the barriers the filing cites, but retailers import directly from low-cost countries to sell private label products, which caps price. Substitution risk for doors and fans is not discussed in the filings and is not established.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 9, 19

Buyer power is high and is the most important force for credit. Two retailers buy on an exclusive basis, mass merchandisers have strong negotiating power, and the filing states that losing either of the two would be materially adverse; this limits price realisation and exposes volume to a decision by one buyer. Supplier power is moderate: steel is available from many sources but price swings pass through with a lag, and some products come only from suppliers in China.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8–9, 19–22

### External factors

Trade policy is the material political factor. Some products and components are sourced from China, a few exclusively, and the filing calls trade actions a continuing risk to the fan business and a smaller risk to Clopay, which manufactures only in the United States. Tariffs can raise the price of China-sourced goods, disrupted customer ordering and contributed to the FY2025 fan impairment, so a further tariff change would hit fan margin first.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_gff-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 52; 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 22–24, 46

The material economic factor is the U.S. housing and commercial property cycle together with consumer credit, because remodeling is discretionary and the filing links 2027 risk to those markets; steel prices are the second economic input. Social and technological factors are not a primary credit driver on these filings.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 17, 21

### Credit-translated SWOT

Strengths that support credit are stable free cash flow after interest and taxes, a repaired maturity profile to 2031 and 2034, leading garage door brands and exclusive retail supply, and low capital intensity. Weaknesses that constrain credit are a shareholder-friendly financial policy with a large authority, concentration in two retailers and in remodel demand, price-led margin erosion, and thin tangible asset cover.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 20, 54, 72–74, 105; calculation C1; calculation C3

Opportunities that could improve credit are retaining more free cash flow for debt repayment, a recovery in existing home sales that restores volume, and monetising the joint venture notes for cash. Threats that could weaken credit are debt-funded buybacks or acquisitions, tariff and steel cost shocks, a loss of retailer business, and weak performance of the joint ventures that owe Griffon paid-in-kind notes.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 7, 17, 27, 93–95; Business & governance (CP-1A)

### Financial profile and credit quality

Free cash flow stability is the strongest dimension: operating cash flow stayed between USD 304.87 million and USD 307.79 million for three years and capital needs are low. Financial policy and governance is the weakest dimension and business diversification is also weak, because returns absorbed 96.23% of three-year free cash flow and the business rests on two product families, one segment and two customers. Scale and market position, competitive advantage, cost and capex flexibility, margin stability, refinancing access and liquidity are average: each has a real support, such as brands, distant maturities or USD 324.23 million of liquidity, offset by concentration, margin erosion or covenant-conditional availability.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 20, 54, 72–74, 105; calculation C1; calculation C10

The trend is mixed. ### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds

Over the next twelve to twenty-four months the base case is stable cash generation on a lighter interest bill: run-rate interest is about USD 16.39 million or 20.22% below FY2026 cash interest, there is no material principal due, and the fourth quarter of FY2026 showed improving margin. Headwinds are the discretionary remodel cycle that the filing says may weigh on 2027, tariff and steel cost uncertainty, and the board's capacity to return cash, with USD 544.80 million of authority and a dividend of USD 0.25 per share declared for payment on 2026-12-16. Management gives no quantitative guidance, so the outlook is a reading of drivers and not a forecast.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 23, 54, 55–57, 74; calculation C5; calculation C6

Over three to five years the credit depends on whether free cash flow is used to reduce debt or returned: at the FY2026 pace of retained cash it would take 12.58 years to repay net debt, so deleveraging below the current level needs either lower returns or higher earnings. The revolver matures in 2031 and the notes in 2034, so refinancing becomes the medium-term event, and a stable business with moderate leverage should be able to refinance if it has not re-levered. Sector tailwinds are the aging housing stock and existing home sales cited by management, and company tailwinds are the lower interest cost and the narrower, higher-margin portfolio.

### Downside scenario

The scenario starts with a further volume fall in residential repair and remodel, which is 53.34% of revenue and the most discretionary demand, with retailers resisting further price increases. Assume revenue in that end market falls 10%, which is USD 98.59 million, and that overhead and selling costs do not fall, so the lost gross profit of USD 45.71 million comes straight off EBITDA at the FY2026 gross margin of 46.36%. Reported EBITDA of USD 447.53 million would fall by 10.21% to USD 401.82 million, and after tax at the FY2026 effective rate of 28.21% free cash flow would fall by USD 32.82 million to USD 234.14 million, leaving USD 42.43 million after returns at the FY2026 pace.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 45, 72–74, 122–123; calculation C7

On that base, pro forma net leverage would rise from 2.15x to about 2.40x, and to about 3.75x if the whole authority were also debt-funded, which is the combined path that would test the revolver covenants whose levels are not supplied. There would be no liquidity distress from operations alone: liquidity of USD 324.23 million is intact, free cash flow stays positive, run-rate cover stays above 6.21x, and nothing material is due before fiscal 2031. Management levers are the discretionary repurchases, which fell from USD 309.92 million in FY2024 to USD 151.18 million in FY2026, the dividend of USD 40.53 million, capital expenditure of USD 37.91 million and selling costs. Resilience therefore appears sufficient unless financial policy turns the revolver into the funding source for shareholder returns. The primary downside path is a volume decline combined with debt-funded buybacks.

### Overall credit view

Overall, Griffon presents an adequate business profile supported by a leading garage door position, exclusive retailer supply, low capital intensity and stable free cash flow, but credit quality is constrained by concentration in two retailers and remodel demand, price-led margin erosion and a shareholder-friendly financial policy. Cash-flow durability appears strong due to operating cash flow that stayed within a range of USD 2.93 million over three years and capital expenditure of 2.05% of revenue. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on the board's restraint on repurchases, the revolver covenant headroom and continued access to the unsecured note market. The primary downside path is a volume decline combined with debt-funded buybacks. Further analysis would require the executed credit agreement and indenture, the split of profit between doors and fans, management projections, ratings and market prices. Top risk: debt-funded shareholder returns on top of weaker volume.
