## Findings

### Bottom line

Griffon Corp is a listed building-products maker with no sponsor identified, so creditor risk lies in board-controlled financial policy: shareholder returns absorbed 96.23% of three-year free cash flow and 55.42% of the disposal consideration came as non-cash claims on the buyers.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 53–54, 94–95; calculation C5; calculation C3

The continuing business is two product families, garage doors and ceiling fans, sold largely through two home-improvement retailers, with residential repair and remodel at 53.34% of revenue and a gross margin of 46.36% against 27.69% in the divested AMES business. The board alone sets buybacks, with USD 544.80 million of authority available at 2026-11-17, about 2.04x a year of free cash flow. Overall sponsor and governance risk is medium.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 20, 54, 122–123; calculation C1; calculation C2; calculation C6

### Key findings

The remaining business is more profitable but narrower, and it leans on two customers. Home Depot and Menards buy garage doors on an exclusive basis, hold 8% and 11% of net receivables, and the filing says losing either would be materially adverse; over half of revenue is repair and remodel work that follows housing turnover and consumer credit. In fiscal 2026 price and mix of 6% offset a 3% volume fall, so earnings rest on retailers accepting price increases while demand is soft.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 8, 11, 20, 45, 122–123; calculation C1

The disposals cut debt but left creditors holding junior paper. Of the headline consideration at recorded values, 44.58% was cash and 55.42% was PIK notes and equity in the buyers' vehicles. At year-end Griffon carried USD 215.58 million of PIK notes and USD 139.50 million of equity stakes, equal to 115.75% of its shareholders' equity and 20.65% of total assets. The PIK interest brings no cash, the Veritage loan is second lien, and Veritage's liabilities were 83.81% of its assets three weeks after formation, so recovery depends on partners Griffon does not control.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 71, 93–95; calculation C3; calculation C4; calculation C7

No sponsor or controlling holder is identified, so the risk is board policy rather than sponsor extraction. The company is listed and employees own about 8% through the ESOP, but the annual report does not list its largest holders because that information sits in a proxy statement that is not supplied, so a concentrated holder cannot be ruled out. No management, monitoring or advisory fees appear in the filings.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 2, 14, 41; Source readiness (CP-0)

Shareholder returns are the main financial-policy risk. Over three years Griffon returned USD 760.40 million through dividends and buybacks against free cash flow of USD 790.15 million, or 96.23%, and 71.81% of free cash flow in fiscal 2026. Returns fell each year and have been covered by free cash flow since fiscal 2025, but the repurchase authority of USD 544.80 million equals 2.04x a year of free cash flow, so debt-funded buybacks could undo the deleveraging.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 53–54, 73; calculation C5; calculation C6

The board is mostly independent but the chair and chief executive are one person. All directors except the chief executive are independent, a lead independent director and four committees operate, and the auditor gives clean opinions on the statements and on internal control. That supports monitoring, yet the same board sets dividends and buybacks at its discretion and the legal limits on them are not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 15, 41, 68

The refinancing was creditor-friendly on maturity but thin on protection. The term loan was repaid, the 2028 notes were replaced by 2034 notes and the revolver was extended to 2031, which removes near-term refinancing pressure. The new notes carry limited covenants and a higher coupon, so the revolver's maintenance tests are the main restraint on distributions and the covenant levels are not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 53, 55–57, 105

Event risk persists despite the pure-play label. Griffon divested specialty plastics in 2018 and its defense electronics business in 2022, bought CornellCookson, ClosetMaid and Hunter Fan, and in fiscal 2026 sold or closed AMES; the 2025 annual report said it intended to diversify further through acquisitions. A debt-funded acquisition would raise leverage, and the senior notes have limited covenants, so the main contractual protection is the revolver, whose test levels are not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_gff-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 5; 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5, 27

Legal and environmental claims are unquantified. The filing treats a Memphis site liability as probable without an amount, an environmental study at a New York site is due in 2027 and a New Jersey information request was received and answered, and management says none is expected to be material. For creditors this is a possible cash call with no number attached.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 118–120

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence against a cautious view is that policy has been creditor-aligned in practice: no sponsor drains cash, returns fell each year and were covered by free cash flow in fiscal 2025 and 2026, the term loan was repaid in full, maturities were extended to 2031 and 2034, and the board is independent apart from the chief executive. The PIK notes and equity are a minority of total assets at 20.65% and carry 10% PIK interest. On this evidence the risk level could be lower than medium.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 53, 55, 93–95; calculation C4

The view would turn adverse if repurchases are funded from the revolver so that debt rises, if the joint ventures show losses or capitalise rather than pay interest, if a holder concentration or activist campaign emerges, or if the indenture and credit agreement show wide capacity for distributions. It would improve if the executed documents show tight restricted payment limits and the venture accounts show service of the notes.
Basis: 10-K_2025-09-30_gff-20250930.htm.jsonl, page 44; 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 54, 93–95

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

In fiscal 2026 Griffon turned itself into a pure-play building-products issuer. On 2026-06-09 it combined AMES North America with other businesses into the Veritage Brands joint venture, receiving USD 100.00 million of cash, a USD 161.10 million second-lien paid-in-kind loan and a 43% equity interest carried at USD 118.60 million. On 2026-07-31 it sold AMES Australasia to Griffon ANZ for USD 180.91 million of cash, a paid-in-kind note of AUD 69.3 million (about USD 48.59 million) and a 49% interest carried at USD 20.90 million. AMES U.K. ceased trading on 2026-03-31 and its liquidation was substantially complete at year-end.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 5–6, 94–95

The refinancing on 2026-08-18 had two parts. Griffon issued USD 800.00 million of 6.25% senior notes due 2034 in a private placement, unsecured and guaranteed by named domestic subsidiaries, and amended and restated its credit agreement to extend the USD 500.00 million revolver from 2028 to 2031 and remove the credit spread adjustment. The new notes and revolver borrowings repaid all USD 974.78 million of the 5.75% senior notes due 2028, and the USD 449.00 million term loan was repaid in full during the year. The new notes have limited covenants while the revolver carries maintenance tests, and the covenant levels are not supplied.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 27, 53, 55–57, 104–106

After the year-end, on 2026-11-16 the board declared a dividend of USD 0.25 per share payable on 2026-12-16 and added USD 400 million to the repurchase authority, bringing the amount available at 2026-11-17 to USD 544.80 million. These are subsequent events and are not in the year-end figures.
Basis: 10-K_2026-09-30_gff-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 54
