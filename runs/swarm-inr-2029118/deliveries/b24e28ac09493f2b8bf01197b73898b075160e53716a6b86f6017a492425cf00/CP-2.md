## Findings

### Bottom line

Fundamental credit quality is adequate: reported leverage of 1.24x and USD 901.2 million of liquidity are comfortable, but operating cash flow does not fund growth spending, so unsecured noteholders rely on spending discipline and on a revolver that ranks ahead of them. Free cash flow was negative USD 61.6 million over the last twelve months, three purchasers take most revenue, and the primary downside is a commodity price fall that cuts the borrowing base while interest, preferred dividends and transport charges continue.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26–28, 60

Governance risk is medium, as the earlier business and governance work found, and no drawn debt principal falls due before the notes mature in 2031.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 28

### Key findings

Reported leverage is low and liquidity is large, but the debt figure leaves out claims that sit beside it. Total debt was USD 538.8 million at 2026-09-30 against trailing EBITDA of USD 435.4 million, 1.24x, or 1.18x net of USD 26.2 million of cash; adding the USD 336.5 million Series A preferred stock at carrying value lifts the ratio to 2.01x, and the USD 706.3 million of redeemable non-controlling interest is the Legacy Owners' claim on the same assets. The issuer reports no EBITDA, so it is operating income plus depreciation, depletion and amortization; it includes the Antero assets only from 2026-02-23 and leaves out USD 19.0 million of net derivative settlement losses over the period. Liquidity of USD 901.2 million is cash plus an undrawn revolver whose size is set by a borrowing base that lenders redetermine twice a year.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation calc-adj-lev; 10-K_2025-12-31_inr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 75; 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26–27

Cash generation does not yet cover the growth programme, which is the central weakness. Operating cash flow of USD 408.6 million over the last twelve months fell short of capital expenditure of USD 470.1 million, leaving free cash flow of negative USD 61.6 million before the USD 620.5 million paid for Antero, and management expects financing to keep funding spending through the rest of 2026. About USD 41.9 million a year of coupon on the notes and USD 28.0 million of cash preferred dividends now come before debt reduction. A large part of the spending looks discretionary: developing the proved undeveloped and non-producing reserves needs about USD 737 million over five years, or USD 147.4 million a year, against USD 470.1 million spent, although that figure excludes Antero, midstream and land and 23.23% of undeveloped acreage expires by 2030, which holds some drilling in place.

Margins are high and have held through price swings, but they rest on prices the company does not control. EBITDA was 67.97% of revenue over the last twelve months and 66.82% in the first nine months of 2026, against 65.84% in FY2023 and 64.65% in FY2024; FY2025 shows 32.45% because of USD 126.1 million of one-off non-cash IPO share compensation, and 67.82% without it, which is a normalisation by this analysis. Oil supplied 48.46% of nine-month revenue and gas and NGLs 50.14%, and the 2027 hedges cover roughly 39.98% of annualised third-quarter oil volume and 42.63% of gas volume; the hedge book was a net liability of USD 23.1 million, because oil and NGL swaps priced below current benchmarks, such as the 2027 oil swaps at USD 65.08 a barrel against USD 87.22 in the third quarter, outweigh gains on gas swaps priced above them, such as the 2027 gas swaps at USD 3.91 against USD 2.96.

Priority is the main structural weakness for the unsecured notes. The USD 875.0 million revolver carries first-priority liens on substantially all assets and is 1.59 times the USD 550.0 million notes; if it were fully drawn, total debt would be USD 1,425.0 million, 3.27x trailing EBITDA, against a leverage test of 3.0x whose definition differs from the one used here, so headroom cannot be calculated. Reported net debt of USD 512.6 million would reach 3.00x if trailing EBITDA fell 60.75%, to USD 170.9 million, so the cushion on reported figures is wide, but the borrowing base, set by lenders at their sole discretion, decides how much of the revolver can be drawn.

Refinancing risk is low until 2028 and then depends on the revolver. The business is larger but concentrated in one basin and a few buyers. Net production rose 88% in the nine months of 2026, and the third-quarter rate was about double that of a year earlier, yet three purchasers took 84% of FY2025 revenue and all production is in Appalachia, where gas prices were weak in the second and third quarters of 2026 on mild weather and higher output. Proved reserves are 67.90% gas by energy content while 48.46% of revenue is oil, so cash flow follows crude prices and reserve value follows gas prices.

The primary downside path is a price fall that arrives with a borrowing base cut while fixed charges continue. Lower oil and gas realisations reduce EBITDA and the reserve value behind the base; the lenders can cut the base at their discretion and could require immediate repayment of any excess, while interest, the preferred dividend, firm transportation charges and the spending committed to hold acreage do not fall at the same pace, so liquidity shrinks faster than leverage rises. The levers are slower spending as operator, payment in kind of the preferred dividend until the second anniversary of issue, and the hedges, which cover about two-fifths of 2027 volumes.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a better view is balance sheet strength and margin: reported leverage of 1.24x, USD 901.2 million of liquidity, an undrawn revolver, EBITDA margins near two-thirds, no principal due for more than four years and spending that is largely at management's discretion, with run-rate fixed charges of USD 74.3 million covered 5.86x by trailing EBITDA. It would earn a stronger view if free cash flow turned positive with spending held near the development need of the proved reserves, the autumn borrowing base held at USD 875.0 million, the facility maturity were extended, and the material weaknesses were reported as remediated. The view would weaken if the borrowing base were cut while prices fell, if free cash flow stayed negative into 2027 with the preferred dividend paid in cash, if the widened restricted-payment capacity were used for payments to equity, or if run-rate earnings for the Antero assets proved lower than the trailing figures imply.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation calc-fixed; calculation calc-capex-need; 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26–27, 37

### Business and ownership

Infinity is an Appalachian oil, gas and NGL producer that owns gathering and water assets, and it reached its present scale through the 2026 Antero purchase. The listed parent owns 31.8% of INR Holdings, which is the borrower and the notes issuer, so creditors lend to a subsidiary of a holding company whose preferred stock and sponsor holders sit above and beside the debt. This matters for credit because cash can reach the parent and its preferred holders only through distributions that the credit agreement restricts and that a June 2026 amendment widened; it supports cash flow visibility through scale but weakens the clarity of who is paid first.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26, 38–39

Ownership and control are concentrated: Pearl, NGP, Quantum and Carnelian held 68.8% of the voting power at 2026-03-05, Pearl nominates four directors, the preferred holders hold consent rights over dividends, debt above an unstated threshold and hedging, and there are three stacked change-of-control claims. This gives stability of strategy and an event risk at a sale, and the documents that would show limits on payments to equity and guarantees were not supplied, so willingness to extract cash is not inferred from identity.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_inr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 78, 82

### Revenue, cost and cash conversion

Revenue is cyclical and price-taking rather than contracted: oil, gas and NGL sales are priced off market indices with differentials, most contracts last longer than twelve months, and the mix is oil-led while reserves are gas-led, so the revenue stream does not carry the visibility of contracted income. Three purchasers took 84% of FY2025 revenue, the company states that losing one would not be material, and growth has come from drilling and Antero rather than price. The credit consequence is that debt service capacity moves with prices and that concentration is a monitoring point, not a loss estimate.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_inr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 24, 157

Costs are mostly volume-linked with fixed elements that are growing. Per-unit lease operating cost was USD 2.07 per Boe and gathering, processing and transport cost USD 4.25 per Boe in FY2025, direct operating expenses rose to USD 1.28 per Mcfe in nine months 2026 from USD 1.23, and firm transportation added about USD 0.15 per Mcfe in the third quarter, a fixed charge that applies whatever the volumes. Margins can be defended against modest price falls through the low cash cost base, but the fixed transport charge, interest and preferred dividend lift operating leverage if volumes or differentials disappoint.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_inr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 23; 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 43, 53, 55

Capital intensity is high: capital expenditure was 103.52% of revenue in FY2025 and 72.68% in nine months 2026, and operating cash flow covered 86.90% of capital expenditure over the last twelve months. EBITDA converts to free cash flow only after growth spending is cut, so durable cash for debt reduction depends on moving from development of new locations toward the proved development need and on the roughly USD 147.4 million a year that development requires.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); calculation calc-capex-need; calculation calc-ocf-capex; 10-K_2025-12-31_inr-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 51

### Industry forces and macro

Rivalry is intense for acreage, services and capital, and larger competitors can pay more, which limits Infinity's ability to add inventory on good terms. Buyer power is concentrated in three customers but softened by fungible products; supplier power shows in rig, crew and service costs and in transport pipelines the company does not control, which can raise cost or interrupt sales. The threat of new entrants is low where acreage is held by production, with roughly 82% of Ohio acreage held, and substitutes matter mainly through gas competing with other fuels and through energy-transition policy. For credit, rivalry and supplier cost raise the cost of growth and transport constrains realisations rather than creating default risk by themselves.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_inr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 17, 47, 62–63, 69

Political, regulatory and economic factors are material: hydraulic fracturing and water rules, climate policy, oil prices that ranged from USD 60.04 to USD 102.13 a barrel in 2026 amid geopolitical conflict, inflation in service costs and floating-rate borrowing costs with no interest rate hedge all reach margins, spending and liquidity. Social and technological factors do not appear to be a primary credit driver on the materials provided. The credit consequence is volatility in cash flow and in the borrowing base rather than a structural impairment.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 46, 71; 10-K_2025-12-31_inr-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 47, 66

Credit-translated strengths are scale, margin, liquidity, distant maturities and operator control; weaknesses are negative free cash flow, notes behind the revolver, concentrated customers and governance; opportunities are the integration of Antero assets and midstream control that could lower costs, as management says, and threats are a lower gas price, a smaller borrowing base and use of widened restricted-payment capacity.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_inr-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 26, 45–47, 60

### Financial profile

The nine dimensions of the financial profile resolve as follows. Scale and market position is average, because production has nearly doubled but the company is single-basin with a small workforce. Competitive advantage is average, resting on contiguous acreage, owned midstream and operator control rather than contracts. Business diversification is weak, with one basin, three buyers and oil-and-gas-price exposure. Cost and capex flexibility is average, because the development-need gap allows cuts but acreage that must be drilled sets a floor. Margin stability is average, with margins near two-thirds that depend on prices. Free cash flow stability is weak, at negative USD 61.6 million trailing. Ability to refinance is average, helped by distant maturities and an undrawn revolver but dependent on the borrowing base and on market access, which the USD 550.0 million notes issue of March 2026 shows was open then. Liquidity position is strong at USD 901.2 million, subject to the base. Financial policy and governance is average, with sponsor control, preferred claims and unremediated control weaknesses offset by an equity-supported record.

### Outlook

Over the next 12 to 24 months the third-quarter production rate of 431.7 MMcfe per day, the first two full quarters of Antero ownership and 2027 hedges at USD 65.08 for oil and USD 3.91 for gas support cash flow, while headwinds are weak gas prices, hedge losses on oil, preferred dividends in cash, the autumn base redetermination and a facility maturity in September 2028 if not extended. ### Downside scenario

The scenario starts with a gas price near the second- and third-quarter 2026 benchmarks of USD 2.90 and USD 2.96 per MMBtu persisting into 2027 while oil falls back toward the hedged level of USD 65.08, which is lower than the third-quarter benchmark of USD 87.22 per barrel. Management's levers are to slow spending as operator, pay the preferred dividend in kind until its second anniversary and stop repurchases; on reported figures liquidity of USD 901.2 million, unchanged debt maturities and the wide EBITDA cushion suggest resilience is sufficient to avoid distress within 12 to 24 months, but the margin falls if the base and prices fall together.
