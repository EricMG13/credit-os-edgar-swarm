## Findings

### Bottom line
Excelerate's credit is adequate: contracted take-or-pay income and moderate leverage support debt service, but negative 2026 free cash flow, concentrated state-linked customers and delayed deployments leave creditors reliant on the undrawn revolver and unproven covenant headroom.

Net debt was 1.53x last-twelve-months EBITDA at 2026-09-30, or 1.55x pro forma for the dividend and distribution payable on 2026-12-03, and EBITDA covered cash interest 4.45x. Free cash flow was negative USD 59.10 million in the nine months, but operating cash flow still exceeded maintenance capital spending by USD 220.92 million, so the weakness is growth spending, not core earnings, and the next growth costs are not stated.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation F01; calculation F03; calculation F04; calculation C01

### Key findings

- The business profile is adequate, anchored on contracts rather than scale: terminal services were 45.70% of nine-month 2026 revenue and remaining performance obligations were USD 18,329.6 million at 2026-09-30. At the 2025 year-end terminal charters held minimum contracted cash flows of USD 3,315.2 million over a weighted average 5.8 years, against USD 17,031.2 million for LNG, gas and power contracts, which are 83.71% of the combined USD 20,346.4 million. Most long-dated contract value is therefore gas supply with commodity pass-through and counterparty exposure, so headline visibility overstates the protected terminal income.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ee-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 28; 10-K_2025-12-31_ee-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; calculation F06; calculation C10
- Leverage is moderate and has risen from net cash: total debt was 2.28x last-twelve-months EBITDA and net debt 1.53x at 2026-09-30, or 1.55x pro forma for the payment due 2026-12-03. On the issuer's adjusted EBITDA the figures are lower, so the choice of EBITDA does not change the conclusion.

Operating cash flow still exceeded maintenance capital spending by USD 220.92 million, and the final payment of about USD 210 million on the Excelerate Acadia terminal is complete, but the carrier balance and conversion contract that follow have no stated cost.

### Contrary evidence and what would change the view

The strongest evidence for a more favourable view is that the cash weakness reflects growth timing: operating cash flow exceeded maintenance capital spending by USD 220.92 million over nine months and by USD 312.40 million over twelve months, most capital spending is growth, many older contracts have been extended, the issuer states covenant compliance and none of the debt matures before 2029 apart from scheduled amortisation. If the Iraq, Colombia and conversion projects deliver contracted earnings on time, leverage should fall from 1.53x without new borrowing.
The strongest evidence for a weaker view is that protections cannot be verified: covenant levels are dated, an amendment is undescribed, customers are concentrated and sovereign-linked, and the carrier and conversion costs are unknown. The view would improve with executed documents showing wide headroom against a 3.25x distribution gate and the revolver tests, a contract schedule with long remaining terms, and on-time starts for Iraq and Colombia. It would worsen if the Iraq start slipped again, a state customer paid late, force majeure became termination, or free cash flow stayed negative after growth spending eased.
Basis: Financial foundation (CP-1); Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-K_2025-12-31_ee-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 9; 10-K_2025-12-31_ee-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 84; 10-K_2025-12-31_ee-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 103; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ee-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 19; calculation C01; calculation C02

### Limitations

- Covenant levels come from the 2025 annual report and an undescribed seventh amendment may have changed them, so the view that leverage is moderate is relative to the market and not to covenant headroom, which stays not established.
- Contract expiries and customer names are not available, so the adequate revenue durability rests on weighted average terms; a cluster of near-term expiries would lower the profile and the liquidity view.
- The cost of the carrier purchase and the conversion contract is not stated, so the adequate liquidity assessment excludes a funding call that could be material in 2027 and would reduce it.
- No rating, market price or peer data was supplied, so the business profile is judged against the filings alone and its standing against comparable issuers is not established.

### Strengths and weaknesses
Strengths, ranked. First, long-term take-or-pay terminal and supply contracts: remaining performance obligations of USD 18,329.6 million and a 5.8-year weighted average remaining terminal term give visible cash flow, which supports debt service and refinancing confidence. Fourth, a redeployable fleet of 12 floating regasification terminals, one onshore terminal and two LNG carriers at 2026-09-30, which lets a terminal move to a new contract, as Excelerate Acadia did to Jordan, and supports recovery value.

### Company description and credit linkage
Excelerate owns and operates floating regasification terminals, an onshore terminal, LNG carriers and a combined heat and power plant, and sells LNG, gas and power. Its history of acquisition is short: a public listing in 2022, the purchase of the Sequoia terminal in 2023 and the Jamaica business in 2025. Visibility comes from fixed-rate take-or-pay charters under which customers generally bear fuel, port fees and boil-off, which supports debt service, but contracts can end on breach, change in law or extended force majeure, so visibility is conditional. Concentration is high and cyclicality sits in the LNG, gas and power line, which was 54.30% of nine-month revenue.

### Ownership and group structure
The registrant is a holding company with a controlling interest in the operating partnership, which borrows and issues the notes; 72.5% of the votes and of the partnership interests sit with a holder controlled by Kaiser. This is a controlled company relying on exemptions, with pro rata distributions, tax receivable agreement payments and a related-party sale-leaseback financing. Financial policy is moderate on evidence: payouts of USD 76.40 million in nine months (buybacks, dividends and distributions) sit within revolver restricted-payment limits of leverage at most 3.25x with liquidity of at least USD 200.0 million, or up to 3.0% of equity value a year, although the capacity used is not shown, and no dividend recapitalisation, priming or creditor exchange appears in the filings; the Sponsor and governance risk level from the earlier analysis is Medium. The creditor risk is leakage capacity, not demonstrated leakage, and the notes are guaranteed only by certain restricted subsidiaries.

### Revenue durability
Revenue is recurring in terminals and cyclical and concentrated in gas supply. Terminal charters are fixed-rate with escalators, customers generally bear fuel and port costs, many older contracts have already been extended and all owned floating terminals were contracted at 2025-12-31, so terminal income is predictable but terminable on stated events. LNG, gas and power revenue follows commodity prices and a few customers, and Qatar supply is under force majeure. Credit linkage: contracted income supports debt service as long as counterparties pay, while pass-through revenue inflates the denominator of any revenue-based ratio.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ee-20251231.htm.jsonl, pages 9–10; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ee-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 27; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ee-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36; Business & governance (CP-1A); calculation C10

### Margin resilience
Adjusted EBITDA rose from USD 129.3 million to USD 133.5 million between the third quarters (about 3.21%) even though LNG, gas and power revenue fell on fewer short-term sales and the timing of deliveries under the Atlantic Basin agreement, which cost USD 11.0 million, because the Jordan charter (USD 13.9 million) and short-term LNG opportunities (USD 6.1 million) more than offset it. Terminal margins are shielded from fuel and port costs that customers generally bear; LNG margins depend on back-to-back pricing that the issuer describes but the filings do not quantify. Business development costs rose by USD 3.4 million, so margins can be defended against volume timing but not shown to be defended against a protracted supply interruption.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ee-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 39; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ee-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 43–44; calculation C07

### Cash conversion and capital intensity
Last-twelve-months operating cash flow was almost the same as capital spending (1.03x), so free cash flow was thin, while maintenance spending was small: 33.585 of 320.278 gross in nine months (10.49%). Cash interest paid took 22.46% of last-twelve-months EBITDA, and the notes' annual coupon of USD 64.0 million is paid in May and November. Capex flexibility is real because most spending is growth and the largest 2026 outflow, the Acadia milestone, is complete, but projects with signed contracts (Iraq, Colombia, the carrier and conversion) are committed.

### Porter's five forces
Rivalry is moderate: the issuer competes with other floating terminal owners, LNG sellers and LNG-to-power developers, and awards turn on experience, delivery speed and price. Entry is possible: the annual report notes limited availability of floating terminals in recent years but expects state-sponsored entities and major energy companies may buy, build or convert terminals, which would pressure rates on new and renewed contracts. The filings do not rank substitutes; the issuer's own example is gas displacing coal in Pakistan and Bangladesh. Buyer power is high because customers are few and state-linked, offset by take-or-pay terms and early-termination fees. Supplier power is high for LNG supply, as the Qatar force majeure shows, and for shipyard capacity, which drives the delivery dates of new terminals.
Basis: 10-K_2025-12-31_ee-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 14; 10-K_2025-12-31_ee-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 28; 10-Q_2026-09-30_ee-20260930.htm.jsonl, page 36

### PEST factors
Political and regulatory factors are a primary driver here: the Middle East conflict, shipping through the Strait of Hormuz and sovereign counterparties directly changed the Iraq timetable, Qatar supply and the Colombia plan. Economic factors matter through LNG prices (the Dutch gas benchmark averaged USD 17.62 per MMBtu and the Japan-Korea benchmark USD 19.77 in the third quarter, up from USD 15.61 and USD 17.65 in the second) and foreign currency fluctuations, which the annual report names as a risk. Social and technological factors are secondary; the planned reliquefaction unit on one terminal is an operating improvement, not a credit driver.
Basis: 10-Q_2026-09-30_ee-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 36–38; 10-K_2025-12-31_ee-20251231.htm.jsonl, page 72; calculation C12

### SWOT
Strengths are the contracts, a redeployable fleet and a record of extensions; weaknesses are customer concentration, negative free cash flow and structural subordination. Opportunities that could improve credit quality are the Iraq, Colombia and Bahamas contracts and the conversion project, which add contracted earnings from 2027 and 2028, while the Sequoia extension through 2038 lengthens an existing contract. Threats are prolonged conflict, force majeure turning into termination, counterparty payment delay and any loosening of creditor protections through further amendments.
Basis: Business & governance (CP-1A); 10-Q_2026-09-30_ee-20260930.htm.jsonl, pages 36–37

### Outlook, tailwinds and headwinds
Over twelve to twenty-four months tailwinds are the Colombia charter and Bahamas deliveries from the first quarter of 2027 and the Iraq start in the second quarter of 2027; headwinds are the Gulf conflict, the Qatar force majeure, the end of the nine-month Jordan charter that began in July 2026 unless it is extended, the carrier remainder due in the first quarter of 2027 and the conversion spending. Global LNG trade held up at about 101 million tonnes in the third quarter of 2026 against 106 in the third quarter of 2025, so import demand is resilient but supply is tight. Over three to five years the credit events are the 2029 revolver maturity and the May 2030 notes. Sector headwinds are price volatility and state counterparties; company headwinds are covenant uncertainty and control.

### Downside scenario
An issuer-specific downside begins with the Gulf conflict continuing past the revised dates: the Iraq terminal slips again and Qatar supply stays under force majeure, interrupting LNG sales to Bangladesh. First break: LNG, gas and power margins and operating cash flow fall while committed growth spending on the carrier and conversion continues. Financial transmission: free cash flow stays negative, cash declines and the company draws the revolver, which lifts net leverage from 1.53x. Refinancing: the notes mature in May 2030 and a weaker leverage position raises refinancing cost, while distributions are gated at 3.25x leverage. Management levers are deferring growth spending, suspending buybacks and distributions and extending contracts; resilience looks sufficient to avoid liquidity distress in the next twelve months because maintenance spending is small, but not shown beyond that. Observable triggers are quarterly operating cash flow, revolver drawings and customer payment timing; no numerical stress is established.

### Overall credit view
Overall, Excelerate presents an adequate business profile supported by long-term take-or-pay charters, a redeployable fleet and moderate leverage, but credit quality is constrained by negative free cash flow in 2026, concentrated state-linked customers, structural subordination of the unsecured notes and control by one holder. Cash-flow durability appears adequate before growth spending and weak after it. Refinancing capacity is likely to depend on market conditions in 2030, when most principal falls due, and on whether the Iraq and Colombia contracts start on time. The primary downside path is continued Gulf disruption delaying contracted earnings while growth spending drains cash. Further analysis would require executed debt documents, covenant compliance figures, contract expiries and the cost of the carrier and conversion. Fundamental credit completed. Top risk: deployment delay and negative free cash flow.
